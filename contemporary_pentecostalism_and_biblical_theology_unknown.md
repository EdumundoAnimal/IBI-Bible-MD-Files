# Contemporary Pentecostalism and Biblical Theology
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text examines the theological underpinnings of Pentecostalism, emphasizing the role of religious experience as a primary source for understanding God, often prioritizing encounter over intellectual doctrine. It covers the historical evolution of Pentecostal stages of grace (from the Holiness Movement's three-stage view to the Finished Work Doctrine's two-stage view), the debate surrounding the 'baptism in the Spirit', and the distinct 'Oneness' theology. Furthermore, it analyzes Pauline perspectives on spiritual gifts (charismata) in 1 Corinthians, arguing for the necessity of edification within the church and the importance of intelligible communication.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theology of Encounter
Pentecostal theology prioritizes experiential encounters with God over cerebral, systematic doctrine. It posits that theological understanding follows the act of God.

### Theme: Spirit-led Epistemology
An approach to interpreting scripture that emphasizes the necessity of the Holy Spirit for understanding, while warning against subjective interpretation that ignores the text's objective meaning.

### Theme: Oneness Theology
A theological framework ('Jesus Name' Pentecostalism) rejecting Trinitarian doctrine as non-scriptural, asserting that Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are personal manifestations of one indivisible God.

### Theme: Charismata and Edification
The Pauline view that spiritual gifts must be evaluated based on their utility for edifying the Body of Christ and their intelligibility to the community.
