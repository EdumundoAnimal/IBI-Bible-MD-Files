# Hebrews 5:11-6:20 Study Notes
**Author:** [[Paul F Perry]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Introductory comments to this class outline: Our class today covers a portion of scripture that has produced much discussion. These scriptures contain texts whose interpretation are widely debated within the Christian church and have been so since its early beginnings. These notes are designed to give a starting point for further discussion of these texts within the context of the Hebrews course. As such they do not constitute a completely developed analysis at all points of the arguments below, but rather introduce broad overviews of some of the various interpretative suggestions. The importance of Hearing and obedience. The author of Hebrews was about to explore the central and deep message of Christ's priesthood and his sacrifice. However, he had to prepare them first as he could not speak to them intelligibly (deeply) since they had become hard of hearing. They were not listening, and as a result they had become sluggish in understanding. Hearing the word = central topic throughout the sermon (2:1-4; 3:7-19; 4:12-13). Hearing and obedience are closely linked in Hebrews. "Hearing" denotes the person's complete response to the message of God, and it is sluggishness with regard to this wholehearted response that is in view throughout 5:11-6:12. This correlation between listening and obedience explains why the preacher prepares his friends for what he has to say about Jesus as our priest and sacrifice by the strong words of 5:11-6:20. This section serves to introduce the central division of the sermon. It is balanced by a later section in which the truths developed in the central division will be applied to the situation of the congregation. The structure of the development in the central division can be exhibited in a chart. The section consists of two major units. 1. He explores first the peril of spiritual immaturity (5:11-6:12). The variation in tone is calculated to engage the attention of the listeners in the development. In the second unit of the season (6:13-20) a basis for steadfastness is shown to exist in the reliability of God's promise. The pastor thus holds before his friends two options: peril or promise. The initial paragraph, extending from 5:11-14, is sharply critical in tone. Prior to addressing the important themes of Christ as our priest and sacrifice the preacher must speak solemnly and candidly to these friends for whom he was so deeply concerned. The difficulty of the teaching he has to share with them is difficult. But compounding the difficulty is the fact that his friends have become hard of hearing (5:11). The close proximity of 5:9 and 5:11 underscores the importance of listening as a prerequisite to the obedience we have been called to render unto Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spiritual Immaturity
The author argues that sluggishness in listening and obedience leads to spiritual immaturity, creating a barrier to understanding deep theological truths about Christ's priesthood.

### Theme: Hearing and Obedience
A core premise where 'hearing' is defined not as passive reception but as a wholehearted response to God's word, intrinsically linked to obedience.

### Theme: Peril vs. Promise
The preacher presents the congregation with two paths: the peril of falling away due to sluggishness or the promise of steadfastness anchored in God's reliability.
