# An Introduction to the Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Gospel of John is an anonymous work that explores the identity of Jesus Christ. Tradition credits John the son of Zebedee, but historical debates about authorship and the reliability of the narrative persisted until modern times. The document examines the 'beloved disciple', the unique theological focus on 'signs' (semeion), and the structural layout of the book, which includes the 'Book of Signs' and the 'Book of the Passion'. The Gospel emphasizes 'belief' and uses a series of dualistic opposites to illustrate spiritual truths. Structurally, the document highlights the transition from a public ministry to the Jews toward private ministry with the disciples.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Authorship
Historical debate surrounds the identity of the author, balancing early church tradition (John son of Zebedee) with modern scholarly questioning regarding the text's anonymity and historical reliability.

### Theme: Theological Signs
The Gospel records seven specific miracles (signs) intended to prove that Jesus is the Son of God, leading to belief and life in His name.

### Theme: Literary Structure
The text utilizes a series of 'opposites' (e.g., life vs. death, light vs. dark) and is divided into the 'Book of Signs' and the 'Book of the Passion' to present a unified theological narrative.

### Theme: Historical Progression
A notable shift occurs in the Gospel narrative where Jesus' public ministry to the Jews diminishes while His private relationship and instruction with the disciples increase.
