### Historical/Cultural Context

Firstly, we must examine the historical and cultural circumstances at the time the biblical text under examination was written. We must remember the historical and cultural gap. Each passage was God's Word to other people before it became God's Word to us. Klein, Blomberg & Hubbard: 'Each passage was God's word to other people before it became God's word to us. In a sense, the Bible always comes to us secondhand, through others who lived at different times and in different places'.

### Example of broad historical context: Zech. 7

We will be using Zechariah 7 as a test case for the next three sessions. Read together to get the feel of it. (NIV). Author - Zechariah: means 'Yahweh remembers'; priestly family, the son of Berechiah and descendant of Iddo; called to be a prophet - there was obviously at this point priestly men who were also prophetic. Recipients - people of Israel, most likely in Jerusalem where Zechariah would have been stationed as a priest most likely. Date - first date is given in 1:1, 2nd year of Darius, or the 18th year after the exile had ended by decree of Cyrus. Thus Zech. is a POST-EXILIC BOOK - very important. Context in Old Testament - The book of Zechariah takes place after the return of Israel from exile, most likely during the time of Ezra, where Zechariah is mentioned as the son of Berechiah and descendant of Iddo. The temple has been rebuilt as people are coming there to worship and sacrifice. At end of OT history. Politics - In 538 BCE King Cyrus of Persia conquered Babylon and published a decree allowing exiles from many countries, including Judah, to return home. The Jews had permission to rebuild the temple at Jerusalem (Ezr. 1:1-4).

### Example of specific historical context: Zech. 7

Purpose - What fast are the people asking about? The specific fast in question was instituted during exile to commemorate the burning of the temple in 587 B.C., as described in 2 Kings 25. This fast would have been done while in exile to remember the loss of the temple, which was destroyed by the Babylonians. Purpose - Exile is over, people are trying to figure out how to move on; Temple which was destroyed is now built again - should they still fast over its destruction? Seems like a reasonable question... Bethel: Important city of Israelite history. Since Jacob had been place of meeting with God, and had history as a place of worship. During divided kingdom was the spot of worship in the northern half. Jerusalem, surrounding towns, Negev, foothills: A reminder of the times when all of Israel flourished and they had control of the entire land. Jerusalem at this point would have been a far cry from the royal city it had once been. Widows, orphans/fatherless, strangers/aliens, poor - Common terms used to describe those who tended to be on the margins of society as this was a patriarchal society. Often used in connection with showing mercy and extending God's righteousness to all.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Historical Context
The necessity of examining the historical and cultural circumstances surrounding the original writing of a biblical text to bridge the historical and cultural gap between the author and the modern reader.

### Theme: The Secondhand Nature of Scripture
The argument that because the Bible was written to specific people in specific times and places, it reaches modern readers 'secondhand', requiring careful historical reconstruction to understand the original intent.

### Theme: Levels of Context
The interpretive framework consisting of historical context (world of the author), literary context (world of the text), and theological context (the God factor).

### Theme: Prophetic Interpretation
The distinction between 'foretelling' and 'forthtelling' as a key principle for interpreting the genre of prophecy.
