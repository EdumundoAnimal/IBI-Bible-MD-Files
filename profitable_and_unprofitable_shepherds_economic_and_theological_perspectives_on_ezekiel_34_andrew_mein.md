# Profitable and Unprofitable Shepherds: Economic and Theological Perspectives on Ezekiel 34
**Author:** [[Andrew Mein]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article examines Ezekiel 34's good shepherd metaphor in the light of the economics of animal husbandry in ancient Near East. In 34.1-16, YHWH is presented as the owner of the sheep, whose hired hands, the shepherds of Israel, have failed to perform their duties of care and protection. In economic terms, the problems are those of misappropriation of property and the failure to produce an adequate profit, and the disadvantage to the owner is of more significance than the suffering of the sheep. The theological consequences of this reading is that commentators may be mistaken in seeing YHWH's recovery of the flock as motivated by selflessness and compassion for Israel. Rather, YHWH's recovery and protection of his flock is a piece of the puzzle with the emphasis on divine power and self-interest found throughout Ezekiel's restoration oracles.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Economic Metaphor of Shepherding
The article interprets the 'good shepherd' imagery in Ezekiel 34 not merely as a pastoral ideal but as a reflection of ancient Near Eastern economic practices regarding animal husbandry, where the shepherd acts as a hired agent accountable to the owner (YHWH).

### Theme: Divine Self-Interest
The author argues that YHWH's restoration of Israel is motivated by divine prerogative and ownership rights rather than solely by altruistic compassion for the people, positioning the shepherds' failure as a breach of contract.

### Theme: Theological Re-evaluation
By analyzing the text through the lens of economic accountability and legal property relations, the author challenges the traditional theological assumption that YHWH's primary concern in this passage is the immediate emotional comfort of the sheep.
