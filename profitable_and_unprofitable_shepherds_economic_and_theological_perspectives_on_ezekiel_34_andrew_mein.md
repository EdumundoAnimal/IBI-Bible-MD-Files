# Profitable and Unprofitable Shepherds: Economic and Theological Perspectives on Ezekiel 34
**Author:** [[Andrew Mein]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article examines Ezekiel 34, specifically the "good shepherd" metaphor in light of ancient Mesopotamian animal husbandry. It explores how the prophet utilizes this common ancient Near Eastern practice to articulate a theological argument about YHWH's relationship with Israel. The author argues that Ezekiel uses the economic relationship between a shepherd and an owner as an analogy for the relationship between YHWH and the human leaders of Israel. The failure of human leaders to protect and care for the flock results in YHWH taking back direct responsibility for the people, emphasizing restoration as an act of divine compassion.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Shepherd Metaphor
The use of the shepherd-flock dynamic as a framework for understanding divine leadership versus human, failed leadership in the Old Testament.

### Theme: Economic Realism
The integration of historical and economic practices of ancient Near Eastern animal husbandry to contextualize the prophet's theological metaphors.

### Theme: Divine Restoration
The theological argument that when human leaders fail, YHWH intervenes directly to care for and restore the community, motivated by divine self-interest and compassion for the name.
