# Profitable and Unprofitable Shepherds: Economic and Theological Perspectives on Ezekiel 34
**Author:** [[Andrew Mein]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article examines Ezekiel 34's 'good shepherd' metaphor in the light of the economics of animal husbandry in ancient Near East and Mesopotamia. In 34.1-16, YHWH is presented as the owner of sheep, whose hired hands, the 'shepherds of Israel', have failed to perform their duties of care and protection. In economic terms, the problems are those of misappropriation of property and the failure to produce an adequate profit, and the disadvantage to the owner is of more significance than the suffering of the sheep. The theological conclusion of this reading is that commentators may be mistaken in seeing YHWH's recovery of the flock in ch. 34 as motivated by selflessness and compassion for Israel. Rather, YHWH's recovery and protection of his flock is a piece of the work with the emphasis on divine power and self-interest found throughout Ezekiel's restoration oracles.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Economic Metaphor of Shepherding
Analysis of how Ezekiel 34 uses the imagery of animal husbandry, accounting practices, and the roles of hired shepherds versus property owners to critique the leadership of Israel.

### Theme: Radical Theocentrism
The argument that YHWH's actions in restoration oracles are driven by divine self-interest and the protection of His property/name rather than purely by compassion for the people.
