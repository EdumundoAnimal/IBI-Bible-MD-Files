### 1. Linguistic & Syntax Report

Textual Variants & Text-Critical Apparatus The Masoretic Text (MT) of Psalm 122 preserved in the Leningrad Codex (B19A) aligns closely with the major witnesses, with select notable variations: ● Superscription (v. 1a): MT reads ד ִו ָד ְל) lə-Dāwīd, "Of David"). The Septuagint (LXX 121:1) includes τῷ Δαυιδ. However, several Greek minuscules, the Syriac Peshitta, and Codex Alexandrinus omit the Davidic attribution, treating it as an anonymous Shir HaMa'alot. 11QPs^a (11Q5) preserves Psalm 122 with the Davidic superscription intact. ● v. 3 (שֶׁ חֻ בְּ רָ ה־לָ ּה יַחְּ דָ ו): MT preserves the relative prefix שֶׁ־ (še-), characteristic of Northern dialect, vernacular Late Biblical Hebrew (LBH), or archaic poetic colloquialism. LXX translates ἧς ἡ μετοχὴ αὐτῆς ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό ("whose participation is in the same place"), reflecting a reading focused on communion and social participation rather than purely physical masonry. ● v. 4 (עֵ דּות לְּ יִשְּ רָ אֵ ל): MT vocalizes עֵ דּות ('ēdût, "testimony / ordinance"), treated syntactically as an adverbial accusative of manner or an appositional parenthetical clause ("as an ordinance for Israel"; cf. Exod 23:17; Deut 16:16). LXX reads μαρτύριον τῷ Ἰσραήλ. Targum Psalms renders this as "the testimony of the law which He testified to Israel." ● v. 6 (יִשְּ לָ יּו אֹהֲ בָ יְִך): MT provides the 3rd person masculine plural imperfect יִשְ לָ יּו (yišlāyû, "may they prosper / be secure", from šālâ). A minority of medieval Kennicott manuscripts read יִשְ לָ מּו (yišlāmû, "may they have peace"), likely an assimilatory scribal correction to match the dominant š-l-m root throughout verses 6–8.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Fellowship
The document argues that identity is fundamentally relational and communal, anchored in the 'compact joining together' of the people of God, contrasting with modern expressive individualism.

### Theme: Eschatological Zion
Zion is presented as a sacred polis centered on the transcendent presence of God, where justice replaces oppression and peace replaces exploitation, serving as a canonical trajectory from the Torah to the New Jerusalem.

### Theme: Davidic Christology
The 'thrones of the house of David' are interpreted as the institutional justification for Jerusalem's role, fulfilled in the spiritual kingdom of Jesus Christ, who is the true Davidic King.
