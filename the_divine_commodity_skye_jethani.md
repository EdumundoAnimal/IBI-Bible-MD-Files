# The Divine Commodity
**Author:** [[Skye Jethani]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Divine Commodity explores how modern consumer culture has fundamentally reshaped American Christianity. The text posits that institutions and individuals have drifted from traditional theological commitments toward a brand-oriented faith, where religious identity is constructed through the consumption of commercialized religious goods. Drawing on examples ranging from Air Jordan sneakers to the 'WWJD' phenomenon, the author critiques how the marketing of identity conflicts with the biblical call to self-denial and the transformation of the heart. The book contrasts this 'Consumer Christianity' with the lives of figures like Vincent van Gogh, who modeled a faith defined by suffering and authentic connection rather than external branding or material status.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumer Christianity
The critique of how modern churches and individuals use market logic, branding, and commercial products to define religious identity rather than theological substance.

### Theme: Identity and Branding
An analysis of how commodities serve as artificial markers of status and internal worth, which creates a false sense of security and belonging.

### Theme: The Theology of Suffering
The argument that authentic Christian growth requires moving away from the culture of immediate gratification toward accepting trials and suffering as tools for spiritual formation.
