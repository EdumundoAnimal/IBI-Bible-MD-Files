# Hermeneutics (BI 115) Course Syllabus
**Author:** [[Bradford Anderson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Carraig Eden Theological College Hermeneutics (BI 115) First Semester 2008/09. Course Instructor: Bradford Anderson. Level: 4. Credits: 10. Learning Hours: 100 hours made up as follows: 20 hours lectures, tutorials and seminars; 15 hours of reading assigned material; 40 hours preparation of practical assignments; 1 1/2 hours examination; 23 1/2 hours revision for examination. Aim(s): The aim of this module is to enable the student to conduct biblical exegesis and understand how a given passage applies in a contemporary setting. Learning Outcomes: Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to: A. understand, in general, the history of hermeneutics; B. define hermeneutics, recognise the need for study of hermeneutics and demonstrate an understanding of the nature of such a study; C. explain the basic features of the grammatical, historical method of interpretation and demonstrate how this method is applied; D. demonstrate an understanding of the operation of figures of speech and literary devices such as riddles, fables, allegory, proverbs, types and parables; E. demonstrate a basic knowledge of the rules and guidelines relating to the interpretation of different genres of biblical literature. Assessment: I. Essay (50%) - The student will complete an essay of no more than 1,250 words excluding footnotes and bibliography. II. Examination (50%) - Each student must take a final comprehensive examination lasting 1 1/2 hours on Wednesday 17 December at 09:30.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Exegesis
The course emphasizes the methodology of interpreting biblical texts, specifically focusing on the grammatical-historical method and understanding how to apply ancient texts to modern contexts.

### Theme: Literary Criticism
Exploration of literary devices such as parables, allegories, and riddles to decode the intended meaning behind biblical genres.

### Theme: Hermeneutical Theory
A study of the history and definition of hermeneutics, providing a framework for students to interpret scripture systematically.
