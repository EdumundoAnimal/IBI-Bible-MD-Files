# Hermeneutics (BI 115) Course Syllabus
**Author:** [[Bradford Anderson]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Course Information

Carraig Eden Theological College Hermeneutics (BI 115) First Semester 2008/09 Course Instructor: Bradford Anderson Level: 4 Credits: 10 Learning Hours: 100 hours made up as follows: 20 hours lectures, tutorials and seminars 15 hours of reading assigned material 40 hours preparation of practical assignments 1½ hours examination 23½ hours revision for examination

### Aim(s) and Learning Outcomes

Aim(s): The aim of this module is to enable the student to conduct biblical exegesis and understand how a given passage applies in a contemporary setting. Learning Outcomes: Upon completion of the course, the student will be able to: A. understand, in general, the history of hermeneutics; B. define hermeneutics, recognise the need for study of hermeneutics and demonstrate an understanding of the nature of such a study; C. explain the basic features of the grammatical, historical method of interpretation and demonstrate how this method is applied; D. demonstrate an understanding of the operation of figures of speech and literary devices such as riddles, fables, allegory, proverbs, types and parables; E. demonstrate a basic knowledge of the rules and guidelines relating to the interpretation of different genres of biblical literature.

### Teaching and Learning Strategies

Lectures and Seminars, directed learning, study groups

See the Academic Manual for policies in relation to late submission of essays. E. ## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Exegesis
The course focuses on the practical application of the grammatical-historical method to interpret biblical texts, specifically requiring students to explore historical, literary, and theological contexts of Old Testament prophetic passages.

### Theme: Hermeneutical Theory
The curriculum covers the history of hermeneutics, the definition of the discipline, and the study of various literary genres and devices including allegory, parables, and types.
