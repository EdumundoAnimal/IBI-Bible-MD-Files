# The Hedgehog Concept
**Author:** [[Jim Collins]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Hedgehog Concept, derived from an ancient Greek parable and popularized by Isaiah Berlin, illustrates that great organizations simplify a complex world into a single organizing idea. By focusing on three intersecting circles—what you can be the best in the world at, what drives your economic engine, and what you are deeply passionate about—companies can achieve sustained success. Good-to-great companies avoid the 'fox' approach of pursuing many different strategies, opting instead for the 'hedgehog' simplicity that leads to breakthrough results. This involves confronting brutal facts while maintaining unwavering faith in the core concept.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Three Circles
The framework of the Hedgehog Concept requires identifying the intersection of what a company can be best at, what drives its economic engine, and what it is deeply passionate about.

### Theme: Simplicity vs. Complexity
Great companies simplify their world by focusing on a single, clear, unifying concept rather than juggling multiple competing strategies.

### Theme: The Economic Denominator
Identifying the single ratio (e.g., profit per customer visit) that has the greatest impact on the economic engine of an organization.
