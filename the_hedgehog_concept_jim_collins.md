# The Hedgehog Concept
**Author:** [[Jim Collins]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article introduces the 'Hedgehog Concept' as a core strategic framework for businesses to transition from good to great. Based on Isaiah Berlin's parable of the fox and the hedgehog, it posits that successful entities simplify complex realities into a single, unified guiding principle based on the intersection of three circles: what an entity can be the best at, what drives its economic engine, and what it is deeply passionate about. The text contrasts this with the 'fox' approach, which pursues many goals simultaneously. Through case studies of companies like Walgreens, Wells Fargo, and Abbott, Collins demonstrates that achieving 'greatness' requires profound insight, egoless clarity, and the discipline to adhere to this simple concept even under pressure. It also emphasizes the importance of 'economic denominators' (e.g., profit per customer visit for Walgreens) as a metric to drive focus and strategic alignment.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Hedgehog Concept
A strategic framework based on the intersection of three circles: deep passion, economic engine, and the ability to be the best in the world.

### Theme: Simplicity and Focus
The necessity of reducing complex challenges into a single, unified, and simple idea to guide organizational decision-making.

### Theme: Economic Denominator
Identifying the single most important ratio (like profit per customer visit) that can systematically increase an organization's economic vitality.

### Theme: Discipline and Confronting Brutal Facts
The requirement to maintain discipline and face harsh realities without succumbing to delusions, ensuring long-term institutional greatness.
