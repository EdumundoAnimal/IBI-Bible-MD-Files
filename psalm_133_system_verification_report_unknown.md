## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document is a detailed exegetical and theological report on Psalm 133. It provides a linguistic analysis of the Hebrew text, structural breakdown of the psalm's 'staircase parallelism,' and an examination of its canonical context within the Songs of Ascents (Psalms 120–134). It explores the two primary metaphors of the psalm—the anointing oil of Aaron and the dew of Mount Hermon—as symbols of a vertical, descending divine gift of unity. The report further engages with historical-theological reception, tracing interpretations from the Patristic era through the Reformation to modern existential challenges. It concludes with an application framework designed to contrast modern atomistic secularism with the biblical model of covenantal communion.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Descending Divine Grace
The central argument that true unity in a community is not a horizontal, human-manufactured construct but a vertical gift that descends from God, illustrated by the metaphors of anointing oil and life-giving dew.

### Theme: Covenantal Communion vs. Secular Atomism
An analytical comparison contrasting the modern secular view of the individual as autonomous and transactional with the biblical view of the person as constituted through relation to God and the covenantal community.

### Theme: Staircase Parallelism
A structural analysis of Psalm 133 identifying a triadic symmetrical structure where the descent motif creates a progression from the Head/Source to the Body/Reception.
