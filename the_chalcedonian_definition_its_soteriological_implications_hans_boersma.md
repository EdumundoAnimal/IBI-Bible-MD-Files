# The Chalcedonian Definition: Its Soteriological Implications
**Author:** [[Hans Boersma]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article examines the complex and controversial relationship between Karl Barth's theology and the Chalcedonian Definition. It explores whether Barth aligns with or rejects the traditional Chalcedonian Christology, citing various scholars who hold conflicting views on whether Barth engages in a radical transformation (Umbildung) or reinterpretation (Umdeutung) of the dogma. The text also provides a historical overview of the Council of Chalcedon, detailing the political and ecclesiastical pressures involving Leo, Anatolius, Dioscorus, and the imperial court, ultimately leading to the condemnation of Eutyches and the formalization of the Christological definition in 451.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Barthian Christology
An analysis of how Karl Barth navigates the traditional Chalcedonian definition, balancing his rejection of liberal criticism with his own 'actualization' of the doctrine of the incarnation.

### Theme: Historical Context of Chalcedon
The political and theological shifts in 451 AD, involving the 'Robber Synod,' Emperor Marcian, and the orchestration of the Council of Chalcedon to resolve Christological controversies.
