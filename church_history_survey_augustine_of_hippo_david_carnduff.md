# Church History Survey: Augustine of Hippo
**Author:** [[David Carnduff]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides an academic overview of the life, theological development, and historical influence of Augustine of Hippo. It details his early life in Tagaste, his conversion in Milan following a struggle with Manichaeism, his role as Bishop of Hippo, and his significant intellectual contributions to Western Christianity. The text highlights his transition from a professor of rhetoric to a foundational church father, his engagement with Neo-Platonism, and his long-lasting impact on both Catholic and Protestant traditions, specifically regarding doctrines of sin, grace, and predestination.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Conversion and Spiritual Struggle
Augustine's life was defined by a internal conflict between his early personal desires and his eventual conversion to Christianity, catalyzed by the influence of his mother Monica and the preaching of Ambrose.

### Theme: Intellectual Synthesis
Augustine integrated elements of classical philosophy, particularly Neo-Platonism, into Christian theology, creating a framework that influenced Western church doctrine for centuries.

### Theme: Augustinian Influence
His works, such as Confessions and The City of God, established him as a central figure bridging classical antiquity and the medieval church, maintaining influence across denominational boundaries including Catholicism and the Reformation.
