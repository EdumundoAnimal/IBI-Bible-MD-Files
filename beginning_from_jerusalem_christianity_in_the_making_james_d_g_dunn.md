# Beginning from Jerusalem: Christianity in the Making
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a historical and analytical timeline of early Christianity, starting from the crucifixion of Jesus (c. 30 AD) through the Roman Imperial period up to the early 4th century. It explores the formation of the early church, its self-designation as a Messianic sect, and its theological development within the context of Second Temple Judaism. The text evaluates the impact of external pressures (persecutions) and internal identity formation, specifically examining how early Christians navigated their relationships with Jewish law, apocalyptic expectations, and their status as a unique movement under the broader umbrella of Judaism.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Messianic Sect Identity
Early Christians viewed themselves as a sect within Second Temple Judaism, characterized by their belief in Jesus as the Messiah, his resurrection, and the apocalyptic expectation of his imminent return (parousia).

### Theme: Socio-Religious Tension
The movement faced significant internal pressure from Jewish authorities and external pressure from Roman imperial persecutions, leading to evolving definitions of authority, discipline, and martyrdom.

### Theme: Apocalyptic Eschatology
A core feature of early Christian belief was the conviction that the 'end of days' had been initiated, necessitating a specific moral and communal conduct ('halakhah') as the people of God.
