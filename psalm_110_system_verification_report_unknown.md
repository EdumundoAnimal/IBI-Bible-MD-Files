Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants (BHS & LXX Apparatus) Verse 3a: bə-haḏrê-qōḏeš (בְּ הַ דְּ רֵ י־קֹ דֶ ׁש) vs. bə-harrê-qōḏeš (בְּ הַ רְּ רֵ י־קֹ דֶֶׁש): The Masoretic Text (MT) vocalizes bəhaḏrê-qōḏeš ("in holy splendor/attire," supported by Targum and Symmachus as ἐν δόξῃ ἁγίων). Dozens of medieval Hebrew manuscripts, the Septuagint (ἐν ταῖς λαμπρότησιν τῶν ἁγίων), the Syriac Peshitta, and Jerome's Vulgate (in splendoribus sanctorum) presuppose either haddār (splendor) or har (mountains, i.e., "upon the holy mountains," pointing to Zion). The lectio difficilior is MT's liturgical/priestly clothing metaphor, reflecting the holy vestments of the royal army. Verse 3c: yalədūṯeḵā (יַלְּ דֻ תֶ יָך) vs. yəlīḏtîḵā (יְּ לִ דְּ תִ יָך): The noun yalədūṯ with 2ms suffix ("your youth," hence "the dew of your youth is yours"). The Septuagint vocalizes the consonantal frame as a Qal perfect 1cs verb with 2ms suffix: ἐκ γαστρὸς πρὸ ἑωσφόρου ἐξεγέννησά σε ("From the womb before the morning star I have begotten you," mēreḥem mišḥār yəlīḏtîḵā). This variant formed the bedrock of early Patristic Christology regarding the eternal, pre-temporal generation of the Son (Athanasius, Gregory of Nyssa). Modern text-critical consensus (WBC, ICC) treats MT as the preserved poetic meter, representing the dawn-fresh vigor of the king’s consecrated forces, though the LXX demonstrates an early inner-Jewish or proto-Messianic reading of royal begetting (cf. Psalm 2:7). Verse 6b: māḥaṣ rō’š ‘al-’ereṣ rabbāh (מָ חַ ץֶר ֹאׁשֶעַ ל־אֶ רֶ ץֶרַ בָ ה): Singular rō’š (head/leader) vs. collective/plural. LXX translates plural κεφαλάς ("heads"), harmonizing with the plural nations (gôyim). MT’s singular preserves the deliberate singular typology pointing to an archetypal adversary or cosmic ruler (Protoevangelium echo of Gen 3:15).

### Grammatical Mechanics

Verse 1a: Nə’um YHWH la’dōnî (נְּ אֻ ם יְּ הוָה לַא ֹד ָנ ִי): Nə’um is an utterance-formula strictly reserved for prophetic/divine speech, immediately investing the royal recipient with transcendent divine backing. La’dōnî consists of the preposition lə + the singular noun ’ādôn with the 1cs pronominal suffix ("to my lord"). This grammatical marker isolates the addressee as distinct from YHWH (the speaker) and elevated above the speaker/psalmist (David). Verse 1b: Šēḇ lîmînî ‘ad-’āšît (שׁ ֵב ל ִימ ִינ ִי עַד־א ָשׁ ִית): Šēḇ is a Qal imperative 2ms (from yāšaḇ), denoting an authoritative seated posture of co-regency, not passive rest. The prepositional temporal clause ‘ad-’āšît (‘ad + Qal imperfect 1cs of šît) denotes duration until completion: the session is not terminated when enemies are subdued, but the active process of pacification continues until universal subjugation is finalized. Verse 4: Nišba‘ YHWH wəlō’ yinnāḥēm (נִ ׁשְּ בַ ע יְּ הוָה וְּ לֹא יִ נָחֵ ם): Nišba‘ is a Niphal perfect 3ms; yinnāḥēm is a Niphal imperfect 3ms of nāḥam. The use of the double affirmation (affirmative oath + negative apodosis "will not relent") creates an absolute, unbreakable divine covenant, superseding the provisional Levitical economy. Verse 4b: ‘Al-diḇrātî Malkî-Ṣeḏeq (עַ ל־ד ִבְּ רָ ת ִי מַ לְּ כ ִי־צ ֶד ֶק): The compound ‘al-diḇrātî contains an archaic genitive î marker, functioning adverbially ("after the manner/order of"). Malkî-Ṣeḏeq is a proper noun meaning "My King is Righteousness" or "King of Salem."

### Lexical Profiles

’Āḏôn (א ָדו ֹן): Strong's H113 / HALOT 17: Distinguished from ’Adōnāy (the divine title reserved exclusively for God). ’Āḏôn denotes a human or angelic master, ruler, or sovereign superior. In the first-century context, David’s address of his descendant as ’Ādōnî breaks standard ancient Near Eastern kinship hierarchies, where ancestors never address descendants as superiors. Hăḏōm (ה ֲד ֹם): Strong's H1916 / HALOT 236: "Footstool." Associated with the ark of the covenant as the footstool of YHWH (1 Chr 28:2; Ps 99:5; 132:7) and the Egyptian/Assyrian iconography of vanquished kings placed physically beneath the soles of the Pharaoh's or Emperor's feet. Malkî-Ṣeḏeq (מַ לְּ כ ִי־צ ֶד ֶק): Strong's H4442 / HALOT 592: Combined form of meleḵ (king) and ṣedeq (righteousness/order). In canonical usage (Gen 14:18-20), an archaic Canaanite-Jebusite pre-Levitical priest-king of Salem (Jerusalem) who serves El Elyon without recorded genealogy, mediating blessing and receiving tithes.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Eschatological Royal Priesthood
The document argues that Psalm 110 presents a unique fusion of kingly and priestly offices in a single eschatological figure, transcending the Mosaic separation of the tribe of Judah (kingship) and the tribe of Levi (priesthood).

### Theme: Divine Session
The text emphasizes the 'session at the right hand' as an authoritative posture of co-regency, where the Messiah shares divine executive authority and cosmic administration, rather than passive rest.

### Theme: Willing Consecration
The document interprets verse 3 as a model of freedom where subjects are not conscripted slaves but 'willing volunteers' (nədāḇōt) transformed by grace into a kingdom of priests.
