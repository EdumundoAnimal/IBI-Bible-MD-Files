# The Epistle to the Romans: Romans 7
**Author:** [[Leon Morris]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text provides a scholarly commentary on Romans 7, specifically focusing on the Apostle Paul's discussion of the law, sin, and the internal struggle of the believer. The author, Leon Morris, analyzes the debates surrounding whether Paul is describing his pre-conversion experience or the struggles of a regenerate Christian. Key sections include an examination of verses 7:1-6, 7:7-12, and the broader theological implications of the law in the believer's life. The text also delves into historical interpretations and linguistic nuances within the biblical text.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Function of the Law
The author explores Paul's complex relationship with the law, arguing that while the law is holy and good, it reveals human sinfulness and creates a consciousness of transgression rather than providing a way of salvation.

### Theme: Regenerate vs. Unregenerate Experience
A central theme of the commentary is evaluating whether Romans 7 describes the spiritual battle of a Christian (the regenerate) or the experience of a person under the law before conversion (the unregenerate).

### Theme: The Nature of Sin
The commentary defines sin not merely as an action, but as a power or 'base of operations' that exploits the law to produce death, highlighting the internal struggle between the flesh and the Spirit.
