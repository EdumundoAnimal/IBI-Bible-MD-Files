# Synoptic Gospels Study: Authorship, Thematic Overview, and The Synoptic Problem
**Author:** [[Dr. Miriam A. Kelly]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides an overview of the Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke), exploring their respective authorship, dates of composition, and intended audiences. It addresses the 'Synoptic Problem'—the complex relationship and interdependency of these texts—and examines various hypotheses regarding their origins, such as the Two-Document Hypothesis and the Four-Source Hypothesis. The text also analyzes the theological distinctives of each Gospel, noting how Matthew emphasizes Jesus as King, Mark portrays Jesus as the Suffering Servant, and Luke presents Jesus as the Saviour of all mankind.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Synoptic Problem
An analytical study of the verbal similarities, shared material, and structural parallels between Matthew, Mark, and Luke, suggesting a common interdependency or reliance on shared sources.

### Theme: Theological Distinctives
Each Gospel author maintains a unique focus: Matthew emphasizes the fulfillment of Jewish Messianic expectations, Mark highlights the servant-nature of Jesus' ministry, and Luke provides a broader historical view of Jesus as a universal Savior.

### Theme: Source Critical Hypotheses
Evaluation of theories including the Two-Document Hypothesis (Mark and Q) and the Four-Source Hypothesis, which attempt to account for the origins and development of Gospel traditions.
