# Hebrews 1:1-4
**Author:** [[Carol Steele]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text is an exegesis of Hebrews 1:1-4, exploring the rhetorical shifts between the Old Testament prophetic tradition and the Christian interpretation of Christ. It analyzes the author's argument regarding how God has spoken in these last days through his Son, contrasting this with prior revelation. The author, Carol Steele, highlights the theological tension in interpreting this passage in a way that respects the Jewish roots of the faith while articulating the perceived supremacy of Christ, noting the complications of supersessionist theology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Supersessionism
The theological tension inherent in interpreting Hebrews 1:1-4, which can be read as positing Christ as a replacement for, rather than a complement to, the covenant God initiated with the Jewish people.

### Theme: Theological Rhetoric
Analysis of the rhetorical function of the 'but' in verse 2, which acts as a pivot point separating the clauses of previous prophetic history from the arrival of the Son.

### Theme: Christology
The conceptualization of Christ as the 'exact imprint' of God's being, emphasizing his pre-existence and his role as the final mediator of God's word.
