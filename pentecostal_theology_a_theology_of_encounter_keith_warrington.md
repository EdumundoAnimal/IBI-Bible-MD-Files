# Pentecostal Theology: A Theology of Encounter
**Author:** [[Keith Warrington]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores core tenets of Pentecostal theology, specifically focusing on the doctrine of God. It highlights the Pentecostal view of God as an active, personal, and loving creator who engages in human relationship. The text examines the challenges Pentecostals face in reconciling their theological identity with the traditional Trinitarian framework, discussing both the diversity within Pentecostalism—such as the Oneness Pentecostal movement—and the shared emphasis on the experiential nature of faith, the divinity of Christ, and the role of the Holy Spirit. Further sections address theological definitions of human sin, the nature of Jesus as both human and divine, and the centrality of the resurrection in the Christian experience.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Experiential Nature of God
Pentecostal theology prioritizes a personal, dynamic encounter with God, who is seen as intimately involved in creation and responsive to human need, rather than merely adhering to rigid dogma.

### Theme: Trinitarianism vs. Oneness
While most Pentecostals hold to the traditional Trinitarian creed, there is a significant sub-movement known as 'Oneness Pentecostalism' that emphasizes the absolute unity of God in the person of Jesus Christ.

### Theme: Christology and Soteriology
The text argues that Pentecostalism emphasizes a high view of Jesus' humanity and divinity, viewing his sacrifice and resurrection as the essential mechanisms for salvation and the restoration of human relationship with God.
