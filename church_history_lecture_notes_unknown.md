**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Week 10, Church History, 2/12/08. Question 1: How did the Gospel spread from Jerusalem to Rome? Pax Romana, Greek culture, Lingua franca (Greek), Romans embraced Greek gods, Romans conquered militarily while Greeks conquered literarily. Influence on Jewish faith: Septuagint (Greek version of Old Testament), the Jewish Diaspora (voluntary dispersion, infrastructure of synagogues), and popularity of LXX among Gentiles. Concepts: Access to unsaved, hospitality, pastoral care. St. Patrick: The life and ministry of Patrick (Question 2). Background: France/Gauls/Galatians, patricians vs. plebeians, British Church, family clergy heritage, learned Irish Gaelic, clear calling, medieval liturgy, 6th-century missionary island. Approach to mission: 60 days duration, love for Irish people, fluent speaker, understanding druid paganism, grace, 'Gospel burns within him'. Page 2: Commitment to pay the cost, working knowledge of Scripture, eschatological urgency (Act 1:8, preaching to ends of the earth). Columbanus writing to the Pope. Augustine of Hippo: Baker's Dictionary of Religion, TC Hammond's 'In Understand Be Men', influence transcends denominations. Polemics vs. Apologetics. Influences of Augustine: Waldensers, Reformers, Ambrose of Milan. Question: What are the influences of Post-Nicene fathers? Note the Trinity. Jerome, 8 Crusades, Richard the Lionheart (3rd Crusade).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spread of the Gospel
Examines the historical and cultural conditions in the Roman Empire, such as the Pax Romana, the common use of Greek, and the Jewish diaspora, that facilitated the expansion of Christianity.

### Theme: Missionary Theology
Analyzes the methods of St. Patrick, emphasizing cultural adaptation, fluent language acquisition, and a sense of eschatological urgency.

### Theme: Apologetics and Polemics
Distinguishes between the defense of truth (apologetics) and the active attacking of doctrinal error (polemics) as foundational tools for theologians like Augustine.
