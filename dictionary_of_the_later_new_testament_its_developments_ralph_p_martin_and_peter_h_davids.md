# Dictionary of the Later New Testament & Its Developments
**Author:** [[Ralph P. Martin and Peter H. Davids]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document discusses the theological concept of spirits (pneumata) in early Christian literature and patristic writings. It explores various interpretations of 'spirits,' including the Holy Spirit, evil spirits, and human spirits as depicted in the works of Hermas, Clement of Rome, Ignatius, Justin Martyr, and Irenaeus. The text highlights how early Christian authors distinguished between these entities, often personifying sin or contrasting divine versus demonic influences in the context of salvation and the church's experience.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Pneumatology in Early Christian Thought
The text examines the complex and evolving understanding of 'spirits' in early Christian literature, distinguishing between the Holy Spirit, angelic beings, and evil spirits.

### Theme: Personification of Sin
An analysis of how certain authors, specifically Hermas, treated sin as a personified force acting upon or dwelling within individuals.

### Theme: Patristic Perspectives on Evil
The writings of Justin Martyr and Irenaeus are used to demonstrate how early theologians categorized demonic influences and the corruption of human nature.
