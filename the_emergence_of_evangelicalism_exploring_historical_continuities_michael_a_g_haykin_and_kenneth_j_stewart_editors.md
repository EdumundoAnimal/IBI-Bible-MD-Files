# The Emergence of Evangelicalism: Exploring Historical Continuities
**Author:** [[Michael A. G. Haykin and Kenneth J. Stewart (Editors)]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the historical continuity and emergence of evangelicalism, particularly focusing on conversion narratives, spiritual autobiography, and theological assurance. It examines how evangelical identity is rooted in both earlier Christian traditions and specific developments in the 18th century, addressing the works of various historical figures such as Augustine, Luther, and Calvin, and analyzing the evolution of conversion accounts from early martyrdom records to later Protestant narratives.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Conversion Narrative
The evolution of how individuals articulate their religious transformation, transitioning from early Christian martyrology and formal confessions to more inward, psychologically oriented spiritual autobiographies.

### Theme: Historical Continuity
The argument that evangelicalism is not merely a post-1730s phenomenon but is deeply connected to earlier Protestant and even pre-Reformation theological and experiential traditions.

### Theme: Doctrinal Assurance
A core hallmark of evangelicalism involving the theological and personal certainty of salvation, often contrasted with older Reformation perspectives on assurance.
