# The Use of Psalms in the Shaping of a Text: Hebrews 1:1-14 and Psalm 110:1
**Author:** [[David Wallace]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article analyzes how the author of Hebrews utilizes Old Testament Psalms, specifically Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1, to construct a christological argument in the book's exordium (Hebrews 1:1-4) and the subsequent catena (Hebrews 1:5-13). It examines how these Psalms function to establish Christ's superiority over angels, his status as the Son, and his exaltation at the right hand of God. The text further explores the theological framework of these citations, suggesting they serve as a core component of the early church's liturgical or tradition-based Christology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Exaltation
The argument that the author of Hebrews uses Psalm 2:7 and 110:1 to frame the exordium and the catena to establish Christ's superiority over angels and his status as the enthroned Son.

### Theme: Intertextuality
The reliance of New Testament writers on the Old Testament, specifically the Psalms, to articulate and legitimize their christological assertions.

### Theme: Liturgical Origin
The possibility that the arrangement of citations in Hebrews 1 originated from early Christian liturgy or tradition, serving as a structured confession of faith.
