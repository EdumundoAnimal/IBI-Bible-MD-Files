# How to Read Church History and What Christians Believe
**Author:** [[Jean Comby, Alan F. Johnson, and Robert E. Webber]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document encompasses two distinct works: Jean Comby's 'How to Read Church History' (Volume 1) and 'What Christians Believe' by Alan F. Johnson and Robert E. Webber. Comby's text examines the development of the church in the 4th and 5th centuries, specifically the Arian crisis and the Council of Nicaea. The text delineates the theological conflict between Arius and Alexander regarding the nature of the Son and the Father, and discusses the shift in the church's status under Constantine. 'What Christians Believe' provides an overview of the theological and historical development of Christian doctrine, specifically focusing on the historical development of the Trinity, the roots of Trinitarian thought, and medieval scholastic proofs for the existence of God.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Arianism vs. Nicene Orthodoxy
The theological conflict concerning whether the Son (Logos) is of the same substance as the Father or created and subordinate.

### Theme: The Church under Imperial Influence
The transformation of the church's status under Constantine and Theodosius from a persecuted sect to a state-aligned power.

### Theme: Historical Development of Trinitarian Doctrine
The reconciliation of biblical monotheism with the complexity of divine plurality through doxological and philosophical discourse.

### Theme: Scholasticism and Natural Theology
The medieval approach to proving God's existence through Aristotelian philosophy and rational logic, exemplified by Thomas Aquinas.
