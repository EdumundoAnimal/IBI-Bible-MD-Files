Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants (BHS / Septuagint / Dead Sea Scrolls Apparatus) The Hebrew text of Psalm 131 (Codex Leningradensis, BHS) is remarkably well-preserved across the textual tradition, exhibiting minimal substantial corruption, yet presenting critical syntactical nuances preserved in the ancient witnesses: ● Superscription (v. 1a): MT reads ש ִׁ֥ יר הַַּֽ מַּ עֲל֗ ֹות לְ דָ ו ִ֫ ד (šîr hammaʿălôt lǝdāwid). The Septuagint (LXX 130:1) translates ωδή των αναβαθμων των Δαυιδ. In 4QPs^e and 11QPs^a, the collection of the Songs of Ascents (Pss 120–134) appears with minor orthographic variation, but 11QPs^a omits the lamed-David attribution in some related ascend-psalms, reflecting early scribal fluctuations regarding Davidic groupings. The Peshitta retains the Davidic heading, adding a contextual note linking it to David’s forbearance toward Saul or his transport of the Ark.

### Grammatical & Syntactical Mechanics

● Verse 1: ○ לֹא־גָב ַ֣ ּה לִ בִ י (lōʾ-gāḇah libbî): Verb gāḇah (Qal perfect 3ms) with subject lēḇ (noun ms + 1cs suffix). The perfect aspect denotes a settled, established condition or fixed posture: "My heart has not grown haughty." ○ וְ ל ֹא־רָ מּו עֵ ינ י (wəlōʾ-rāmû ʿênay): Conjunction waw + negative particle lōʾ + rāmû (Qal perfect 3cp from rûm) + subject ʿênay (noun dual + 1cs suffix). Parallel clause to the former: heart (interior intention) matches eyes (exterior orientation). ○ וְ ל ֹא־הִ ל כְ תִ י (wəlōʾ-hillaktî): Conjunction + negative + hillaktî (Piel perfect 1cs from hālak). The Piel is frequentative/iterative: "I have not habitually walked / exercised myself." ○ בִ גְ דֹ לֹות ּובְ נִ פְ לָאֹות מִ מֶּ נִ י (bigdōlôt ûḇəniplāʾôt mimmennî): Inseparable preposition bə prefixed to feminine plural adjectives used substantivally. Niplāʾôt is the Niphal participle feminine plural of pālāʾ ("to be extraordinary, surpassingly difficult, miraculous"). The preposition min prefixed to the 1cs pronoun mimmennî denotes comparative separation and superiority: "things too great and too arduous/miraculous beyond me" (cf. Gen 18:14; Deut 30:11). ● Verse 2: ○ א ֹ֤ ם־ל א ש ּו ִּ֨ ית י ׀ וְ דֹומַּ֗ מְ ת י נִַּ֫ פְ ש ִׁ֥ י (ʾim-lōʾ šiwwîtî wədômamtî napšî): ʾim-lōʾ is the standard formula of solemn asseveration introducing an oath (Gesenius §149b; Joüon-Muraoka §164c), suppressing the apodosis ("If I have not done this, [then let calamity strike me]"), equivalent to an emphatic positive assertion: "Surely I have composed and silenced my soul." ○ Šiwwîtî is Piel perfect 1cs of šāwāh ("to make flat, level, smooth, even"). ○ Wədômamtî is Polel (or Poel) perfect 1cs of dāmam ("to quiet, silence, bring to stillness"). ○ כְְּ֭ גָמֻ ל עֲל ֵ֣ י א מּ֑ ֹו כַּגָמֻ ֻ֖ ל עָ לַּ ֻ֖י נַּפְ ש ַֽ י (kəgāmul ʾimmô ʿălê kaggāmul ʿālay napšî): Comparative preposition kə prefixed to gāmūl (Qal passive participle masculine singular of gāmal). Preposition ʿălê (poetic form of ʿal) + noun ʾēm with 3ms suffix. The second line repeats the phrase with the definite article fused in the preposition kaggāmul (kə + ha + gāmūl): "Like the weaned child upon me is my soul." ● Verse 3: ○ יַּח ֵ֣ ל י ְּ֭ שְ רָ א ל א ל־יְ הוָ ּ֑ה (yaḥēl yiśrāʾēl ʾel-YHWH): Verb yaḥēl is imperfect Piel 3ms (functioning as a jussive/injunctive; Gesenius §109b) from the root yāḥal ("to wait, hope, tarry with expectation"). The construction with ʾel indicates directed, relational, expectant orientation toward Yahweh.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenant Quietude
The document argues that Psalm 131 represents a transition from an unweaned, demanding soul to a weaned, contented soul. This state is not passive but a result of deliberate, costly discipline where the individual renounces hubris and intellectual overreach to rest in God's sovereign providence.

### Theme: Epistemic Humility
The text highlights the concept of 'niplāʾôt mimmennî' (things too wonderful for me), asserting that there are divine mysteries beyond human comprehension. It contrasts this with the 'Faustian' modern desire for total epistemic mastery, framing humility as the foundation of true wisdom.

### Theme: Christological Kenosis
The report maps the psalm's movement onto the life of Christ, specifically identifying His kenosis (self-emptying) and Gethsemane surrender as the ultimate embodiment of the 'weaned' posture, where the human will is perfectly aligned with the Father's.
