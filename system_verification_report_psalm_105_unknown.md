### Grammatical Mechanics

Volitional Staccato (vv. 1–5): The psalm opens with an uninterrupted chain of ten imperative and cohortative verbal forms: 1. וּודֹה (hôdû – Hiphil imperative: "Give public acknowledgment") 2. וּא ְר ִק (qir’û – Qal imperative: "Proclaim/invoke") 3. וּיע ִודֹה (hôdî‘û – Hiphil imperative: "Make known") 4. וּיר ִש (šîrû – Qal imperative: "Sing") 5. וּר ְמַז (zammərû – Piel imperative: "Make melody / strum") 6. וּיח ִשׂ (śîḥû – Qal imperative: "Meditate / recount") 7. הִ תְ הַ לְ לּו (hithaləlû – Hitpael imperative: "Boast / glory") 8. ח ַמ ְשִׂי (yiśmaḥ – Qal jussive: "Let the heart rejoice") 9. וּש ְר ִדּ (diršû – Qal imperative: "Seek / inquire") 10. וּש ְק ַב (baqqəšû – Piel imperative: "Search out zealously") The Sovereign Consecutive Chain (vv. 16–44): The historical narrative is carried by the waw-consecutive with the imperfect (narrative preterite), attributing every historical turning point to God as the sole grammatical and theological agent: v. 16: ב ָע ָר א ָר ְקִיַו (wayyiqrā’ rā‘āb – "And He summoned a famine") v. 17: יש ִא ם ֶיהֵנ ְפ ִל חַל ָש (šālaḥ lipnêhem ’îš – "He sent a man before them") v. 24: וֹמַת־ע ֶא ר ֶפֶיַו (wayyeper ’et-‘ammô – "And He caused His people to be fruitful") v. 25: ם ָב ִל ךְַפ ָה (hāpak libbām – "He turned their heart") v. 44: וַיִתֵ ן לָהֶ ם אַ רְ צֹות גֹויִם (wayyittēn lāhem ’arṣôt gôyim – "And He gave them the lands of the nations") The Teleological Climax (v. 45): Introduced by the subordinating conjunction ורּבֲע ַב (ba‘ăbûr – "to the end that / in order that"), governing the imperfect verbs וּר ְמ ְשִי (yišmərû) and וּרֹצְנִי (yinṣōrû). The historical movement of redemption does not terminate upon national liberation or geographic possession, but upon covenantal alignment with divine holiness (Torah).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Unconditional Covenant Fidelity
The document argues that Psalm 105 is an 'unconditional, unilateral Abrahamic Covenant' that bypasses conditional Sinaitic/Davidic covenants to ground Israel's survival in God's character rather than human performance.

### Theme: Sovereign Teleology
History is presented as a 'Providential Teleology' where God is the sole agent. Even famine, imprisonment, and imperial oppression are 'brushstrokes of an unyielding divine decree' used to refine and elevate His people.

### Theme: Theology of the Crucible
Suffering is not viewed as meaningless, but as an 'intentional crucible' for character maturation. Joseph's iron shackles are described as the 'divine instrument forging a soul capable of ruling an empire'.
