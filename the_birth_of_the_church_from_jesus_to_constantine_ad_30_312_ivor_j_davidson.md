# The Birth of the Church: From Jesus to Constantine, A.D. 30-312
**Author:** [[Ivor J. Davidson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a historical and theological analysis of the early Christian movement. It examines the cultural, social, and political landscape of first-century Palestine under Roman rule, detailing the diverse expressions of Judaism, the significance of the destruction of the Temple, and the emergence of the Jesus movement. The text further explores the theological development of the Church as it moved from its Jewish roots toward broader interactions with the Roman world, Greco-Roman philosophical traditions, and its eventual distinct identity.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Contextual Framework
Explores how the early Church was shaped by its Hellenistic, Jewish, and Roman surroundings, including the pressures of the Pax Romana and the influence of Greek thought.

### Theme: The Shift After 70 A.D.
Analyzes the profound impact of the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple on both Jewish identity and the nascent Christian community's efforts to define its unique faith.

### Theme: Partings of the Ways
Documents the growing estrangement between Jewish Christian groups (such as the Ebionites) and the broader, increasingly Gentile, Church as theology and practices diverged.
