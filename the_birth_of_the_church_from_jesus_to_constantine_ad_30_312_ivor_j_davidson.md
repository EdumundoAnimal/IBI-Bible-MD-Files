# The Birth of the Church: From Jesus to Constantine, A.D. 30-312
**Author:** [[Ivor J. Davidson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an historical and theological overview of the cultural and religious setting of the early Christian movement. It examines the impact of the Roman Empire, the diverse nature of 1st-century Judaism, and the influence of Greek philosophical traditions. Key segments analyze the internal tensions between Jewish-Christian groups and the developing church, specifically focusing on the destruction of the Temple, the shift toward Gentile inclusion, and the evolving self-definition of Christianity as it moved away from its Jewish roots and responded to Roman persecution.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Jewish-Christian Identity
Explores the complex internal conflict of early followers of Jesus who sought to maintain Jewish tradition, adherence to the Law, and their identity as God's chosen people, while simultaneously grappling with the destruction of the Temple and the rising inclusion of Gentiles.

### Theme: Graeco-Roman Context
Analyzes how the infrastructure of the Roman Empire, including its roads, common languages (Latin/Greek), and legal systems, facilitated the spread of the early Jesus movement.

### Theme: Philosophical Synthesis
Discusses how early Christianity interacted with and adapted to classical philosophical traditions, including Platonism and Stoicism, to articulate its theological claims.

### Theme: Parting of the Ways
Describes the historical process through which Christianity eventually distanced itself from its original Jewish context, driven by theological shifts, the failure of Jewish revolts, and an increase in polemic literature.
