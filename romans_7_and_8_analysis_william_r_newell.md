# Romans 7 and 8 Analysis
**Author:** [[William R. Newell]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a theological commentary on Romans chapters 7 and 8, examining the believer's relationship to the Law, the struggle with indwelling sin, and the liberation provided by the Holy Spirit. It argues that believers are dead to the Law and united with Christ, emphasizing that the 'wretched man' of Chapter 7 is a believer wrestling with the flesh, while Chapter 8 introduces the triumph of life in the Spirit.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Dead to the Law
The argument that believers, through their union with Christ's death, are no longer subject to the Mosaic Law as a principle of justification or condemnation.

### Theme: Indwelling Sin vs. Spirit
The internal conflict experienced by believers in the flesh, where the Holy Spirit empowers the believer to resist the law of sin, moving from struggle to victory.

### Theme: The Believer's Identity
Being 'in Christ' implies a new standing that transcends the old self, where one is no longer defined by the Adam-nature but by the life of the Risen Christ.
