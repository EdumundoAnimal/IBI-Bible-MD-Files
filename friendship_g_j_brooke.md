# Friendship
**Author:** [[G. J. Brooke]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text provides an academic exploration of the concept of friendship in the New Testament and its historical and cultural context in the Greco-Roman world. It discusses the intersection of ancient Mediterranean ideas of social, political, and personal friendship with early Christian teachings, particularly the redefinition of these relationships by Jesus. The author examines how the concept of friendship was transformed into a spiritual relationship with God and how this challenged existing social hierarchies and patronage models. It covers various aspects, such as political alliances, the role of friends in marriage customs, the concept of sacrifice for friends, and the metaphorical use of friendship in religious contexts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greco-Roman Social Ideals
Friendship in the ancient world was often characterized by social hierarchy, reciprocity, patronage, and the political or practical utility of relationships.

### Theme: Redefinition in the New Testament
Jesus transforms the concept of friendship by shifting it from worldly status or political utility to an act of voluntary sacrifice and a spiritual relationship with God, characterized by the 'sharing of truth'.

### Theme: Patronage vs. Friendship
The New Testament often contrasts conventional ancient notions of patronage and social inequality with the new, spiritualized equality found in Christian fellowship.
