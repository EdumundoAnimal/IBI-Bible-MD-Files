# Analysis of the Fourth Gospel
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The fourth Gospel's authorship is traditionally attributed to John, son of Zebedee, as maintained by early Church fathers like Irenaeus. While critical debate emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries, the Gospel is internally structured through the 'Book of Signs' and the 'Book of Passion,' framed by a prologue and epilogue. The narrative focuses on key signs, such as the water into wine and the raising of Lazarus, intended to produce belief in Christ. The Gospel employs a distinctive series of 'opposites'—light vs. dark, life vs. death—and highlights the role of the Holy Spirit. A notable shift in the Gospel's trajectory involves a diminishing public ministry to the Jews and an increasing focus on the disciples' private instruction.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Authorship
Historical debate regarding the identity of the author, balancing early church tradition (John the son of Zebedee) against scholarly challenges.

### Theme: Theological Structure
The Gospel is divided into the 'Book of Signs' and the 'Book of the Passion' to demonstrate the identity of Jesus as the Son of God.

### Theme: Literary Patterns
The author utilizes a series of thematic opposites such as light vs. dark and life vs. death to convey theological truths.

### Theme: Narrative Progression
The Gospel documents a transition from Jesus' public ministry to the Jewish nation toward exclusive private instruction for His disciples.
