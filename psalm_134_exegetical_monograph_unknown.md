# Psalm 134: Exegetical Monograph
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The Ancient Context)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants. Psalm 134:1 — ballêlôt (ותֹילֵּל ַּב): The Masoretic Text (MT) witnesses the plural construct/adverbial form ballêlôt ("in/during the nights"). LXX: Reads ἐν ταῖς νυξίν (en tais nyxin, "in the nights"), matching the plural of the MT. However, Codex Vaticanus ($B$), Codex Sinaiticus ($ℵ$), and the Lucianic recension interpolate the clause ἐν αὐλαῖς οἴκου θεοῦ ἡμῶν (en aulais oikou theou hēmōn, "in the courts of the house of our God") at the end of verse 1. This is a harmonizing scribal expansion assimilated directly from Psalm 135:2 (beḥaṣrôt bêt ’ĕlōhênû). Dead Sea Scrolls (11QPs^a / 11Q5): Psalm 134 is preserved in the Great Psalms Scroll column III. The text conforms to the proto-MT consonant base without the liturgical expansion found in the Septuagint, corroborating the priority and brevity of the MT reading. Peshitta & Targum: The Syriac Peshitta reads b-lyly’ (singular/collective "in the night"), whereas Targum Psalms preserves bə-lêlyā’, interpreting the setting specifically as the nocturnal watch kept by the Levites under the captain of the Temple guard. Psalm 134:2 — qōdeš (קֹדֶ ׁש): MT: Unprefixed absolute noun qōdeš, functioning either as an adverbial accusative of direction ("toward the sanctuary") or an accusative of manner ("in holiness"). LXX: Translates εἰς τὰ ἅγια (eis ta hagia, "into/toward the holy places"), adopting the directional local accusative reading. Hieronymus (Vulgate): Translates in sancta / ad sanctum, supporting the local/directional sense toward the Holy of Holies.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenant Reciprocity
The psalm is structured on a bidirectional axis of the verb b-r-k. Humanity blesses YHWH through declarative adoration, while YHWH blesses humanity through causative, productive covenantal action.

### Theme: Nocturnal Priestly Vigilance
The 'abdê YHWH (servants of the Lord) maintain continuous, fixed-state liturgical duty. This standing posture signifies unwavering attentiveness and intercession, serving as a bulwark against cosmic instability.

### Theme: Cosmic Doxology
The psalm integrates local Zion theology with universal protological sovereignty, framing the nocturnal watch as a confident, restful doxology offered to the Maker of heaven and earth.
