# Psalm 134: Exegetical Monograph
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 134 is analyzed as the grand finale of the Songs of Ascents, focusing on nocturnal priestly vigilance, covenant reciprocity, and cosmic doxology. The text examines linguistic variants in the Masoretic Text, Septuagint, and Dead Sea Scrolls, while offering grammatical breakdowns of key terms like ballêlôt, qōdeš, and hā‘ōmədîm. The monograph highlights the structural chiasm of the psalm, the historical context of Second Temple night-watches, and provides a canonical trajectory from the Aaronic blessing in Numbers to the apocalyptic vision of Revelation. It concludes with existential reflections on modern secularism versus biblical liturgical life.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Nocturnal Priestly Vigilance
The theological understanding that nocturnal liturgical service acts as a continuous act of cosmic maintenance and doxology, countering the existential fear of the night.

### Theme: Covenant Reciprocity
The bidirectional relationship expressed through the verb b-r-k, where humanity blesses God in adoration and God blesses humanity through covenantal favor.

### Theme: Sacerdotal Identity
The status of being an 'ebed YHWH, representing high-status fiduciary representation in the temple rather than generic servitude.
