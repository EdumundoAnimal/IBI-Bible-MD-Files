# Word Biblical Commentary: Isaiah 34-66
**Author:** [[John D. W. Watts]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This excerpt from the Word Biblical Commentary on Isaiah 63:7-64:11 explores theological themes regarding Israel's historical relationship with Yahweh. Watts examines the metaphor of a honeymoon period in the wilderness, the manifestation of God's presence as a saving agent, and the theological significance of collective memory and the covenant. The commentary addresses the tension between divine favor in the past and the contemporary experience of divine absence, referencing the Exodus, the role of Moses and the Levites, and the challenges faced by the post-exilic community in Jerusalem.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Providence and Memory
The author explores how Israel uses historical memory of divine acts, particularly the Exodus and wilderness period, to frame their understanding of God's relationship with them as a 'honeymoon' and to provide a basis for hope during times of perceived abandonment.

### Theme: Theological Tension of Absence
The text analyzes the discrepancy between the powerful manifestations of Yahweh in Israel's past and the contemporary plea for God to 'look down from heaven,' illustrating the existential crisis of a community feeling estranged from its historical protector.
