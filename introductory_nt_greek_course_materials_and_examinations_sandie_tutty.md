# Introductory NT Greek Course Materials and Examinations
**Author:** [[Sandie Tutty]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Lessons 1-2: Present Indicative Active Verbs

Each Verb has: Tense: Past, Present or Future. Person: First Person - I / we, Second Person - You, Third Person - he / she / it / they. Number: Singular or Plural. Greek verbs are formed in one word that incorporates all of these components. Every Verb has a Stem (the first part that does not change) and an Ending (the second part that does change). Questions: If the verb is written as a question (i.e. the question mark [;] appears after it, it must be translated as a question. E.g. λυω; means "Do I loosen?" or "Am I loosening?"

### -ω Verbs

Some Greek verbs have a stem that ends with the letter ε. When the stem ends with this letter, certain contractions take place. Rules of Contraction: 1. ε + ε => ει, 2. ε + ο => ου, 3. ε + long vowel (η or ω) or diphthong => ε drops out. To Form These Verbs: Take the 1st person singular, which you will learn in its uncontracted form, e.g. φιλεω. Remove the -ω to get the verb stem, e.g. φιλ-ε-. Select the required ending: -ω, -εις, -ει, -ομεν, -ετε, or -ουσι(ν). Apply the rules of contraction. Example: φιλεω => ω + φιλε = φιλο, φιλεεις => ει + φιλε = φιλεις, φιλεει => ει + φιλε = φιλει, φιλεομεν => ου + φιλε = φιλουμεν, φιλεετε => ειτε + φιλε = φιλειτε, φιλεουσι(ν) => ουσι(ν) + φιλε = φιλουσιν.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greek Verb Morphology
The document outlines the fundamental structure of Greek verbs, noting that they are formed by combining a stem (the unchanging part) with an ending (the changing part) to indicate tense, person, and number.

### Theme: Rules of Contraction
The text provides specific rules for handling verbs with stems ending in epsilon (ε), detailing how they contract with subsequent vowels or diphthongs to form the final conjugated verb.
