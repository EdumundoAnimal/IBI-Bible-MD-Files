# Faith in Contemporary Culture: Christian Thinking about Consumerism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### THESIS 1: God gives responsibility and good gifts to his people

God is the owner of all things: We are stewards. Work is good and important. - God is to be honoured with first fruits of the harvest (Pr 3:9-10). - Warnings about laziness in OT : Pr 10:4. - Mt 25:26 you wicked and lazy servant!: Heb 6:12. - Col 3:23 "whatever you do work with all your heart as for the Lord". - Work is a blessing. Right to private property. - give to poor Mt 5:42; 6:2-4. - Simon and Andrew's home Mk 1:29. Simon Peter's home Lk 4:38. - Jesus supported by wealthy benefactors including women (Lk 8); Joseph of Arimathea; Poverty not a good thing! - Poor not somehow more spiritual. - Responsibility to look after ourselves, family etc. - [See Blomberg Neither Poverty nor Riches]. BUT we are fallen - and cannot be naive about our attitudes to wealth, consumerism and money!

### IN THE NEW TESTAMENT

i. Money and possessions are not the goal of life and a life spent pursuing them is wasted. Lk 12:15. See also 2 Cor 4:7-18. ii. Possessions and wealth only have value as they are used for the good of others and the purposes of Jesus. Mt 5:42. Mk 8:34-8. iii. Wealth and prosperity more a sign of greed than righteousness. Poverty, hardship and oppression more likely a sign of faithful discipleship. See 2 Cor 11-12. iv. Jesus announced that it is impossible for a rich man to enter the kingdom of God. Mt 19:16-24. He pronounced woes on the rich Luke 6:20-26. The rich young ruler could not give up his idol. v. The pursuit of money breeds discontent and a lack of trust in God. Heb 13:5-6. vi. The rich are specially tempted to greed, idolatry, overconsumption, and treat others as means to an end. 1 Cor 5:9-11. James 5:1-6 warns the rich about their injustice. vii. Consumerism promotes selfishness. In the church, as in the OT, there are not to be poor among you - Which is why 1 Cor 11:17-34 is so scandalous to Paul. viii. Lust for more and more represents a futility of thinking and a futility of life. See Eph 4:17-19. ix. Jesus warns that you cannot serve two masters at the same time :You cannot serve both God and money - Lk 16:13. He says 'where your treasure is, there your heart will be also'. He says to sell your possessions and give to the poor (Lk 12:32-34). x. Consumerism is a preoccupation with self, money and pleasure and which is seen as GOOD and NORMAL and ECONOMICALLY VIRTUOUS. In 2 Tim 3:1-5 these are signs of the last days - of life on the brink of destruction. xi. Wealth and possessions breeds self-satisfaction, spiritual complacency, pride - and work against humility. See 1 Tim 6:6-10 and how 'godliness with contentment is great gain' and the love of money is the root of evil. xii. The sign that wealth and possessions do not possess us is that we give them away - 1 Tim 6:17 - be rich in good deeds. Store up riches in heaven.

### THESIS 3: GOD IS ON THE SIDE OF THE POOR AND MARGINALISED

Justice reflects the character of God: God is the defender of the poor and the fatherless and the widow. Have a read at Deut 10:17-18 and many other texts in the OT. 17 For the LORD your God is God of gods and Lord of lords, the great God, mighty and awesome, who shows no partiality and accepts no bribes. 18 He defends the cause of the fatherless and the widow, and loves the foreigner residing among you, giving them food and clothing. Is God on the side of the poor? Yes. God consistently shows special care for the powerless and those in need of protection. See Proverbs 31:8 and many other texts. 8 Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute. Have a read at Isaiah 58:6-7 for how just living leads to loosing the chains of oppression and feeding the hungry. It's worth quoting here: - 6 "Is not this the kind of fasting I have chosen: to loose the chains of injustice and untie the cords of the yoke, to set the oppressed free and break every yoke? 7 Is it not to share your food with the hungry and to provide the poor wanderer with shelter - when you see the naked, to clothe them, and not to turn away from your own flesh and blood?

### JESUS and the NT

Far from 'moving on' from the OT's concern for justice, Jesus stands in full continuity with the prophetic tradition in his passionate concern for justice for the vulnerable, poor and marginalised. - Like an OT prophet Jesus equates a concern for justice with the state of one's heart. - Jesus was poor, marginal, rural, he arrives in poverty and humility; he is a refugee, he has no place to lay his head, he has no home or possessions or wealth. - His parents offered a humble offering at the temple Lk 2:22-24. - He proclaims Good News to the Poor in Luke 4. . - His followers are to identify with and care for the poor as if they were Jesus - Matthew 25:40. - His followers were soon known for their care for one another - their possessions were used freely for the common good. Acts 2; 2 Cor 8:1-15 the collection given generously from the poor Macedonian churches. See Phil 4 and the giving of the church to help Paul. - God chooses again and again not to work through the rich but the poor and the weak. o Mary o The Corinthian church 1 Cor 1:16-31 o The disciples o The whole birth stories of Jesus o James 2:5 - A vital sign of obedience is what we do with our money and how we care for those in need. Money and how we use our time for others are spiritual matters. Quite simply how we love God will be shown in how we love our neighbour (who may even be our enemy). - Christians are to give sacrificially. Our whole lives are to be living sacrifices lived to honour God and serve others. Rom 12:1-2: Mk 8:31-38. - Care for the poor means getting involved with real people in all the messiness of life, not just those with whom we are comfortable.

### THESIS 7: MONEY AND WHAT WE DO WITH IT IS A DEEPLY SPIRITUAL ISSUE. CHRISTIANS NEED TO THINK ABOUT AND PLAN HOW YOU USE OUR MONEY AND HOW TO LIVE COUNTER-CULTURALLY IN A CONSUMER SOCIETY

"Even the least self-examined person cannot but feel uncomfortable when confronted with the Scripture's plain teaching on these matters. One is left with recourse to one of three possible options: (1) ignore the teaching altogether, (2) employ a self-justification hermeneutic that satisfactorily explains why such uncomfortable teaching cannot apply to one's personal situation, or (3) repent and be converted." Bonk, Missions and Money, 156. But what does it mean in practice to 'repent and be converted'? Practical Steps. - Think critically about the culture of consumerism and capitalism in which you live - don't be an unthinking passive consumer, be a disciple of Jesus! - Maintain a budget and plan what to give. - 2 Cor 8-9: give according to your means 2 Cor 8:11b. - Equality within the body (concern for the poor). - Give cheerfully 2 Cor 9:7. - Avoid indebtedness [except perhaps for a house or education??]. - Simplicity of lifestyle : be counter-cultural - no new cars; resist consumer greed; - Live to needs rather than wants. - Give away money til it hurts. - Avoid the 'buysphere' as much as possible!! - Serve others - not the self (a cross-centred life). - Give away a % of the value of any luxury items we buy. - Own things loosely - be able and ready to give them away. - Become a creator rather than a passive consumer (ie have fun without having to spend money!). - Live in community accountability. o big decisions of spending over a certain amount - car, big holidays, investments, house, to talk it over with others in the church family? What might be some reactions to this idea!!??

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Stewardship and Ownership
The document asserts that God is the owner of all things and humans are stewards. Work is presented as a blessing and a responsibility, while poverty is not viewed as inherently spiritual.

### Theme: Biblical Justice for the Poor
God is characterized as the defender of the poor, fatherless, and widow. The text argues that justice is a reflection of God's character and that believers are called to speak up for the destitute and share resources.

### Theme: Counter-Cultural Discipleship
The author calls for Christians to reject the 'faith of consumerism' by living simply, avoiding debt, practicing community accountability, and prioritizing the needs of others over self-centered consumption.
