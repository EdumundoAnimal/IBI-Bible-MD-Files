# Psalm 21: The Joy of the Vindicated King
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 21 is a Royal Thanksgiving and Eschatological Triumph Hymn. It is structurally divided into Section A (vv. 2–8), focusing on past deliverance and royal endowment, and Section B (vv. 9–13), focusing on the oracle of enemy subjugation. The psalm serves as a liturgical liturgy of fulfillment, answering the supplications of Psalm 20, and acts as a typological shadow of the Messianic King, Jesus Christ. It functions through themes of divine kingship, covenantal deliverance, and the unshakeable nature of divine trust (bōṭ ēaḥ), ultimately contrasting the fragility of human power with the permanence of God's covenantal steadfastness (ḥesed).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Kingship
The Psalm depicts the Davidic king as a recipient of derived, not autonomous, power, whose authority is entirely dependent on Yahweh's covenantal grace and strength.

### Theme: Eschatological Triumph
The imagery of the 'fiery oven' and the destruction of enemies serves as a prophetic, typological indicator of Christ's eventual and absolute victory over sin, death, and demonic powers.

### Theme: Covenantal Trust (bōṭ ēaḥ)
The theological fulcrum of the psalm, asserting that royal stability is not born of geopolitical might but of unreserved, existential reliance upon the Lord.
