# Viticulture and John 15:1-6
**Author:** [[Gary W. Derickson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article provides an exegesis of John 15:1-6 by analyzing first-century Palestinian viticultural practices. The author argues that interpreting these verses requires understanding the distinction between the 'abiding' relationship of believers and the 'fruitless' state of non-abiding individuals, emphasizing that the imagery of pruning and burning refers to spiritual discipline and agricultural management rather than the loss of salvation. The author explores the etymology of key terms such as 'airei' (lifting/removing) and 'kathairei' (cleansing/pruning) and concludes that the passage serves as an encouragement for believers to remain in fellowship with Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Viticultural Exegesis
The application of historical agricultural practices in first-century Palestine to clarify the metaphorical language used by Jesus in John 15 regarding vines and branches.

### Theme: Sanctification and Discipline
The argument that the pruning of branches symbolizes God's loving discipline of believers to increase their fruitfulness, rather than condemnation or loss of salvation.

### Theme: Linguistic Analysis
Critical examination of Greek terms like 'airei' and 'kathairei' to distinguish between removal and the act of pruning/cleansing.
