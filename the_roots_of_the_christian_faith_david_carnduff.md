# The Roots of the Christian Faith
**Author:** [[David Carnduff]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the conflict and contrast between Hebrew and Hellenistic (Greek) worldviews and their impact on Christian theology, doctrine, and church history. It examines how early church challenges, such as Gnosticism and Dualism, originated from Hellenistic influences, contrasting these with the holistic, God-centered nature of the Hebraic worldview found in Scripture. Key themes include the 'sign and wisdom' dichotomy, the rejection of the body-soul separation in favor of a holistic view of humanity, and the historical tension between relying on divine revelation versus human reason, particularly during the Enlightenment and the development of Cessationist theology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hellenistic vs. Hebraic Worldviews
A fundamental comparison identifying Greek thought as man-centered, dualistic, and focused on reason, whereas Hebrew thought is God-centered, holistic, and focused on revelation and experiential knowledge.

### Theme: Dualism and Gnosticism
The critique of the Gnostic and Hellenistic tendency to view the spirit as good and material matter as evil, which the author argues leads to distorted theology regarding the body, Christ's humanity, and ethics.

### Theme: Reason vs. Revelation
An analysis of the historical shift from biblical authority to human authority, specifically examining how Enlightenment rationalism and Scottish Common-Sense Philosophy influenced theological trends like Cessationism.

### Theme: Holistic Biblical Anthropology
The argument that biblical faith presents a unified view of the person as a 'BODY-SOUL' rather than a spirit imprisoned in a body, grounding the Christian hope in the redemption of the whole creation.
