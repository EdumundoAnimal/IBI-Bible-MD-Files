# Hermeneutics, Session 4: Historical Context
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This module covers the importance of historical and cultural context in biblical interpretation. It outlines three levels of context: historical (world of the author), literary (world of the text), and theological (the God factor). Using Zechariah 7 as a case study, the text explains how broad historical context (author, date, politics, audience) and specific historical context (purpose of fasts, geography, social classes) are essential for hermeneutics. It emphasizes that while historical context is an aid to interpretation, it must be integrated with literary and theological analysis.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Levels of Context
Interpretation requires analyzing historical context (author), literary context (the text itself), and theological context (divine significance).

### Theme: Secondhand Nature of Scripture
The Bible was originally written to other people in different times and places, requiring a bridge over the historical and cultural gap to understand its original message.

### Theme: Broad vs. Specific Context
Broad context encompasses authorship, date, and political climate; specific context includes the direct purpose of the writing, geographical setting, and social hierarchies of the time.
