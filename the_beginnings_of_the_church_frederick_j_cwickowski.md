# The Beginnings of the Church
**Author:** [[Frederick J. Cwickowski]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the origins of the early Christian communities, emphasizing their relationship with Judaism, the temple, and their eschatological expectations. It examines the socio-religious groups of first-century Judaism—Pharisees, Sadducees, Scribes, and Zealots—and their roles in the world of Jesus. The text highlights how early believers, initially identifying as devout Jews, lived within the framework of Jewish rituals and the temple, even as their conviction about Jesus as the Messiah and the arrival of the end-time shaped their unique communal life and practices like baptism and the breaking of bread.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Jewish-Christian Identity
Early Christians viewed themselves as a sect of Judaism rather than an outside, separate entity. They practiced traditional Jewish rituals, attended the temple, and were viewed by outsiders as a Jewish group.

### Theme: Eschatological Urgency
The conviction that the end-time was already initiated through the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus and the gift of the Spirit profoundly influenced the communal life of the earliest Christians.

### Theme: Communal Lifestyle
The practice of sharing possessions and communal meals, often referred to as 'the breaking of bread,' was central to the early Jerusalem community, reflecting their sense of oneness and anticipation of the end-time.
