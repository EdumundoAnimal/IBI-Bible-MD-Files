EXCURSIS: THREE MAJOR AREAS ADDRESSSED BY THE BOOK OF JOB: COMMUNICATION, JUSTICE OF GOD, AND KNOWLEDGE. 1. Communication. 2. Justice of God. (Theodicy) 3. Knowledge SCRIPTURE REFERENCES TO JOB Our character Job is mentioned in other areas of Scripture: Ezekiel 14:14 Rom. 11:35 (citation from Rom. 41:11) James 4:11ff Questions for consideration: What is your first response in most situations to friends who are enduring a period of suffering? Why do you think you respond in that way? Even though you may not know anyone who has lost their family, home, or wealth, how might you use the book of Job in your current life and service? ### Themes, Knowledge, and Wisdom

10. THEMES Wisdom; The Fool vs. The Sluggard; Adultery; The family; The friend; The tongue; Life and Death, etc. 11. KNOWLEDGE AND WISDOM A key theme is Knowledge and Wisdom. Both are used extensively in the book (knowledge 50 times and wisdom 110 times). H.L. Wilmington in his classic work simply entitled Wilmington’s Guide to the Bible states that knowledge is the “ability to correctly acquire facts.” And wisdom is the “ability to rightly apply those facts.” He further provides the following: a. They will deliver from the immoral woman 2:16 12. THE NATURE OF PROVERBS 13. THEOLOGY AND THE PROVERBS The presupposition of all wisdom: The fear of the Lord. (1:7) 14. THE CARDINAL RULE The proverbs should be regarded as observations, not guarantees. Proverbs are not promises; they are generalizations of how things usually work out. Some of the proverbs are true only in certain situations. Only one who is wise will know the situation in which a particular proverb applies. Much harm is done when this principle is ignored. Be careful.

Sinners prospered. I failed. lines 235-42 (Job 8:5-7; 18:5-21) APPENDIX E – Egyptian Wisdom Literature Egyptian wisdom consists of two basic genres: instructions and discussions. The instructions (or teachings) are largely the product of the pharaonic court as the king prepared his son to assume the administration of the monarchy. Egyptian instruction literature contains striking parallels to the Hebrew wisdom of the OT. Note portions of the “Teachings of Amenemope.”, an Egyptian sage writing near 1200 B.C., in comparison with the “Sayings of the Wise” in Proverbs 22:17-24:23.73 Proverbs and the Instruction of Amenemope Proverbs: Direct your ear and hear wise words. Set your heart to know them. For it is pleasant if you keep them in your inmost self. (Prov. 22:17-18a) Amenemope: Give your ears and hear what is said, give your mind over to their interpretation: It is profitable to put them in your heart. (3,10) Proverbs: Have I not written for you thirty counsels and teachings to teach you what is right and true? (Prov. 22:20) Amenemope: Mark for your self these thirty chapters: They please, they instruct, they are the foremost of all books. (27,7) Proverbs: Do not make friends with people prone to anger. With the hotheaded person do not associate. (Prov. 22:24) Amenemope: Do not fraternize with the hot-tempered man, nor approach him to converse. (11,12) Proverbs: When you sit down to eat with a ruler, observe what is before you. Put a knife to your throat if you have a big appetite. (Prov. 23:1-2) Amenemope: Look at the cup in front of you, and let it suffice your need. (23,16) Egyptian wisdom literature also has its parallels. Many of these works are contemplative, analytical, and even pessimistic in tone and mood and are reminiscent of the wisdom of Qoheleth in Ecclesiastes. Among the topics we find in Egyptian literature are: joy in life; social injustice; the problem of evil; and the reality of pain and death. Note the similarity between the “Harper Song” of Egyptian literature and the book of Ecclesiastes. Harper Song, Let not thy heart flag, Follow thy desire and thy good. Fulfil thy needs upon earth, after the command of thy heart. Until there come for thee that day of mourning.74 Ecclesiastes 8:15, So I commend the enjoyment of life, because nothing is better for a man under the sun than to eat and drink and be glad. Then joy will accompany him in his work all the days of the life God has given him under the sun. What do these similarities imply? Besides providing clues that there was sharing of ANE wisdom literature between cultures, it also provides insight into the universal attempt to cope with problems associated with human existence: the search for meaning and purpose in life; mystery and death; suffering; pain; injustice; and the relationship of the divine to the problem of evil. One important difference should always be noted in that ANE wisdom lit. often pays homage to a pantheon of deities whereas Israelite wisdom acknowledged only one God, Yahweh. (Prov. 22:17-19). This allowed Heb. Wisdom to deny materialism, pantheism, and dualism.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Cardinal Rule of Proverbs
The document asserts that proverbs are observations rather than absolute guarantees or promises. They represent generalizations of how life typically functions, and wisdom is required to discern the specific situations in which a particular proverb applies.

### Theme: Knowledge and Wisdom
Defined by H.L. Wilmington as the ability to acquire facts (knowledge) and the ability to rightly apply those facts (wisdom). The text emphasizes that both begin with the 'Fear of the Lord' and are essential for a godly life.

### Theme: Theodicy and Suffering
The text explores the problem of suffering through the Book of Job and comparative Ancient Near Eastern literature (The Babylonian Job), highlighting the tension between traditional piety and the reality of human experience.
