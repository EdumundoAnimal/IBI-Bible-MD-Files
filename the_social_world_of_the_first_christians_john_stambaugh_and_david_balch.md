# The Social World of the First Christians
**Author:** [[John Stambaugh and David Balch]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the social structures, daily life, and mobility of early Christian communities within the Roman Empire. It covers topics such as the transient nature of early followers, the role of travelers and businessmen in spreading the faith, the influence of existing cults like that of Serapis, the internal social hierarchy of early Christian communities including the importance of household leadership, and the overarching relationship between these communities and Roman authorities. The analysis emphasizes how early Christians navigated their faith amidst Greco-Roman social expectations, using rituals such as baptism and the Lord's Supper to reinforce group identity and moral conduct.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Early Christian Mobility
Explores how early Christians, including missionaries and businessmen, moved across the Roman Empire, facilitating the spread of their faith through established social and transportation networks.

### Theme: Social Identity and House Churches
Discusses how early Christian identity was shaped by meeting in houses, often led by the property owners, and how they balanced the egalitarian nature of their internal community with the existing patriarchal social structures of the time.

### Theme: Relationship with the State
Analyzes how the Christian community maintained its distinct religious identity while attempting to demonstrate loyalty to Roman authorities to avoid persecution and suspicion of subversive activities.
