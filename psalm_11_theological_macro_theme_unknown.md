### Textual Variants (BHS Apparatus & Dead Sea Scrolls)

Verse 1: lamnaṣṣēaḥ lədāwîd. The Masoretic Text (MT) assigns the prepositional dedication/attribution to David. The Septuagint (LXX) translates Εἰς τὸ τέλος· τῷ Δαυιδ ("To the end; of David"). No substantial variants emerge in the Qumran manuscripts (4QPs^a / 4Q83), confirming the high degree of textual stability of the superscription. Verse 1: nûdû, Qere vs. nûdî, Kethiv. Kethiv preserves the feminine singular imperative (nûdî harəkem ṣippôr, "Flee [thou, O soul/bird] to your mountain"), agreeing grammatically with the feminine noun napšî, "my soul") as an implied vocative. Qere standardizes to the plural imperative (nûdû, "Flee ye"), matching the plural pronominal suffix in harkem, "your mountain," plural suffix). Resolution: The Kethiv reflects an archaic construct where David's advisors speak directly to his soul (napšî), metaphorically conceptualized as a vulnerable avian creature, yet utilizing a collective address (-kem) to his entire entourage. Verse 6: yamṭēr ‘al-rəšā‘îm paḥîm. The MT reads paḥîm ("snares/traps"). Historically, scholars (e.g., Symmachus, Syriac Peshitta) and critical emendations (such as Cheyne, Duhm, and early ICC notes by Briggs) proposed reading peḥām, "coals of fire") to sustain parallel symmetry with ēš wəḡoprît, "fire and brimstone"). However, HALOT and WBC (Craigie) defend MT paḥîm as a deliberate, terrifying paradox: raining down "snares" (inescapable bird-traps) upon the very hunters who set snares in the dark (v. 2). The MT reading is strictly maintained as the lectio difficilior. Verse 7: yāšār yeḥĕzû pānêmô. Syntax Analysis: yāšār is singular ("the upright one"), while the verb yeḥĕzû is plural ("they shall behold"). Pronominal Suffix: pānêmô contains the archaic poetic suffix -ēmô, which can be read either as a 3rd person plural ("His face beholds the upright") or as an objective plural ("The upright shall see His face"). The LXX (εὐθύτητας εἶδεν τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ) interprets yāšār collectively as abstract righteousness or "the upright," seeing the face of God.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Refuge
Refuge is not a geographical escape but an ontological and relational state of alignment with Yahweh, even when social structures collapse.

### Theme: Divine Retribution
Retribution is depicted as a calibrated, formal distribution of justice (the 'allotted cup') rather than arbitrary cruelty, serving as a guarantee that tyranny will not have the final word.

### Theme: Cosmic Sanctuary
The temple is not an immanent local talisman but the transcendent command center of the universe, remaining stable while earthly foundations (šātôt) are demolished.
