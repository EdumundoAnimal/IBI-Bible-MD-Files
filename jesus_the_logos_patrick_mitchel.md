# Jesus the LOGOS
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives: Explore the meaning of John's affirmation that Jesus is the Logos; examine why and how John used Logos as a description for Jesus; discuss the Christological implications. Background to John's Gospel includes the purpose (evangelism/confirmation of faith), the Hellenistic context, and traditional authorship by John the son of Zebedee. The text notes a 'High Christology' throughout, specifically citing 'I am' sayings (Bread, Light, Gate, Good Shepherd, True Vine, Resurrection and Life, Way Truth and Life) and the intimate Father/Son language. The 'Book of Signs' emphasizes miracles and speeches revealing His glory. Background to LOGOS: The term is found in the prologue of John, 1 John, and Revelation. While Hellenistic concepts (Heraclitus, Platonism, Philo) exist, a Jewish Old Testament background is argued as more significant, citing Genesis (the creative Word), Proverbs 8 (Wisdom), the Torah (as the word of God), and the prophetic tradition of God's word accomplishing His plans. Conclusion: John synthesizes philosophical background to provide unique insight into the person and work of Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Logos
An exploration of whether the Johannine usage of 'Logos' stems from Hellenistic philosophical influences or traditional Jewish Old Testament theological foundations.

### Theme: High Christology
The presentation of Jesus as divine through 'I am' declarations and the intimate Father/Son relationship dynamic.

### Theme: Synthesis of Traditions
The argument that John uses a term with a rich, multifaceted background to convey a distinct Christian theology.
