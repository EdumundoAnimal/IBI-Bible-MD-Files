# Jesus the LOGOS
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives of this class: 1. To explore the meaning of John's affirmation that Jesus is the Logos. 2. To examine why and how John used Logos as a description for Jesus. 3. To begin to discuss the Christological implications of 1 and 2. 1. Background to John's Gospel a. Purpose and Situation: Jn 20:31 - to evangelise and/or to confirm faith? b. Cultural Situation: Hellenistic context (Language, Symbolism). c. Author: Traditionally John the Son of Zebedee, writing in Ephesus. "John, a Jew writing in a non-Jewish context". d. A High Christology throughout: 'I am sayings' (Bread, Light, Gate, Good Shepherd, True Vine, Resurrection and life, way truth and life). Intimate Father/Son language and 'Book of Signs' (1:19-12:50). 3. Background to LOGOS: Logos is only found in the prologue of John, and in other Johannine literature: 1 Jn 1:1 and Rev. 19:13. Certainly a non-Christian concept well known elsewhere. The ultimate question is what did the Evangelist mean by using the term Logos in John 1:1 and 1:14? i. Hellenism (Greek world): Heraclitus, Platonism, Justin Martyr, Philo. ii. Jewish background: A Jewish OT background makes far more sense of why and how John uses Logos. Genesis theme of "in the beginning" (en arche) and the creative "Word" of God. OT ideas of wisdom on Proverbs 8:22-31. In Jewish thought the Torah is often called the 'word of God' (Ps 119:105). In the OT the 'word of the Lord' is a promise or judgement that is sure to come to happen. 4. Conclusion: Is John taking up and using an idea with a rich philosophical background and using it to give a unique and profound insight into the person and work of Jesus?

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: High Christology
The presentation of Jesus in the Gospel of John through 'I am' statements, father/son language, and the 'Book of Signs' which emphasize his divine nature.

### Theme: Logos (Word) Theology
Analysis of the term Logos as both a Hellenistic philosophical concept and a rooted Jewish OT theme concerning the creative word of God and divine wisdom.

### Theme: Cultural Synthesis
The scholarly debate regarding whether John's Christology is a product of Greek philosophical influence or rooted in the Jewish OT tradition.
