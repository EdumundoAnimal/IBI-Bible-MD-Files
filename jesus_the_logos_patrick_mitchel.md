# Jesus the LOGOS
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives of this class: 1. To explore the meaning of John's affirmation that Jesus is the Logos. 2. To examine why and how John used Logos as a description for Jesus. 3. To begin to discuss the Christological implications of 1 and 2. 1. Background to John's Gospel: Purpose and Situation - Jn 20:31 - to evangelise and/or to confirm faith. Cultural Situation - Hellenistic context (Language, Symbolism). Author - Traditionally John the Son of Zebedee, writing in Ephesus. "John, a Jew writing in a non-Jewish context". A High Christology throughout (8:58; 10:30; 14:6) including 'I am' sayings (Bread, Light, Gate, Good Shepherd, True Vine, Resurrection and life, Way truth and life) and intimate Father/Son language. 'Book of Signs': 1:19-12:50. Miracles and speeches. 3. Background to LOGOS: Logos is only found in the prologue of John, and in other Johannine literature: 1 Jn 1:1 and Rev. 19:13. Certainly a non-Christian concept well known elsewhere. i. Hellenism (Greek world) - Heraclitus - 6th Cent BC, Platonism, Justin Martyr (c. 100-165AD), Philo (a 1st cent. Hellenistic Jewish philosopher living in Alexandria in Egypt). ii. Jewish background - A Jewish OT background makes far more sense of why and how John uses Logos. Genesis theme of "in the beginning" (en arche) and the creative "Word" of God; OT ideas of wisdom on Proverbs 8:22-31; In Jewish thought the Torah is often called the 'word of God' (Ps 119:105); In the OT the 'word of the Lord' is a promise or judgement that is sure to come to happen. God's word accomplishes God's plans. (Is 9:8; Ps 107:20; 147:15, 18). 4. Conclusion: Is John taking up and using an idea with a rich philosophical background and using it to give a unique and profound insight into the person and work of Jesus? The LOGOS in early church history.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Foundations
Exploration of the 'High Christology' in John's Gospel, emphasizing Jesus's unique identity through 'I am' statements and the Father-Son relationship.

### Theme: Logos (Word) Etymology and Context
Analysis of the tension between Hellenistic philosophical influences and Jewish Old Testament roots in the conceptualization of the Logos.
