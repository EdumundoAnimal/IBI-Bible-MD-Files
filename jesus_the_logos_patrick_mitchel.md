# Jesus the LOGOS
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Jesus the LOGOS. Objectives of this class: 1. To explore the meaning of John's affirmation that Jesus is the Logos. 2. To examine why and how John used Logos as a description for Jesus. 3. To begin to discuss the Christological implications of 1 and 2. 1. Background to John's Gospel. a. Purpose and Situation: Jn 20:31 - to evangelise and/or to confirm faith. b. Cultural Situation: Hellenistic context (Language, Symbolism). c. Author: Traditionally the author is John the Son of Zebedee, writing in Ephesus. "John, a Jew writing in a non-Jewish context". d. A High Christology throughout (8:58; 10:30; 14:6). I am sayings: 7. i. Bread (6:35, 48); ii. Light (8:12; 9:5); iii. Gate (10:7); iv. Good Shepherd (10:11); v. True Vine (15:1); vi. Resurrection and life (11:25); vii. way truth and life (14:6). Intimate Father/Son Language: eg. 5:30. A unique relationship. Multiple father / son language. Son of God - major theme. 'Book of Signs': 1:19-12:50. Miracles and speeches. 7 Signs revealing his Glory - causing mixed reactions and confusion and debate. 3. Background to LOGOS. Logos is only found in the prologue of John, and in other Johannine literature: 1 Jn 1:1 and Rev. 19:13. Certainly a non-Christian concept well known elsewhere. The ultimate question is what did the Evangelist mean by using the term Logos in John 1:1 and 1:14? i. Hellenism (Greek world): Heraclitus - 6th Cent BC, Platonism, Justin Martyr (c. 100-165AD), Philo (a 1st cent. Hellenistic Jewish philosopher living in Alexandria in Egypt). ii. Jewish background: However, a Jewish OT background makes far more sense of why and how John uses Logos. i. Genesis theme of "in the beginning" (en arche) and the creative "Word" of God. ii. OT ideas of wisdom on Proverbs 8:22-31. iii. In Jewish thought the Torah is often called the "word of God" (Ps 119:105). iv. In the OT the "word of the Lord" is a promise or judgement that is sure to come to happen. God's word accomplishes God's plans. (Is 9:8; Ps 107:20; 147:15, 18). 'Gone are the days when scholars can plausibly argue that John's Christology must have been reproduced only a very late stage of a slow evolutionary development of Christian faith, removed from its Jewish roots with a dramatically Greek milieu.' Blomberg, 73. 4. Conclusion. Is John taking up and using an idea with a rich philosophical background and using it to give a unique and profound insight into the person and work of Jesus? The LOGOS in early church history.

Exploration of the 'high Christology' in John's Gospel, emphasizing Jesus as the Logos and the unique Father-Son relationship.

### Theme: Contextual Origins of Logos
A comparative analysis between Hellenistic philosophical influences and the foundational Jewish Old Testament background regarding the concept of the Word.
