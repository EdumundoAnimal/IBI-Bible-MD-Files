# Missions and Money: Affluence as a Missionary Problem... Revisited
**Author:** [[Jonathan J. Bonk]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the complex and often problematic relationship between Western affluence and Christian missionary work. It provides an extensive analysis of both Old and New Testament teachings on wealth, personal possessions, and the economic responsibilities of believers. The text argues that excessive wealth often creates a barrier to mission by fostering self-reliance, security in material goods, and a lack of authentic solidarity with the poor. It critiques the tendency of missionary efforts to reflect the consumerist values of the home culture rather than the sacrificial, kingdom-focused values of Jesus. Key sections include an examination of biblical prohibitions against usury, the Year of Jubilee as a model for economic justice, and Jesus' teachings on wealth as a test of discipleship. The author emphasizes that genuine repentance for wealthy individuals involves radical stewardship and tangible sacrifice to benefit the needy, challenging Western missionaries to align their economic lives with their theological message.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Affluence as a Missionary Obstacle
The author argues that Western affluence acts as a spiritual barrier, creating a 'theological bankruptcy' where dependence on God is replaced by dependence on material security.

### Theme: Economic Justice in Scripture
The document highlights the Old Testament concepts of Sabbath and Jubilee as mechanisms for periodic economic leveling and the prohibition of exploiting the poor for interest or gain.

### Theme: Genuine Repentance
Repentance for the wealthy is defined not by internal feelings but by tangible, sacrificial action, such as the redistribution of resources and the practical care of the poor and marginalized.

### Theme: Stewardship vs. Ownership
A core theological argument that everything a believer possesses is a trust from God, meant to be used for the benefit of the community and the furtherance of the mission.
