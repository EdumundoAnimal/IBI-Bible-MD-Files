# Hebrews: Peril or Promise
**Author:** [[Paul F. Perry]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Introductory comments to this class outline

Our class today covers a portion of scripture that has produced much discussion. These scriptures contain texts whose interpretation are widely debated within the Christian church and have been so since its early beginnings. These notes are designed to give a starting point for further discussion of these texts within the context of the Hebrews course. As such they do not constitute a completely developed analysis at all points of the arguments below, but rather introduce broad overviews of some of the various interpretative suggestions.

### The importance of Hearing and obedience

The author of Hebrews was about to explore the central and deep message of Christ's priesthood and his Sacrifice. However, he had to prepare them first as he could not speak to them intelligibly (deeply) since they had become "hard of hearing" (5:11). They were not listening, and as a result they had become sluggish in understanding. Hearing the word = central topic throughout the sermon (2:1-4; 3:7-19; 4:12-13) and are closely linked in Hebrews. "Hearing" denotes the person's complete response to the message of God, and it is sluggishness with regard to this wholehearted response that is in view throughout 5:11-6:12. This correlation between listening and obedience explains why the preacher prepares his friends for what he has to say about Jesus as our priest and sacrifice by the strong words of 5:11-6:20.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Peril of Sluggishness
The document argues that 'hearing' in Hebrews is not merely auditory but denotes a complete response to God's message. Sluggishness in this response is the primary danger addressed in Hebrews 5:11-6:12, necessitating the preacher's stern warnings.

### Theme: Christ's Superior Priesthood
The text emphasizes that Jesus' priesthood is superior to the Levitical order because it is based on a 'better covenant' and an 'indestructible life,' moving beyond the limitations of earthly, repeated sacrifices.
