# Word Biblical Commentary: Psalms 101-150
**Author:** [[Leslie C. Allen]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Psalm 110:1-7

1 Davidic. A Psalm. Yahweh's oracle b to my master: 'Sit enthroned on my right, c until d I make your enemies a stool e for your feet.' 2 Your strong sceptre will Yahweh extend a from Zion so that you rule b over your enemies around you. 3 Your people will volunteer b on your day of power. On the holy mountains d from the womb of dawn e you will have the dew f of your youth. g 4 Yahweh has sworn an irrevocable oath: 'You are a perpetual b priest on the pattern of Melchizedek.' c 5 The Lord d on your right b shatters e kings on his day of anger. 6 He executes judgment among the nations, heaping up corpses, a shattering heads the wide world over. 7 He drinks from the stream beside the road: b therefore he holds his head high.

### Notes/Comments

1.a Unless the heading points to the royal nature of the psalm, it may refer to its origin in David's reign. The first line of the psalm indicates that the king is addressed (cf. 1 Sam 22:12; 1 Kgs 1:19). Mark 12:35-37, which reflects the contemporary Jewish understanding of the psalm as a messianic and of the psalm in terms of authorship, may represent an argumentum ad hominem. The insertion of the psalm into the Psalter, either because it reflects Davidic psalms, Ps 108, 109, or subsequently attracting them to Ps 110, is likely. A study of a messianic understanding (G. Westermann, Theologia Viatorum 8 [1961/62] 284; cf. the note on 101:1).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Messianic Interpretation
The document discusses how Psalm 110 was interpreted in the New Testament (specifically Mark 12:35-37) as a messianic text, noting that this may have been an 'argumentum ad hominem' used by Jesus to address contemporary Jewish views on authorship and the nature of the Messiah.

### Theme: Royal Theology
The psalm is identified as a royal psalm, likely associated with the Davidic covenant and the enthronement of the king, with references to the king sitting at Yahweh's right hand and being a priest in the order of Melchizedek.
