# Entangled in the Trinity: Economic and Immanent Trinity in Recent Theology
**Author:** [[Fred Sanders]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article examines the contemporary theological discourse surrounding the doctrine of the Trinity, specifically focusing on 'Rahner's Rule'—the assertion that the 'economic Trinity is the immanent Trinity, and the immanent Trinity is the economic Trinity.' Sanders explores how various theologians have interpreted and applied this rule, categorizing them into 'tight' and 'loose' interpreters. The text discusses the potential risks of collapsing these two modes of God's being, such as the loss of divine freedom and the subordination of God's internal reality to salvation history. Key figures analyzed include Karl Rahner, Piet Schoonenberg, Jürgen Moltmann, Wolfhart Pannenberg, Catherine Mowry LaCugna, Yves Congar, and Thomas Torrance, highlighting the tension between affirming the revelation of the Trinity in history and preserving the transcendence of God in himself.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Rahner's Rule
The foundational assertion that the economic Trinity (God in history) and the immanent Trinity (God in himself) are identical, serving as a watershed for modern trinitarian theology.

### Theme: Economic vs. Immanent Trinity
The tension between understanding God through his actions in human history (economy) versus understanding God's eternal, ontological reality independent of creation (immanent).

### Theme: Divine Freedom
The theological concern that if the economic and immanent Trinity are strictly identified, God's freedom is compromised, potentially making God dependent on creation.

### Theme: Tight vs. Loose Interpretation
A classification of theologians based on how strictly they adhere to the identity proposed by Rahner's Rule and how much space they allow for distinction between God's inner life and his external actions.
