# Syllabus for Paul's Epistle to the Romans (BI 130)
**Author:** [[Thomas Todd]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Carraig Eden Theological College Syllabus for Paul's Epistle to the Romans (BI 130) Second Semester 2008/09. Course Instructor: Thomas Todd. Level: 4. Credits: 10. Prerequisites: None. Learning Hours: 100 hours made up as follows: 20 hours lectures, tutorials and seminars, 15 hours of reading assigned material, 40 hours preparation of research paper, 1 1/2 hours examination, 23 1/2 hours revision for examination. Aim(s): The aim of this course is to provide the student with a good understanding of the book of Romans in its historical and cultural setting and its application and relevance for today. Learning Outcomes: On completion of this course, the student will be able to: A. Discuss the background of this epistle such as date of writing, occasion for writing, and the recipients of this letter. B. Demonstrate understanding of key theological passages in Romans. C. Identify the specific and unique contributions of this letter to the discipline of biblical theology. D. Appreciate more deeply the life, character and ministry of the apostle Paul. E. Understand more fully the way of the cross with the denial of self and the life in the Spirit that is to be discovered in this epistle. Teaching and Learning Strategies: Lectures and Seminars, Directed learning. Included is a bibliography of scholarly commentaries. A notice regarding a 2-week extension for the Romans assignment, moving the due date to Monday 16 March.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Hermeneutics
Focuses on understanding the Epistle to the Romans within its original historical and cultural context while bridging the gap to contemporary relevance.

### Theme: Pauline Theology
Exploration of the theological contributions, the character of Paul the Apostle, and key doctrinal themes such as the 'way of the cross' and 'life in the Spirit'.

### Theme: Academic Rigor
The course structure emphasizes deep research, evidenced by the 40-hour requirement for a research paper and the provision of a dense academic bibliography.
