# Syllabus for Paul's Epistle to the Romans (BI 130)
**Author:** [[Thomas Todd]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Carraig Eden Theological College Syllabus for Paul's Epistle to the Romans (BI 130) Second Semester 2008/09. Course Instructor: Thomas Todd. Level: 4. Credits: 10. Prerequisites: None. Learning Hours: 100 hours made up as follows: 20 hours lectures, tutorials and seminars, 15 hours of reading assigned material, 40 hours preparation of research paper, 1 1/2 hours examination, 23 1/2 hours revision for examination. Aim(s): The aim of this course is to provide the student with a good understanding of the book of Romans in its historical and cultural setting and its application and relevance for today. Learning Outcomes: On completion of this course, the student will be able to: A. Discuss the background of this epistle such as date of writing, occasion for writing, and the recipients of this letter. B. Demonstrate understanding of key theological passages in Romans. C. Identify the specific and unique contributions of this letter to the discipline of biblical theology. D. Appreciate more deeply the life, character and ministry of the apostle Paul. E. Understand more fully the way of the cross with the denial of self and the life in the Spirit that is to be discovered in this epistle. Teaching and Learning Strategies: Lectures and Seminars, Directed learning. (List of bibliography follows on page 2). Notification: In response to some feedback we have received regarding submission deadlines, we are giving you a 2 week extension on your Romans assignment. This essay was originally due on 2 March; it will now be due on Monday 16 March. The submission criteria remain the same (time, method of submission, etc.).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Theology of Romans
Focuses on identifying the unique theological contributions of Paul's epistle and interpreting its core messages.

### Theme: Historical Contextualization
Emphasis on understanding the occasion for writing, the intended recipients, and the cultural setting of the epistle.

### Theme: Applied Spirituality
The pedagogical intent includes both academic understanding and personal spiritual formation, specifically concerning self-denial and life in the Spirit.
