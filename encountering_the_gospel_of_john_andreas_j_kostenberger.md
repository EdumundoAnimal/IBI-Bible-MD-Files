# Encountering the Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Andreas J. Kostenberger]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text provides an in-depth analysis of the Gospel of John, covering authorship, historical context, theological themes, and the history of its interpretation. It examines internal and external evidence regarding apostolic authorship, the purpose of writing in light of the destruction of the temple in A.D. 70, and the relationship between John and the Synoptic Gospels. Key theological themes explored include the identity of Jesus as God and Son, salvation, the role of the Holy Spirit (Paraclete), and the nature of eternal life. The text also reviews modern scholarly debates, from historical-critical approaches to more recent attempts to rehabilitate the historical and apostolic reliability of the Gospel.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Apostolic Authorship
The argument that external and internal evidence supports the traditional Johannine authorship, identifying the author as the 'disciple whom Jesus loved', one of the Twelve.

### Theme: Purpose and Occasion
The Gospel was written to address the needs of a community following the destruction of the Jerusalem temple in A.D. 70, serving as an evangelistic tool to present Jesus as the Messiah and Son of God.

### Theme: Theological Christology
John's Gospel emphasizes the deity of Jesus and his incarnation, presenting him as the one who makes the Father known.

### Theme: Johannine Pneumatology
The Holy Spirit is presented as the Paraclete (Helper/Advocate) who guides believers, bears witness to Jesus, and convicts the world, continuing the mission of Jesus.
