# God's Son: Our Priest - Hebrews 7:1-28
**Author:** [[Paul F. Perry]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Lesson 6. God's Son: Our Priest - Hebrews 7:1-28

The heart of the sermon (7:1-10:18) is devoted to setting forth, and developing aspects of this teaching and clarifying the high priestly office of Jesus. It is striking that Hebrews describes Jesus as high priest before any clarification has been offered (2:17, 3:1-2, 4:14, 4:15, 5:10) Not until 5:1-10 is any explanation given for this distinctive way of referring to Jesus. Structurally, Hebrews 7:1-28 consists of three units. In 7:1-10 the preacher introduces the person of Melchizedek, focusing attention on the details of Genesis 14:17-20. In 7:11-25, Psalm 110:4 is brought prominently into the argument to advance the discussion toward the consideration of the priest who is like Melchizedek, the person of Jesus. In 7:26-28, the writer moves beyond the biblical passages in order to construct a bridge to the exposition of Jesus as our priest and sacrifice in 8:1-10:18.

### 3 Texts concerning Melchizedek in the Bible

The first mention of Melchizedek: Melchizedek is a historical figure who is remembered in Scripture because of a single incident in the life of the patriarch Abraham. The account of the incident is found in Genesis 14. The second mention of Melchizedek: is in Psalm 110:4 The third (and only other) mention of Melchizedek is in Hebrews. The unexpected reference to Melchizedek prompts a whole series of questions: o Jesus Like Melchizedek: 1) He will owe his appointment to the action of God. 2) He will be both priest and king. 3) Moreover, God swears that he will be a priest forever. The fulfilment of this sworn promise is guaranteed by the divine oath: 'The Lord has [the oath serves to emphasise the unchangeable character of God's purpose]. o Differences between Melchizedek and the Levitical priests - The appointment of the Levitical high priest was regulated by the Mosaic Law. According to the Law the high priest had to be able to: 1) Trace a line of physical ancestry back to Aaron on his father's side. His mother had to be a pure Israelite woman. 2) There is a second fundamental difference between Melchizedek and the Levitical priests. There was a succession of Levitical priest's. But this is not true of Melchizedek. - Scripture does not refer to any lineage - For that reason whenever one thinks of Melchizedek in terms of Scripture, it is necessary to think of him as possessing a permanent priesthood. His priesthood was not passed on to another.

### Hebrews 7:11-28 (overview)

o Two considerations, then, explain why God speaks of a new kind of priesthood in Psalm 110:4 when he promises the coming of one who is a priest like Melchizedek. (1) He will be a priest who owes his appointment to God rather than to the law of physical descent. (2) He will enjoy a permanent priesthood acknowledged in the formula, 'You are a priest forever' Both of these considerations were important to the preacher who wrote Hebrews. They become crucial categories when he speaks of the priesthood of Jesus for they demonstrate that Jesus is a priest like Melchizedek. In this section the preacher 'presents the Levitical priesthood as a type which was fulfilled by the priesthood of Christ' The preacher recognizes Aaron...called of God (5:4) 'but from the perspective of the event of Christ only as a foreshadowing of a new and superior priesthood announced in the OT itself' (italics mine). Hebrews 7:11-20 1. The prophecy of a new priesthood demonstrated that the old priesthood was insufficient (vv. 11-12) 2. Psalm 110:4 was actually a prophecy off the coming of High priest (vv.13-14)

### Hebrews 7:20-28

3. The new priest is superior in quality to the priests of the Levitical order (vv.15-17) '...on the basis of the power of an eternal life' (v. 16) The term 'indestructible', which occurs elsewhere in the Greek Bible only in 4 Macc 10:11, appears to have been carefully chosen. It was well suited to acknowledge that although Jesus' human life had been exposed to destruction through crucifixion, his life was not destroyed by the death suffered on the cross'. There is a description in the phrases used here of 'the new quality of life with which Jesus was endowed by virtue of his resurrection and exaltation to the heavenly world, where he was formally installed in his office as high priest' 'The author refers to an objective event in which Christ participated, rather than to a quality which belonged to him inherently. The power of life that the resurrection conferred upon Jesus demonstrated that his priesthood is not limited by the temporal, transitory character of the old priesthood based on physical descent, it is undergirded by a power that overcame morality and corruption, and consequently is beyond the reach of morality and corruption'. 4. The Levitical priesthood and the old law have been set aside by the new and better hope (vv. 18-19) The Pastoral implications of these truths above are drawn more fully in the remaining verses. In this section the writer wants to assure them that in Christ they have what he has earlier described as 'a better hope' (7:19) Jesus exercises a 'more excellent ministry' than any Jewish priest. He mediates a far better covenant, far superior to the old 'since it is enacted on better promises' (8:6). The priesthood of Christ is here claimed to be infinitely preferable to anything these Jewish Christians had known in their unconverted days.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Superiority of Christ's Priesthood
The text argues that Jesus' priesthood is superior to the Levitical order because it is based on an eternal, indestructible life rather than physical lineage or the Mosaic Law. It is characterized as a 'more excellent ministry' enacted on 'better promises'.

### Theme: Typology and Fulfillment
The author uses Melchizedek as a type or foreshadowing of Christ. The Levitical priesthood is presented as a temporary institution that was fulfilled and superseded by the priesthood of Christ, which is eternal and sworn by divine oath.

### Theme: Divine Appointment
Unlike the Levitical priests who relied on genealogical descent, Jesus' appointment as high priest is directly from God, as evidenced by the prophecy in Psalm 110:4, which establishes him as both priest and king forever.
