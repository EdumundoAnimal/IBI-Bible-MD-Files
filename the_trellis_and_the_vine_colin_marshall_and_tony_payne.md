# The Trellis and the Vine
**Author:** [[Colin Marshall and Tony Payne]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Trellis and the Vine presents a significant shift in ministry philosophy. The core argument distinguishes between the 'trellis' (the structures, organization, and administrative support of the church) and the 'vine' (the actual growth of the gospel through the prayerful teaching of God's word and evangelism). The authors argue that many modern churches have become preoccupied with building the trellis to the neglect of the vine. True Christian ministry requires prioritizing the cultivation of people—making disciples—over maintaining programs and organizational events. The text challenges the current model of the clergy-led church where the pastor does all the work, urging instead a shift toward a culture where every member is equipped and empowered to participate in the gospel's growth.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Trellis vs. Vine
The foundational metaphor: 'Trellis' represents the structures, administrative systems, and programs of a church, while 'vine' represents the organic, living work of gospel growth through teaching and evangelism.

### Theme: Discipleship-Centered Ministry
The authors argue that the primary goal of the church is to make disciples rather than just managing institutions or events, emphasizing that people are the true ministry focus.

### Theme: Decentralization of Ministry
A move away from the model where only the ordained minister does the 'real' work, advocating for the empowerment of the whole congregation to participate in gospel-centered personal ministry.
