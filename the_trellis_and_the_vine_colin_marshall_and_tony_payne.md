# The Trellis and the Vine
**Author:** [[Colin Marshall and Tony Payne]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The book examines the fundamental shift needed in Christian ministry, moving from a preoccupation with institutional structures (the trellis) to prioritizing the growth of people (the vine). It critiques the tendency of modern churches to be overly consumed by administrative, event-based programs, which often distract from the central task of discipleship and gospel-centered ministry. The authors argue that while organizational structures are necessary, they should support, not replace, the personal, relational work of teaching, mentoring, and prayer that is at the heart of evangelism and spiritual growth. The text calls for a move toward equipping individual believers to be disciple-makers, advocating for a culture that values training, reproduction, and the long-term work of building a team over the immediate pressures of maintaining existing institutional programs.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Trellis vs. Vine
A metaphorical distinction between the structures of ministry (trellis) and the actual growth of people through the gospel (vine). The authors argue that churches often prioritize the trellis over the vine.

### Theme: Disciple-making
The central mission of the church is the ongoing, relational process of teaching and equipping believers, rather than just managing church attendance or events.

### Theme: Leadership Empowerment
A call for church leadership to move from being managers of programs to mentors of people, freeing up time to develop other leaders for the gospel.
