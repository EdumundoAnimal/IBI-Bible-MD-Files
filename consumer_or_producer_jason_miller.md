# Consumer or Producer?
**Author:** [[Jason Miller]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The sermon 'Consumer or Producer?' by Jason Miller addresses the spiritual condition of the church, drawing an analogy between economic consumerism and spiritual productivity. The author argues that modern churches often produce consumers—people who want to be entertained—rather than producers who actively engage in the mission of the gospel. Using John 15:1-8, Miller likens God to a gardener (vinedresser) who purges non-producing branches and prunes productive ones to ensure greater fruitfulness. The message emphasizes that Christians are called to be connected to the vine (Christ) and actively bring others into the fold, rather than just absorbing spiritual resources for themselves. The text concludes by urging believers to provide evidence of their faith through mission-mindedness, mirroring God's desire to reach those who are lost.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spiritual Consumerism
A critique of churchgoers who seek entertainment and self-benefit without contributing to the outward mission of the church, analogous to economic consumers.

### Theme: The Theology of Fruitfulness
Based on John 15, the argument that the primary purpose of a believer is to 'bear fruit' for the Kingdom, which necessitates connection to Christ the vine.

### Theme: God the Vinedresser
God's active role in the life of the believer through purging (removing non-productive elements) and pruning (correcting and disciplining) for the sake of greater spiritual growth.

### Theme: Mission-Mindedness
The conviction that the church's ultimate goal is to reach the lost, reflecting God's own heart as demonstrated in the parable of the 99 and the prodigal son.
