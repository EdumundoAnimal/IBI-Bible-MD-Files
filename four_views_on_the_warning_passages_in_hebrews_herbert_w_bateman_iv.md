# Four Views on the Warning Passages in Hebrews
**Author:** [[Herbert W. Bateman IV]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the five warning passages in the Book of Hebrews, analyzing them through different theological lenses including Classical Reformed, Classical Arminian, and Wesleyan Arminian perspectives. The text examines the rhetorical function of these warnings, the concept of apostasy, and the necessity of believers remaining steadfast in their faith. It highlights the author of Hebrews' use of the Jewish historical record and the thematic progression from Old Testament models to the superior revelation found in the Son. Specific attention is given to the warnings in Hebrews 2:1-4, 3:7-4:11, 6:4-8, 10:19-39, and 12:14-29, emphasizing the author's argument for perseverance and the danger of drifting away from the divine message.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Warning Passages
The five specific sections in Hebrews (2:1-4, 3:7-4:11, 5:11-6:12, 10:19-39, 12:14-29) intended to facilitate reflection on courses of action and deter believers from abandoning their faith.

### Theme: Apostasy and Perseverance
The central debate regarding whether true believers can lose their salvation or whether these warnings function as a guardrail to ensure the perseverance of the elect.

### Theme: Superiority of the Son
The theological framework that contrasts the mediation of the Mosaic Law through angels with the superior, final revelation provided by the Son, thereby heightening the responsibility of the hearers.

### Theme: Theological Pluralism
The text serves as a dialogue between different theological traditions (Reformed and Arminian) to examine how each interprets the tension between divine sovereignty and human responsibility in Hebrews.
