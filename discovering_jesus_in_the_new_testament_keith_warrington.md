# Discovering Jesus in the New Testament
**Author:** [[Keith Warrington]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a theological analysis of the Book of Hebrews, focusing on the portrayal of Jesus as a superior figure compared to Old Testament prophets, angels, Moses, and the Levitical priesthood. It highlights Jesus's dual nature as both divine and human, emphasizing his role as an eternal high priest who offers unique intercession. Key themes include the finality of Jesus's sacrifice, the fulfillment of Old Testament promises, and the practical implications for believers living in obedience. Additionally, it contains brief insights into the Epistle of James regarding the lordship of Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Supremacy of Christ
The text argues that Jesus is superior to all previous figures in Hebrew history, including prophets, angels, and Moses, because he is the definitive manifestation of God.

### Theme: Christ's Priesthood
Analysis of Jesus as a 'High Priest' in the order of Melchizedek, emphasizing his eternal, perfect sacrifice compared to the transient Levitical system.

### Theme: Soteriology and Intercession
Exploration of how Jesus's human experience allows him to empathize with believers while his divine status grants him the power to offer efficacious, ongoing intercession.

### Theme: Christian Obedience
The theological necessity of responding to Jesus's sacrifice with a life of faith, endurance in suffering, and outward mission.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "The writer elevates Jesus’s status and the good news he preached by describing him as a ‘Son’ (1:2). He is not implying that God has more than one son, that Jesus is merely a son or even simply his son. He is, rather, stating that God used this superior medium of ‘son’ to reveal himself."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 167*

> "Jesus provides believers with a ‘sure and steadfast anchor,’ offering ‘hope’ to believers that they can repeatedly ‘enter the presence of God’ (6:19). He has gone before as ‘a forerunner’ to ensure that all is ready for when they need divine help (6:20)."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 177*

> "Unlike ordinary priests whose intercession was limited because of death, ‘he continues forever’ (7:24–25). Rather than assume that Jesus is constantly engaged in actual prayer on behalf of believers, it is better to appreciate the awesome message that the Hebrews is presenting here."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 177*
