# Psalm 107 — System Verification Report
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The Ancient Chair)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants & Apparatus (BHS / 4QPs / LXX). Superscription Absence: The Masoretic Text (MT) contains no authorial superscription (titulus), opening immediately with the liturgical imperative (Hôḏû Yahweh kî-ṭôḇ). Codex Vaticanus and the Septuagint (LXX Ps 106) lack an authorial assignment, though several later Greek recensions and the Vulgate add Alleluia. The Dead Sea Scrolls (4QPs^a and 11QPs^a) preserve the text with minor orthographic variations (fuller matres lectionis), confirming the antiquity of the canonical placement as the inaugural text of Book V of the Psalter. The Inverted Nuns (Nunim Hafukhim): In MT tradition, seven inverted nuns are placed before verses 21–26 and verse 40. Rabbinic tradition (b. Shabbat 115b, b. Rosh Hashanah 17b) interprets these as scribal indicators of distinct textual movements, highlighting structural relocations or signaling that these verses operate as independent liturgical vignettes depicting the reversal of fortunes. Verse 3 (ūmiyyām vs. ūmiddārôm): MT reads (ūmiyyām, "and from the sea") parallel to east, west, and north. Because yām typically designates the Mediterranean (West), critical commentators (e.g., Graetz, Duhm, and BHK/BHS apparatus) propose emending to (ūmiddārôm, "from the south") or reading yām as the Red Sea / Southern Ocean (the Erythraean Sea). The preservation of yām in 4QPs^a and LXX demonstrates that MT preserves the harder reading (lectio difficilior), signifying the southern waters or the chaotic abyss. Verse 17 (ʾĕwilîm vs. ʾăwālîm): MT reads (ʾĕwilîm, "fools"). Some modern commentators (Dahood, Driver) suggested an emendation to (ʾăḇēlîm, "mourners") or derived it from a root meaning "sick." However, the MT reading is confirmed by the ancient versions (LXX; Targum shatyē), pointing intentionally to the sapiential motif where self-willed rebellion produces psychosomatic and mortal distress.

### Grammatical Mechanics & Clause Breakdown

The Liturgical Imperative (v. 1): (Hôḏû) is a Hiphil imperative, masculine plural, from (yāḏāh), denoting a public confession, thanksgiving, and declaration of God’s character. The causal conjunction (kî) introduces the dual rationale: God's intrinsic moral excellence (ṭôḇ) and the eternal duration of His covenant loyalty (ləʿôlām ḥasdô). The Dual Refrain Pattern (vv. 6, 8; 13, 15; 19, 21; 28, 31): 1. The Cry of Distress: (wayyiṣʿăqû ʾel-Yahweh baṣṣar lāhem mimməṣûqôṯêhem yaṣṣîlēm) — Imperfect with waw-consecutive expressing narrative historical succession, shifting to the imperfect (yaṣṣîlēm, Hiphil prefix conjugation) expressing either habitual divine deliverance or vivid historical present. In verses 13, 19, and 28, the verb alternates to (wayyizʿăqû), an orthographic variant emphasizing vocal screeching under acute pressure. 2. The Eucharistic Refrain: (yôḏû Yahweh ḥasdô wənip̄ləʾôṯāw liḇnê ʾāḏām) — The verb (yôḏû) is a 3rd masculine plural Jussive (Hiphil), functioning as an urgent, normative exhortation: "Let them give thanks to Yahweh for His steadfast love and His miraculous interventions for the sons of Adam." Participial Chains in the Epilogue (vv. 33–35): The divine actions are depicted through a series of active participles: (yāśēm, "turning/placing"), functioning as timeless expressions of divine sovereign prerogatives over cosmic ecology and human sociopolitical hierarchies.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Hesed
Defined as unyielding, loyal, covenantal love; grace and fidelity bound to an oath. It is not merely an emotional disposition, but an active, historical intervention by which Yahweh preserves covenant partners from utter disintegration.

### Theme: Divine Sovereignty over Chaos
The psalm rejects both deism and naturalistic materialism, presenting a cosmos where God remains sovereign over comfort and catastrophe, including the command of storms and the reversal of human fortunes.
