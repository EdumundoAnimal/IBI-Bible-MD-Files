# Hebrews: Superiority of Christ with the Angels
**Author:** [[Eamon P Parkes]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The aim of this essay is to show how the author of Hebrews compares the superiority of Christ with the angels to stimulate a response from his audience, and why he needed to do this. First, we will set down the reasons why he has chosen to compare Christ with the angels. Second, we will investigate how this was brought about by the author. And finally we will examine how this could have an effect on the original reader and on a twenty first century generation. This section examines what we know of (1) the author identity, (2) his reasons for writing Hebrews, and the condition of the recipients. Finally, (3) the approximate date in which it was written. The author speaks of stunted growth and worries of the recipients drifting away to former Jewish traditions and sacrifices. Paul Achtemeier confirms that the author was not the founder of the congregation, plus his relationship to them is unclear, yet he was concerned about their predicament and well being enough to be addresses by them as pastor. The author warns and encourages his readers not to be lazy in their faith towards Christ. Additionally in Heb. 3:12 the writer senses that some members of the group were in serious danger of apostasy, which he defines as a turning away from the Living God. Furthermore Ellingworth sees that their, inner weakness may have been a chronic condition presupposing some of the readers to abandon, at some critical point, their faith in Christ. The author also uses the Old Testament to support his claim of Christ's superiority. The Son's name is declared greater than that of the angels (v.4). The author supports his claim with an impressive catena of Old Testament scriptures references in which God describes the Son as a divine Davidic King-priest to whom angels offer worship and service (vv 5-14). Christ's supremacy is the nautical anchor proposed by the author to secure the sluggish Christian reader to the faith during tempestuous seas of persecution. In (Heb. 2:1) we see nautical sailing expression used here and it conjures up a picture of a ship which has lost its anchor hold and is about to drift out into dangerous waters.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Superiority of Christ
The central argument of the book of Hebrews, using Old Testament citations to establish Christ's status as superior to angels, prophets, and previous Mosaic traditions.

### Theme: Apostasy and Drifting
The author identifies the recipients' danger of spiritual stagnation and turning away from faith, utilizing nautical metaphors like the 'anchor' to warn against drifting.

### Theme: Lesser to Greater Argument (Qal Wahomer)
A theological method employed in Hebrews to demonstrate that if the Old Testament revelation through angels carried weight, then the New Covenant revelation through the Son is infinitely more authoritative.
