# The Use of Rhetorical Topoi in the Characterization of Jesus in the Book of Hebrews
**Author:** [[Brian C. Small]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the characterization of Jesus in the Book of Hebrews through the lens of rhetorical topoi and narrative criticism. By applying Greco-Roman rhetorical principles—specifically the six elements of characterization identified by Theon—the author analyzes how the epistle constructs the identity of Jesus. The study focuses on how 'topoi' such as Origin/Race, Names/Titles/Roles, Training, and Actions/Events function to persuade the reader of Jesus's divine and priestly nature. The text draws on scholarly works, including those of Kenneth Schenck and Jerome Neyrey, to argue that Hebrews creates a 'story world' that attributes specific deeds, divine origins, and salvific roles to Jesus, ultimately demonstrating his perfection through suffering and his unique status as the Son of God.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Rhetorical Characterization
The application of Greco-Roman rhetorical topoi, as defined by Theon, to analyze how the author of Hebrews constructs the character of Jesus.

### Theme: Narrative Criticism of Hebrews
The argument that despite its non-narrative form, Hebrews utilizes narrative elements, such as characterization and setting, to form a coherent 'story world'.

### Theme: Theology of Jesus
The examination of Jesus's identity through various topoi, including his role as the 'Son', his perfection through suffering, and his function as the high priest and mediator of the new covenant.
