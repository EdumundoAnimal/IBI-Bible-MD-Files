# The Use of Rhetorical Topoi in the Characterization of Jesus in the Book of Hebrews
**Author:** [[Brian C. Small]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the rhetorical techniques used in the Book of Hebrews to characterize Jesus. It analyzes how the author of Hebrews employs various topoi—drawn from ancient rhetorical traditions, such as those articulated by Theon and Cicero—to construct a narrative and theological characterization of Jesus. The essay examines titles, actions, and events attributed to Jesus to demonstrate how these elements serve the author's purpose of presenting Jesus as superior to angels, a high priest, and the mediator of a new covenant. Key themes include the use of narrative criticism, the importance of Jesus's obedience and perfection through suffering, and the role of rhetorical topoi in shaping the audience's perception of Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Rhetorical Criticism
The application of Greco-Roman rhetorical devices, such as topoi (commonplaces), to understand how the author of Hebrews constructed the character of Jesus for the audience.

### Theme: Characterization of Jesus
An investigation into how Hebrews uses titles (Son, High Priest, Mediator, Pioneer), actions, and events to portray Jesus's divine nature, humanity, and salvific role.

### Theme: Narrative World
The argument that even non-narrative literature like Hebrews contains an underlying 'story world' that functions through characters, settings, and events to convey a persuasive argument.

### Theme: Perfection Through Suffering
The thematic focus on Jesus's learned obedience and perfection through his earthly suffering, which serves as a model for the believer and qualifies him for his high priestly role.
