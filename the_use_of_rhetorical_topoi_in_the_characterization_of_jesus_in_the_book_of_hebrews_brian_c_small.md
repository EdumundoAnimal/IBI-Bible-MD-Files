# The Use of Rhetorical Topoi in the Characterization of Jesus in the Book of Hebrews
**Author:** [[Brian C. Small]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the rhetorical techniques used in the Book of Hebrews to characterize Jesus. It examines how the author of Hebrews employs various topoi—a concept from classical rhetoric—to depict Jesus' nature, origin, and role. The author suggests that even though Hebrews is not a narrative, it uses tools of characterization to construct a 'story world' of Jesus. The essay analyzes specific topoi such as origin/race, names/titles/roles, training, and actions/events, demonstrating how the author uses these rhetorical devices to affirm Jesus' divine character and his role as the mediator of the new covenant, while also emphasizing his shared human experience.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Rhetorical Characterization
The application of classical rhetorical topoi (commonplaces) to develop the portrait of Jesus within the Book of Hebrews, moving beyond strictly narrative forms.

### Theme: Jesus' Divinity and Humanity
The theological tension and synthesis in Hebrews where Jesus is depicted simultaneously as the eternal Son of God and as a human figure who learns obedience through suffering.

### Theme: Titles and Roles
The examination of various designations attributed to Jesus, including 'Son', 'Apostle', 'High Priest', 'Mediator', and 'Pioneer', to delineate his functions in the economy of salvation.
