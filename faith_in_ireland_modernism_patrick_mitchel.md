# Faith in Ireland: Modernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Objectives of this class

1. To give an overview of the beliefs and attitudes of the modern era out of which Ireland is now emerging.

### Pre-modernism: God is the source of all knowledge

Middle ages - to about end of Reformation c 1600. Reneé Descartes, 'I think therefore I am' - the transition point to modernism.

### The Modern Era: 'The world can be understood and mastered'

Shaped by the following assumptions: i. Belief in human progress. Dating from the Enlightenment (c.1750 on) The age of reason. an age of optimism. . towards utopia. no limits on man's potential and ability. connected to the industrial revolution, the rise of science, medicine and technology. connected to aggressive colonialism. ii. "I" at the centre. iii. knowing things for sure (epistemological certainty) is both desirable and attainable. Desirable - progress obviously a 'good thing'. Attainable - Connected to scientific discovery (science grew out of Christian convictions that the world was made by a rational and knowable God). all truth could be discovered through human investigation. Connected to this is the Kantian view of the objective / subjective split. Authority (religious) could no longer dictate truth - only that which was substantiated by reason

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Modernism
The document defines modernism as an era characterized by the belief that the world can be understood and mastered through human reason, progress, and scientific investigation, moving away from the pre-modern view of God as the primary source of all knowledge.

### Theme: Epistemological Certainty
A core assumption of the modern era where truth is considered both desirable and attainable through human investigation, rooted in the Enlightenment's focus on reason and the scientific method.

### Theme: The Shift in Authority
The transition from religious authority dictating truth to a model where truth must be substantiated by reason, influenced by the Kantian objective/subjective split.
