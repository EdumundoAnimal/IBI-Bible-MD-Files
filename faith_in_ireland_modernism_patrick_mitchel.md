# Faith in Ireland: Modernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives of this class: 1. To give an overview of the beliefs and attitudes of the modern era out of which Ireland is now emerging. Pre-modernism: God is the source of all knowledge. Middle ages to about end of Reformation c 1600. René Descartes, 'I think therefore I am' the transition point to modernism. The Modern Era: 'The world can be understood and mastered'. Shaped by the following assumptions: i. Belief in human progress: Dating from the Enlightenment (c.1750 on) The age of reason, an age of optimism, towards utopia, no limits on man's potential and ability, connected to the industrial revolution, the rise of science, medicine and technology, connected to aggressive colonialism. ii. 'I' at the centre. iii. knowing things for sure (epistemological certainty) is both desirable and attainable: Desirable progress obviously a 'good thing'. Attainable Connected to scientific discovery (science grew out of Christian convictions that the world was made by a rational and knowable God). all truth could be discovered through human investigation. Connected to this is the Kantian view of the objective / subjective split. Authority (religious) could no longer dictate truth only that which was substantiated by reason.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Modernism
The shift from pre-modern dependence on divine knowledge to the modern belief that the world is inherently rational, knowable, and subject to human mastery.

### Theme: Enlightenment Values
An era defined by scientific discovery, industrial progress, and optimism, underpinned by the idea that human reason is the ultimate source of truth.

### Theme: Epistemological Certainty
The belief that objective truth is both desirable and attainable through systematic human investigation, moving away from reliance on religious authority.
