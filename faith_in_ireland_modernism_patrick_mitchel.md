# Faith in Ireland: Modernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives of this class: 1. To give an overview of the beliefs and attitudes of the modern era out of which Ireland is now emerging. Pre-modernism: God is the source of all knowledge (Middle ages - to about end of Reformation c 1600). Reneé Descartes, 'I think therefore I am' - the transition point to modernism. The Modern Era: 'The world can be understood and mastered'. Shaped by the following assumptions: i. Belief in human progress: Dating from the Enlightenment (c.1750 on) The age of reason. An age of optimism. Towards utopia. No limits on man's potential and ability. Connected to the industrial revolution, the rise of science, medicine and technology. Connected to aggressive colonialism. ii. 'I' at the centre. iii. Knowing things for sure (epistemological certainty) is both desirable and attainable. Desirable - progress obviously a 'good thing'. Attainable - Connected to scientific discovery (science grew out of Christian convictions that the world was made by a rational and knowable God). All truth could be discovered through human investigation. Connected to this is the Kantian view of the objective / subjective split. Authority (religious) could no longer dictate truth - only that which was substantiated by reason.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Modernism
The historical and philosophical transition from a God-centered worldview to one defined by human reason, scientific mastery, and individual autonomy.

### Theme: Epistemological Shift
The transition from religious authority as the primary source of truth to the Enlightenment belief that truth is attained through human reason and investigation.

### Theme: Human-Centric Progress
The ideological assumption that humanity can achieve unlimited potential through scientific discovery and the rejection of external dictation of truth.
