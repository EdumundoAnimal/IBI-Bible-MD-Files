# Faith in Ireland: Modernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Objectives of this class: 1. To give an overview of the beliefs and attitudes of the modern era out of which Ireland is now emerging. Pre-modernism: God is the source of all knowledge (Middle ages to about end of Reformation c 1600). Rene Descartes, 'I think therefore I am' - the transition point to modernism. The Modern Era: 'The world can be understood and mastered'. Shaped by the following assumptions: i. Belief in human progress: Dating from the Enlightenment (c. 1750 on), The age of reason, an age of optimism, towards utopia, no limits on man's potential and ability, connected to the industrial revolution, the rise of science, medicine and technology, connected to aggressive colonialism. ii. 'I' at the centre. iii. knowing things for sure (epistemological certainty) is both desirable and attainable: Desirable - progress obviously a 'good thing'; Attainable - Connected to scientific discovery (science grew out of Christian convictions that the world was made by a rational and knowable God); all truth could be discovered through human investigation; Connected to this is the Kantian view of the objective/subjective split; Authority (religious) could no longer dictate truth - only that which was substantiated by reason.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Modernism vs Pre-modernism
The document defines the shift from a God-centered pre-modern era to a human-centered modern era characterized by the belief that the world can be understood and mastered through reason.

### Theme: Epistemological Certainty
The conviction that truth is both desirable and attainable through human investigation, moving away from religious authority as the primary source of truth.

### Theme: Humanism and Progress
The rise of optimistic belief in human potential, fueled by the Enlightenment, scientific advancement, and the positioning of the individual ('I') at the center of reality.
