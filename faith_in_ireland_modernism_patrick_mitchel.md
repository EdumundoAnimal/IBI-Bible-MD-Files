# Faith in Ireland: Modernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
FAITH in IRELAND: Modernism. Objectives of this class: 1. To give an overview of the beliefs and attitudes of the modern era out of which Ireland is now emerging. Pre-modernism: God is the source of all knowledge. Middle ages to about end of Reformation c 1600. Reneé Descartes, 'I think therefore I am' - the transition point to modernism. The Modern Era: 'The world can be understood and mastered'. Shaped by the following assumptions: i. Belief in human progress (Dating from the Enlightenment (c.1750 on) The age of reason, an age of optimism, towards utopia, no limits on man's potential and ability, connected to the industrial revolution, the rise of science, medicine and technology, connected to aggressive colonialism). ii. "I" at the centre. iii. knowing things for sure (epistemological certainty) is both desirable and attainable (Desirable - progress obviously a 'good thing', Attainable - Connected to scientific discovery (science grew out of Christian convictions that the world was made by a rational and knowable God), all truth could be discovered through human investigation, Connected to this is the Kantian view of the objective / subjective split, Authority (religious) could no longer dictate truth - only that which was substantiated by reason).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Transition to Modernism
The shift from a pre-modern worldview where God is the source of all knowledge to a modern one centered on human reason and individual subjectivity, marked by Descartes' philosophy.

### Theme: Assumptions of the Modern Era
The era is defined by the belief in human progress, the centrality of the individual, and the pursuit of epistemological certainty through science and human investigation.

### Theme: Reason and Authority
The rejection of religious authority as a source of absolute truth in favor of truths substantiated by reason and scientific discovery.
