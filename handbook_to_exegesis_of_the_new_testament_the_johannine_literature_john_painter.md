# Handbook to Exegesis of the New Testament: The Johannine Literature
**Author:** [[John Painter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Johannine literature consists of the Gospel of John, the Epistles of John, and Revelation or the Apocalypse. Contemporary scholars generally recognize that the Gospel and Epistles came from the Johannine school, if not from the same author. The Johannine literature is marked by a dualistic worldview: God and the devil, the children of light and children of darkness. The fourth gospel is written in simple but correct Hellenistic Greek, using a limited and repetitive vocabulary. The document explores the authorship of the Johannine corpus, suggesting it is a product of a school or community rather than a single individual. It emphasizes that the Gospel, Epistles, and Revelation, while distinct in genre and purpose, share a common Johannine theology and focus on the witness of Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Dualism
The persistent pattern of binary opposition between light and darkness, truth and falsehood, and the world under evil versus the children of God.

### Theme: The Johannine School
The scholarly argument that the literature (Gospel, Epistles, and Revelation) originated from a specific community or 'school' rather than a single individual, explaining the variations in style and theology.

### Theme: Signs and Witness
The functional purpose of miracles in the Fourth Gospel, serving as 'signs' that point to Jesus as the Christ and demand a decision from the audience.

### Theme: Historical Reconstruction
The methodology used by exegetes to date the literature, focusing on the sociopolitical context of the late first century, specifically regarding the conflict with the synagogue.
