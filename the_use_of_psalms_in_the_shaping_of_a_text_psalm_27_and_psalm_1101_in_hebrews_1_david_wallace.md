# The Use of Psalms in the Shaping of a Text: Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1 in Hebrews 1
**Author:** [[David Wallace]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document investigates the rhetorical and theological function of Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1 within the first chapter of the Epistle to the Hebrews. The author argues that the author of Hebrews utilizes these specific Psalms to construct a Christological argument establishing the superiority of the Son over the angels. The first chapter is structurally divided into an exordium (verses 1-4) and a catena (verses 5-13), both of which frame the argument around the royal and divine status of Jesus. The use of these texts serves to connect the Son's appointment as heir, his role in the creation of the world, and his final exaltation at the right hand of God, effectively contrasting the ephemeral status of angels with the eternal, divine authority of the Son.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Exordium and Catena Structure
The author divides Hebrews 1 into two rhetorical units: the exordium (1:1-4), which serves as a prelude to Christological concepts, and the catena (1:5-13), a series of quotations from the Psalms that provide scriptural authority for the Son's superiority.

### Theme: Christological Supremacy
The primary argument of the text is that the author of Hebrews uses Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1 to demonstrate that Jesus is superior to angels, possessing a unique status as the Son and the heir of all things.

### Theme: Use of Old Testament in New Testament
The document analyzes how the writer of Hebrews creatively employs the Jewish Scriptures, specifically Psalms, to form the foundation of his argument regarding the divinity and exaltation of Christ.
