# The Use of Psalms in the Shaping of a Text: Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1 in Hebrews 1
**Author:** [[David Wallace]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text examines how the author of the Epistle to the Hebrews utilizes Psalm 2:7 and Psalm 110:1 as foundational elements to structure the theological argument in the first chapter of Hebrews. The argument posits that the author of Hebrews employs these two specific Psalms to frame the exordium and the following catena, effectively establishing Christ's superiority over angels and his divine role as both Son and King. By framing the exordium with these allusions, the author weaves together themes of divine appointment, exaltation, and the mediator role of Christ, distinguishing his identity from that of ministering angels. The analysis covers the interplay between the catena, the exordium, and the broader theological aim of demonstrating the divine exaltation of Jesus as described in the Old Testament Scriptures.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Exaltation
The author of Hebrews uses Psalm 2:7 and 110:1 to establish Jesus's divine authority and superiority over angels through his exaltation.

### Theme: Exordium and Catena Structure
The literary structure of Hebrews 1 is framed by the use of Old Testament citations, creating an inclusio that highlights Christ's role as Son and King.

### Theme: Jewish Scriptural Precedence
The interpretation relies on the foundational authority of the Hebrew Bible (Old Testament) to validate the new revelation in Christ.
