### 1. Linguistic & Syntax Report

Textual Variants (BHS Apparatus & Ancient Versions) ● v. 11–12: (וְ גִ ילּו בִ רְ עָדָ ה׃ נַשְ קּו־בַ ר) The primary crux interpretum of Psalm 2 resides across the juncture of verse 11b and 12a. ○ The Masoretic Text reads wəgîlû bir‘ādāh (ה ָד ָע ְּר ִׁבַוּיל ִׁג ְּו -" rejoice with trembling") followed by naššəqû-ḇar (ר ַו־בּק ְּשַנ -" kiss the son" or "kiss purely"). ○ LXX: Renders naššəqû-ḇar as $\delta\rho\alphá\xi\alpha\sigma\theta\epsilon\ \pi\alpha\iota\delta\epsilon\acute{\iota}\alpha\varsigma$ ("lay hold of instruction / discipline"), understanding bar not as the Aramaic loanword for "son" (bar, ר ַב (nor the Hebrew noun for purity (bor, ר ֹּב(, but deriving it from mûsār (instruction) or reading the Hebrew root br as related to pure discipline (cf. Targum: qabbilû ulpānā’, "receive instruction"). ○ Syriac Peshitta: Reads nāšqû ləḇaryā’ ("kiss the pure one" or "kiss the son"), aligning closer with the MT's vocative/accusative construction. ○ Vulgate (Jerome): Followed the LXX in the Gallican Psalter (apprehendite disciplinam), yet in his Hebraica Veritas (translation directly from the Hebrew), Jerome rendered the phrase adorate pure ("worship purely") or adorate filium ("adore the son"), acknowledging the ambiguity of bar. ○ Aquila & Symmachus: Aquila translates $\kappa\alpha\tau\alpha\phi\iota\lambda\etá\sigma\alpha\tau\epsilon\ \epsilon\mathcal{\iota}\kappa\rho\iota\nu\tilde{\omega}\varsigma$ ("kiss purely / sincerely"); Symmachus renders $\pi\rho\omicron\sigma\kappa\upsilon\nu\etá\sigma\alpha\tau\epsilon\ \\kappa\alpha\theta\alpha\rho\tilde{\omega}\varsigma$ ("worship purely"). ○ Text-Critical Synthesis: The MT reading naššəqû-ḇar as "kiss the Son" reflects an early West-Semitic / Aramaic royal court idiom (bar used in royal diplomatic contexts alongside bēn in v. 7 to prevent auditory alliteration/cacophony with pen-ye’ĕnap, "lest he be angry"), or an intentional polysemy where homage to the royal son and pure submission coalesce. ● v. 9: (תְ רֹעֵם בְ שֵ בֶ ט בַ רְ זֶל) ○ The MT vocalizes təro‘ēm from the root rā‘a‘ (עַע ָר -" to break, smash, shatter"). ○ The LXX translates $\pi\omicron\iota\mu\alpha\nu\epsilon\tilde{\iota}\varsigma\ \alpha\nu\acute{\tau}\omicron\upsiloń\varsigma$ ("you shall shepherd / rule them"), reading the unpointed consonantal text tr‘m as deriving from the root rā‘āh (הָע ָר -" to shepherd, pasture"). ○ Revelation 2:27, 12:5, and 19:15 universally cite the LXX reading ($\pi\omicron\iota\mu\alpha\nu\epsilon\tilde{\iota}$), cementing the messianic paradox of the royal shepherd who rules and smashes rebellious opposition with an iron scepter.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Sovereign Royal Eschatology
Psalm 2 is interpreted as a forward-looking eschatological template rather than a historical antiquity, asserting that the Davidic throne is established by the decree of the Creator of heaven and earth.

### Theme: Cosmic Rebellion
The text identifies human history as a perpetual struggle for ultimate authority, where nations and rulers conspire to throw off divine restraints, which the Psalm diagnoses as 'rîq' (emptiness/nihilism).

### Theme: Redemptive-Covenantal Sonship
The Psalm establishes the Messianic paradox of the royal shepherd who rules and smashes opposition, with the 'Son' being the eternally begotten King whose coronation is ratified by the Resurrection.
