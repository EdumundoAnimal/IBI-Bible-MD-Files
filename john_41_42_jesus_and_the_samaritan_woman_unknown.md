# John 4:1-42: Jesus and the Samaritan Woman
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The commentary analyzes the encounter between Jesus and the Samaritan woman in John 4. Key themes include the shift in focus from the Jerusalem and Mount Gerizim temples to a new worship in spirit and truth, the use of water as a metaphor for divine life, and the challenge to traditional Jewish-Samaritan animosity. The passage also addresses the theological significance of Jesus as the fulfiller of Old Testament promises and the transition of salvation history, noting that 'salvation is from the Jews' while simultaneously declaring the obsolescence of existing temple locations.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Shift in Worship
Jesus challenges the localized nature of worship, arguing that neither Mount Gerizim nor Jerusalem is the exclusive site for worshiping God, shifting focus to 'spirit and truth'.

### Theme: Water Metaphor
The 'living water' serves as a symbol for divine life and spiritual sustenance that surpasses physical water, drawing connections to Old Testament imagery of God providing life-giving water.

### Theme: Theological Supersessionism
The author argues that Jesus positions himself as the fulfillment of Old Testament promises, rendering the Samaritan and Jewish temple conflicts obsolete.
