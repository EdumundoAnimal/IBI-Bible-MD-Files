# Psalm 149: Expository Analysis
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 149 explores the theological tension between doxology (worship) and cosmic justice (eschatological judgment). The text frames the psalm within a post-exilic context, contrasting the ancient Near Eastern imperial model of conquest with a canonical subversion model where the 'anāwîm (the humble) find vindication through YHWH. The analysis bridges the linguistic structure of the Hebrew text—including terms like ḥasîdîm (covenant ones), mishpāṭ kātûv (written judgment), and the two-edged sword (ḥerev pîfiyyôt)—with New Testament fulfillment in Christ and historical reception from Patristic to Reformation eras. The document concludes by proposing a sermon design and application framework that encourages a posture of rest and faithful witness over carnal aggression or escapism.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Eschatological Kingdom and Doxology
The psalm demonstrates that true worship is not merely contemplative but serves as an instrument of divine justice, where the Church participates in the eschatological victory of Christ.

### Theme: Covenantal Vindicatory Theocracy
The 'anāwîm (humbled ones) are not victims of fate but are commissioned by YHWH to execute a pre-revealed, objective divine judgment, transforming their social status from marginalized to participants in the divine verdict.

### Theme: Canonical Transposition of Holy War
The shift from physical, nationalistic 'holy war' motifs (herem) to the spiritualized 'two-edged sword' (the Word of God) in the New Covenant context (Heb 4:12, Eph 6:17).
