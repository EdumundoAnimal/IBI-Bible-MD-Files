# The Holy Trinity: In Scripture, History, Theology, and Worship
**Author:** [[Robert Letham]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document covers early trinitarian theology, focusing on the developments in the thought of Tertullian and Origen. It explores Tertullian's contribution to Western theology regarding the distinction of persons within the unity of the Godhead, and Origen's eastern perspective on the eternal generation of the Son and his trinitarian schema. The text analyzes how these thinkers grappled with scripture, heresy, and the philosophical language available to them in the early centuries of the church.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Tertullian's Trinitarianism
Tertullian established important terminology for the Western Church, using 'persona' (person) to define personal distinctions while maintaining the unity of substance (substantia). He addressed modalism and subordinationism, grounding his theology in scriptural interpretation.

### Theme: Origen's Theology of Eternal Generation
Origen, an Eastern theologian, proposed the 'eternal generation' of the Son to ensure the Son's full divinity while avoiding the material implications of gnostic emanations. His theology was occasionally criticized for subordinationist tendencies.

### Theme: Early Trinitarian Development
The struggle to articulate the relationship between the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit using philosophical terms (like substantia or hypostasis) while remaining faithful to biblical revelation.
