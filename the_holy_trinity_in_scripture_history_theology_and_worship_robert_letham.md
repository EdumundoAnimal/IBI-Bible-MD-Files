# The Holy Trinity: In Scripture, History, Theology, and Worship
**Author:** [[Robert Letham]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document covers historical development of Trinitarian thought. It discusses Tertullian's contributions, focusing on his introduction of the term persona, his rejection of modalism, and his distinction of persons within the divine essence. It also examines Origen's theological views, specifically his doctrine of the eternal generation of the Son, the relationship between the Father and the Son, and the role of the Holy Spirit. The text highlights how both figures navigated the balance between maintaining the unity of God and the distinctness of the three persons, while also noting criticisms of their respective potential for subordinationist readings.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Eternal Generation
The theological argument that the Son is eternally generated by the Father, ensuring his divinity while preserving the distinct personal existence within the Godhead.

### Theme: Subordinationism
A recurring concern in early Trinitarian thought where the Son and Spirit are viewed as having a dependent or lesser ontological status compared to the Father, often criticized in later orthodoxy.

### Theme: Economic Trinity vs. Immanent Trinity
The tension between the biblical revelation of the Trinity in the history of salvation (economic) and the essential, eternal existence of the three persons within the divine life (immanent).
