# The Antecedents of Evangelical Conversion Narrative
**Author:** [[D. Bruce Hindmarsh]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the historical and theological development of the evangelical conversion narrative, tracing its roots from early Christian spiritual autobiographies, through the Reformation, and into the modern evangelical period. It highlights how conversion narratives serve as testimonies of personal faith, often used to assert religious identity or justify individual actions. The author analyzes key figures such as Augustine, Thomas Bilney, John Calvin, and Richard Baxter, noting how these accounts evolved from apologies or legal justifications into deeply personal expressions of internal change. The text also examines the role of spiritual autobiography in the context of the gathered churches and how modern evangelicalism adapted these older forms to emphasize personal experience.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Conversion Narrative as Identity
Conversion stories are analyzed not merely as historical records but as intentional narratives used by individuals and groups to define religious identity and authority.

### Theme: Continuity and Discontinuity
The text investigates the tension between modern evangelicalism's unique character and its historical antecedents, questioning whether it constitutes a new phenomenon or a continuation of earlier traditions.

### Theme: Spiritual Autobiography
The evolution of the autobiography genre is linked to theological developments, where introspective accounts of faith become central to confirming one's standing before God and the community.
