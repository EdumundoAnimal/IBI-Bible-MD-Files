Linguistic & Syntax Report PSALM 108 MACRO-STRUCTURAL ANTHOLOGY [PART A: Cosmic Doxology] (vv. 1–5 / MT 2–6) Source: Psalm 57:7–11 Key Motif: *Ḥesed* & *'Ĕmet* Exalted Above Heavens [PART B: Territorial Conquest] (vv. 6–13 / MT 7–14) Source: Psalm 60:5–12 Key Motif: Divine Oracle & Subjugation of Foes Textual Variants (BHS Apparatus, Qumran, Versions) ● Psalm 108:1 (MT 108:2): The Masoretic Text (MT) reads נָכ֣ ֹון לִ בִ֣ י אֱֹלהִִ֑ ים אָ שִִׁ֥ ירָ ה וַַ֝אֲזַמְּ רָָ֗ ה אַ ף־י ִִֽודֹבְּכ (nākôn libbî ’ĕlōhîm ’āšîrāh wa’ăzammərāh ’ap-kəbôdî). In comparison to its source text in Psalm 57:8 (MT), which repeats נָכ֣ ֹון לִ בִ֣ י (nākôn libbî) twice, Psalm 108:1 omits the second iteration and appends אַ ף־י ִִֽודֹבְּכ (ap-kəbôdî, "even my glory / with my whole being"). The Septuagint (LXX Psalm 107:2) translates ᾄσομαι καὶ ψαλῶ ἐν τῇ δόξῃ μου (āsomai kai psalō en tē doxē mou), treating אַ ף־י ִִֽודֹבְּכ as the instrumental means of praise ("with my glory/soul"). Dead Sea Scrolls fragments (4QPs^q and 11QPs^a) align with the MT's condensed single-clause structure. ● Psalm 108:6 (MT 108:7): MT presents a classic Kethiv/Qere alternation: Kethiv reads וַַ֝אֲנֵנִֽי (wa‘ănēnî, "and answer me", 1st person singular pronominal suffix), while the Qere (supported by LXX ἐπάκουσόν μου, Syriac Peshitta, and Jerome's Vulgate exaudi me) confirms the singular, maintaining grammatical continuity with the singular speaker of verses 1–5, despite the plural י ֶיד ִדְּךָ (yədîdeykā, "your beloved ones") in the immediate preceding clause. ● Psalm 108:9 (MT 108:10): The MT reads עָלַי פְּ לֶשֶ ת אֶ תְּ רֹועָעִ י (‘ālay pəlešet hitrô‘ā‘î, "over Philistia I shout in triumph" — Hithpalel imperfect 1st person singular of רּועַ, rûa‘). This diverges from Psalm 60:10 (MT), which features the imperative עָלַי פְּ לֶשֶ ת הִ תְּ רֹועָעִ י (‘ālay pəlešet hitrô‘ā‘î, "over Philistia, shout in triumph [O my soul] / cry aloud to me"). LXX renders Psalm 108:9 as ἐμοὶ ἀλλόφυλοι ὑπετάγησαν ("to me the foreigners were subjected"), reflecting an interpretive harmonization with the motif of sovereign subjugation.

### Grammatical Mechanics & Syntax Breakdown

● Verse 1 (MT v. 2): נָכ֣ ֹון לִ בִ֣ י (nākôn libbî) employs a Niphal participle of כּון (kûn), functioning as a predicate adjective denoting an immovable, fixed state of intentionality. The subsequent cohortatives אָ שִִׁ֥ ירָ ה (āšîrāh, Qal cohortative) and וַַ֝אֲזַמְּ רָָ֗ ה (wa’ăzammərāh, Piel cohortative) express resolve and willful self-summoning. ● Verse 2 (MT v. 3): עּורָה (ûrāh, Qal imperative with paragogic heh, directional/intensive) directed to musical instruments (וְּנֵבֶ ל וְּכִ נֹור), followed by the striking temporal direct object: אָ עִ ירָ ה שָ חַ ר (ā‘îrāh šāḥar, Hiphil cohortative of עּור with שַ חַ ר as the accusative of time/object). Rather than the dawn waking the psalmist, the poet syntactically personifies the action: "I will awaken the dawn!" ● Verse 5 (MT v. 6): רּומָ ה עַ ל־שָ מַ יִם אֱֹלהִ ים (rûmāh ‘al-šāmayim ’ĕlōhîm) uses an imperative with paragogic heh, functioning as an urgent doxological petition for God's manifest transcendence to be displayed higher than the physical cosmos (שָ מַ יִם), paralleled by His glory extending over all the earth (וְּעַל כָל־הָ אָ רֶ ץ כְּבֹודֶ ָך). ● Verse 7 (MT v. 8): אֱֹלהִ ים דִ בֶ ר בְּ קָ דְּ שֹו (ĕlōhîm dibber bəqodšô). The verb is a Piel perfect of ד ַב ָד (dābar), indicating a definitive, historical decree issued from His sanctuary/holiness. The following cohortatives אֶ עֱלֹזָה (e‘lōzāh, "I will exult"), אֲחַלְּקָ ה (ăḥalləqāh, "I will divide/apportion"), and אֲמַדֵּד (ămaddēd, Piel imperfect/cohortative, "I will measure out") transition the divine voice into the first person: Yahweh Himself acts as conqueror and sovereign surveyor. ● Verses 11–13 (MT vv. 12–14): Verse 11 opens with the interrogative/relative particle הֲלֹא־אֹל ֲהִ ים זְּנַחְּתָּנּו (hălō’-’ĕlōhîm zənaḥtānû, "Is it not God who rejected us?"). Verse 13 concludes with an energetic prepositional phrase בְּ אֹלהִ ים נַעֲשֶ ה־חָ יִל (bē’lōhîm na‘ăseh-ḥāyil, "Through God we shall do valiantly"), where the preposition בְּ (bə) denotes efficient instrument and covenantal sphere, while the emphatic pronoun וְּהּוא (wə-hû’) introduces the divine subject who alone will trample down (יָבּוס, Qal imperfect of בּוס) Israel's adversaries.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Cosmic Doxology
The document argues that Psalm 108 represents a transition from personal lament to cosmic praise, where the psalmist's steadfast heart (nākôn libbî) anticipates God's deliverance as a certainty, effectively 'waking the dawn' through faith.

### Theme: Divine Territorial Sovereignty
The text posits that God's speech in His sanctuary serves as an immutable, cosmic title deed. By invoking geographical locations like Shechem, Succoth, Gilead, and Edom, the psalm re-claims the land as the Creator's property, subject to His sovereign allocation and judgment.

### Theme: Eschatological Subjugation
The document interprets the subjugation of hostile nations (Moab, Edom, Philistia) as a typological framework for the final ingathering of the cosmos under the rule of God, fulfilled ultimately in the resurrected Christ who treads down all enemies.
