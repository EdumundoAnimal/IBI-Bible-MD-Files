# Isaiah 63:7-64:11: Exposition and Theological Analysis
**Author:** [[Brevard S. Childs]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a detailed exegetical and theological analysis of the biblical text Isaiah 63:7-64:11. It explores the communal complaint structure of the passage, detailing its form, history, and major themes. Key theological motifs examined include the memory of God's saving acts, the role of divine messengers (the 'angel of his presence'), the themes of divine fatherhood and judgment, and the historical context of the post-exilic community. The commentary addresses complex textual issues, including the relationship between divine mercy and the experience of divine abandonment, as well as the interpretation of controversial phrases regarding justice and judgment.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Communal Complaint
The passage is identified as a communal complaint psalm, characterized by the alternation between praise and lament, reflecting a community struggling with divine abandonment.

### Theme: Divine Memory and Intervention
The text emphasizes the importance of remembering historical acts of salvation, such as the Exodus, as a basis for pleading with God in current crises.

### Theme: Fatherhood of God
The theme of God as Father is analyzed in the context of the post-exilic period, noting its theological development and its divergence from ancient Near Eastern deity concepts.

### Theme: Theodicy and Judgment
The commentary explores the tension between acknowledging God's past mercy and the current reality of suffering, grappling with the theological implications of God's role in the community's destruction.
