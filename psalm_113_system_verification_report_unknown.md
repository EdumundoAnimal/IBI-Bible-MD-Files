## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This report provides a comprehensive exegetical, historical, and homiletical analysis of Psalm 113. It examines the Hebrew linguistic structure, including the archaic hireq compaginis, and explores the theological theme of 'Sovereign Transcendence via Redemptive Condescension.' The study maps the intertextual trajectory from the Song of Hannah (1 Sam 2) through the Magnificat (Luke 1) to Pauline theology. It also contrasts the biblical inversion model—where God stoops to lift the marginalized from the 'ash-heap'—with ancient Near Eastern pagan hierarchies and modern secular notions of meritocracy and cosmic indifference.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemptive Condescension
The central theological argument that God’s sovereignty is not defined by distance or aloofness, but by His intentional 'stooping' to engage with human suffering and marginalization.

### Theme: Theological Inversion
The reversal of human hierarchies where the low, poor, and barren are exalted by God, contradicting the meritocratic and power-based values of both ancient and modern societies.

### Theme: Christological Fulfillment
The identification of Christ as the ultimate expression of the psalm’s paradox, where the enthroned King descends into the 'ash-heap' of Golgotha to elevate fallen humanity.
