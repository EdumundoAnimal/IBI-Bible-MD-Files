The LXX translates this with plural substantives: diephtharēsan kai ebdelychthēsan en epitēdeumasin, harmonizing the singular noun with the plural verbs. ● Psalm 14:3 (The Expansive Septuagintal Catena): The most glaring variant in the textual tradition occurs in LXX Psalm 13:3 (Codex Vaticanus, Alexandrinus, Sinaiticus). Following the clause ouk estin poiōn chrēstotēta, ouk estin heōs henos, the Greek text interpolates six additional poetic lines drawn from Psalms 5:9, 140:3, 10:7, Isaiah 59:7–8, and Psalm 36:1:taphos aneōgmenos ho laryngx autōn... ouk estin phobos theou apenanti tōn ophthalmōn autōn. This interpolation is absent in M, the Targum, and the Syriac. It represents a classic secondary scribal accretion: early Christian copyists, recognizing this string of citations from Paul’s catena on universal sin in Romans 3:10–18, back-interpolated the full Pauline string directly into the LXX manuscript of the Psalm. The Masoretic reading is unquestionably superior and original to the Hebrew Psalter. ● Psalm 14:5–6 vs. Psalm 53:5–6 (M Divergence): Psalm 14 is a Yahwistic recension; Psalm 53 is its Elohistic counterpart. The consonants in both versions are extraordinarily close phonetically and graphically (e.g., vs. ), revealing either an intentional theological reworking of a localized crisis (Ps 53 referencing an imperial siege, such as Sennacherib) or a scribal metathesis that diverged into two distinct liturgical traditions during the exilic or post-exilic redaction of the Psalter.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Practical Atheism
The document defines practical atheism not as a theoretical or metaphysical denial of God's existence, but as an operational life philosophy where the individual lives as if God is irrelevant, unseeing, and morally detached from daily life. It is described as a 'delusion of the heart' that manifests in social exploitation.

### Theme: Universal Depravity
Based on Psalm 14:2-3, the text argues that God's divine inspection reveals a universal moral putrefaction. It asserts that sin has infected every faculty of the human person, rendering it impossible for anyone to seek God or produce meritorious goodness by their own power, thus necessitating salvation from outside the system.
