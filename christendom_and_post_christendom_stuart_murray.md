# Christendom and Post-Christendom
**Author:** [[Stuart Murray]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the historical rise of Christendom—a period marked by the integration of church and state, beginning with Constantine—and its current decline in the Western world. It examines how this historical alignment has influenced biblical interpretation, mission, and the church's role in society. The author highlights that we are currently in a transitional 'post-Christendom' era, characterized by a loss of cultural dominance and the need to re-imagine the church's witness in a pluralistic society, moving away from past paradigms of imperial power and legal establishment.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Post-Christendom Transition
The decline of historical Christendom as a dominant cultural and political force in the West, necessitating a re-evaluation of mission and church identity in a pluralistic, de-centered society.

### Theme: Constantinian Shift
The historical integration of church and state initiated by Constantine, which fundamentally altered Christian theology, biblical interpretation, and the church's relationship with secular power.

### Theme: Biblical Interpretation and Mission
The impact of the Christendom mindset on how the Bible is read and how mission is conducted, often privileging imperial or state-aligned frameworks over New Testament communal ethics.
