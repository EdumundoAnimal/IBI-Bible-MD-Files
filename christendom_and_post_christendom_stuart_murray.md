# Christendom and Post-Christendom
**Author:** [[Stuart Murray]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the historical shift from the era of Christendom—a period where Christianity was deeply entwined with European political and social power structures—to a post-Christendom era. Murray analyzes the legacy of this union, the challenges of contemporary secular society, and the necessity for the church to re-imagine its mission in a world where Christian influence is waning. The text discusses the historical, theological, and practical implications of this transition, emphasizing that the church must move away from its former position of power and toward a new identity rooted in its original biblical context.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Shift from Christendom to Post-Christendom
The decline of the historical union between the state and the church in Western society, leading to a landscape where Christianity no longer dictates cultural and political norms.

### Theme: Biblical Re-interpretation
The necessity for Christians to move beyond the legacy of imperial Christendom and re-engage with biblical texts, specifically examining how passages like Romans 13 were historically used to justify state power and should be viewed through a new mission-oriented lens.

### Theme: Post-Christendom Mission Strategy
The call for the church to adapt to a de-centered, pluralistic society by focusing on voluntary membership, distinct Christian identity, and witness rather than institutional dominance.
