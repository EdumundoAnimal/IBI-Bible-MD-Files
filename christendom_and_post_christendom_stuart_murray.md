# Christendom and Post-Christendom
**Author:** [[Stuart Murray]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text examines the historical rise and current fall of the Christendom era, characterized by a close partnership between the church and state. It explores the transition into a post-Christendom society where church influence is waning, state religion is absent, and the church must re-evaluate its mission. The author discusses how biblical interpretation and theology were shaped by the Christendom paradigm, noting that as this era ends, Christians must develop new ways of engaging with culture without the reliance on state authority or assumed social dominance.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christendom Paradigm
A historical era defined by the close alliance between church and state, where biblical interpretation and theology were often used to justify state authority and social control.

### Theme: Post-Christendom Transition
The current period of decline in cultural dominance for the church, requiring a shift in mission, evangelism, and identity away from institutional privilege.

### Theme: Biblical Interpretation
The author argues that standard readings of texts like Romans 13 were influenced by the need to maintain social order and state power during the Christendom era, necessitating a re-reading in a post-Christendom context.
