# The Whole Gospel
**Author:** [[David K. Bernard]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This paper examines Oneness Pentecostal perspectives on Christian initiation, centering on the interpretation of Acts 2:38. The author argues that the full New Testament conversion experience includes repentance, water baptism in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and receiving the Holy Spirit, often evidenced by speaking in tongues. The text traces these theological concepts through early Pentecostal figures like Charles Parham, William Seymour, and William Durham, noting their influence on the development of the United Pentecostal Church International (UPCI) doctrine. The author also draws comparisons between these Oneness views and broader Pentecostal/Charismatic traditions, emphasizing the normative role of the Acts 2:38 pattern in Christian initiation.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Acts 2:38 Paradigm
The central argument that Acts 2:38 represents the comprehensive New Testament standard for conversion, consisting of three distinct but integrated steps: repentance, water baptism in the name of Jesus, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit.

### Theme: Baptismal Formula
The historical and theological debate regarding whether water baptism should be performed in the triune name (Father, Son, and Holy Spirit) or exclusively in the name of Jesus Christ, with the text favoring the latter as the biblical standard.

### Theme: Baptism of the Holy Spirit
The conviction that this experience is a necessary part of salvation, typically accompanied by the initial sign of speaking in tongues, serving as a vital empowerment for the church.

### Theme: Christian Initiation
The process of entering into a full relationship with Christ, which the author argues involves both human obedience (repentance and baptism) and divine action (reception of the Spirit).
