# Handbook to Exegesis of the New Testament
**Author:** [[Stanley E. Porter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text serves as a scholarly introduction to the principles and methodologies of New Testament exegesis, covering critical topics such as structure, rhetoric, and linguistic analysis. The work addresses how various methodologies, including historical-critical analysis, discourse analysis, and rhetorical criticism, inform the interpretation of New Testament books like Hebrews, James, and 2 Peter. It emphasizes the importance of understanding the author's cultural context, the structural dynamics of the text, and the need for exegesis to be aware of the interpreter's own presuppositions. The text concludes by discussing the application of these methods to the New Testament, highlighting how linguistic awareness and the understanding of ancient literary conventions aid in the scholarly study of the canon.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Structural Analysis in Exegesis
The text argues that understanding the structure of a book is crucial for sound exegesis. Divergent interpretations often stem from different assessments of an author's organizational strategy, particularly in epistles.

### Theme: Rhetorical Criticism
Utilizing Greco-Roman rhetorical conventions to understand how New Testament authors argued their points and persuaded their original audiences.

### Theme: Interpreter's Presuppositions
The work acknowledges that every interpreter comes to the text with a set of 'glasses' or preconceptions that must be identified to avoid distorting the biblical message.

### Theme: Linguistic and Semantic Precision
The importance of precise word studies, avoiding the 'fallacies' identified by scholars like James Barr, and understanding language within its specific ancient context.
