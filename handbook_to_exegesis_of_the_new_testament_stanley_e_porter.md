# Handbook to Exegesis of the New Testament
**Author:** [[Stanley E. Porter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document serves as a guide for New Testament exegesis, covering structural assessment, linguistic analysis, and rhetorical criticism. It discusses various methodologies for examining New Testament texts, emphasizing the role of authorship, provenance, and historical context. Specific sections analyze the Catholic Epistles (Hebrews, James, Peter, Jude), focusing on how structural elements and rhetorical strategies inform the theological arguments. The text underscores the importance of understanding the author's intentionality and the impact of the interpreter's own presuppositions on the analytical process.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Structural Assessment in Exegesis
Exegesis requires careful attention to the structure of a book, as decisions made regarding the arrangement of passages influence the interpretation of the text. Failure to properly grasp structural dynamics can lead to misreadings, such as those found in scholarly debates concerning Hebrews and James.

### Theme: The Role of Rhetorical Criticism
Rhetorical criticism is an important tool for interpreting New Testament documents. It involves analyzing stylistic features and comparing them to Greco-Roman rhetorical standards, which aids in understanding how authors structured their messages to persuade or exhort their audiences.

### Theme: Linguistic and Semantic Analysis
Exegesis involves evaluating the specific language and vocabulary used by authors. This includes examining the influence of Septuagintal terminology and avoiding linguistic fallacies that arise from incorrect assumptions about the philosophical background of specific terms.
