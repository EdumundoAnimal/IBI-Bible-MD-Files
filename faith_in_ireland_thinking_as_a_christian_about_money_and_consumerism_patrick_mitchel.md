# Faith in Ireland: Thinking as a Christian about money and consumerism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document outlines a biblical theology of money, possessions, and giving, urging Christians to adopt a counter-cultural approach to modern consumerism. It emphasizes stewardship, rejecting laziness, and supporting the poor as core obligations. The text contrasts biblical values with the 'fallen' nature of consumerist culture, highlighting that money should never be the goal of life. It calls for practical steps such as maintaining a budget, avoiding greed, living with simplicity, and practicing accountability within the church community to serve others rather than the self.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Stewardship and Responsibility
The biblical understanding that God is the owner of all things, making humans stewards tasked with work and responsible use of resources.

### Theme: Counter-Cultural Living
The call for Christians to resist the passive consumption of modern capitalism and instead prioritize needs, generosity, and community accountability.

### Theme: Justice for the Marginalized
The theological stance that God is consistently on the side of the poor and oppressed, demanding that believers act as defenders of the vulnerable.

### Theme: Spiritual Dangers of Wealth
The argument that the pursuit of money breeds discontent, pride, and idolatry, distracting from a life centered on God.
