# Faith in Ireland: Thinking as a Christian about money and consumerism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document outlines a biblical theology of money, possessions, and giving, urging Christians to adopt a counter-cultural stance against the dominant faith of consumerism. It emphasizes that while God provides resources for stewardship and work is a blessing, believers must be wary of fallen human nature regarding wealth. New Testament teachings suggest that money should not be the goal of life, but rather a tool used for the good of others and the purposes of Jesus. The text highlights God's special care for the poor and marginalized, contrasting this with consumerist selfishness. Practical steps for Christians include thinking critically about capitalism, maintaining a budget, avoiding indebtedness, simplifying lifestyles, and serving others through a cross-centered life.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Stewardship
The argument that God is the owner of all things, making humans stewards who are responsible for using possessions according to God's will.

### Theme: Counter-cultural living
The mandate for Christians to resist passive consumption and greed, prioritizing the needs of others and community accountability over individual want.

### Theme: Divine Justice for the Poor
The theological stance that God is consistently on the side of the poor and marginalized, and that righteous living entails actively protecting the vulnerable.
