# Oneness Pentecostals and the Trinity
**Author:** [[Gregory A. Boyd]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly critique of the Oneness Pentecostal theological perspective, specifically regarding its understanding of the Godhead and the Trinity. The author, Gregory A. Boyd, examines the central Oneness argument, which posits that there is only one God and that Jesus Christ is that one God in his entirety. The text analyzes how Oneness advocates argue that the titles 'Father,' 'Son,' and 'Holy Spirit' are merely different modes or manifestations of the one person of Jesus Christ. Boyd evaluates these claims against standard Trinitarian theology, addressing key scriptural proofs often cited by Oneness adherents, such as Isaiah 9:6, John 10:30, and the baptismal formula in Matthew 28:19. He provides counter-arguments to the Oneness contention that the doctrine of the Trinity is polytheistic or unbiblical, emphasizing instead the biblical and historical basis for the Trinity as a mystery of one God in three distinct persons. The document concludes that the Oneness position rests on flawed methods of scriptural interpretation and fails to adequately explain the distinctions within the Godhead revealed in the New Testament.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Oneness Theology
The theological position that denies the eternal Trinity and asserts that Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are merely modes or manifestations of the one person of Jesus Christ.

### Theme: Trinitarian Orthodoxy
The orthodox Christian belief that God is one in essence but exists eternally in three distinct persons: the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit.

### Theme: Exegesis and Hermeneutics
The critique of the 'patchwork' method of exegesis where Oneness proponents link disparate scriptural verses to support their modalistic view, often disregarding the context of the original authors.
