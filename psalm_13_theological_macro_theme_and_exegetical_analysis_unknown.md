The grammatical tension is intentional: neṣaḥ functions adverbially to modify the verb of forgetting, emphasizing the subjective experience of unending abandonment within temporal progression. The lectio difficilior ("counsels" / ʿ ēṣôt) is sustained: the Psalmist describes the exhausting psychological process of endlessly turning over plans and human stratagems within his inner being to escape impending doom, only to encounter cyclical despair. The LXX renders this βουλάς (boulas, "counsels/deliberations"), supporting the MT reading. Psalm 13:6 [MT 13:6cd]: The LXX adds a third poetic colon not present in the primary MT tradition: καὶ ψαλῶ τῷ ὀνόματι κυρίου τοῦ ὑψίστου ("and I will sing praise to the name of the Lord Most High"), which directly mirrors the closing formula of Psalm 7:17 [LXX 7:18] (וַאֲזַמְּ רָ ה שֵ ם־יְּ הוָה עֶּלְּ יֹון). This is a liturgical expansion or scribal assimilation to a common Psalter doxological formula, rightly excluded from critical reconstructions of the pristine proto-MT text.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenant Fidelity (Hesed)
The document defines ḥesed not as an amorphous sentiment, but as asymmetric covenant loyalty and redemptive commitment. It is the unassailable bedrock of Israel's lament theology, guaranteeing that God will not ultimately abandon His covenant partner to Sheol.

### Theme: Divine Hiddenness (Hester Panim)
The 'hiding of the face' is a formal Old Testament theological motif of divine judgment or disciplinary estrangement. When Yahweh hides His face, the cosmos dissolves into chaos, darkness, and vulnerability to lethal forces.

### Theme: The Anatomy of Lament
Psalm 13 is presented as the archetype of the Individual Lament (Klagelied des Einzelnen), featuring a tripartite architecture: protest (lament), plea (petition), and praise (doxology). The transition is not chronological but psychological and theological, anchored in the memory of the covenant.
