# What Christians Believe: The Historical Development
**Author:** [[Alan F. Johnson and Robert E. Webber]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the historical development of Christian theology, focusing on the doctrine of God. It tracks the origins of Trinitarian thought from the primitive church through the medieval period. Key topics include the challenges faced by the early church in balancing the oneness of God with the diversity of His revelation, the formulation of the Trinity against polytheistic influences, and the medieval proofs for the existence of God, particularly those formulated by Thomas Aquinas. The text emphasizes that the church's understanding of God is rooted in both Biblical revelation and the ongoing intellectual engagement with history.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Trinitarian Doctrine
The evolution of the belief in Father, Son, and Holy Spirit as one God, originating from early church doxologies and Biblical study.

### Theme: Theological Development
The historical process through which the church refined its understanding of God, navigating early heresies and incorporating philosophical frameworks like those of Aristotle.

### Theme: Medieval Scholasticism
The shift in focus during the Middle Ages toward using logical and philosophical proofs (such as Aquinas's proof from motion and contingency) to validate the existence of God.
