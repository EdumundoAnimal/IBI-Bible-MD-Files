# Looking at the Cross
**Author:** [[Michael Austin]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Chapter 1: Impressions

Jesus' death is certainly the most known execution in history, but even so, what kind of view of it can we see, so long after the event? A key issue today is how we see meaning in what we view or read, and everyone has their own slant. So, what do you see when you look at Jesus' death? Did Jesus plan to die on a cross - strange thought - or did he lose control of his destiny? Was it a huge injustice? If it was part of his plan how could it be an injustice? Who knows? Questions about the cross simply will not go away. Some have a folk memory of a link between personal guilt and Jesus' death, but in a postmodern world it has faded into a grey winter's fog. Dan Brown's thriller, The Da Vinci Code, retells the life of Jesus with ingenious novelty and with appeals to historical research that have left many mystified as to whether there is still a difference between fact and fantasy. A lot of people think that the genius of modern novel is just that point - that it doesn't matter any more. Violence is in vogue, and film realism showing Jesus' death slams into us and leaves us reeling. An exciting way to see new meaning in Jesus' death? And why this old idea that it might still mean something? I shall try to answer these questions and invite you to look with me. My main source is an early Jewish Christian writing - the New Testament Letter to the Hebrews. A lot of Hebrews is quite deep and often gets put on one side, not the best course when you are searching for clues! You will quickly see that I am not exploring the complex weave of human motives that wanted to see Jesus removed, (although we shall touch on that so much as why we wanted it to happen! And you will see that the reason for my choice is that the trail leads in that direction. You may have been deeply moved by dramatic film productions of Jesus death, so we shall need to see how far emotional impact may, or may not, propel us on our journey. As to the actual scene of Jesus' death, the Bible does provide some graphic detail but it is mostly low key and not the main focus. Hebrews connects Jesus death with universal themes: 'so that by the grace of God he might taste death for everyone' (2:9). 'Dying for others sounds selfless in the extreme, but how on ear could it be for 'everyone'? For those who may see Jesus' death as the ultimate in sorrowful gloom, I hope that is not your final conclusion. You may even see the sun begin to shine. A typical spin-off gives a chance to think about anti-Semitism, and see how the Jewish Christians who first read Hebrews might have reacted to the people who crucified Jesus. I also hope to show that Hebrews speaks with surprising directness to our own uncertain world.

### Chapter 2: What do You See?

A magnificent vastness permeates Hebrews. We feel dwarfed by the sheer scale of the view that is opening up to us: a view that may be reduced to a single, dominating theme: that Christ died for sinners in order to rescue them from utmost peril and bring 'many sons to glory' (2:10). In a word this is salvation, a rich and full deliverance, and to clarify the meaning of Christ's death, we shall need to more closely at the revealed character of God. How is it that God required something so extraordinary to settle some kind of moral issue? And if I cannot see him, are words enough to share the depth of meaning? Does imagination play a part, as I try to construct what happened when Jesus died? What about that spiritual enlightening - without which everything will appear theoretical, and strangely remote from my life? These are large questions, to which we have only begun to give answers. For the moment, Hebrews confirms that, Christ, having been offered once to bear the sins of many, will appear a second time..." (9:28). Here again, the thought of his death making a vast, effective action in relation to 'sins' is uppermost. We outlined earlier what we meant by a clearer focus on Jesus' death. Now, even if the connection is not yet obvious, we ought to grasp something of the sheer majesty and moral blaze of God's person. It will illuminate Jesus' death. But we are not mere spectators, and besides, a view of him is not available to mortal eyes.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Salvation
The document identifies the central theme of Hebrews as Christ dying for sinners to rescue them from peril and bring them to glory, defining this as a rich and full deliverance.

### Theme: The Character of God
The author argues that understanding the meaning of Christ's death requires a deeper look at the revealed character of God, specifically His majesty and moral blaze.
