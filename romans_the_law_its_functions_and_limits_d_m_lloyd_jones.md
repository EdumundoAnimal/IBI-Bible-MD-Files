# Romans: The Law, Its Functions and Limits
**Author:** [[D. M. Lloyd-Jones]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document consists of a series of expository chapters focusing on Romans 7. Dr. Martyn Lloyd-Jones analyzes the Apostle Paul's argument regarding the role of the Law in the life of the Christian. He explores the concept of the Law being 'holy, just, and good' while simultaneously serving to reveal sin and human depravity. The author systematically breaks down the verses, explaining the meaning of the Apostle's 'I' in the text, the nature of sin as a deceptive force, and the ultimate need for Christ's righteousness. Themes include the spiritual nature of the Law, the inability of the Law to justify or sanctify, and the necessity of union with Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Spiritual Nature of the Law
The author argues that the Law is not merely a set of rules for outward behavior but is fundamentally spiritual, targeting the inner desires and thoughts of a person.

### Theme: The Deception of Sin
Sin is portrayed as an active, deceptive force that uses the Law as a 'fulcrum' to trigger sinful desires and convict the conscience.

### Theme: Total Human Helplessness
A core argument is that the Law strips away human self-confidence and self-righteousness, leaving the individual utterly helpless and dependent on Christ.

### Theme: The Apostle's 'I'
Lloyd-Jones examines the debate over whether Paul describes his past life as a Pharisee or his present state as a Christian, ultimately viewing it as an analysis of the human condition under the Law.
