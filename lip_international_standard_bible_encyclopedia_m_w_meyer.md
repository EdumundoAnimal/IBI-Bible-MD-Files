# LIP (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia)
**Author:** [[M. W. Meyer]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
LIP [Hebr. sapah, sepatayim]; NEB also MOUTH, WORDS, SPEECH, TONGUE, TALK. In general, Hebraic thought regarded lips not only as physical organs but also as organs of speech. As physical organs, the lips may indeed be beautiful, "like a scarlet thread" (Song 4:3), and may be touched, as in Isaiah's vision (Isa 6:7). Like the MOUTH and TONGUE in Hebraic thought, however, the lips are primarily associated with speech; as organs of speech they frequently seem to act independently. The lips may proclaim God's praises (Ps 119:171), shout for joy (71:23), and spread knowledge (Prov 15:7); or they may utter sinful words (Ps 59:12 [MT 13]), speak insolently and dishonestly (31:18 [MT 19]), and talk of mischief (Prov 24:2). The lips may function independently of the heart, in a hypocritical manner (Isa 29:13; Matt 15:8; Mark 7:6). But ultimately the heart and mind, the central and inner life of a person, direct the utterances of the lips: "The mind [AV "heart"; Hebr. leb] of the wise makes his speech judicious, and adds persuasiveness to his lips" (Prov 16:23; also cf. 4:23; Rom 10:8-10). The term occurs in certain Hebrew idioms. Moses' claim to be "a man of uncircumcised lips" (Ex 6:12,30) meant that he was slow of speech, "a halting speaker" (NEB). In the Hebrew idioms "truthful lips" (Prov 12:19), "righteous lips" (16:13), etc., "lips" by synecdoche may designate a person uttering a speech; thus the NEB has "truth spoken" in Prov 12:19 and "honest speech" in 16:13. "Lip" (Hebr. saphah; Gk. cheilos) may also mean language (Isa 28:11; 1 Cor 14:21; the RSV translates "language" in Gen 11:1; Isa 19:18). Three Hebrew terms require further explanation. Saphah refers to the upper lip, moustache, or beard; according to proper Hebrew practice, a person covered his saphah with his hand or his saphah and thus not to follow the rites for mourning when sinful Israel was to be decimated (Ezek 24:17,22). Micah spoke of false prophets who covered their saphah in shame and confusion (Mic 3:7). Mufqa, or mufeqa, refers to one of the lips (or protuberances, spouts, or pipes) for oil lamps (Zech 4:2); perhaps the wicks rested by these lips, or perhaps oil was poured through a mufqa.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hebraic Anthropology of Speech
Lips are viewed not merely as anatomical features but as instrumental organs of speech that act in connection with or independent of the heart and mind.

### Theme: Synecdoche and Idiom
In biblical literature, 'lips' often serves as a synecdoche to represent the entirety of an individual's speech, truthfulness, or even specific languages.

### Theme: Moral Dichotomy
The text highlights the potential for lips to be used for both divine praise and sinful, hypocritical speech, emphasizing the role of the 'heart' or 'mind' in directing moral outcomes.
