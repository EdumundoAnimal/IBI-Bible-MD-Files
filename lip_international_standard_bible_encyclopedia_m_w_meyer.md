# LIP (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia)
**Author:** [[M. W. Meyer]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
LIP [Hebr. sāpâ, śep̄atayim]; NEB also MOUTH, WORDS, SPEECH, TONGUE, TALK, (cattle from our) pens (Hos 14:2 [MT 3]; see comms.), etc.; [śāpām] (Ezek 24:17,22; NEB UP LIP, MOUTHS; [hek] (Hos 8:1); AV MOUTH; [peh] (Ps 50:16; Prov 6:2; Jer 7:28; Ezek 29:21; 33:31); AV MOUTH; NEB also various paraphrases; [mūsāqâ] (Zech 4:2); AV, NEB, PIPES; [Gk. cheilos] (Matt 15:8; Mark 7:6; Rom 3:13; 1 Cor 14:21; Heb 13:15; 1 Peter 3:10); [stóma - mouth] (Luke 22:71; Rom 10:8-10; 1 Peter 2:22); AV MOUTH; NEB also 'retort'; UPPER LIP [Hebr. śāpām] (Lev 13:45). In general, Hebraic thought regarded lips not only as physical organs but also as organs of speech. As physical organs, the lips may indeed be beautiful, "like a scarlet thread" (Song 4:3), and may be touched, as in Isaiah's vision (Isa 6:7). Like the MOUTH and TONGUE in Hebraic thought, however, the lips are primarily associated with speech; as organs of speech they frequently seem to act independently. The lips may proclaim God's praises (Ps 119:171), shout for joy (71:23), and spread knowledge (Prov 15:7); or they may utter sinful words (Ps 59:12 [MT 13]), speak insolently and dishonestly (31:18 [MT 19]), and talk of mischief (Prov 24:2). The lips may function independently of the heart, in a hypocritical manner (Isa 29:13; Matt 15:8 par. Mark 7:6). But ultimately the heart and mind, the central and inner life of a person, direct the utterances of the lips: "The mind [AV "heart"; Hebr. lēb] of the wise makes his speech judicious, and adds persuasiveness to his lips" (Prov 16:23; also cf. 4:23; Rom 10:8-10). The term occurs in certain Hebrew idioms. Moses' claim to be "a man of uncircumcised lips" (Ex 6:12,30) meant that he was slow of speech, "a halting speaker" (NEB). In the Hebrew idioms "truthful lips" (Prov 12:19), "righteous lips" (16:13), etc., "lips" by synecdoche may designate a person uttering a speech; thus the NEB has "truth spoken" in Prov 12:19 and "honest speech" in 16:13. "Lip" (Hebr. śāpâ; Gk. cheilos) may also mean language (Isa 28:11; 1 Cor 14:21; the RSV translates "a language" in Gen 11:1; Isa 19:18). Three Hebrew terms require further explanation. S'pām refers to the upper lip, moustache, or beard; according to proper Hebrew practice, a person covered his śāpām with his hand or his śāpām and thus not to follow the rites for mourning when sinful Israel was to be decimated (Ezek 24:17,22). Micah spoke of false prophets who covered their śāpām in shame and confusion (Mic 3:7). mūsāqâ refers to the palate, gums, or parts of the mouth (in Hos 8:1) in contact with the trumpet. mûf'qâ, or mûfegeh, refers to one of the lips (or protuberances, spouts, or pipes) for oil lamps (Zech 4:2); perhaps the wicks rested by these lips, or perhaps oil was poured through a mûf'qâ. See LAMP I.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Anthropological view of Speech
The document explores the dual nature of lips in Hebraic thought: as physical organs of beauty and as functional instruments of communication that can act independently of the heart, leading to hypocrisy or truth.

### Theme: Biblical Semantics and Idioms
Analysis of how the Hebrew term for lip (śāpâ) functions as a synecdoche for speech, language, or the speaker, including specific idioms like 'uncircumcised lips' to signify speech difficulty.
