# Biblical Personalism
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text explores the theological framework of 'Biblical Personalism' in contrast to classical theism and mystical traditions. It examines the nature of prayer, divine revelation, the concept of agape, and the theological necessity of salvation by grace. The author emphasizes that biblical faith is inherently personal, rooted in an encounter with a God who is both transcendent and immanent, rather than an abstract philosophical principle. Key themes include the struggle of faith, the rejection of self-love, the centrality of Christ in revelation, and the sovereignty of God in human history.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Prayer
Defined as a 'struggle-making' act of submission to God, avoiding both quietism and self-centered manipulation of the divine, aimed at aligning human will with God's will.

### Theme: Agape vs. Cupitas
The text contrasts 'agape'—a self-giving, divine love—with 'cupitas' or self-love, arguing that true faith requires the transcendence of the self.

### Theme: Salvation by Grace
Reflects a Reformation-era understanding that salvation is a gift of God (sola gratia), not an achievement of human effort, while acknowledging the role of faith as the response to that grace.

### Theme: The Scandal of Particularity
The theological tension between the universal God and the necessity of divine revelation through specific historical events and figures, notably Jesus Christ.
