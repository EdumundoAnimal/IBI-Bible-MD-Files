Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants ● Psalm Division (MT vs. LXX/Vulgate): In the Septuagint (LXX Psalm 113:9–26) and Vulgate (Psalm 113:9–26), Psalms 114 and 115 are joined into a single literary unit beginning with In exitu Israel de Aegypto and transitioning at verse 9 (Non nobis, Domine, non nobis). The Masoretic Text (MT), the Targum, and the Syriac Peshitta preserve Psalms 114 and 115 as distinct compositions. The MT division is supported by the distinct metrical, syntactic, and structural shifts: Psalm 114 is an objective historical-theophanic hymn in heptastichs, whereas Psalm 115 is a communal liturgical liturgy alternating between congregational confession, priestly blessing, and anti-idol polemic. ● Dead Sea Scrolls Evidence: 4QPsb (4Q84) and 4QPse (4Q87) confirm the distinct structural identity of Psalm 115 within the Late Second Temple Psalter, exhibiting orthographic stability with standard MT consonantal forms. ● Verse 14 Textual Divergence: In MT, yōsēp YHWH ‘ălêḵem (יֹסֵ ף יְהוָה עֲלֵיכֶם) preserves the jussive form of yāsap (ף ַסָי), translated as "May Yahweh add/increase." Certain Greek recensions read this as a future indicative (prosthei Kyrios), blunting the liturgical benedictory force of the priestly pronouncement. The MT reading preserves the performative, blessing-oriented syntax of the temple liturgy.

### Grammatical Mechanics

● Verse 1: Lō’ lānû, YHWH, lō’ lānû, kî-ləšimkā tēn kāḇôd (לֹא לָנּו יְהוָה לֹא לָנּו כִּי־לְ שִּ מְ ָך תֵ ן ודֹבָכ). The negative particle lō’ with the prepositional lamed and 1st person common plural suffix (lānû) is repeated in immediate succession (epizeuxis), functioning as a categorical disclaimer of human self-glory. The disjunctive particle kî functions causally/adversatively ("but rather"), governing the Qal imperative tēn ("give!"), making the vindication of the divine name the exclusive imperative of the petition. ● Verse 3: Wē’lōhênû ḇaššāmāyim kōl ’ăšer-ḥāpēṣ ‘āśāh (וֵאֹלהֵ ינּו בַ שָ מָ יִּם כֹל אֲשֶ ר־חָ פֵץ עָשָ ה). The adversative waw (wē-) fronts the subject ’ĕlōhênû, establishing an antithetical contrast against the earthbound idols of the nations. The relative clause kōl ’ăšer-ḥāpēṣ acts as the direct object of the Qal perfect 3rd masculine singular ‘āśāh, expressing uninhibited, sovereign execution of divine will. ● Verse 8: Kəmôhem yihyû ‘ōśêhem kōl ’ăšer bōṭēaḥ bāhem (כְמֹוהֶ ם יִּהְ יּו עֹשֵ יהֶ ם כֹל אֲשֶ ר בֹטֵ חַ ם ֶה ָב). The preposition of comparison kə- prefixed to the 3rd masculine plural pronoun môhem opens the apodosis. The Qal imperfect yihyû operates not merely as a descriptive future ("they will be"), but as an inexorable ontological law—a talionic consequence where the worshiper is conformed to the state of the worshiped entity.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Law of Ontological Assimilation
Psalm 115:8 establishes that worship is transformational. Human beings are hardwired to reflect whatever holds their ultimate allegiance. To worship technocratic efficiency is to become cold and computational; to worship market wealth is to become transactional and insecure; to worship individual autonomy is to become isolated and cut off from deep community.

### Theme: The Polemic Against Idolatry
The psalm deconstructs idolatry by reducing it to its material composition and origin. It asserts that idols are merely 'the work of human hands' and that without biological or divine vitality, these artifacts cannot execute any divine function. The idol is an elaborate mask over complete emptiness.
