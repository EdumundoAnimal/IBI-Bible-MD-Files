# Synoptic Christologies
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Objectives of this class

1. To explore the different portraits of Jesus in the Synoptic Gospels, with special focus on Mark

### Introduction

The 3 synoptic gospels. Common events; focused on the same person; similar sources and often word for word the same - so we cannot push unique Christologies too far. 'Mark presents 'a very striking portrait of Jesus as Christ, as Son of God and as Son of Man - the man destined to die, the beloved Son destined to save and be raised from the grave' Ben Witherington, The Gospel of Mark: A Socio-Rhetorical Commentary. Eerdmanns, 2001. p.49. Morna Hooker summarises 4 common themes in all the gospels concerning the person of Jesus (and in Paul as well): i. The gospel had its origins long ago in the purposes of God (Mk 1:1-3; Matt 1:1-17; Lk 1:68-79; Jn 1:1) ii. Jesus is the Messiah (Mk 1:1; Matt 1:18; Lk 1:69; Jn 1:17) iii. Jesus is the Son of God (Mk 1:11; Matt 2:15; Lk 1:35; Jn 1:18) iv. Jesus is anointed and empowered by the Holy Spirit (Mk 1:8; Matt 1:20; Lk 1:35; Jn 1:15). However each gospel has a distinct flavour. As each gospel sets out to explain who Jesus is they do not offer a systematic theology - they tell a narrative of which Jesus is the central figure. All the gospels are packed full of Christology - communicating who Jesus is both in actions, words, images and titles.

### The Prologue of Mark :1:1-1:13

i. 1:1 'The Gospel of Jesus Christ'. The gospel of Jesus (his teaching especially the kingdom of God) and the gospel about Jesus. It is interesting that much of Mark's narrative focuses on questions - WHO is Jesus?

### Jesus and the Kingdom of God

The dramatic start of Jesus' ministry in Mark 1:14-15. 'The time has come! The Kingdom of God is at hand. Repent and believe the good news.' In him is the kingdom wrapped up. He has the authority to inaugurate the Kingdom of God. Entry into the kingdom is consistently tied to a person's response to Jesus himself. Notice this great phrase: - the gospel is of God and belongs to God. It's dramatic because the 'the time has come'. Everything in the long history of Israel has been pointing to this moment. In Jesus, the hopes of Israel and the world are unfolding.

### SCOT McKNIGHT

An upcoming new book The King Jesus Gospel, Zondervan, 2011. 'Did Jesus Preach the Gospel?'. Scot argues that the 4 Gospels are 'gospel' - they tell the story of Israel in the light of Jesus. And this is the gospel they tell: 1. The gospels preach the death and resurrection of Jesus. 2. The gospels preach Jesus' life, death and resurrection 'according to the scriptures'. Both John the Baptist and Jesus present themselves as being written into the fabric of Scripture. The gospels are completely taken up with how Jesus fulfils the story of Israel 'according to the Scriptures'. He is Israel's Messiah. Jesus' message is Self-Centered: he is the fulfillment of Israel's Scriptures. He is Israel's king forming a restored community around himself: - he is the fulfillment of wisdom - of the prophets - of the temple - of a new covenant - Israel's messiah - he fulfils Torah itself - and we can't really imagine how fantastically shocking and impudent such a claim could be in 1st Century Judaism. Jesus' preaching is self-centered!! 3. The gospels preach 'Jesus died for our sins'. 4. The gospel is to declare the story of Jesus which completes the story of Israel. 5. The gospel of Jesus is bound up with the kingdom of God. - Jesus believed, against all odds, that he kingdom of God was actually breaking into history right now. - Jesus declares that there is a new society in the Land. - Jesus declares a new citizenship - Jesus declares that he is the king; the one in whom all this is happening through and in. o He said 'believe in me and you will enter the kingdom of God' o 12 disciples. o His miracles attest to the fact that he is the one o In Luke 4 this is dramatically explicit. This is the gospel that Jesus preaches. The response called for is to believe in him and enter his kingdom

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Narrative Nature of the Gospels
The document argues that the Gospels do not present a systematic theology but rather a narrative where Jesus is the central figure, communicating his identity through actions, words, images, and titles.

### Theme: Jesus as the Fulfillment of Israel's Story
Drawing on Scot McKnight, the text posits that the Gospels are 'gospel' because they tell the story of Israel in the light of Jesus, who fulfills the Scriptures, the law, the prophets, and the temple, effectively completing Israel's history.

### Theme: Self-Centered Preaching
The text highlights that Jesus' message is self-centered, as he presents himself as the king and the one in whom the Kingdom of God is inaugurated, requiring a personal response of belief in him.
