### 1. Linguistic & Syntax Report

Textual Variants & Masoretic Apparatus (BHS, 11QPsᵃ, LXX). Verse 9b–10a (MT vv. 9b–10a): Masoretic Text reads יָרּומּו סֶ לָה׃ רֹאׁש מְ סִ בָ י (yārūmū selāh; rōʾš mǝsibbāy). Textual Problem: The division of the stich between verse 9 and verse 10 presents syntactical ambiguity. The MT places Selah after the verb יָרּומּו (Qal imperfect 3mp from רוּם, "let them not be exalted"). The subsequent noun construct phrase רֹאׁש מְ סִ בָ י (rōʾš mǝsibbāy, "the head of those surrounding me") stands syntactically dislocated as a nominative absolute (casus pendens). Apparatus: LXX reads μὴ ὑψωθῶσιν. διάψαλμα. ἡ κεφαλὴ τοῦ κυκλώματος αὐτῶν (mē hypsōthōsin. Diapsalma. Hē kephalē tou kyklōmatos autōn), validating the MT division. Symmachus and the Syriac Peshitta connect yārūmū with rōʾš: "The head of those who surround me lifts itself up." Critical Evaluation: The MT casus pendens is the harder reading (lectio difficilior). Retaining rōʾš mǝsibbāy as the subject of the compound clause in verse 10 governed by the optative/jussive יְכַסֵּמֹו (yǝḵassēmô) preserves the parallel structure of retributive talionic justice without emendation.

### Grammatical Mechanics & Syntax Breakdown

Verse 2 (MT v. 2): {חַ לְ צֵּנִי יְהוָה מֵּ אָ דָ ם רָ ע מֵּ אִ יׁש חֲמָ סִ ים תִ נְצְ רֵּ נִי}text. Syntax: Direct imperative חַ לְ צֵּנִי (ḥallǝṣēnî, Piel imperative 2ms with 1cs suffix from חָלַץ, "rescue/extricate me forcefully") parallel with the imperfect/jussive תִ נְצְ רֵּ נִי (tinṣǝrēnî, Qal imperfect 2ms + 1cs suffix from נָצַר, "guard/preserve me"). Grammatical Nuance: The shift from the Piel imperative (demanding an immediate, aggressive extrication) to the continuous imperfect highlights both an immediate emergency and a continuous, sustained divine preservation against the collective singular אָדָם רָע (ʾādām rāʿ, generic "evil man") broken down into the intensive plural of attribute אִישׁ חֲמָסִים (ʾîš ḥămāsîm, "man of violences/cruelties"). Verse 4 (MT v. 4): {שָׁנֲנּו לְ שֹׁונָם כְמֹו־נָחָשׁ חֲמַ ת עַכְשּׁוב תַ חַ ת שְ פָתֵּ ימֹו סֶ לָה}text. Syntax: שָׁנֲנּו (šānǝnū, Qal perfect 3cp from שָׁנַן, "they sharpen/whet"). Used elsewhere strictly for swords and arrows (Deut 32:41; Ps 64:4). The syntax constructs an ontological weaponization of speech: the tongue is not merely deceptive; it is a forged, sharpened blade bearing biological neurotoxins (ḥămat ʿakšūḇ, construct noun phrase: "the venom of an adder"). Verse 8 (MT v. 8): {יְהוִה אֲדֹנָי עֹז יְשּׁועָ תִ י סַ כֹתָ ה לְ רֹאשִׁ י בְ יֹום נָשֶׁ ק}text. Syntax: Vocative double divine name יְהוִה אֲדֹנָי (YHWH ʾAdōnāy, pointing of YHWH as ʾĔlōhîm to prevent tautology with ʾAdōnāy). Construct chain עֹז יְשּׁועָ תִ י (ʿōz yǝšūʿātî, "the stronghold/might of my salvation"). Temporal Locative: בְ יֹום נָשֶׁ ק (bǝyôm nāšeq, "in the day of clashing arms/weapons"). The perfect tense סַ כֹתָ ה (sakkōṯāh) serves as a constative/confessional perfect: "You have covered/screened my head," functioning as the structural hinge of absolute trust amidst lethal assault.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Retributive Providence
The document argues that Psalm 140 presents a theology where evil is self-consuming. The imprecations are not personal vendettas but an appeal for lex talionis, where the destructive energy released by the slanderer curves back upon its author.

### Theme: The Weaponized Tongue
The text identifies human speech disconnected from God as inherently predatory and serpentine. It uses the imagery of the adder's venom and sharpened swords to describe how slander functions as a tool for character assassination and social destruction.

### Theme: Covenantal Vindication
The Psalm serves as a legal appeal to Yahweh, the supreme magistrate. The righteous, who are afflicted and needy, find their ultimate security not in their own defenses, but in the sovereign protection of God, who acts as their personal public defender.
