# Figures of Speech in the Bible
**Author:** [[E. W. Bullinger, D.D.]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an introductory examination and specific examples of biblical figures of speech. It posits that language is governed by law and that understanding these figures is essential to interpreting the divine revelation of the Bible correctly. The author categorizes these linguistic devices, noting that while ancient scholars identified over two hundred forms, the knowledge of these figures has largely faded or been misinterpreted. The text specifically explores 'Synecdoche (of the part),' providing numerous biblical citations where specific body parts (such as the face, eye, mouth, or belly) are used to represent the whole person or their complete experience, status, or actions.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Linguistic Legalism in Scripture
The argument that biblical language operates under specific, fixed laws and forms that must be understood to grasp the intended meaning of the divine text.

### Theme: Synecdoche of the Part
A specific rhetorical device analyzed in the text where a part of a human or object is used to signify the entire entity or a comprehensive concept, particularly regarding physical features like the face or mouth.

### Theme: Historical Linguistic Decay
The observation that classical knowledge regarding the technical identification and analysis of rhetorical figures has declined since the Middle Ages, leading to misinterpretations.
