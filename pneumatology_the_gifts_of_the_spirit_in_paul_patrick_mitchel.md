# Pneumatology: The Gifts of the Spirit in Paul
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the nature, relevance, and interpretation of spiritual gifts within the Pauline epistles. It presents various lists of gifts found in 1 Corinthians 12, Romans 12, and Ephesians 4. The text highlights a theological debate regarding the definition of 'charismata'. James Dunn is cited for his view that charismata are visible, supernatural workings of God for the common good. Conversely, Max Turner and Gordon Fee argue that the term does not necessarily mean 'supernatural spiritual gifts' but refers broadly to various gifts granted by God, including 'ordinary' services. The context of the Corinthian church—marked by pride and an emphasis on those identifying as 'spiritual' (pneumatikoi)—is central to understanding Paul's rhetorical intent to broaden the definition of the Spirit's work beyond just miraculous displays.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Charismata Definition
A theological disagreement between those who define charismata strictly as immediate, supernatural expressions of the Spirit and those who define them as any gracious gift or service given by God.

### Theme: Corinthian Context
Paul's rhetoric in 1 Corinthians 12-14 is analyzed as a corrective to the Corinthian church's self-centeredness and elitist fixation on being 'spiritual' (pneumatikoi).

### Theme: Broadening the Locus of the Spirit
The argument that the 'work of the Spirit' is not limited to the miraculous, but extends to include ordinary acts of service and administration within the church community.
