# Pneumatology: The Gifts of the Spirit in Paul
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an overview of spiritual gifts as discussed by the Apostle Paul, contrasting different scholarly perspectives such as those of James Dunn and Max Turner. It examines lists of gifts found in 1 Corinthians 12, Romans 12, and Ephesians 4, arguing that the term charismata does not strictly mean 'spiritual gifts' in a narrow sense, but encompasses various services and manifestations of God's grace for the common good. The text emphasizes that Paul's discourse seeks to broaden the understanding of the Spirit's work beyond exclusively miraculous phenomena to include ordinary services.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Definition of Charismata
Scholarly debate regarding whether charismata refers exclusively to 'supernatural' spiritual gifts or broader 'gracious gifts' and services performed within the church.

### Theme: Broadening the Locus of the Spirit
The argument, supported by Max Turner, that Paul deliberately broadens the definition of the Spirit's work to include both extraordinary manifestations and ordinary services for the common good.

### Theme: Cessationist vs. Charismatic Perspectives
The document briefly acknowledges a spectrum of views ranging from the 'supernatural' emphasis of some to the 'natural abilities' or service-oriented perspectives of others.
