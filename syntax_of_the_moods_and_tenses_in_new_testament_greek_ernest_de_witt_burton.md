# Syntax of the Moods and Tenses in New Testament Greek
**Author:** [[Ernest De Witt Burton]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document serves as an introductory textbook on the syntax of New Testament Greek, specifically focusing on the relationship between verb forms, moods, and tenses. It distinguishes between historical grammar, which studies the origin and development of forms, and exegetical grammar, which prioritizes the interpretation of the text as intended by the author for the reader. The author emphasizes that while linguistic forms often evolve to take on new functions, the interpreter must focus on the specific usage within the New Testament context to ensure accurate translation and theological understanding. The text introduces the four main moods (Indicative, Subjunctive, Optative, Imperative) and the various tenses, classifying them into primary (present, future) and secondary (past) categories to aid in exegesis.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Exegetical Grammar
The analytical approach to interpreting New Testament Greek that prioritizes the function of verb forms as used by the author, rather than focusing solely on the historical or philological origins of those forms.

### Theme: Classification of Tenses and Moods
A systematic breakdown of Greek verb structures, categorizing them into primary and secondary tenses and four primary moods (Indicative, Subjunctive, Optative, Imperative) to facilitate accurate translation and theological exegesis.
