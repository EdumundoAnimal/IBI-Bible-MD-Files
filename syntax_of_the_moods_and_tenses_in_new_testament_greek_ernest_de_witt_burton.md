# Syntax of the Moods and Tenses in New Testament Greek
**Author:** [[Ernest De Witt Burton]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The work serves as an authoritative guide for New Testament Greek students, focusing on the interpretation of verb forms rather than historical philology. It covers the syntax of moods and tenses, emphasizing the need for an interpreter to understand how grammatical functions express thought. The text categorizes verb tenses into Primary (Present, Future, Perfect) and Secondary (Imperfect, Aorist, Pluperfect) groups, while detailing the four moods: Indicative, Subjunctive, Optative, and Imperative. It includes scholarly references and detailed explanations of the functions of the Aorist, Present, and dependent moods.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Exegetical Grammar
The author argues that grammar is an essential tool for the interpreter, emphasizing that the focus should be on the functional meaning of the language rather than purely historical philology.

### Theme: Verb Form and Function
The text posits that multiple forms may share the same function and that the interpreter must move beyond identifying forms to understanding the thought the author intended to convey.

### Theme: Classification of Tenses
Tenses are divided into Primary (Present, Future, Perfect) and Secondary (Imperfect, Aorist, Pluperfect), serving as a foundation for understanding New Testament Greek syntax.
