# Syntax of the Moods and Tenses in New Testament Greek
**Author:** [[Ernest De Witt Burton]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text serves as a scholarly introduction to the syntax of New Testament Greek, focusing specifically on the functions of moods and tenses. It outlines the necessity of interpreting Greek through an understanding of historical grammar rather than just philology or direct translation. The author argues that students must move beyond arbitrary names of verbal forms to understand their actual usage and development. The work establishes a distinction between historical grammar, which tracks the origin and development of forms, and exegetical grammar, which concerns itself with the function and meaning of those forms in specific contexts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Historical vs. Exegetical Grammar
The text delineates between historical grammar, which studies the origin and evolutionary development of linguistic forms, and exegetical grammar, which prioritizes the functional meaning of these forms in the interpretation of the New Testament.

### Theme: Function of Verbal Forms
The author posits that verb forms often evolve to carry meanings distinct from their original functions and that students must understand the 'actual usage' rather than relying on traditional labels or literal translations.

### Theme: Classification of Tenses and Moods
The book categorizes Greek verb tenses into primary (present/future) and secondary (past) groups, while also defining the four moods: Indicative, Subjunctive, Optative, and Imperative.
