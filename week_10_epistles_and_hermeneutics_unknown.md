**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Week 10 focuses on the study of the Epistles, building upon previous lessons on general and special hermeneutics. The material defines epistles as letters that are 'occasional' documents, meaning they address specific circumstances. The methodology for studying epistles involves examining the literary, canonical, and theological contexts. An example is provided using 1 Corinthians 11:17-34, analyzing its historical context (Corinth as a large, cosmopolitan center) and theological implications. The document emphasizes that communion is communal rather than purely personal, warning against abusing the Lord's Supper by failing to recognize others. Additional notes cover the interpretation of parables, specifically Luke 10:25-37 regarding the Good Samaritan, and various biblical references related to the Holy Spirit and historical religious sects.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Epistolary Methodology
A structured approach to interpreting letters, requiring the reader to consider the literary context (reading as a whole), theological/canonical context (identifying central themes), and practical application.

### Theme: Communal Interpretation of Scripture
The argument that passages like 1 Corinthians 11:17-34, often read as individualistic instructions, are actually addressed to the communal health of the church and how members interact with one another.

### Theme: Occasional Documents
The understanding that biblical epistles were written to address specific, local situations, which necessitates identifying the original historical context to properly apply the text.
