# Transfiguration: The New Exodus in the Books of Luke
**Author:** [[Jindřich Mánek]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
In the narrative of the Transfiguration on the mountain, contained in all the synoptics, there appear the figures of Moses and Elias speaking with Jesus. This conversation of the two important servants of God with the Lord Jesus Christ is possible because, in the faith of the people of the Old Testament, Moses and Elias passed from death to life. The author posits that Moses and Elias are precursors of the Messiah, their appearance representing a turning point towards the final time. The document explores the typology of the Exodus in relation to Jesus' suffering and death, identifying the crossing of the Red Sea and the crossing of the Jordan as foundational parallels to the baptism and passion of Jesus. The author argues that Luke frames Jesus' ascent to the mountain as a departure from the earthly Jerusalem, analogous to the Israelites' exodus from Egypt to the Promised Land. The concept of submersion, cited in the context of the cup and baptism, is used to bridge the Old Testament Exodus with the 'New Exodus' of the New Testament.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Typology of the Exodus
The author draws parallels between the historical Exodus of the Israelites under Moses and the theological 'New Exodus' of Jesus, specifically linking the crossing of the Red Sea and the Jordan to Jesus' baptism and passion.

### Theme: Eschatological Significance of the Transfiguration
Moses and Elias serve as eschatological figures whose presence confirms Jesus' messianic mission and signifies a critical turning point in the timeline of the final days.

### Theme: The Baptism as Submersion
The baptism of Jesus is interpreted not merely as a ritual, but as an anticipatory act of 'submersion' into the suffering and death that characterizes the climax of his mission.
