# A Textual Commentary on the Greek New Testament
**Author:** [[Bruce M. Metzger]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This volume provides a scholarly analysis of textual variations in the Greek New Testament. It establishes criteria for evaluating conflicting manuscript readings, dividing them into external evidence (date and character of witnesses) and internal evidence (transcriptional and intrinsic probabilities). The text explains that textual criticism acts as a tool to determine the most accurate original wording amidst thousands of scribal alterations, categorizing witnesses into textual families such as Alexandrian, Western, and Byzantine. The second portion of the document illustrates these methods with specific examples from the Gospels of Matthew and John, addressing issues like variant names and grammatical structures found in the manuscripts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: External Evidence
Evaluation of witnesses based on the date, geographic distribution, and the genealogical relationship of manuscripts to establish text families.

### Theme: Internal Evidence
Probability-based assessment comprising Transcriptional Probabilities (scribal habits) and Intrinsic Probabilities (what the author was more likely to have written).

### Theme: Textual Families
Classification of manuscripts into groups such as Alexandrian, Western, and Byzantine to organize textual analysis.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "The chief categories or kinds of criteria and considerations that assist one in evaluating the relative worth of variant readings are those which involve (I) External Evidence, having to do with the manuscripts themselves, and (II) Internal Evidence, having to do with two kinds of considerations, (A) those concerned with Transcriptional Probabilities and (B) those concerned with Intrinsic Probabilities."
