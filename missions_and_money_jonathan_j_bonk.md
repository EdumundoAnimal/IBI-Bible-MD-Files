# Missions and Money
**Author:** [[Jonathan J. Bonk]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document examines the relationship between Western missionary affluence and the theological/ethical implications of this wealth within the context of Christian mission. It reviews both Old and New Testament teachings on poverty, stewardship, and personal security, arguing that the modern Western missionary often exhibits a degree of affluence that contradicts the fundamental tenets of their own religious tradition. The text specifically highlights how traditional mission efforts are often compromised by the disparity in wealth between the missionary and the local populations they serve, urging a reconsideration of lifestyle and material usage in light of biblical mandates for justice, humility, and sacrificial living.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Missionary Affluence
The author argues that Western missionaries frequently possess personal wealth levels that create a barrier or conflict with the gospel message, as they live in a manner largely inconsistent with the realities of those they aim to serve.

### Theme: Biblical Mandates on Wealth
Both the Old and New Testaments are used to establish that wealth is not inherently evil, but the attachment to it, the security derived from it, and the failure to use it for justice and the care of the poor are viewed as spiritual failures and threats to faith.

### Theme: Economic Justice
The text emphasizes the biblical requirement to protect the vulnerable, pay laborers fairly, and avoid the exploitation of the poor, framing these as core religious duties rather than optional charity.
