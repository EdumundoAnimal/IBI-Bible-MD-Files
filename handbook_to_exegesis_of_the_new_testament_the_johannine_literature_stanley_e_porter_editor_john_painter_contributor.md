# Handbook to Exegesis of the New Testament: The Johannine Literature
**Author:** [[Stanley E. Porter (Editor); John Painter (Contributor)]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an in-depth exegetical analysis of the Johannine literature, specifically covering the Gospel of John, the Epistles, and the Apocalypse (Revelation). It examines themes such as authorship, date of composition, language, and provenance. The text explores the tension between Judaism and the Johannine community, the dualistic worldview within these writings, and the distinctive structure of the Fourth Gospel, including its prologue and various discourses. Scholarly debates regarding the relationship between these texts and their shared reliance on early tradition are also addressed.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Dualism
The Johannine literature is marked by a dualistic worldview, specifically the antithesis between light and darkness, which reflects the struggle between the forces of evil and the divine power of the Lord.

### Theme: Conflict with Judaism
The documents reflect the trauma of the Johannine community's expulsion from the synagogue, leading to the construction of a new identity that differentiates believers in Jesus from the surrounding Jewish authorities.

### Theme: Authorship and Community
The text discusses the anonymity of the Fourth Gospel and the likelihood that it was produced by a Johannine 'school' or community that preserved and adapted the teachings of the 'beloved disciple'.
