# The Person of Christ
**Author:** [[Donald Macleod]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the Christological debates surrounding the person of Christ, specifically focusing on the Epistle to the Hebrews and its treatment of Christ's pre-existence, his Sonship, and his relationship to the Father. Macleod engages with various scholars like J.D.G. Dunn and J.A.T. Robinson regarding adoptionist interpretations of Christ's nature, arguing that the epistle consistently asserts Christ's divine pre-existence and his unique status as the Son of God, distinct from angels and prophets. The text examines key theological concepts such as 'radiance' (apaugasma) and 'image' (charakter) to illustrate the writer's argument that Christ is the exact representation of God's essence. Furthermore, it addresses the tension between Christ's humanity and his divinity, concluding that the writer of Hebrews presents a view where Christ is simultaneously and fully human, yet ontologically divine.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Pre-existence of Christ
The author argues that Hebrews and the Johannine tradition assert that Christ existed with the Father prior to his incarnation, rejecting the adoptionist claims that Christ's divinity was a later development.

### Theme: Christ's Sonship and Subordination
Analysis of the specific language used to define Christ's status as the Son of God, navigating the complexities of his divine status versus his functional subordination to the Father in the economy of salvation.

### Theme: Incarnation and Humanity
The necessity of Christ's true humanity to allow for legitimate sympathy and mediation, while simultaneously maintaining that this humanity does not nullify his eternal divine nature.
