**Author:** [[Colin Brown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Remain

μένω (menō), remain; ἑπιμένω (epimenō), stay, remain, continue; ἀπομένω (apomenō), stay, remain, continue; διαμένω (diamenō), stay, remain, continue; παραμένω (paramenō), stay, remain, continue; προσμένω (prosmenō), stay, remain, continue.

### Vine, Wine

ἄμπελος (ampelos), vine; ἀμπελουργός (ampelourgos), vine-dresser; οἶνος (oinos), wine; οἰνοπότης (oinopotēs), wine-drinker; οἰνοφλυγία (oinophlygia), drunkenness.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theological Significance of 'Remain' (menō)
The term 'menō' is used in the New Testament to describe the believer's relationship with Christ, emphasizing a reciprocal, indwelling connection. It is not merely a static position but an active, ongoing state of being in love and obedience, symbolized by the vine and branches imagery.

### Theme: Symbolism of the Vine and Wine
The vine serves as a metaphor for Israel and, in the New Testament, for Christ. Wine is used in both positive contexts (as a blessing or part of the Lord's Supper) and negative contexts (as a symbol of judgment or the dangers of drunkenness and debauchery).

## 4. Key Verbatim Assertions & Quotes
> "The unbroken fellowship of Jesus with the Father 'causes the word of Jesus to be the word of God and his work to be the work of God' (A. Schlatter, Der Evangelist Johannes, 1960, 295). Christ was not called, like a prophet, for a particular task and a limited period; his whole person remains in lasting and special nearness to the Father (Jn. 1:32); just as the son, in contrast to the slave, continues for ever in the Father's house (Jn. 8:35)."
*Source: Page 5, Remain*

> "Abiding in Christ is the same as bearing fruit (Jn. 15:6). If there is no fruit, it is a sign that the fellowship has been interrupted (Jn. 15:6). Where this is true, the wrath of God rests upon the unbeliever (Jn. 3:36)."
*Source: Page 6, Remain*

> "The vine was an important part of OT economy. As with other crops, the owner was not permitted to reap the vineyard twice in one harvest; gleanings were to be left for those who lacked possessions (Lev. 19:10; Deut. 24:21). Vineyards were to lie fallow every seventh year (Exod. 23:10 f.; Lev. 23:3 ff.)."
*Source: Page 11, Vine, Wine*

> "Wine is frequently mentioned in lists of produce (e.g. Gen. 27:28; Deut. 7:13; 11:14; 18:4; 2 Ki. 18:32; Jer. 31:12). Because of its colour it could be called 'the blood of the grape' (Gen. 49:11; Deut. 32:14; Sir. 39:26, 50:15; cf. Isa. 63:3; Rev. 14:20)."
*Source: Page 12, Vine, Wine*
