**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides exegetical notes on Wisdom literature, focusing on the Book of Job, Proverbs, and Ecclesiastes (Qohelet). Key topics include the theology of suffering, the nature of biblical proverbs as observations rather than promises, the definitions of knowledge and wisdom, and comparative studies with Ancient Near Eastern (ANE) wisdom texts such as 'The Babylonian Job' and the 'Instruction of Amenemope'. It also outlines the structural forms of wisdom poetry, including various types of parallelism (synonymous, antithetical, and intensifying).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theology of Wisdom
Wisdom is defined as the ability to rightly apply facts, with its foundation being the 'fear of the Lord'. It is depicted as a central, vital element in a godly life.

### Theme: Proverbs as Observations
The 'Cardinal Rule' of the Proverbs is that they are intended as observations of how life generally works, not absolute guarantees or promises.

### Theme: Comparative Wisdom Literature
The document highlights similarities and differences between Hebrew wisdom and other ANE sources like the 'Babylonian Job' and 'Instruction of Amenemope', noting that shared cultural attempts to cope with existence occur while maintaining distinct theological perspectives.
