# The Theology of Paul the Apostle
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores Romans 7:7-13, 8:19-22, and the broader Pauline theology concerning the 'Adam narratives' and human condition. Dunn analyzes Paul's use of Adam as a representative figure, arguing that Paul uses the 'I' in Romans 7 to identify with the human experience under the law. It discusses 'epithymia' (desire/covetousness) as the root of sin, linked to the serpent's temptation and the subsequent human condition of futility and failure. Paul connects this human state to the broader cosmos, where creation shares in the burden of human transgression but also holds a hope for future liberation. The final summary emphasizes that human life is inherently relational and that the 'Adam' motif underscores a universal reality of sin and alienation, which ultimately points toward the need for redemption.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Pauline Use of the Adam Narrative
Paul employs Adam not just as a historical figure, but as an archetypal representative of human experience, using the 'I' in Romans 7 to describe the individual's struggle with law, desire, and sin.

### Theme: Epithymia and the Power of Sin
The text highlights 'epithymia' (desire or covetousness) as the foundational element of sin, which is awakened by the law, leading to human entrapment and spiritual death.

### Theme: Cosmic Solidarity in Futility
Drawing from Romans 8:19-22, the text explores how creation is inextricably linked to human destiny, sharing in its futility and suffering, while simultaneously longing for eventual liberation.
