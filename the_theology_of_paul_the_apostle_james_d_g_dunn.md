# The Theology of Paul the Apostle
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the theological framework of the Apostle Paul, specifically focusing on the intersection of human sin, the role of the law, and the Adamic narrative. Key sections include an analysis of Romans 7:7-13, where the author discusses the function of the law in relation to sinful desire and the historical echo of the Adam narrative in Israel's experience. It also delves into Romans 8:19-22 regarding creation's subjection to futility and the final summary of Paul's indictment of humankind, emphasizing the solidarity of all humanity in sin and the necessity of liberation from the 'slavery of corruption.'

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Adamic Narrative
Paul uses the story of Adam not merely as a historical figure, but as an existential representative of humankind (everyman) to explain the human experience of sin, deception, and death.

### Theme: The Role of Law
The law is examined as a catalyst that exposes human internal desire (epithymia) and sin, creating a psychological tension between moral prohibition and innate human curiosity.

### Theme: Cosmic Solidarity of Sin
Paul posits that human rebellion against God has resulted in a pervasive state of futility that affects not only humanity but the entire created order, tying it to the 'slavery of corruption'.
