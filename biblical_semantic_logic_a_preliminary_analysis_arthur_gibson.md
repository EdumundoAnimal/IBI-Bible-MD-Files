# Biblical Semantic Logic: A Preliminary Analysis
**Author:** [[Arthur Gibson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a critical examination of semantic and logical methodologies applied to biblical interpretation. Arthur Gibson evaluates the misuse of linguistic approaches, specifically critiquing the tendency of scholars to assert synonymy or semantic equivalence between terms based on tenuous logical, empirical, or etymological grounds. Key arguments involve the distinction between sense and reference, the fallacy of conflating biblical translation with logical analysis, and the problematic adoption of presuppositions in linguistic studies of the Bible. The text concludes by discussing the necessity of recognizing the gap between logical structures and actual language usage.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Fallacy of Synonymy
The author critiques the assumption that linguistic expressions can be equated based on loose semantic interpretations, labeling such practices as 'modal fallacies' or 'referential fallacies'.

### Theme: Sense and Reference
Utilizing Fregean distinctions, the author explores how biblical scholars often conflate the 'sense' of words with their 'referents', leading to faulty logical conclusions.

### Theme: Methodological Rigor
A critique of how theologians and linguists often use circular reasoning or unexamined presuppositions when applying modern linguistic theory to ancient biblical texts.
