## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theological Synonymy
The author critiques the practice of 'theological synonymy,' where scholars (such as Bultmann) treat semantically distinct expressions as synonymous based on theological motifs rather than linguistic evidence. This is described as a metaphorical use of the term 'synonymy' that masks data and imposes inconsistent theological criteria on language.

### Theme: Referential Fallacy
The author identifies a 'referential fallacy' occurring when scholars assume that because two terms refer to the same entity or concept, they are semantically identical. This ignores the distinction between sense and reference, leading to the false conclusion that the terms are interchangeable.

### Theme: Presuppositional Characterization
The author argues that many biblical linguists operate under unstated, self-confirming presuppositions. These presuppositions act as 'unwitting signposts' that force linguistic data into predetermined theological frameworks, often resulting in circular reasoning and the misuse of etymological or comparative philological evidence.
