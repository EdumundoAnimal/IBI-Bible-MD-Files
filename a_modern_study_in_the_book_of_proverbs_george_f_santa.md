# A Modern Study in The Book of Proverbs
**Author:** [[George F. Santa]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### CHAPTER TEN 29-32

FLEX THOSE (SPIRITUAL) MUSCLES! Note that the gladness of the righteous is "your strength." In the roughness of the way—"Go in this way"—is the cheering voice. "Have I not sent you?" "He gives strength to the weary. And to him who lacks might He increases power." This promise, however, implies help for our work, not rest from our labor. We shall have strength for the conflict. But there is no discharge from the war. There is supply for real, not for imaginary, wants; for present, not for future, need. The healthful energy of the man of God is also supplied. He is alive in the way. His heart is set in it. This makes it practicable. What before was drudgery is now meat and drink. Indeed, the more godly we are, the more godly we shall be. The habit of grace increases by exercise. One step helps on the next. Thus was the way of the Lord a stronghold to the upright Nicodemus. His first step was feebleness and fear. Walking onward, he grew stronger; standing up in the ungodly council, and ultimately the bold confessor of this Savior, when his self-confident disciples slunk back. "We don't have inborn sufficiency. The strongest in their own strength shall faint and be weary. The weakest in the Lord's strength shall "march on, and not faint"; is Bishop Lowth's version of Isa 40:31. Thus, in the hour of temptation, the upright will find their way to be strength." Joseph in Potiphar's house; Obadiah in a fiery trial, Susanna in the Persian court; held on a fiery trial, sustained from high. Thus—thus alone—"the righteous shall hold to his way... blessed is the man whose strength is in Thee... And in His name they will walk." When we look at our resources, we might "as well despair of moving sin from hearts, as of casting down the mountains with our fingers," said Bishop Reynolds. Yet none of us needs to shrink from the confession—"I can do all things through Him who strengthens me." No such resources support the waters of iniquity, captives instead of soldiers, they know no conflicts, they realize no need of strength. Even now devastation and "destruction are in their paths"; and the voice of the Judge will fearfully seal their doom. "Depart from me, all you evildoers."

### THE BEST OF TWO WORLDS

The righteous will never be shaken, But the wicked will not dwell in the land. The frailty of our present condition, common to all! was not in the wise man's eye, but the state of the two classes was in the purpose and mind of God. His way is a stronghold to the upright. The righteous, walking steadily in the way, shall never be shaken. "They enjoy in this life, by faith and hope, their everlasting life," says Reformers Notes. "No weapon that is formed against you shall prosper." "The mountains may be removed and the hills may shake, But My loving kindness will not be moved from you, and My covenant of peace will not be shaken, says the Lord who has compassion on you." "Is this a confidence, that neither earth nor Hell can ever shake?" The wicked—have they any such confidence? So far from being never removed, they shall never dwell. They have no title, like the righteous, as sons and heirs, to the blessings of earth; no hope or interest in the land, of which the earth is the type. How often are they cut off from dwelling in the one. And yet, they never will be allowed to dwell in the other. As our character is, so is our hope and prospect. We gain or lose both worlds.

### THE GRACIOUS TONGUE

The mouth of the righteous flows with wisdom, But the perverted tongue will cut out. The lips of the righteous bring forth what is acceptable. But the mouth of the wicked, what is perverted. Another image of the faithfulness of a gracious tongue! It bringeth forth wisdom, and that too in the practical exercise of knowing what is acceptable. This gift needs to be deeply pondered, and carefully cultivated, to give it a free scope, while we jealously confine it to its own sphere of influence. There is evidently many kinds of application. The same statement of truth does not satisfy everyone. And how—what—when—to whom—to speak—is a matter of great wisdom! Yet this consideration of acceptableness must involve no compromise of principle. Let it be a thoughtful adjustment of manner to the many kinds of tastes; a patience with lesser prejudices and inborn infirmities; avoiding—not all offense (which faithfulness to our Lord forbids), but all needless offense; all uncalled-for scheming and impatience. "The gentleness of wisdom" should be clearly visible in Christian faithfulness. Thus Gideon melted the rebellion of the men of Ephraim; Abigail held back David's hands from blood; Daniel stood fearless before the mighty monarch of Babylon; Their lips knew what was acceptable, and their God honored them.

### THE SWEETNESS OF PERSUASION

But most of all—Let the minister of God study to clothe his most unwelcome message in acceptable dress. Let him mold it in all the sweetness of persuasion, a compassion, and sympathy; with what parental earnestness does the "preacher" of Jerusalem, in his introductory chapters, allure us to wisdom's voice and instruction. Yet would have his "delightful words... words of truth." And thus must "the lips of a priest... preserve knowledge." He must weigh carefully his statements, without distorting them. The truth is to "be proclaimed upon the housetops" to the multitude. But it is to be kept back from ungodly scorners. He must always gain his people's ears, that he may with their hearts. The perverted tongue, pouring forth its own wickedness provokes its own ruin. It shall be cut out. O my God, what I owe Thee for the bridle of discipline, that holds me back from self-destruction!

### CHAPTER ELEVEN

A false balance is an abomination to the LORD, But a just weight is His delight. TESTIMONY ABOVE PRAISE How valuable is God's Word in its minute detail of principles for everyday living! Business is a providential appointment for our social communication and mutual helpfulness. With men, it is grounded upon human faith; with God, upon divine faith. Balances, weights, money, are its necessary materials. Cheating, double-dealings; the hard bargain struck with self-complacent shrewdness—this is the false balance forbidden by both the law and the gospel. Men may "praise" its wisdom, God not only forbids, but Hates it. "The honest weight often passes unnoticed. But such a just weight or "perfect stone," said Jermin, is a perfect jewel, and a precious stone in the sight of God. It is His delight!—a testimony infinitely above all human praise! We must not put away this proverb as a mere moral saying. It was given as a warning to a flourishing Christian church; and the sin denounced here has been a leprous spot upon many a highly gifted professing believer. Is it not a solemn thought that the eye of God sees all our everyday dealings of life, either as hateful or as a delight? Haven't we ever found, when upon our knees, the frown of God upon some breach in our daily walk? Look and see, whether you "maintain always a blameless conscience both before God and before men." The Lord is righteous; He loves righteousness; the upright will behold His face. "They—they only—"may abide in Thy tent."

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spiritual Strength
The text argues that spiritual strength is not an inherent human quality but a divine provision. It emphasizes that the habit of grace is built through exercise and that believers must rely on God's strength rather than their own, citing the example of Nicodemus and the promise that God increases power to the weary.

### Theme: The Gracious Tongue
The author discusses the importance of wisdom in speech, noting that a 'gracious tongue' requires thoughtful adjustment of manner to the listener. It warns against 'needless offense' while maintaining faithfulness to the Lord, using biblical figures like Abigail and Daniel as examples of those who used their speech effectively.

### Theme: Integrity in Business
The text interprets the 'false balance' as an abomination to God, extending the biblical prohibition against dishonest weights to modern business practices. It asserts that honest dealings are a 'testimony infinitely above all human praise' and that God observes everyday business transactions as either hateful or delightful.
