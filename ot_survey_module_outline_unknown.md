### Historical Books and Exile

What are the historical books and why are they called this? The historical books cover from Joshua through to Esther, recording the history of Israel from crossing of the Jordon to Exile and return theron. Israel was unique among her ancient NE neighbours for her emphasis on history. Most religious expression in the ANE was mythological, however, Israel saw its national history as an arena for divine revelation. The historical books form a narrative of Gods relationship with one nation, and his plan for establishing a relationship with all humankind. Joshua shows value of obedience. Judges tells of the near hopelessness of Israel after the conquest. Ruth illustrates God sovereign care for individuals. Samuel traces the early history of Israels monarchy. Kings tells about the history of the monarchy from Solomon to the fall of Jerusalem. Chronicles is the first commentary on scripture. Ezra and Nehemia present the history of the restoration in the fifth century B.C. Esther demonstrates how God's sovereign care and protection extend to his people. Authorship of all the historical books is anonymous. How do Joshua, Judges and 1-2 Samuel all deal with Israel's leadership structures? Joshua – Military man. Can speak on the change from Moses to Joshua...who leads the people in conquest. Judges – Moving into a political setting. Samuel – Kingship. What are some of the issues surrounding the kingdom of Israel dividing into two? David and Solomon's sin. Davidic Covenant to have someone on the throne, Solomons sin, splitting the throne into two, Davids line on the throne in Judah, new line in Israel. Northern territories rebelled against Solomons son Rehoboam and created their own nation, called Israel, led by Jeroboam. Why are both Kings and Chronicles in the Bible if they have so much similar info? Chronicles was a positive look for post exile Judah to encourage. Kings gives a true idea of what took place during different Kings reigns. What is the Babylonian Exile? How and why did it happen, and what were some of the ramifications for the people of Judah? Deportation of Judah to Babylon by king Nebuchadnezzar. Sin of the land under Manasseh, Jehoiachin rebelled against Nebuchadnezzar however he also revolted. Nebuchadnezzar placed Zedekiah on the throne giving one more chance. Second deportation under Zedekiah around 10 years later. 70 years in exile. (Spoken of in Jeremiah). Destruction of the temple, and Jerusalem. Start of the Diaspora. Persian empire, return, restoration. Power transfer to the priests from Kings. What brought about the end of the exile, and what new challenges did this bring? Persians had taken over from Babylonians. Persians allowing religious freedom. Rebuilding of the land they had been given. Fasts that were appointed for the rebuilding of the temple, what becomes of these now? Transfer of power to the priests.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Revelation in History
The document argues that Israel was unique among its ancient Near Eastern neighbors because it viewed its national history as an arena for divine revelation rather than mythology.

### Theme: Covenant and Monarchy
The text explains the division of the kingdom as a result of Solomon's sin and the failure to uphold the Davidic Covenant, leading to the split between Judah and Israel.

### Theme: The Babylonian Exile
The exile is presented as a consequence of the sin of the land under Manasseh and the subsequent rebellions of Jehoiachin and Zedekiah, resulting in a 70-year period of displacement and the destruction of the temple.
