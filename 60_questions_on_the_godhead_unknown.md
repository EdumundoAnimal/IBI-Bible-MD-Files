# 60 Questions on the Godhead
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Doctrine - 60 Questions on the Godhead. 1. Is the word trinity in the Bible? No. 2. Does the Bible say that there are three persons in the Godhead? No. 3. Does the Bible speak of the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost? Yes. 4. Do these titles as used in Matthew 28:19 mean that there are three separate and distinct persons in the Godhead? No, they refer to three offices, roles, or relationship to humanity. 5. Does the Bible use the word three in reference to God? Only one verse in the entire Bible does so-I John 5:7-It speaks of the Father, the Word (instead of Son), and the Holy Ghost, and it concludes by saying, 'These three are one.' 6. Does the Bible use the word one in reference to God? Yes, many times. 7. Can the mystery of the Godhead be understood? Yes. 8. Has the Christian only one Heavenly Father? Yes. 9. Then why did Jesus say to Philip, 'He that hath seen me hath seen the Father' (John 14:9)? Because Jesus is the express image of God's person. 10. Does the Bible say that there are two persons in the Godhead? No. 11. Does the Bible say that all the Godhead is revealed in one person? Yes, in Jesus Christ. 12. Is the mystery of the Deity hidden from some people? Yes. 13. Who is the Father? The Father is the one God, particularly as revealed in parental relationship to humanity. 14. Where was God the Father while Jesus was on earth? The Father was in Christ. 15. Did the prophet Isaiah say that Jesus would be the Father? Yes. 16. When God said, 'Let us make man in our image' (Genesis 1:26), was He speaking to another person in the Godhead? No. 17. How many of God's qualities were in Christ? All. 18. How may we see the God who sent Jesus into the world? By seeing Jesus. 19. Does the Bible say that Jesus is the Almighty? Yes. 44. Why, then, could Jesus forgive sin in Mark 2:5-11? Because He is God the Savior. 45. Is Jesus the true God? Yes. 46. If God and the Holy Ghost are two separate persons, which was the Father of Christ? Matthew 1:20 says that the Holy Ghost was the Father, while Romans 15:6, II Corinthians 11:31, and Ephesians 1:3 say that God was the Father. There is no contradiction when we realize that God the Father and the Holy Ghost are one and the same Spirit. 47. When Paul asked the Lord who He was, what was the answer? 'I am Jesus.' 48. When Stephen was dying, did he call God Jesus? Yes. 49. Did Thomas ever call Jesus God? Yes. 50. How could Jesus be the Savior, when God the Father in Isaiah 43:11, 'Beside me there is no Savior?' Because 'God was in Christ, reconciling the world unto himself.' 51. Does the Bible say that Jesus was God with us? Yes. 52. Did Jesus ever say, 'I and my Father are one?' Yes. 53. Can it be proved scripturally that Jesus and the Father are one in the same sense that husband and wife are one? No. The Godhead was never compared to the relationship of a husband and wife. 54. Does the Bible say that there is only one wise God? Yes. 55. Does the Bible call the Holy Ghost a second or third person in the Godhead? No. 56. Can Trinitarians show that three divine persons were present when Jesus was baptized by John? Absolutely not. The one, omnipresent God used three simultaneous manifestations. Only one divine person was present--Jesus Christ the Lord. 57. Then what were the other two of whom Trinitarians speak? One was a voice from heaven; the other was the Spirit of God in the form of a dove. 58. What did the voice say at Jesus' baptism? 'Thou art my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.' 59. Does the Bible say that God shed His blood and that God laid down His life for us? Yes. 60. The Bible says that God is coming back with all his saints; Jesus is also coming back.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Oneness Theology
The text argues against the Trinitarian view of three distinct persons, proposing instead that the Godhead is revealed in one person, Jesus Christ, with the Father, Son, and Holy Ghost representing roles or manifestations.

### Theme: Scriptural Inerrancy and Interpretation
The document relies on specific verse citations to resolve apparent contradictions regarding the nature of God, the deity of Jesus, and the Holy Spirit.
