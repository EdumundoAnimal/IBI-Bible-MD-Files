# Stress Unity: Non Essentials
**Author:** [[Eamon]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Non Essentials

Primary Texts: Romans 14 thru 15:7. The non essentials deals mostly with unity. Paul felt that all days were just as holy as the last, and that all food was perfectly acceptable to eat. However, Paul also recognized that some believers were 'weaker', and that certain foods should be abstained from. More than likely they were Jews, who believed that meat cooked by Gentiles was unclean, due to the Jews strict adherence to draining the meat from the animal. You can see how this could cause disunity. Some, like Paul, had no trouble with certain days or food. However, as the 'stronger' Christian, it is our duty to remain unified and accept our weaker brothers. They are Christians all the same, and enjoy the same benefits as we. It is not up to a Christian to judge or to force a Christian to their adherences to abstinence or lack thereof. As a stronger brother, they are to accept the weaker without judgment. They are to ensure that by the exercise of their liberty they do not offend or cause to stumble. To do something just because another believer does it or says it is alright when in fact we believe otherwise means we have sinned (Rom 14-22-23). As a weaker brother, they are not to judge the stronger brother, and to love them just the same. The judging is left to God. They should seek unity.

### Scripture References

In Mark 7:15-19, Jesus declares that all food is clean. It is not what goes into a man that makes him unclean, but what comes out. It is a matter of the heart. In Acts 10:9, Peter was shown a vision of animals that he considered unclean to eat. God told him that nothing He made was unclean. Later in 11, Peter explains saying we baptize not with water but with the holy spirit. Why should we worship differently? In Matthew 7, Jesus tells us not to judge, lest we be judged. We should not thing a Christian any less just because he abstains from certain food, nor should we criticize those who feels it is entirely acceptable to drink. Judgment belongs to God. 1 Corinthians 4:1-5 talks of leaving judgment to God. Colossians 2:16 speaks of not judging because of what someone may eat. It is not what the things that we uphold or the food we abstain from; these are only a 'shadow'. Christ it the reality. Also in Colossians 3, we see regardless of ethnicity or rules, that Christ is all and in all. In 1 Timothy 4 Paul instructs Timothy of false teachers; those teach abstinence from certain foods and marriage. Whether we give thanks to God for what we CAN eat (those who abstain), or we give thanks that we can eat anything, it does not matter.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christian Unity and Liberty
The text argues that believers should prioritize unity over personal convictions regarding non-essential practices like diet or holy days. Stronger believers are instructed to accept weaker ones without judgment, ensuring their exercise of liberty does not cause others to stumble.

### Theme: The Sovereignty of Judgment
The document emphasizes that judgment belongs solely to God. Citing Matthew 7 and 1 Corinthians 4, it asserts that Christians should not judge one another for their personal abstinences or freedoms, as these matters are secondary to the reality found in Christ.

### Theme: Shadow vs. Reality
Drawing from Colossians 2:16, the text posits that dietary laws and religious rules are merely 'shadows' of the truth, whereas Christ is the 'reality'. Therefore, adherence to such rules is not a requirement for spiritual standing.
