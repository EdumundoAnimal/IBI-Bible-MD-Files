Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants ● Psalm 118:14: The phrase הָּי ת ָר ְמִּזְו י ִּז ָע’) ozziy v’zimrat Yah, "The Lord is my strength and song") matches the Vorlage of Exodus 15:2 and Isaiah 12:2. In the Masoretic Text (MT), zmrth is vocalized with a silent he, functioning as an archaic genitival construct or an old 1st-person pronominal suffix (zimrati). 4QPsb and 11QPa (11Q5) preserve this reading with minor orthographic variations (plene spellings of Yah and ‘oz), corroborating the antiquity of this liturgical citation. The Septuagint (LXX 117:14) renders this as ἰσχύς μου καὶ ὕμνησίς μου ὁ κύριος (ischys mou kai hymnēsis mou ho kyrios), confirming the dual pronominal sense ("my strength and my praise/song"). ● Psalm 118:27: The phrase אִּ סְ רּו־חַ ג בַ עֲבֹתִּ ים עַד־קַ רְ נֹות הַ מִּ זְבֵּ חַ (’isru-chag ba‘avotim ‘ad-qarnot hammizbeach). MT reads literally: "Bind the festival [offering] with cords up to the horns of the altar." ○ LXX renders: συστήσασθε ἑορτὴν ἐν τοῖς πυκάζουσιν ἕως τῶν κεράτων τοῦ θυσιαστηρίου ("Coordinate the feast with dense boughs/branches up to the horns of the altar"). The Targum aligns with the MT sacrificial-animal interpretation (qurban bound with ropes), whereas the LXX understands ‘avotim as interwoven leafy boughs, matching the lulav traditions of Sukkot (Feast of Tabernacles). ○ Syriac Peshitta supports the reading of "festal procession with branches," reflecting early Near Eastern liturgical practice. Grammatical Mechanics [Structure: Inclusio Framework] v. 1: הֹודּו לַיהוָה כִּ י־טֹוב כִּ י לְעֹולָם חַסְ ּדֹו (Imperative Plural + Causal) vv. 2–4: Threefold cohortative jussive expansion (יֹאמְ רּו־נ ָא) vv. 5–18: Royal narrative recount: Min-hametzar (Preposition + Spatial Noun of distress) vv. 19–27: Gate-liturgy dialogue: Imperative (פִּ תְ חּו־לִּי) -> Perfect of vindication (הָיְתָ ה) v. 29: Exact Inclusio Repetition (הֹודּו לַיהוָה כִּ י־טֹוב כִּ י לְעֹולָם חַסְ ּדֹו) ● Verbal Aspect in vv. 10–12: The triple recurrence of the root סבב (sbb, "to surround/encircle") employs the Perfect/Qal stem (סְ בָ בּונִּי, s’vavuni) paired with the polar particle כִּי (ki, "surely/indeed") and the imperfect/apocopated form with cohortative/affirmative force: בְ שֵּ ם יְהוָה כִּ י אֲמִּילָם (b’shem YHWH ki ’amilam, "in the name of the Lord I cut them off/warded them off"). The verb מול (mwl, Hiphil) denotes circumcision or severe cutting off—a graphic military metaphor of total decimation of hostile nations under divine authority. ● The Spatial Contrast (vv. 5, 19–20): Verse 5 pivots upon the prepositional contrast between מִּ ן־הַ מֵּ צַר (min-hammetzar, "out of the narrow distress") and בַ מֶּ רְ חָ ב יָּה (bammerchav Yah, "into the open expanse of Yah"). Syntax signals an ontological liberation: from spatial confinement under siege to the boundless sovereignty of God's presence. ● The Architectonic Passive (v. 23): מֵּ אֵּ ת יְהוָה הָ יְתָ ה זֹאת הִּ יא נִּפְ לָ את בְ עֵּינֵּינּו (Me’et YHWH hay’tah zo’t; hi’ niflat b’‘eneinu). The feminine demonstrative זֹאת (zo’t) and pronoun הִּ יא (hi’) refer to the preceding architectural reversal of the rejected stone. The Niphal participle נִּפְ לָ את (niflat) functions as an objective passive of divine agency: "It is a thing performed wondrously beyond human comprehension."

### Lexical Profiles

● Chesed (ד ֶס ֶח, vv. 1–4, 29): HALOT 336b–338a. Far exceeding sentimental "mercy" or "lovingkindness," chesed defines unyielding, legally bound covenant fidelity. In royal-liturgical contexts, it represents Yahweh's pledged obligation to preserve His covenant partner, securing Davidic continuity despite imperial aggression. ● Yeshu‘ah (ה ָועּשְׁי, vv. 14, 15, 21): HALOT 449a–450b. Root ישע (yš‘), meaning to be spacious, broad, liberated from mortal peril. In the Psalter, it carries simultaneous military, judicial, and eschatological weight: a public vindication in which the righteous King is rescued from the jaws of Sheol and enthroned over hostile nations. ● Rosh Pinnah (ה ָנ ִּפ שׁאֹר, v. 22): HALOT 909a, 1167b. Denotes either the primary load-bearing foundation cornerstone (akrogōniaios) upon which the entire structural geometry aligns, or the terminal capstone/keystone (kephalē gōnias) binding the arch of the temple gates. In either architectural reading, it designates the indispensable element determining structural integrity. ● Hoshi‘ah-nna’ (אָנ ה ָיע ִּשׁוֹה, v. 25): HALOT 448b. Hiphil imperative of yš‘ with the enclitic particle of urgent entreaty אָנ (na’). Transliterated in the Second Temple era into Aramaic/Greek as Ὡσαννά (Hōsanna), it transitioned from an urgent cry for military/spiritual rescue to an acclamation of messianic triumph.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Vindication
The document argues that Psalm 118 functions as a royal thanksgiving liturgy where the Davidic King, representing the nation, is vindicated by Yahweh after being encircled by hostile nations. This vindication is not merely historical but eschatological, pointing toward the Messiah's resurrection.

### Theme: Architectural Paradox
The 'rejected stone' (Rosh Pinnah) serves as a central metaphor for the Gospel. The builders (human authorities) reject what God chooses as the foundation. This inversion of human values is presented as the core of divine redemptive logic, where the cross is the keystone of the universe.

### Theme: Critique of Human Autonomy
The text contrasts the 'refuge in the Lord' with the 'trust in princes.' It argues that human systems, technocracy, and political power are inherently fragile and mortal, whereas true security is found only in the immutable covenantal fidelity (chesed) of God.
