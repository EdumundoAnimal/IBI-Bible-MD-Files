# New Testament Commentary: Exposition of the Gospel According to John
**Author:** [[William Hendriksen]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Chapter XV

15 1 "I am the real vine, and my Father is the vine-dresser. 2 Every branch in me that bears no fruit he takes away, and every one that does bear fruit he cleanses, in order that it may bear more fruit. 3 Already you are clean because of the word which I have spoken to you. 4 Abide in me, and I (will abide) in you. Just as the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither can you unless you u abide in me. 5 I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in me, with me abiding in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you u can do nothing. 6 If a man does not abide in me, he is thrown away as a (mere) branch... and such branches are picked up, thrown into the fire, and burned. 7 If you u abide in me and my words abide in you, ask whatever you u will, and it will take place for you u. 8 By this my Father is glorified that you u bear much fruit, and so you u will become my disciples. 9 Just as the Father has loved me, so have I loved you u; abide in my love. 10 If you u keep my precepts, you u will abide in my love, as I kept my Father's precepts and abide in his love. 11 These things I have spoken to you u, in order that my joy may be in you u, and in order that your joy may be full."

### Discussion of Certain Basic Points in connection with the Allegory of the Vine and the Branches

We can begin by repeating what was said in connection with the Allegory of the Good Shepherd: "In the interpretation of this sublime allegory commentators differ widely." It is probably best to read these verses through, from beginning to end, several times before attempting any explanation. The earlier verses cannot be properly understood unless they are seen in the light of that which follows and, we may add, of that which preceded during this same night.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Vine and the Branches
The text interprets the allegory of the vine as a metaphor for the relationship between Christ and his followers. The Father is the vine-dresser, Christ is the real vine, and believers are the branches. The central argument is that abiding in Christ is the necessary condition for bearing fruit, and that those who do not abide are like branches that are cut off and burned.

### Theme: The Necessity of Abiding
Hendriksen emphasizes that 'abiding' is not a passive state but an active, continuous relationship. He argues that the believer's ability to bear fruit is entirely dependent on this connection to Christ, stating, 'apart from me you can do nothing.'

### Theme: The Role of the Father
The Father is identified as the vine-dresser who actively cleanses the branches so they may bear more fruit. This implies a process of sanctification where the believer is pruned and purified to increase their spiritual productivity.
