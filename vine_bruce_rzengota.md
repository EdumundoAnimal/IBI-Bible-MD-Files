# Vine
**Author:** [[Bruce Rzengota]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Kudzu was introduced to the United States in 1876 at the Centennial Exposition in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. During the Great Depression, the Soil Conservation Service promoted kudzu for erosion control. The problem is that it just grows too well, and the USDA declared it a weed in 1972. Jesus talked about a vine in John 15:1-8, contrasting the fruitfulness of a true vine with the vegetative, non-fruit-bearing growth of a weed. To live a discipled life is to learn the truth of the vine: yield is everything. Believers must move beyond just growing 'green leaves and strong vines' and focus on actually bearing fruit, which involves pruning, hardship, and a thriving relationship with God. Fruitfulness is not just about the internal spiritual life, but about outward impact, sharing one's testimony, and reproducing discoveries in others. The sermon concludes with an invitation to pray for pruning and fruitfulness, and an application to take steps toward new relationships and sharing one's story.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Bearing Fruit vs. Vegetative Growth
The author uses the kudzu vine as a metaphor for spiritual growth that appears impressive (green, sprawling) but is ultimately unfruitful and invasive, contrasting it with the true vine (Christ) that requires pruning and produces tangible results.

### Theme: The Necessity of Pruning
Hardships and struggles are presented as necessary tools God uses to prune believers, similar to how vines are managed for better yield, suggesting that comfort does not necessarily lead to spiritual maturity.

### Theme: Yield as Evangelism and Discipleship
Fruitfulness is defined not as a private spiritual feeling, but as an active, outward reproduction—leading others to the 'forgiver' and being comfortable sharing one's faith publicly.
