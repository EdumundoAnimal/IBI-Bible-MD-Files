# Drifting: Hebrews 2:1-4
**Author:** [[R. Kent Hughes]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text explores Hebrews 2:1-4, focusing on the warning against spiritual drifting. The author uses the nautical metaphor of a ship breaking free from its moorings to describe how Christians can unintentionally move away from their faith due to inattention, familiarity with the truth, and busyness. The author explains the 'qal wa homer' argument (light and heavy/lesser to greater) used in the passage to emphasize that if the law mediated by angels was binding, then the message of salvation brought by Christ is far more critical, making the consequence of ignoring it significantly greater. The text concludes by identifying the antidote to drifting: paying careful attention to the Word of God through memorization, meditation, and worship, focusing on the supremacy of Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spiritual Drifting
The subtle, unintentional process of moving away from faith in Christ caused by busyness, familiarity, and lack of spiritual vigilance.

### Theme: Qal Wa Homer Argument
A rabbinic logic style used in Hebrews, moving from a 'lesser' premise (Old Testament law given by angels) to a 'greater' conclusion (salvation offered by Christ) to argue for the gravity of the Christian message.

### Theme: Supremacy of Christ
The theological framework that Christ is superior to prophets and angels, acting as the ultimate mediator and Creator.
