# Drifting: Hebrews 2:1-4
**Author:** [[R. Kent Hughes]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This essay explores the warning against spiritual drifting found in Hebrews 2:1-4. The author characterizes drifting as a subtle, often unintentional process of neglecting the truth of Christ, much like a ship losing its moorings. The author contrasts the severity of the Mosaic Law, mediated by angels, with the superior revelation of the Gospel, mediated by Christ himself. Using the qal wahomer argument (from the lighter to the heavier), the text reasons that if the Old Testament violations were met with strict judgment, the rejection or neglect of the 'great salvation' offered through the Son must result in an even more severe consequence. The author emphasizes that this warning is directed at believers—those who have heard—rather than those who reject the gospel outright, highlighting symptoms of drifting such as losing wonder at the atonement, waning desire for the Word, and a lack of prayer. The solution proposed is an antidote of careful attention, focusing on Christ’s supremacy—prophetic, cosmic, Levitical, and angelic—and engaging in consistent meditation, worship, and memorization of Scripture.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Qal Wahomer Argument
A rabbinic style of logic often used in Hebrew scriptures, where an argument moves from a 'lighter' case to a 'heavier' or more serious one to emphasize the gravity of a specific truth or requirement.

### Theme: Spiritual Drifting
A state of gradual, often unintentional detachment from one's spiritual moorings, caused by busyness, familiarity with the truth, and a lack of intentional focus on the person and work of Christ.

### Theme: Supremacy of Christ
The central theological pillar in Hebrews, framing Christ as superior to angels, prophets, and the Old Covenant system in his role as Creator, Sustainer, and High Priest.
