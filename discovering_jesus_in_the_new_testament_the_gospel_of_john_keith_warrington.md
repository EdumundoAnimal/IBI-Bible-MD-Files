# Discovering Jesus in the New Testament: The Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Keith Warrington]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This chapter examines the Gospel of John, focusing on its distinct theological perspective compared to the Synoptic Gospels. The author argues that John presents a 'high' Christology, emphasizing the divinity of Jesus as the eternal Word (Logos) who became flesh. Key themes include Jesus as the Supreme, the Logos, the Son of God, and the unique Savior. The text highlights how John uses signs, miracles, and specific titles to establish Jesus's divine authority and mission, culminating in the resurrection and the call to faith. John's narrative emphasizes that Jesus is not merely a teacher but the divine Son who provides eternal life to those who believe.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: High Christology
The theological emphasis in the Gospel of John on the divinity of Jesus, identifying him as the eternal Word (Logos) who existed before creation and is one with God the Father.

### Theme: Signs and Miracles
The use of specific events and miracles in John's Gospel to reveal Jesus's true identity, inspire faith, and point toward deeper spiritual truths beyond the physical action.

### Theme: Eternal Life
The central promise in John's Gospel, presented as a gift from God through Jesus, involving a personal, ongoing relationship with the divine rather than just a future state.

### Theme: The Logos
A unique theological term used by John to describe Jesus as the pre-existent creative and revelatory force of God, bridging Greek philosophical thought with Jewish understanding.
