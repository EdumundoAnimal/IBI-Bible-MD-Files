# Discovering Jesus in the New Testament: The Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Keith Warrington]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Gospel of John emphasizes the eternal Son of God and focuses on Jesus' divinity. Unlike the synoptics, the Gospel of John begins by identifying Jesus as the Word made flesh. The author argues that John uses the logos, a philosophical concept, to explain Jesus' divine origin and role as the light and life. Jesus is presented as uniquely divine, yet also authentic in his humanity. The text explores key theological titles for Jesus, such as the Son of God, the Messiah, the Lamb of God, and the Giver of the Spirit. Furthermore, the Gospel employs 'signs of truth'—miracles that point toward a deeper spiritual reality—and concludes with Jesus' passion, death, and resurrection, calling for a personal response of faith from the reader.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christology in John
The author highlights a deeply christological focus in the Gospel of John, specifically detailing how the text elevates Jesus as the eternal Son of God and the 'Word' made flesh, asserting his divinity while maintaining his authentic humanity.

### Theme: Signs and Miracles
The document interprets the miracles in the Gospel of John not just as historical events but as 'signs' or 'pointers' intended to evoke faith and lead to a deeper understanding of Jesus' identity and divine authority.

### Theme: The Logos
The author examines the use of the Greek term 'logos' (word) as a philosophical bridge used by John to introduce Jesus to both Jewish and Gentile audiences, establishing his pre-existence and divine nature.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "This Gospel is traditionally believed to have been written by John the son of Zebedee, one of the Twelve. While his Gospel also focuses on Jesus, John writes from a different perspective than the synoptists and repeats only about ten percent of the material included in their Gospels."
*Context Source: Chapter 2, The Gospel of John, page 44*

> "A unique title that John use in his writings to refer to Jesus is the Greek word logos. John probably use the title to intrigue his readers. Both Jew and Gentile readers would have been aware of the term and recognized the logos as central to their religious or philosophical worldviews."
*Context Source: Chapter 2, The Logos, page 47*

> "John uses the title 'son of God' more than the other Gospels do to refer to Jesus. In view of the fact that the term was used to describe Israel, the king, or angels, it is not surprising that John uses it distinctively when referring to Jesus, and especially to identify Jesus' uniqueness."
*Context Source: Chapter 2, The Son of God, page 49*
