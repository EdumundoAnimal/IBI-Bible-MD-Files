# The Finality of Christ: An Exposition of Hebrews 1:1-4
**Author:** [[David J. Macleod]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article provides a detailed exposition of Hebrews 1:1-4. The author examines the historical context of the epistle, noting its significance for Jewish Christians living during a time of immense political and social turmoil in the Roman Empire. The study then explores the seven-fold description of the Son's majesty, specifically addressing his roles as the appointed Heir of all things, his status as the radiance of God's glory, and his dual nature. The text further categorizes the ministry of Christ into three major theological themes: the Son as Prophet, the Son as Priest, and the Son as King, emphasizing the supremacy of Christ's revelation over all previous prophetic utterances.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Supremacy
The argument that the revelation of the Son is greater than that of all human prophets and angels, marking the finality of God's communication.

### Theme: The Seven-fold Description of the Son
An analysis of the opening verses of Hebrews which delineate the Son's divine attributes and his inheritance as Heir of all things.

### Theme: Tripartite Ministry of Christ
The categorization of Christ's work into three roles: as Prophet (final agent of revelation), as Priest (offering sacrifice to remove sin), and as King (the rightful ruler over the world to come).
