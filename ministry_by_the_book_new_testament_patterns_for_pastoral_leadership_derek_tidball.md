# Ministry by the Book: New Testament patterns for pastoral leadership
**Author:** [[Derek Tidball]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the Johannine imagery of ministry, specifically focusing on the 'spiritual desert' and the 'good shepherd'. It challenges the common scholarly assumption that the Gospel of John lacks a concept of the church or formal ministry. By analyzing the 'beloved disciple' and the 'vine' metaphor, the author posits that John provides a model of ministry rooted in intimate relationship with Jesus, mutual love among disciples, and active fruit-bearing. It connects these themes to Old Testament motifs like the exodus and the tabernacle, situating Johannine ministry within a broader salvation-historical context.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Individualism vs. Ecclesiology
The author argues against the academic view that John's Gospel focuses solely on individual salvation, asserting instead that it contains a sophisticated concept of the church and shared, organic ministry.

### Theme: Ministry as Intimacy and Fruitfulness
Ministry in John is defined by remaining in Jesus ('the vine'), which necessitates an internal life of intimacy that naturally results in external fruit-bearing and mutual love among the fellowship.

### Theme: Old Testament Typology
The author highlights how John uses wilderness imagery, the Passover lamb, and the tabernacle to frame the ministry of Jesus and his followers, suggesting a continuity between Israel's history and the new community of believers.
