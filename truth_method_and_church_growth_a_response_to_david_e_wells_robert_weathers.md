# Truth, Method, and Church Growth: A Response to David E. Wells
**Author:** [[Robert Weathers]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article explores the tension between traditional evangelical theology and modern church growth methodologies in a postmodern cultural context. It specifically responds to David Wells' critiques of the seeker-targeted church movement, addressing how modern evangelicalism has inadvertently adopted worldly philosophies, such as therapeutic individualism and consumerism. Weathers examines the shift from modernity to postmodernity, noting that while the cultural landscape has fundamentally changed, the mission of the church remains the translation of the gospel message into accessible forms without compromising theological truth. The text advocates for a shift toward community-centered discipleship and conversational, incarnational teaching methods that move beyond the limitations of modernist rationalism and consumer-driven ministry.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Seeker-Targeted Model
An approach to church growth that emphasizes reaching unchurched populations by adapting the presentation of the gospel to modern, culturally relevant forms.

### Theme: Theological Integrity vs. Cultural Adaptation
The central conflict between maintaining biblical truth and the necessity of making that truth understandable to a postmodern audience.

### Theme: David Wells' Critique
Arguments by David Wells that modern evangelicalism has professionalized ministry and traded theological substance for market-driven methodologies.

### Theme: Postmodern Discipleship
The transition from an individualistic faith to a community-oriented model, focusing on holistic discipleship rather than fragmented, consumer-based religion.
