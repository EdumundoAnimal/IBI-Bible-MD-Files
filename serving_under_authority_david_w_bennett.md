# Serving under authority
**Author:** [[David W. Bennett]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Serving under authority

In the previous three chapters we have examined terms and images based on people—family members, citizens, freedmen, priests, friends, patrons, and so on—where the emphasis is on the relationships that link followers of Jesus to the Lord and to one another. We now turn to human images which focus our attention instead on the task. In Part One we saw that Jesus invited his disciples to be 'with him', that is, to enter a personal relationship, but also sent them out to teach and to serve in his name, that is, to engage in a task. Of course it is not always possible to make hard and fast distinctions between terms which stress the relationship and those which emphasize the task, because images by their very nature are so fluid, and also because the reality to which these images point, the life of discipleship, necessarily involves both relationship and task dimensions.

### Servant

In this chapter we will explore the terms which describe those who function under the authority of another, including the many words that deal with servanthood. As we noted in Part One, the two most prominent clusters of images in Jesus's description of his followers have to do with family (especially 'child of God', and 'brother/ sister'), and with servanthood. The same emphasis continues throughout the writings of the early church.

### SERVANT (DOULOS)

The most general word for servant or slave is doulos, with the related words syndoulos (fellow-servant), douleuo (to serve) and douloo (to make someone a slave). The word group has a strong emphasis on being under the authority of another—most often, the authority of God. There were a good number of slaves in the early churches, but these were exhorted to serve their masters as if they were serving the Lord (Ephesians 6:5-7; Colossians 3:22-23). Even menial work took on a new dignity, and found a new and higher standard of excellence, when seen as service offered to Christ himself.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Servanthood (Doulos)
The concept of the servant or slave (doulos) emphasizes being under the authority of another, specifically the authority of God. In the early church, this imagery was applied to slaves who were exhorted to serve their earthly masters as if serving the Lord, thereby elevating menial work to a higher standard of excellence.

### Theme: Relationship vs. Task
The document distinguishes between images based on relationships (family, friends, citizens) and those based on tasks. However, it argues that the life of discipleship is fluid and necessarily involves both dimensions, as Jesus invited disciples to be 'with him' (relationship) while also sending them out to teach and serve (task).
