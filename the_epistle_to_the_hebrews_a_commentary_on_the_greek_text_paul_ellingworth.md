# The Epistle to the Hebrews: A Commentary on the Greek Text
**Author:** [[Paul Ellingworth]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Introduction: The Circumstances in Which Hebrews Was Written

From that point, the Pauline origin of Hebrews was firmly established in the eastern churches, and any contrary view was increasingly condemned. Speaking in his own name, Eusebius (fl. c. 450) writes: The fourteen letters of Paul are obvious and plain, yet it is not right to ignore that some dispute the Epistle to the Hebrews, saying that it was rejected by the church of Rome as not being by Paul. But Eusebius also refers, for example, to the 'letter to the Hebrews and the rest of Paul's letters [f. ... προς Ἑβραίους καὶ τὰ λοιπὰ πάντα τοῦ Παύλου]' suggesting some difference of status or circumstances. Epiphanius (c. 315-403) claims that many of the Arians reject Hebrews, and that it is in all manuscripts of the NT. The canon of fourteen Pauline epistles is constantly reaffirmed. Theodore of Mopsuestia (c. 350-428) explicitly identifies canonicity with authenticity when he writes that believers accept the epistle [to the Hebrews] as having been written by Paul, like the others. If this were not so, what is written [in it] would not be profitable for them.

### The Theology of Hebrews

To summarize in words other than those of Hebrews itself the theology of the epistle is a dangerous enterprise, as discussion of the literary structure of Hebrews has suggested. It is easy to distort what Michel has called the theological purpose or intention (Wille) of Hebrews, either on the one hand, by excessive concentration on its antecedents, or, on the other hand, by pressing its message into credal or systematic theological categories of a later period. One thing is certain: Hebrews is a unique piece of early Christian writing; it cannot be understood merely as the product of religious and/or intellectual influences, or as raw material from which mature, rounded, and definitive dogmas will in the fullness of time be formed. Hebrews must be understood on its own terms, the study of its prehistory, and of its later influence, is subordinate to such understanding of the letter itself.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theological Uniqueness of Hebrews
The author argues that Hebrews is a unique piece of early Christian writing that cannot be reduced to mere religious or intellectual influences or later systematic dogmas. It must be understood on its own terms, with its prehistory and later influence being subordinate to the study of the letter itself.

### Theme: Pauline Authorship Controversy
The text documents the historical debate regarding the authorship of Hebrews, noting that while it was firmly established in the Eastern churches, it faced rejection in the Roman church, leading to a long-standing tension in its canonical history.
