# The Epistle to the Hebrews: A Commentary on the Greek Text
**Author:** [[Paul Ellingworth]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a formal academic commentary on the Epistle to the Hebrews, focusing on historical-critical interpretation, Greek philology, and theological analysis. It provides an introduction addressing authorship (the debate surrounding Paul, Barnabas, Luke, Silas, and Peter), destination, date, and historical context. It explores themes like the influence of Gnosticism and Philo on the text's composition, the relationship between the New Testament and the Old Testament (with specific attention to the author's use of the Septuagint/LXX), and the structural analysis of the epistle. The commentary also delves into Christology, discussing the Son's supremacy, high priesthood, and sacrificial work in relation to the new covenant.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christology
Exploration of the supremacy of Christ as the Son of God, his role as a high priest, and his sacrificial work as the mediator of the new covenant.

### Theme: Authorship Debates
Analysis of external and internal evidence regarding the authorship of Hebrews, including theories involving Paul, Barnabas, Luke, Silas, and Peter.

### Theme: Literary and Historical Context
Investigation into the readers' circumstances, the target destination, and the influences of Gnosticism, Philo, and Qumran on the theological arguments.
