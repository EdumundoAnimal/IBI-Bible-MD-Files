# Introduction to the New Testament: The Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Werner Georg Kümmel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly analytical overview of the Gospel of John, covering its historical-religious setting, theological aims, authorship, and the debates surrounding its origin. The text critically examines the relationship between Johannine theology and Gnostic thought, the potential influence of Palestinian Judaism, and the persistent scholarly questions regarding the identity of the 'beloved disciple' and the traditional attribution of authorship to John the son of Zebedee. It also addresses the date and provenance, leaning toward a composition in Ephesus during the late first century.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Historical-Religious Setting
The author explores the complex theological environment of the Fourth Gospel, specifically its dualism and its potential links to Gnosticism, Mandaean mythology, and Palestinian Jewish sects (like Qumran). The author remains cautious about equating John solely with any single movement.

### Theme: Authorship and The Beloved Disciple
A major theme is the critical breakdown of the traditional attribution to John the son of Zebedee. The author analyzes the 'beloved disciple' motif and the possibility of a multi-stage composition involving an anonymous eyewitness and a later supplementary redactor.

### Theme: Polemical Context
The document argues that the Fourth Gospel contains polemical elements directed against both Jews (regarding the Messianic identity of Jesus) and competing Christian sects (such as the followers of John the Baptist or Gnostic teachers).
