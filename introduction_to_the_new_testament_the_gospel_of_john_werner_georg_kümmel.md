# Introduction to the New Testament: The Gospel of John
**Author:** [[Werner Georg Kümmel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This excerpt from an introduction to the New Testament focuses on the Gospel of John, covering its history-of-religions setting, theological character, authorship, and time/place of writing. The text explores critical debates regarding the relationship between the Johannine community and Gnosticism, the role of the Beloved Disciple, and the tradition of John the son of Zebedee in Ephesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: History-of-Religions Setting
Analysis of the conceptual parallels between the Johannine text and Hellenistic, Gnostic, and Qumran literature, arguing against a simplistic reliance on any single source.

### Theme: Johannine Authorship
Examination of the identity of the author and the 'Beloved Disciple,' concluding that the tradition linking the Gospel to John the son of Zebedee is historically tenuous.

### Theme: Theological Polemics
The argument that the Gospel of John serves a polemical purpose within the early church, particularly regarding the separation from Judaism and the definition of true belief.
