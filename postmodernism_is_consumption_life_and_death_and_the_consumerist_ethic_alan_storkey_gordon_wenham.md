# Postmodernism Is Consumption / Life and Death and the Consumerist Ethic
**Author:** [[Alan Storkey / Gordon Wenham]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the intersection of consumerism as a modern 'faith' and its implications for Christian ethics. Alan Storkey discusses how consumption replaces traditional values with individualism and short-term utility. Gordon Wenham extends this analysis to the 'consumerist ethic' regarding birth, marriage, death, and social structures, contrasting them with biblical principles.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumption as Faith
Consumerism is presented as a dominant, secular religion that centers on individual self-worship and the pursuit of infinite material needs.

### Theme: Neoclassical Shift
The transition from classical to neoclassical economic thought transformed the focus from production and labor to consumer utility and subjective preference.

### Theme: Consumerist Ethics
A critique of how consumerist logic impacts life decisions (birth, marriage, death), often prioritizing individual autonomy and comfort over sacrifice and relational commitment.

### Theme: Biblical Conflict
The authors argue that consumerism is fundamentally at odds with Christian teachings regarding stewardship, the sanctity of life, and communal responsibility.
