# Isaiah 63:7-64:11: A Commentary
**Author:** [[Brevard S. Childs]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly commentary on Isaiah 63:7-64:11, analyzing the form, structure, and theological exposition of the text. The author, Brevard S. Childs, explores the communal complaint psalm genre reflected in this passage, examining the interplay between divine memory, the 'servant' motif, and the struggle with divine absence vs. presence. The text addresses the historical context of the post-exilic period, discussing the shift in theological understanding regarding God as Father and the specific concerns regarding the 'grieving' of the Holy Spirit. Critical analysis is provided regarding the transmission history, the textual challenges of certain verses, and the overarching thematic concern with Israel's historical relationship with God.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Communal Complaint Genre
The passage is analyzed as a communal complaint psalm, characterized by an initial expression of praise followed by a lament concerning divine absence and a plea for intervention.

### Theme: Theological Tension of Divine Presence
The text examines the tension between the belief in God's presence as evidenced in historical deliverance (exodus tradition) and the experience of divine abandonment in the post-exilic period.

### Theme: God as Father
The shift in post-exilic theology regarding the naming of God as 'Father' is noted as a significant thematic development, potentially linked to the Hellenistic period.

### Theme: Servant Motif
The identification of 'servants' and 'tribes of your heritage' as central to the community's self-understanding after the destruction of the temple.
