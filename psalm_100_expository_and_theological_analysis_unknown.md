# Psalm 100: Expository and Theological Analysis
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants (BHS / DSS / LXX Apparatus). Verse 3b: Kethiv (וְ לֹא אֲנַחְ נּו) vs. Qere (וְ לֹו אֲנַחְ נּו). Kethiv (Consonantal Text): (wəlōʾ, negative particle) → "He made us, and not we [ourselves]." Supported by the Septuagint (LXX: καὶ οὐκ ἡμεῖς), the Latin Vulgate (et non ipsi nos), the Syriac Peshitta, and the Old Latin. Qere (Masoretic Margin/Reading Tradition): (wəlô, preposition + 3ms pronominal suffix) → "He made us, and to Him we belong [and we are His]." Supported by the Targum (wədîleh ʾănaḥnāʾ), several Masoretic manuscripts, the Dead Sea Scrolls fragment 11QPs^a (col. XIV), and medieval Jewish interpreters (Rashi, Ibn Ezra, Kimchi). Text-Critical Assessment: The Kethiv/Qere divergence represents an ancient homophonic scribal ambiguity (lōʾ / lô). While the Kethiv provides an antithetical polemic against human self-origination, the Qere forms a synthetic parallel with the subsequent clause: "His people and the sheep of His pasture" (וֹית ִע ְר ַמ אןֹצ ְו וֹמַּע). The Qere carries stronger structural coherence within Book IV of the Psalter, establishing Yahweh's covenantal possession of His elect.

### Grammatical Mechanics

Psalm 100 is constructed with two parallel strophes, each structured by a sequence of imperatives followed by a causal grounding clause introduced by (kî, "for/because"). Strophe A (vv. 1–3): [vv. 1–2] Three Imperatives: (Hiphil), (Qal), (Qal). [v. 3] Grounding Directive + Causality: (Qal Imperative) + (kî, for/because). Strophe B (vv. 4–5): [v. 4] Four Imperatives: (Qal), [implicit: sing], (Hiphil), (Piel). [v. 5] Causal Grounding Clause: (Attributive / Predicative Triad).

### Lexical Profiles

Tôdāh, HALOT 1672: Derived from (y-d-h, Hiphil: "to acknowledge, praise, confess"). Represents both the interior confession of Yahweh's majesty, the liturgical hymn of thanksgiving, and the material thank-offering sacrifice (Lev 7:12–15; 2 Chron 29:31). Ḥesed, HALOT 336: Unilateral, unmerited, covenant-keeping fidelity; asymmetrical loyalty binding Yahweh to His people. ʾĔmûnāh, HALOT 64: Firmness, stability, steadfastness, veracity; the immutable reliability of Yahweh's covenant promises across successive generations.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Ownership
The text argues that Psalm 100:3 establishes Yahweh as the Creator and covenantal Owner of His people, refuting the modern notion of self-origination.

### Theme: Doxological Worship
Worship is presented not as an emotional response, but as a cognitive and affective reaction grounded in the objective reality of God's character (ṭôv, ḥesed, ʾĕmûnāh).
