# iChurch: All We Like Sheep
**Author:** [[Skye Jethani]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the intersection of consumerism and modern Christianity, critiquing how the church has adopted market-driven logic to attract and retain members. The author, Skye Jethani, argues that viewing church attendance through the lens of 'consumer choice' erodes genuine spiritual community and transformation. By drawing parallels between retail shopping and church selection, Jethani highlights how the prioritization of individual preferences over spiritual discipline leads to a fragmented, commodified faith. He contends that historic Christian practice relied on submission to community and corrective discipline to foster maturity, whereas current 'consumer Christianity' prioritizes personal satisfaction, ultimately turning church leaders into service providers and parishioners into customers.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerism in Religion
The application of market-based consumer logic to the church experience, leading to churches competing for congregants based on preferences rather than spiritual formation.

### Theme: Erosion of Community
The shift from being committed to a local, rooted church community to a 'DIY' model of spirituality where individuals curate personal 'tapestries' of faith experiences.

### Theme: The Commodification of God
The concern that consumerism has stripped value from life and human connection, reducing both God and human relationships to products to be used or discarded based on personal satisfaction.
