# iChurch: All We Like Sheep
**Author:** [[Skye Jethani]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text discusses how consumerism has deeply influenced the modern church, framing church membership as a transactional experience based on personal preferences rather than communal commitment. The author shares an anecdote about a family leaving their church because of changing musical or lifestyle preferences for their teenagers. It transitions into a critique of modern capitalism, noting how advertising creates a cycle of perpetual desire that conflicts with Christian values of self-control and contentment. The author notes that churches have increasingly adopted business models where pastors act as marketing agents, treating congregants as customers. This shift compromises spiritual formation, as it prioritizes individual desire over the communal discipline necessary for Christian maturity. The piece concludes by noting that this commodification extends to our perception of God and human relationships.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerism in the Church
The adoption of market-driven strategies in ministry, where church members are treated as customers and leaders as service providers, leading to a focus on preferences rather than spiritual formation.

### Theme: The Cult of Choice
The modern emphasis on personal autonomy and choice as the highest good, which directly conflicts with the biblical tradition of submitting to a community and spiritual discipline.

### Theme: Commodification of Religion
The transformation of religious experience into a product to be consumed, where identity is tied to choices rather than sustained commitment to a local body of believers.
