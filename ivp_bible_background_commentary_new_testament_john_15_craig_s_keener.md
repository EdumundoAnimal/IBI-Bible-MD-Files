# IVP Bible Background Commentary: New Testament (John 15)
**Author:** [[Craig S. Keener]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Dwelling in the Vine (15:1-8). The word 'abide' (KJV, NASB, NRSV), 'remain' (NIV, TEV) or 'dwell' (15:4-10) is the verb form of 'dwelling place'. In the Old Testament, God had promised to dwell with his obedient covenant people always. The Old Testament and Jewish literature sometimes portrayed Israel as a vineyard or a vine, and God as the vinegrower. A golden vine in the temple symbolized Israel's power, and Jesus may here portray the disciples as the remnant of Israel. The most basic point of the imagery is the obvious dependence of branches on the vine for their continued life. Regarding 15:2-3, 'prunes' also means 'cleanses,' a motif in John. The Old Testament prophets often called on Israel to 'bear fruit' for God. Regarding 15:4-8, dead, fruitless branches of vines are of no use for carpentry; their only possible value is for fuel. Jewish teachers believed that God had awful punishments in store for apostates, because those who had known the truth and then rejected it had no excuse. Abiding in Love (15:9-17). Keeping the commandments, here epitomized as love, was supposed to bring joy, as seen in Psalms and later Jewish teachings.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Imagery of the Vine
The metaphor of the vine and branches draws upon Old Testament and Jewish literary tradition, where Israel is the vine and God is the vinegrower, highlighting the necessity of dependence on God.

### Theme: Theological Motif of Abiding
The term 'abide' functions as a verb form of 'dwelling place,' signifying the covenantal relationship and continuous connection required between the believer and God.

### Theme: Judgment and Apostasy
The commentary notes that the rejection of truth after having known it carries severe consequences, reflected in the imagery of fruitless branches being removed.
