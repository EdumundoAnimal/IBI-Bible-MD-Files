# Biblical Images for Leaders and Followers
**Author:** [[David W. Bennett]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text examines various metaphors and terminology used in the New Testament to describe leadership and followership within the early church. Bennett categorizes these images based on relational dynamics and task-oriented functions, exploring roles such as 'servant' (doulos, diakonos), 'manager' (oikonomos), 'shepherd' (poimen), 'guardian' (paidagogos), 'worker' (ergates), 'apostle' (apostolos), 'messenger' (euangelistes/keryx), 'ambassador' (presbeu), and 'soldier' (stratiotes). The analysis highlights that these roles emphasize service to God and others rather than worldly power or hierarchical superiority. The text concludes by synthesizing these images into a taxonomy that illustrates how diverse roles function under Christ's authority to achieve the church's mission.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Servanthood vs. Status
Leadership in the New Testament is consistently inverted, prioritizing service to God and others over earthly power, where even the lowest menial tasks are dignified through Christ-centered excellence.

### Theme: Relational Leadership
Metaphors such as 'shepherd' or 'guardian' emphasize that leadership is not merely a set of tasks but a deep, relationship-based responsibility requiring nurture, protection, and faithfulness.

### Theme: Accountability to Authority
The metaphor of the 'manager' (oikonomos) serves as a primary example of leadership being accountable to a higher master, where faithfulness to the task entrusted is the core requirement.
