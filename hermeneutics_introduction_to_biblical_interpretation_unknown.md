# Hermeneutics: Introduction to Biblical Interpretation
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This course module (BI 115) introduces the principles of hermeneutics, defined as the art and science of interpretation. It emphasizes overcoming obstacles to understanding the Bible—specifically the four distances: time, culture, geography, and language. The course requires students to analyze Scripture by examining historical, literary, and theological contexts. Practical exercises involve applying these frameworks to texts like Jeremiah 29:11 and Zechariah 7, alongside a reflective exercise evaluating the permanence or temporary nature of various biblical commands.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hermeneutical Framework
Defining hermeneutics as the 'art and science of interpretation' which requires navigating four primary distances: time, culture, geography, and language.

### Theme: Contextual Analysis
The methodology for faithful interpretation involves tri-fold contextual analysis: historical (world of the author), literary (the text itself), and theological (the 'God' factor).

### Theme: Command Permanence
An evaluative framework to distinguish between commands that possess eternal significance versus those tied to the historical/cultural situation of the original audience.
