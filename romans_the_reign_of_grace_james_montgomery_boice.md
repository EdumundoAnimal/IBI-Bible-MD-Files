# Romans: The Reign of Grace
**Author:** [[James Montgomery Boice]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This volume is an expositional commentary on Romans 5:1-8:39, examining the role of the Law in exposing human sinfulness, the internal struggle with temptation, and the foundational necessity of grace. The text explores Paul's teaching in Romans 7-8, highlighting how the Law serves to make sin appear exceedingly sinful, while simultaneously emphasizing that the Law itself is holy, righteous, and good. The author analyzes the Ten Commandments as a standard that reveals the depths of human transgression—ranging from murder and adultery to covetousness—thereby leading the believer to recognize their inability to achieve self-righteousness. Consequently, the commentary argues that the believer's only hope resides in Jesus Christ, who provides redemption from the death brought by the Law.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Function of the Law
The Law acts as a mirror that exposes the true nature of sin, demonstrating that humans are incapable of perfect obedience and are therefore in need of a Savior.

### Theme: The Sinfulness of Sin
Sin is presented as a corrupting power that uses the good commandments of God to produce rebellion within the human heart.

### Theme: Grace as the Only Remedy
Because the Law results in death when applied to sinful humanity, the only resolution is the intervention of Jesus Christ's grace.
