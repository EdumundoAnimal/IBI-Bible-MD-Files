# Romans: The Reign of Grace
**Author:** [[James Montgomery Boice]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document consists of selected chapters from Volume 2 of James Montgomery Boice's expository commentary on the Book of Romans, specifically covering chapters 7:7-12, 7:13, and 7:14-20. The text explores Paul's teachings on the nature of the law, the inherent sinfulness of humanity, and the internal struggles of the believer. Boice argues that the law serves to reveal human sin and to drive individuals toward reliance on Jesus Christ. The text includes detailed sections on the Ten Commandments as reflections of God's requirements and concludes with an analysis of the 'man' described in Romans 7, addressing theological debates regarding whether the passage depicts the struggle of a regenerate or unregenerate individual.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Law's Function
The law is not meant to save but to reveal the depth of human sin and the necessity of a Savior.

### Theme: Human Depravity
Paul's teaching in Romans demonstrates that even with knowledge of the law, humanity is naturally inclined toward rebellion and sinful action.

### Theme: The Struggle of the Believer
The internal conflict described in Romans 7:14-20 represents the ongoing battle between the flesh and the spirit within a person.

### Theme: Relevance of the Decalogue
The Ten Commandments serve as an enduring moral standard that exposes the universal need for grace through Jesus Christ.
