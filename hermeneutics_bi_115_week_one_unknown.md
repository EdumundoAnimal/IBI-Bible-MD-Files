### Introduction and Definition

Introduction... Hermeneutics: my story. Introduction... Syllabus, Course outline, Reading Assignments, Essay assignments, Exam. Today's session: Definition of hermeneutics, Need for hermeneutics, Obstacles to interpretation, Approach for this module. Definition: Hermeneutics. Hermeneutics: 'The art and science of interpretation'. Greek hermeneia: 'interpretation, translation'. Describes the principles used to understand what something means, to understand what a message intends to communicate. Why might interpretation be both an 'art' and a 'science'?

### Obstacles to Understanding the Bible

Obstacles to understanding the Bible... There is a tension between eternal significance and historical particularity. Yes, this is God's word to us. But there are also DISTANCE and OBSTACLES that separates it from us. We will refer to these obstacles as the 'four distances'. 1. The distance of time. The first documents of the Bible are over 3000 years old, the most recent being 1900 years old. Hosea 10:5-10. Vs. 5 - the calf-idol of Beth Aven? Vs. 6 - Assyria and Ephraim? Vs. 6 - ashamed of wooden idols? Vs. 8 - high places of wickedness? Vs. 9 - days of Gibeah? 2. The distance of culture. Closely related to time is the issue of culture. The culture of the biblical world was agrarian, with its own customs and beliefs. Ruth 4:6-8 - sandal ceremony? Leviticus 19:19 - blending fabrics? 3. The distance of geography. Most of us never get to the Middle East, and if we do, it is for a short period of time. Thus places, weather, etc are all different from what we know. Luke 10:30 - 'down' from Jerusalem to Jericho. Jonah 1:1-3 - Ninevah, Tarshish, Joppa. 4. The distance of language. The Bible is translated from Hebrew and Aramaic (OT), as well as Greek (NT). Translations are only approximations of what a text says. Some places in the Bible are impossible for us to clearly understand because of the language barrier. Isaiah 7:3 - Shear-Jashub. 1 Cor. 6:18-19; 1 Cor. 3:16-17 - body is a Temple.

### Context and Methodology

So... what do we do about these obstacles? You will hear one word over and over again this term: Look at the context, context, context! Context! We overcome these various obstacles by looking at several different contexts of Scripture: Historical context, Literary context, Theological context. What we will be doing... Throughout the semester we'll be looking at guidelines to help us establish these contexts and thus produce faithful interpretations. Historical context (the world of the author), Literary context (the text itself), Theological context (the 'God' factor). What we will be doing... Together, in class, we will explore the historical, literary, and theological contexts of Zechariah 7, as a test case. For your essay, you will interpret a passage from the Minor Prophets. You will investigate the historical context, the literary context, and the theological context. Review: 1. Because we are all already interpreters, 2. And because there are obstacles to interpretation (four distances), 3. We look at the historical, literary, and theological context of Scripture in order to help us better understand it, and in turn, live it out. Example: Jeremiah 29:11. 'For I know the plans I have for you,' declares the LORD, 'plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.' What is the historical context of this verse? What is the literary context? What is the theological context?

### Jeremiah 29:11 Case Study

Example: Jeremiah 29:11. The theological context is informed by the historical and literary context: What is the historical context of this verse? The Israelites are in captivity in Babylon, a long way from home, because of disobedience. What is the literary context? Jeremiah is writing a letter to them to tell them that it won't be a short time, but it will be 70 years, contrary to what the false prophets have been saying. Jeremiah 29:10-11. This is what the LORD says: 'When seventy years are completed for Babylon, I will come to you and fulfil my gracious promise to bring you back to this place. 'For I know the plans I have for you,' declares the LORD, 'plans to prosper you and not to harm you, plans to give you hope and a future.' Jeremiah 29:11: theological context. God does indeed have good plans for the people, but those plans include and only come after 70 years of exile! That means that the majority of people hearing this promise won't be alive to see the fulfilment of it! God's plans and purposes often happen in ways that we don't expect, and in ways that we can't see or understand. Sometimes God's good plans for the future include enduring suffering and pain. The Israelites in exile needed to understand that in spite of their difficult circumstances, God was still in control, and he still had good plans for them as a people. Example: Jeremiah 29:11. Everyone is an interpreter: we come with preconceived ideas of what a text means. We face obstacles - distances of time, culture, history - that we're not always aware of. So, we need to investigate the various contexts - historical, literary, and theological. And as we do, we have fresh insight and understanding about God and his Word. Review: Definition of hermeneutics, Need for interpretation, Obstacles in interpretation, Various contexts. For next week: Read Klein, Blomberg & Hubbard, chapter 1, and chapter 2, pg. 23-45. History of interpretation: see where we have come from and how we have gotten to the place we have with regard to interpreting the Bible.

### Zechariah 7 Notes

Zechariah 7. Little is known of Zechariah; son of Iddo. May have been a priest (was passionate about seeing the Temple rebuilt) (look to fourth vision in Zech 3). The background is post exile 518 B.C (Late 6 cent B.C.) after the Babylonian exile. The Persian Empire, with their emphasis on religious tolerance, allow the Israelites to return to Jerusalem and rebuild the temple. An issue brought up, with the re-building of the temple near at hand, 'Should we still fast for its destruction? Reference 2 Kings 25:8. Should we continue to fast in the fifth month. Literary Breakdown: Oracle. vs. 1 historical backdrop. vs 2-3 questions of fasting for temple. vv. 5-7 Prophetic disputation (Just as someone needs to eat and drink (vs6) for selfish reasons, so are your reasons for fasting. Self Pity for their wrong doing & not true repentance). vv 8-14 use prophecy/prophetic teaching. Zechariah used this to help the people remember their father's troubled past. God can still destroy them, even after coming back from the Exile. Do not repeat their mistakes. Ch 8 speaks of changing the days of fasting to feasting; changing the theme from repentance to redemption / Sorrow to joy. Theological: Tie in matthew 6 - fast for wrong reason - selfish outward reasons.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hermeneutics
Defined as the art and science of interpretation, specifically the principles used to understand the meaning and intent of a message.

### Theme: The Four Distances
The obstacles to biblical interpretation: time (age of documents), culture (agrarian context), geography (Middle Eastern setting), and language (Hebrew, Aramaic, and Greek).

### Theme: Contextual Interpretation
The methodology of overcoming interpretive obstacles by analyzing historical, literary, and theological contexts.
