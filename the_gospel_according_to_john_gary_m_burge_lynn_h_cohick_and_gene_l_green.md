# The Gospel According to John
**Author:** [[Gary M. Burge, Lynn H. Cohick, and Gene L. Green]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Gospel of John is a retelling of the life of Jesus that explores deep meaning and theology, moving beyond the synoptic outline. Key theological themes include the 'I am' sayings, the 'Book of Signs' (chapters 1-12), the 'Book of Glory' (chapters 13-21), and the role of the Paraclete (Spirit). The text examines the authorship, tradition, and the setting of the Johannine community, noting that the Gospel was likely composed between AD 80 and 100 in Ephesus. It emphasizes themes such as the divinity of Christ, the symbolism of living water, and the dualism between darkness and light.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The I Am Sayings
A series of self-identifying statements by Jesus (e.g., 'I am the bread of life', 'I am the light of the world') that claim divine nature and connection to the Old Testament name of God.

### Theme: Johannine Dualism
The persistent pattern in the Gospel of John depicting the contrast between light and darkness, faith and unbelief, and the world vs. those belonging to God.

### Theme: Sacramental Theology
The author argues that John presents a sacramental view of history where physical elements like water and bread, and events like the crucifixion, convey divine significance.
