# Hebrews
**Author:** [[Robert S. Rayburn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Letter to the Hebrews is an anonymous work addressed to a specific community facing spiritual crisis. Scholarly arguments against Pauline authorship are considered decisive, with various candidates like Barnabas, Apollos, and Luke suggested. The letter serves as a 'word of exhortation,' functioning as a sermon to warn against apostasy and encourage perseverance. It emphasizes Christ's supremacy as the mediator of a new covenant, contrasting the temporal nature of the Levitical priesthood with the eternal efficacy of Christ's sacrifice. The community addressed is likely composed of second-generation Christians of Jewish background who were tempted to retreat into non-conformist Judaism. The letter consistently directs its audience toward the future 'world to come,' prioritizing the assurance of salvation and the necessity of faith over the preoccupation with present benefits.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Supremacy
The central argument of the epistle is the superiority of Christ's mediatorial work, priesthood, and sacrifice over the obsolete Levitical system of the Old Testament.

### Theme: Apostasy and Perseverance
The letter acts as a stern warning against turning back from the faith, urging the community to endure in their conviction by maintaining a focus on the future eschatological promise.

### Theme: Unity of Covenant History
The author posits a single continuity of faith throughout salvation history, where the Old Testament is seen not as an inferior system but as an incomplete one that finds its necessary fulfillment in Christ.

### Theme: Eschatological Orientation
A focus on the 'world to come' serves as the anchor for the readers' faith, distinguishing the Christian perspective from contemporary Judaism.
