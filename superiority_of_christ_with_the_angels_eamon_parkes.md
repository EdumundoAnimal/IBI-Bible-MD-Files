# Superiority of Christ with the Angels
**Author:** [[Eamon Parkes]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is an academic assignment for the Irish Bible Institute, focusing on the book of Hebrews. The work analyzes the theological theme of the superiority of Christ over the angels, exploring the author's use of Old Testament scriptures, specifically Psalms 2 and 110, to construct his arguments. The text discusses the historical context of the letter, the warning passages directed at the original recipients, and the application of these truths for contemporary believers. It utilizes a comparative methodology, weighing scholarly interpretations of difficult verses such as Hebrews 6:4-8 and exploring the 'lesser-to-greater' argumentative style employed throughout the Epistle.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Superiority of Christ
The central argument of Hebrews that Jesus is exalted above the angelic hierarchy, emphasizing his role as the Son and the mediator of a new covenant.

### Theme: Lesser-to-Greater Argumentation
A rhetorical device used by the author of Hebrews to warn readers that if God's message through prophets/angels was binding, his message through the Son holds even greater weight.

### Theme: Use of Old Testament Catena
The author utilizes a string of citations (catena) from the Old Testament, particularly Psalms 2 and 110, to establish the theological framework of Christ's divinity and priestly office.
