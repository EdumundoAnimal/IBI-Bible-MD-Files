## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Background: The Genre of Hebrews

Most contemporary scholars would classify Hebrews as a homily or sermon. Heb 13:22 describes what has gone before as 'a word of exhortation' - a phrase by which Acts 13:15. There are certain features exhibited by Hebrews which would have been recognized then, as now, as homiletic. Thus the preacher captures the attention of his congregation by employing a variety of strategies. These include: (i) Identifying himself with them by the use of 'we' (2:1-4); (ii) addressing them as 'brethren' (3:1, 12; 10:19; 13:22 - obscured by the NRSV's inclusive 'brothers and sisters'); and (iii) using the familiar second person 'you' (3:12-14; 5:11-6:12). The essential orality of the work is further signaled by the plethora of rhetorical questions (1:5, 13; 3:16-18; 9:14; 10:29; 11:32) and the use of alliteration (1:1; 11:28; 12:11) and anaphora (i.e., the repetition of the same word or phrase, e.g., 'by faith' throughout chapter 11) for aural effect. However, chief among the features exhibited by Hebrews which would have been recognized then, as now, as homiletic is the alternation between exposition and exhortation, whereby the preacher moves back and forth between explication of the scriptures and direct address to his audience in an attempt to relate to the two and draw out the scripture's implications for their present situation.

### B) Also on a more macro scale the Five warnings of Hebrews

The Book is unique in the New Testament cannon in that one main thesis is developed and sustained throughout the document. Although it is generally agreed that Hebrews is the most well-structured of all the New Testament's writings, there is no scholarly consensus as to exactly where one section begins and another ends. Yet in all the various analyses we should not lose sight of Hebrews' genre as homily.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Homiletic Genre
The document argues that Hebrews is a homily or sermon, supported by the author's self-identification with the audience, the use of 'brethren' and 'you', rhetorical questions, alliteration, and anaphora.

### Theme: Alternation of Exposition and Exhortation
A primary structural feature of Hebrews is the movement between explaining scripture (exposition) and direct application to the audience's current situation (exhortation).

### Theme: Thematic Unity
The text asserts that Hebrews is unique in the New Testament for having one main thesis that is developed and sustained throughout the entire document.
