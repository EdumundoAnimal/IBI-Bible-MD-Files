# Biblical Hermeneutics: Gospels, Narratives, and Poetry
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document outlines a series of study sessions on biblical hermeneutics, covering Old Testament narratives, the Gospels, and Old Testament poetry. It details principles for interpreting these genres, specifically focusing on the importance of historical, literary, and theological context. Key examples analyzed include Daniel 1, the Psalms, and Lamentations 3, emphasizing how these texts inform faith, worship, and the understanding of suffering and God's character.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Hermeneutics
The application of historical, literary, and theological principles to interpret different genres of biblical literature, moving beyond general rules to special hermeneutics for specific genres.

### Theme: Interpretation of OT Narratives
Narratives are often descriptive rather than prescriptive, requiring the reader to understand characters, plots, and contexts without assuming every action is an example to follow.

### Theme: Interpreting the Gospels
The study of the Gospels requires a 'horizontal' approach (comparing perspectives across different Gospel accounts) and a 'vertical' approach (understanding the specific theological purpose and structure within each individual Gospel).

### Theme: Old Testament Poetry and Lament
Poetry, such as the Psalms and Lamentations, serves as a resource for expressing a full range of human emotions, including honest protest and lament, while maintaining hope in God's character.
