# Remaining
**Author:** [[David Flowers]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The sermon discusses the concept of 'remaining' in God, based on John 15:1-12. The author argues that true Christian faith is not merely a past confession of belief, but a present-tense, moment-by-moment participation in the life of God. This involves a difficult, ongoing process of self-denial and the rejection of ego, bitterness, and sin. The author emphasizes that religious rituals or past prayers are insufficient if one does not actively remain connected to Christ. Practical ways to remain include silence, meditation, and consciously choosing to align one's actions with love and God's will. The sermon concludes that spiritual maturity is a lifelong practice, often requiring spiritual direction, and is defined by the ability to know and live from the resources of Christ rather than simply believing in him as a distant truth.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Present-Tense Nature of Faith
The author distinguishes between past conversion experiences and the present, continuous requirement of 'remaining' in God. He argues that faith must be a moment-by-moment experience.

### Theme: The Necessity of Self-Denial
Remaining in Christ is described as a 'slow, death of self' that requires actively putting to death ego, resentment, bitterness, and anger.

### Theme: Spiritual Practices
The author advocates for silence, meditation, and seeking spiritual direction as essential tools for growth, noting that many Christians fail because they neglect these disciplines.

### Theme: Love as the Goal
Love is framed as the ultimate output of the spiritual life, where remaining in Christ naturally extends to loving others, including one's enemies.
