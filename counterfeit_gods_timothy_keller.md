# Counterfeit Gods
**Author:** [[Timothy Keller]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the theme of greed and idolatry through both secular and biblical lenses. It begins by examining corporate greed as seen in the Yellowstone Club collapse, then transitions to a theological study of how money functions as a counterfeit god in the human heart. Keller argues that money provides a false sense of security and identity, leading to idolatry. He discusses the biblical account of Zacchaeus as a model for transformation through grace, rather than just behavioral change. Finally, the text explores 'deep idols'—the internal motivations for power, approval, and comfort—and suggests that only the gospel can reorient the heart, illustrated by the self-reflection of Andrew Carnegie.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greed as Idolatry
The author argues that greed is not merely a behavioral problem but a form of idolatry where money becomes a 'counterfeit god' that people trust and serve instead of God.

### Theme: Deep Idols
Beyond material money, individuals possess 'deep idols'—internal drivers such as the need for power, approval, or comfort—that dictate how they use money and interact with the world.

### Theme: Transformative Grace
True change in an individual's relationship with wealth does not come from legalistic compliance but from a heart transformation prompted by the grace of Christ, as exemplified by the story of Zacchaeus.
