# The Epistle to the Romans
**Author:** [[Karl Barth]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly commentary on the Epistle to the Romans by Karl Barth. It explores the theological tension between human religious effort and the divine reality of God's grace, focusing specifically on the interpretation of Romans 7. Barth argues that religion, as a human construct, often masks the underlying human rebellion against God. True encounter with the divine occurs through an acknowledgement of the 'law' as an impossible barrier that exposes human sinfulness, leading to an existential crisis. The text emphasizes that salvation is not a product of human morality or religious observance, but an intervention of God that transcends human finite existence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Dialectic of Religion
Barth argues that religion acts as both a vehicle for and an obstacle to divine truth. It represents human attempt to bridge the infinite gap between God and man, which ultimately highlights human impotence and sin.

### Theme: The Function of the Law
The law is not a path to righteousness but a mirror that reveals human sinfulness. It serves to produce the 'crisis' necessary for recognizing the need for grace.

### Theme: The Crisis of Knowledge
Human knowledge and morality are seen as finite and trapped within their own 'relative' frameworks, necessitating a 'Wholly Other' divine intervention to achieve true meaning.
