# The Indelible Image: The Theological and Ethical Thought World of the New Testament
**Author:** [[Ben Witherington III]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly theological analysis focusing on the New Testament, specifically the Johannine literature. It examines key themes such as the 'I am' statements, the role of the Holy Spirit (Paraclete) as an advocate, and the relationship between divine agency and human response in the context of sanctification and obedience. The work provides a detailed look at the 'Theological Plot' of the Gospel of John, covering everything from the prologue and signs to the farewell discourses and the Passion narrative, emphasizing the Christological and ethical dimensions of Jesus' life and mission.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The 'I Am' Sayings
Witherington analyzes the seven 'I am' declarations in the Gospel of John, viewing them as reflections of wisdom literature and manifestations of Jesus as the revealer of truth, life, and the Father.

### Theme: The Paraclete
The Holy Spirit is presented as the 'advocate' or 'counselor' who guides believers into truth, serves as an agent of God's presence, and helps the community maintain its witness after Jesus' departure.

### Theme: Theological Plot of the Fourth Gospel
The author argues that the narrative structure of the Fourth Gospel is highly intentional, using signs and discourses to create a progression that leads the reader to a confession of faith in Jesus as Son of God.

### Theme: Sanctification and Obedience
A core ethical argument that sanctification is driven by obedience to the truth, facilitated by the Holy Spirit, and modeled on the indwelling relationship between the Son and the Father.
