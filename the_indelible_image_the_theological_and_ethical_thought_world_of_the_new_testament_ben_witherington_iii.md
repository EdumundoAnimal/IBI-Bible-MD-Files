# The Indelible Image: The Theological and Ethical Thought World of the New Testament
**Author:** [[Ben Witherington III]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This work is a comprehensive theological and ethical examination of New Testament literature, specifically focusing on the Fourth Gospel. The text explores key concepts such as the 'I am' sayings, the role of the Spirit as an advocate, the theological significance of miracles as signs, and the overarching concept of the 'indelible image' of God in humanity. It synthesizes sapiential traditions, specifically drawing connections between Wisdom literature and the Gospel of John, while addressing the ethical implications of Christian identity, obedience, and the communal life of believers. The analysis spans the narrative arc of Jesus's life, from his divine origins to his death and resurrection, positioning these events within a framework of theological necessity and fulfillment of Scripture.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The 'I Am' Sayings
Examines the seven direct-object 'I am' statements in the Gospel of John, viewing them as divine self-revelations rooted in sapiential tradition and the narrative plot of the Gospel.

### Theme: The Role of the Spirit
Analyzes the Spirit as an advocate (parakletos), an agent of divine revelation, and an instrument for guiding the community into truth and continuing the mission of Jesus.

### Theme: Wisdom and Logos
Investigates the intersection of the Fourth Gospel with Jewish Wisdom literature, framing the incarnation of the Logos as the ultimate embodiment of divine wisdom.

### Theme: Theology of Signs
Interpretative framework where the miracles performed by Jesus are not mere wonders but 'signs' revealing his identity and divine significance.
