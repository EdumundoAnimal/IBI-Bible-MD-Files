**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a comprehensive exegetical, homiletical, and theological analysis of Psalm 136. It explores the liturgical structure, emphasizing the 26-fold refrain 'kî lǝʿôlām ḥasdô' (for His steadfast love endures forever). The analysis traverses textual criticism, structural mechanics, historical and cultural contexts (including the Second Temple period), and provides a christological synthesis. The text argues that Hesed is not mere sentimentality but a covenantal force that involves both preservation and the active dismantling of oppressive systems (theomachy/exodus). It also engages with modern secular worldviews, presenting Psalm 136 as a theological counter-narrative to modern autonomy and reductionism.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Hesed
Defined as binding relational loyalty and unmerited constancy, not merely 'kindness' or 'mercy,' which acts as the metaphysical foundation of the universe.

### Theme: Liturgical Antiphony
The 26-fold repetition of the refrain establishes an acoustic and structural instantiation of YHWH's covenantal name ringing through creation and history.

### Theme: Active Covenantal Justice
The argument that divine love (Hesed) is not passive but inherently involves the destruction of oppressive powers (Pharaoh, Sihon, Og) to liberate the enslaved.

### Theme: Christological Continuity
The psalm is presented as an 'Exodus' shadow fulfilled in the Cross and Resurrection, viewing Jesus as the Logos who sustains the creation praised in the psalm.
