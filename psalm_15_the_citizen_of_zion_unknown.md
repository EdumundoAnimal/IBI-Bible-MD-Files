## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an exegetical and homiletical analysis of Psalm 15, framed as a Temple Entrance Liturgy. It explores the linguistic, syntactic, and structural dimensions of the text, emphasizing its role in defining covenantal integrity. The analysis covers the linguistic nuances of key terms like gûr (sojourn), šākan (dwell), and tāmîm (integrity). It maps the eleven ethical requirements onto the ancient Israelite sanctuary context while contrasting these with contemporary secular worldviews. The text culminates in a Christological reading, asserting that the perfect moral requirements of Psalm 15 are only fulfilled through Jesus Christ, whose righteousness is imputed to the believer.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Integrity
The requirement for ethical consistency, relational holiness, and economic justice as the necessary posture for those dwelling in Yahweh's presence.

### Theme: Christological Imputation
The theological resolution to the impossibility of human attainment of the Psalm's moral standard, emphasizing that only Christ's righteousness allows entrance.

### Theme: Temple Entrance Liturgy
The genre classification of the Psalm as a formal requirement list for pilgrims seeking to enter the holy presence, contrasting with pagan cultic practices.
