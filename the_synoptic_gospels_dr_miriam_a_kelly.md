# The Synoptic Gospels
**Author:** [[Dr. Miriam A. Kelly]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### The Synoptic Gospels

Because of the way in which Matthew, Mark and Luke have been written, they have been called the Synoptic Gospels. (J.J. Griesbach, a German scholar first used the term 'synoptic' toward the end of the 18th century). This is because they have a similar perspective or optic on the life and work of Christ.

### The Gospel of Matthew

Authorship, Date and Place of the Gospel. Since none of the gospel writers identify themselves in their writings, it is necessary to rely on other material called external evidence, for example from the gospel writers themselves. Traditionally the Gospel is ascribed to Matthew Levi, a tax collector who was called by Jesus and became one of the 12 disciples, (Mt 9: 9-13). The writings of Papias, Bishop of Hierapolis in Phrygia, about A.D. 130, is the earliest evidence. (Arthur Robertson, Matthew (Chicago: Moody, 1983), 8). Before following Christ, Matthew was a tax collector for the Roman government. Tax collectors were highly unpopular with the Jews. They were regarded as thieves. Most of these collectors were very wealthy from pocketing extra money from the Jewish people as they often inflated the value to the goods being taxed. Date of writing: Conservative scholars generally date Matthew before A.D. 70 or before the destruction of Jerusalem because he speaks of that event as future. But it is difficult to give an exact date, so somewhere between the years 50 to 70 A.D. Place of writing: Modern scholars often suggest Antioch as one possible place of writing, while others have suggested that Jerusalem as the place. Purpose and themes of Matthew: Some time had passed since the resurrection and ascension of Christ and the Jews needed to know that Jesus was indeed their promised Messiah. Many of the Jews who believed in Christ would have been accused by their fellow Jews of having abandoned the true faith of Abraham and Moses. This Gospel provides the Jewish Christian with the material to back up his/her faith.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Kingdom of God
Matthew emphasizes Jesus as the King. The term 'Kingdom of Heaven' occurs 32 times, while 'Kingdom of God' occurs 5 times. The document notes that 'Kingdom' refers to God's rule over His creation.

### Theme: The Suffering Servant
Mark depicts Jesus as the Servant of the Lord, emphasizing the powerful deeds of Christ and his ministry to the Roman audience, focusing on the Servant Songs of Isaiah 42:1-4.
