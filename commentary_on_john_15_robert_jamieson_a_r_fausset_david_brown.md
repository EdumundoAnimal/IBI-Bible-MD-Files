# Commentary on John 15
**Author:** [[Robert Jamieson, A. R. Fausset, David Brown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides a detailed theological commentary on John chapter 15, focusing on the discourse of the True Vine. It explores the relationship between Christ as the vine and the believers as branches, emphasizing the necessity of spiritual union, fruit-bearing through pruning, and the mandate to love one another. The text interprets the shift from the status of servant to friend and the implications of this new relationship. It also includes an outline structure detailing themes of fruit bearing, loving, suffering, and witnessing.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Organic Union
The metaphor of the vine and branches illustrates that spiritual fruitfulness is impossible without a continuous, vital connection to Christ.

### Theme: Disciplinary Pruning
The concept that the Father, as the husbandman, conducts a painful but necessary process to ensure believers bear more fruit.

### Theme: Servant to Friend
The transition in the believer's status from a servant who obeys orders to a friend who understands the purposes of God through intimate fellowship.

### Theme: The Theology of Suffering
The expectation that disciples will experience hatred from the world because of their association with Christ and their witness to the truth.

> "As a branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine, &c.—As all spiritual fruitfulness had been ascribed to the mutual inhabitation, and living, active interpenetration (so to speak) of Christ and His disciples, so here the keeping up of this vital connection is made essential to continued fruitfulness."

> "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends—The emphasis lies not on 'friends,' but on 'laying down his life' for them; that is, 'One can show no greater regard for those dear to him than to give his life for them, and this is the love ye shall find in Me.'"
