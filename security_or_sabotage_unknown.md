# Security or Sabotage
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores how emotional insecurity among church leaders leads to ineffective leadership and destructive behaviors. It frames leadership struggles not as theological or programmatic issues, but as matters of the heart. The author identifies six key behavioral symptoms of insecurity, each illustrated with a biblical character study: comparison (Vineyard Workers), victimization (Jacob), self-centeredness (Older Prodigal Son), people-pleasing (Martha), judgment (Elijah), and control (Sarah). The text emphasizes that leaders often subconsciously believe lies that keep them in emotional bondage. A process for breaking these patterns is provided, moving from identifying trigger events to adopting truthful perspectives. The article concludes with practical steps for achieving emotional security by anchoring identity in Christ, embracing God-given purpose, and cultivating healthy accountability.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Emotional Insecurity in Leadership
The author argues that church leadership failures are often rooted in the leader's internal emotional state rather than external theological disagreements or program deficiencies.

### Theme: Biblical Case Studies
The text utilizes biblical narratives as psychological frameworks to diagnose common leadership insecurities such as jealousy, pride, performance-based validation, and control.

### Theme: Truth vs. Lies
A cognitive-behavioral approach to leadership where the leader must identify the lies they believe (e.g., identity tied to performance) and replace them with scriptural truths to escape emotional bondage.

### Theme: Identity in Christ
The core solution proposed for emotional insecurity is decoupling self-worth from human approval and performance, and instead grounding it firmly in one's identity within Christ.
