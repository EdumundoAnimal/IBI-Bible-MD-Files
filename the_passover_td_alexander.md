# The Passover
**Author:** [[T.D. Alexander]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The chapter details the theological and historical significance of the Passover as recorded in the book of Exodus, its ritualistic requirements, and its subsequent typological fulfillment in the New Testament through the crucifixion of Jesus. It highlights the rescue of the Israelite first-born as a defining redemptive moment, the connection between the sacrificial lamb and the Feast of Unleavened Bread, and how the New Testament authors, particularly through the Synoptic Gospels and the Apostle John, link Christ's death to the timing and symbolism of the original Passover.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemptive History
The Passover is positioned as the primary redemptive act for Israel, serving as a historical foundation for their identity and their future relationship with God.

### Theme: Ritualistic Symbolism
The blood on the doorposts and the consumption of the roasted lamb are interpreted as acts of sanctification and community, protecting the Israelites and marking them as holy.

### Theme: New Testament Typology
The document examines the parallel between the Passover lamb and the sacrifice of Jesus, discussing the complexities of the timing of the Last Supper and the crucifixion narrative.
