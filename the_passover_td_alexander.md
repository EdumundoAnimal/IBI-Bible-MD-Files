# The Passover
**Author:** [[T.D. Alexander]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Passover is presented as a seminal event in Israelite history, commemorating God's deliverance from Egypt. It involves specific rituals, such as the slaughter of a lamb and the marking of doorposts with blood, which serve to signify protection and purification. The text explores the Passover's theological significance, its instructional content within the Pentateuch, and its typological fulfillment in the New Testament through the crucifixion of Jesus Christ. The author addresses the timing of the Passover, the symbolic role of the blood, and the historical continuity from the Old Testament redemptive act to the Christian understanding of Jesus as the true Passover lamb.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemption
The Passover serves as the primary act of God's redemptive power, delivering the Israelites from slavery and establishing them as a holy people.

### Theme: Typology
The New Testament connects the death of Jesus to the Passover, identifying him as the sacrificial lamb whose death provides purification and redemption.

### Theme: Ritual and Instruction
The text highlights the detailed instructions given to the Israelites regarding the selection, slaughter, and eating of the lamb, as well as the timing of the feast.
