### Appendix 3.1: Publishers, References Works, and Commentaries

Publishers: Here is a list of publishers you will encounter, running roughly from more critical to less critical: University Press... (Cambridge, Oxford, Sheffield, Harvard, Yale, Chicago, etc.) Doubleday Abingdon Paulist Press Augsburg T&T Clark/Continuum Scholars Press Fortress Press Eerdmans Westminster/John Knox SPCK Baker Academic Paternoster InterVarsity Press (IVP) SCM Hendrickson Zondervan Baker Word Moody This list is not exhaustive: it does not cover every publisher. Thus, you will need to do a little research and background reading to find out if a particular publisher is worth reading. This list is not etched in stone: there are a few newer Zondervan sources that are of very high quality and worth reading, and some older IVP sources that are frankly not academic at all. Moreover, this list is one person's interpretation: others may disagree and you may come to find you do also. Read widely and use your own judgment. Your research should reflect the use of good, scholarly, critical sources (and critical is not a dirty word!). Therefore if at all possible your bibliography should contain more from the upper three-fourths of the list than from the bottom.

Quality research begins with the knowledge and use of good reference materials. This is because reference sources give you a broad overview of a particular topic, and often include helpful bibliographies to point you toward deeper research. Bruce (ed) (****) Evangelical Commentary on the Bible, Elwell (ed) (****) Commentary Series (more recent): Anchor Bible (AB) Hermenia International Critical Commentary (ICC) Old Testament Library (OTL) New Testament Library (NTL) Interpretation (Interp) Word Biblical Commentary (WBC) Forms of Old Testament Literature (FOTL) New International Commentary of the New Testament (NICNT) New International Commentary of the Old Testament (NICOT) Westminster Bible Companion (WB) New Century Bible (NCB) The Interpreter's Bible William Barclay, The Daily Study Bible Tyndale Old Testament Commentaries (TOTC) Tyndale New Testament Commentaries (TNTC) The Bible Speaks Today (BST) It is important when doing academic research to distinguish between scholarly commentaries and devotional commentaries. Words in the title such as 'exposition', 'devotional', 'preacher' and 'sermon' will indicate that a commentary is not as suitable for academic purposes. This does not mean that these are worthless books! It means they cannot be the basis for your research. Consult D.A. ### Older Commentary Series

Older Commentary Series: Many theological libraries and churches have vast collections of older commentaries. While this is a valuable source to have, one needs to be careful when using these sources in academic work. Thus, the majority of these listed below should be avoided when doing research for essays; you can look up what they have to say, but we would encourage you not to cite them as references. The only exceptions would be Calvin's Commentaries, as John Calvin is still considered one of the most influential biblical interpreters of all time, and Keil and Delitzsch, whose work in the late 1800's is still relevant in many ways. Calvin's Commentaries (*****) Keil-Delitzsch Old Testament Commentary (***) The Biblical Illustrator (*) The Expositor's Bible Commentary (*) Young's Commentary (*) Matthew Henry (*) Critical and Experimental Commentary (*) The People's Bible (*) The Pulpit Commentary (*) The Wesleyan Bible Commentary (*) Expositions of Holy Scripture (*) Clarke's Commentary (*) Individual commentaries: Some commentaries are published outside of a series, on their own. You need to take extra caution to make sure such commentaries are useable for your research. Some of these individual commentaries (such as those by James Montgomery Boice, John Macarthur, or older sources such as those by Charles Spurgeon) are intended to be homiletical or expositional commentaries; this means they are meant for pastors writing sermons, not students writing essays. Others (such as I. Howard Marshall's Luke commentary or Claus Westermann's three-volume commentary on Genesis) are extremely critical and academic and would be very useful for your work. Make sure you do your background reading in these instances, to see where the author is coming from and what the purpose of their commentary is.
