# Are the Spiritual Disciplines of Silence and Solitude Really Biblical?
**Author:** [[Robert L. Plummer]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article critically examines the modern evangelical emphasis on the 'spiritual disciplines' of silence and solitude. The author argues that while these practices are popular, their classification as normative biblical disciplines is often based on poor hermeneutics and the selective use of proof texts. Plummer explores whether Jesus or the apostles established these as mandates, finding that scriptural precedents are often interpreted out of context. He warns against 'syncretistic spirituality' and the danger of adopting practices from non-Christian traditions under the guise of biblical discipline. The author concludes that silence and solitude should be viewed as human conditions or circumstances rather than universal spiritual disciplines that Christians are commanded to practice.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hermeneutical Integrity
The author argues that contemporary proponents of silence and solitude often engage in poor hermeneutics by isolating biblical passages from their historical and narrative contexts to support pre-existing personal practices.

### Theme: Syncretism in Spirituality
Plummer expresses concern that the church is increasingly adopting practices from non-Christian traditions (such as Eastern religious meditation) and attempting to justify them with thin or misunderstood biblical connections.

### Theme: Nature of Spiritual Disciplines
The author suggests that true spiritual discipline is rooted in obedience to the Word of God, whereas silence and solitude are more accurately understood as human experiences or temporary states rather than commanded religious activities.
