# A Basis for Faithfulness
**Author:** [[Paul F Perry]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Lesson 4. A Basis for Trust Hebrews 4:15-5:10. In Hebrews 3:1-4:14 the pastor sets forth for his friends a basis for faithfulness in the fact that the Son of God is faithful. Passage Breakdown: 3:1-6a Consider Jesus who was faithful to the one who appointed him. 3:6b-4:13. 4:14. We are called to consider the faithfulness of Jesus as an incentive to faithfulness in our own lives. The basis of our confidence that we are not abandoned in a hostile world and that we can be faithful in that world is the faithfulness of our priest. Jesus compared to Moses: 1. The person of Moses the mediator of Israel's covenant and worship was of central importance to Hebrews Author. 2. The author has emphasized that Jesus is the transcendent Son of God the mediator of God's final word (1:1-2:18). This authority claimed for the Son invites comparison with the unique authority of Moses. Outline of this passage: 3:1-2 Comparison between Jesus and Moses. 3:3 Jesus superior to Moses. 3:4-6a Explanation for this superiority. 3:6b Significance of this superiority. The preacher interpreted Ps. 95 7-11 in terms of the account of Israel at Kadesh-Barnea (Numbers 13-14). Near the end of the story, the people responded to the Lord with hardness of heart (Numbers 14:10; Hebrews 3:8, 15). Two conclusions: 1. Become partakers of God. We must be faithful with firm intention to hold to the end what we had in the beginning. 2. Israel was unable to enter God's rest because of unbelief. Hebrews 4:1-14, Rest: study verses 3, 11, and 14. What is this rest? When is this rest? How do we attain this rest? Repeated plea to calling us to faithfulness.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Superiority
The text argues that Jesus is superior to Moses by defining his role as the Son 'over' the house of God, rather than a servant 'in' the house.

### Theme: Faithfulness as Response
Faithfulness is presented as an incentive for believers, contrasted against the historical example of Israel's hardness of heart and subsequent inability to enter God's rest.
