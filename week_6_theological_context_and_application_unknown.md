### Zechariah 7

Zechariah 7. Historical/cultural context: Zechariah is a post-exilic prophet encouraging the people who have returned to the land of Judah after the exile. The Temple has been rebuilt, but the people are struggling to know how best to serve God in their new situation. This comes at the very end of the OT. Zechariah is also a priest, and so is located in Jerusalem. Some men from Bethel come to Jerusalem to ask if they still need to fast for the destruction of the Temple now that the Temple is rebuilt. Literary context/genre: The message in chapter 7 is closely related to chapter 8, which continues God's message to the people. While it deals with some similar themes that can be found in ch. 1-6, ch. 7-8 stand on their own as an isolated event. The passage is prophecy mixed with elements of narrative. The specific forms of prophecy are prophetic disputation, where God uses questions to challenge the people, and a prophecy of disaster, to warn the people that destruction could come again, as it came in the past. Theological/canonical context: This passage is concerned with several issues, including justice, true worship, and discerning God's will. It is not quoted elsewhere in Scripture, but has much in common with what Isaiah, Jesus and others have to say on fasting, taking care of the marginalise, and what God really desires from his people: which is true justice that reflects God's nature, not just religious observance. Application: There are several ways this text can be applied: we could look at taking care of the marginalised, what true worship looks like, or the idea of discerning God's will. I choose to focus on one issue, the question of 'discerning God's will'. The people from Bethel are asking about a particular fast, but the bigger issue seems to be knowing God's will, knowing what God expects of them or wants them to do. How can the people know what God expects of them?

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theological Exegesis
The process of identifying the theological or spiritual issues within a text and determining their application to contemporary situations, serving as the ultimate aim of Christian exegesis.

### Theme: Subjectivity in Application
The document notes that theological application is the most subjective part of exegesis due to the wide variety of potential options available to the interpreter.

### Theme: Prophetic Disputation
A specific literary form identified in Zechariah 7 where God uses questions to challenge the audience and warn of potential future disaster based on past patterns.
