# A New History of Early Christianity
**Author:** [[Charles Freeman]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an examination of the origins of early Christian thought, specifically focusing on the Johannine tradition, the theological distinctions between the gospel of John and the synoptic gospels, and the subsequent conflicts within early Christian communities regarding the interpretation of Christ's nature. It discusses themes of divinity, the 'logos,' the transition from Judaism, and the eventual movement toward establishing a canon of New Testament texts. The text also touches upon the historical context of early Christianity in the Roman Empire.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Theology
Analysis of the Gospel of John's unique portrayal of Jesus, emphasizing pre-existence, the 'logos,' and a distinct theological framework that contrasts with the synoptic gospels.

### Theme: Early Christian Schisms
Exploration of internal conflicts within early communities, particularly between 'loyalists' and 'secessionists' regarding the nature of Christ and the authority of teachings.

### Theme: Emergence of the Canon
Discussion on how the diverse collection of early Christian texts was gradually narrowed and codified into the New Testament by later church leadership, such as Athanasius.
