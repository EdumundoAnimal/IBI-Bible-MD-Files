# A New History of Early Christianity
**Author:** [[Charles Freeman]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the development of early Christianity, focusing on the composition of the Gospel of John and the subsequent theological and social tensions within the early church. It traces the Johannine community's expulsion from the synagogue, the unique theological perspective of John's gospel regarding Christ's divinity and the pre-existence of the Logos, and the internal conflicts that arose as described in the Johannine epistles (1, 2, and 3 John) regarding 'loyalists' versus 'secessionists'. Additionally, it touches upon the historical process of canon formation in the fourth century.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Christology
The author argues that the Gospel of John presents a high Christology, emphasizing the divinity of Jesus and the concept of the pre-existent Logos, which differentiates it from the Synoptic Gospels.

### Theme: Community Expulsion and Crisis
Evidence suggests the Johannine community faced expulsion from Jewish synagogues, which led to significant tension and, eventually, internal schisms (the 'secessionists' vs. 'loyalists') over the interpretation of Christ's nature.

### Theme: Development of New Testament Canon
The text highlights that the creation of the New Testament canon was a slow, centuries-long process involving debates over which texts constituted authentic apostolic authority.
