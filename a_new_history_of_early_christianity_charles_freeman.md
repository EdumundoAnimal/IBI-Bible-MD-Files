# A New History of Early Christianity
**Author:** [[Charles Freeman]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the development of early Christianity, focusing on the composition of the Gospel of John, the community of Jerusalem Christians, and the eventual canonization of the New Testament. It details the theological shifts, internal conflicts regarding the divinity of Jesus, and the emergence of docetism and the letters of John. Furthermore, it touches upon the timeline of major historical events in the Roman Empire's relationship with the nascent Christian faith.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Theology
The Gospel of John presents a unique, sophisticated narrative emphasizing Jesus's divinity, pre-existence as the Logos, and a direct relationship with the Father, distinct from the synoptic gospels.

### Theme: Community Crisis and Secession
The Johannine community experienced internal schism, characterized by tensions over the 'true' nature of Jesus, specifically countering docetic views that denied Jesus came in the flesh.

### Theme: Canonization Process
The formation of the New Testament was not immediate; it was a slow, multi-century process of choosing from a wide variety of circulating texts, eventually formalized by leaders like Athanasius.
