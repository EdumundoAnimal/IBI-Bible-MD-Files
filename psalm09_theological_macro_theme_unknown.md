**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The First-Century Chair)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants & Textual Criticism (BHS, LXX, DSS, Peshitta, Targum). The textual constitution of Psalm 9 cannot be detached from its structural relationship with Psalm 10. In the Masoretic Text (MT), Psalms 9 and 10 are cataloged as discrete compositions. Conversely, the Septuagint (LXX Psalm 9), the Latin Vulgate, and the Syriac Peshitta consolidate them into a single, continuous alphabetic acrostic poem.

### Grammatical Mechanics & Syntax Breakdown

The opening verbs, ʾôḏeh (ה ֶודֹא, Hiphil cohortative of ydh) and ʾăsappərāh (ה ָר ְפ ַס א, Piel cohortative of spr), mark intentional, volitional commitment. The cohortative endings (-āh) signify a resolute act of will by the Davidic speaker to proclaim God’s covenantal acts. The movement from bə-ḵol-libbî ("with my whole heart") to the cosmic nifləʾôṯeyḵā ("your miraculous acts") demonstrates that internal subjective devotion is bound to objective historical acts of divine deliverance.

### Structural Mechanics & Synthesis of Academic Commentaries

Psalm 9 employs a sophisticated architecture that balances internal retrospective thanksgiving with prospective, universal petition.

### 2. Historical & Cultural Report

While historical-critical scholarship often categorizes Psalm 9 as a generic post-monarchic communal thanksgiving adapted from royal typologies, the internal superscription (ləḏāwiḏ) and thematic topography ground the core composition within the Davidic monarchy. The occasion reflects an international crisis wherein hostile coalitions (gôyim) sought the annihilation of the Davidic state. David interprets these geopolitical attacks not as mere military skirmishes, but as a direct assault on Yahweh's covenantal kingdom. Having received divine vindication through the defeat of these confederacies, the king leads the covenant community in a national liturgy. This service acknowledges that the preservation of Israel is a direct manifestation of Yahweh’s supreme enthronement over the cosmic court.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Theocracy and Cosmic Rectification
The document argues that Psalm 9 presents God as the supreme Judge (šôfēṭ) whose throne is established for justice (mišpāṭ). This justice is not merely regional but universal, as Yahweh judges the world (tēḇēl) with righteousness, providing an eschatological vindication for the afflicted.

### Theme: The Theology of the Afflicted (ʿānî / daḵ)
The text highlights the theological weight of terms like ʿānî and daḵ, which describe those who are socially and economically marginalized. The document asserts that Yahweh acts as the 'Blood-Avenger' (dōrēš dāmîm) for these individuals, ensuring that their cries are not forgotten by history.
