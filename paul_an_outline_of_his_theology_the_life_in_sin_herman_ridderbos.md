# Paul: An Outline of His Theology - The Life in Sin
**Author:** [[Herman Ridderbos]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores Paul's theology concerning 'The Life in Sin', detailing the corruption of man and the anthropological implications of Paul's teachings. It distinguishes between the outward and inward aspects of human existence, the role of the body (soma), mind (nous), heart (kardia), and soul (psyche), and how these relate to humanity's fallen state, divine judgment, and the need for regeneration through the Spirit.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Anthropological Dualism
Ridderbos discusses Paul's distinction between the outward man and the inward man, where corruption permeates both the physical body and the cognitive/spiritual faculties.

### Theme: The Corruption of Man
Human corruption is described not just as individual sins, but as a holistic bondage that affects the total human existence, stemming from a darkened mind and hardened heart.

### Theme: Divine Judgment and Grace
Sin is presented as a state incurring divine wrath; yet, the text emphasizes that human effort cannot remove sin; only the Spirit can provide renewal.
