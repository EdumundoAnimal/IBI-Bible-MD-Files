# Social and Religious world of Judea in the New Testament era
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The purpose of this essay is to describe the social and religious world of Judea and their effects on the New Testament. The social pressures in the Greco-Roman society in the New Testament era can be broken down into four sections: (1) social networks, (2) honour and shame within the community, (3) moral depravation and (4) pagan/man worship in the form of Emperor/State worship which brought about the superstitious atmosphere of paganism. Religious pressures to conform were present in Judea as the church faced ideological conflicts with established Roman rule, Jewish tradition, and the expectation of conformity to state religious practices. The conclusion suggests that Christianity offered a new kind of freedom from the anxieties of the era, distinguishing it from the bondage of the prevailing religious systems of the time.
