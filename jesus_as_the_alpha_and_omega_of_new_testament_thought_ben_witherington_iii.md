# Jesus as the Alpha and Omega of New Testament Thought
**Author:** [[Ben Witherington III]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the fundamental role of Christ in New Testament thought, focusing on Paul's letters and the broader theological context of the early church. It examines how the 'Christ event' reshaped Jewish traditions, the narrative shape of Pauline Christology, and the transformation of key concepts like the law, salvation, and the concept of God. The text argues that early Christian christology was not developed in a vacuum but as a response to the experienced reality of Christ, which necessitated a restructuring of existing Jewish thought on messianism, divinity, and eschatology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Christ Event as a Catalyst
The author argues that the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus served as the foundational catalyst that forced early Christians to radically re-evaluate and transform traditional Jewish categories of theology, including messianism, salvation, and the nature of God.

### Theme: Narratological Shape of Christology
Pauline Christology is characterized as a multi-layered narrative encompassing the pre-existence of Christ, his role as the Messiah of Israel, the story of a fallen world, and his ultimate role as Lord and God, which together inform the moral and theological life of the believer.

### Theme: The Transformation of Monotheism
Witherington discusses how early Christians, including Paul, expanded their strict Jewish monotheism to accommodate the worship of Jesus as divine, incorporating Christ into the identity and functions of God without violating the core commitment to monotheism.
