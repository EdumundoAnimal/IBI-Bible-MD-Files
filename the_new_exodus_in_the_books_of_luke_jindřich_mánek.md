# The New Exodus in the Books of Luke
**Author:** [[Jindřich Mánek]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article discusses the theological significance of the Transfiguration in the Gospel of Luke, specifically focusing on the presence of Moses and Elias. The author explores the typology of the New Exodus, connecting the Old Testament Exodus and the lives of Moses and Elias to the ministry, suffering, and death of Jesus. Key themes include the 'baptism' of Jesus as a submersion, the comparison between the crossing of the Red Sea and the crossing of the Jordan, and how these events mirror the experiences of Jesus. The text posits that these biblical figures act as precursors, pointing toward the eschatological significance of Jesus' mission.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Typology of the New Exodus
The author argues that Luke portrays Jesus' ministry and suffering as a fulfillment or 'New Exodus', drawing direct parallels to the Israelite departure from Egypt, the crossing of the Red Sea, and the journey to the Promised Land.

### Theme: Eschatological Significance of the Transfiguration
The presence of Moses and Elias is interpreted as a symbolic turning point that points toward the final time and the celestial world, affirming Jesus' authority and the coming suffering.

### Theme: Submersion and Baptism
Jesus' suffering and death are interpreted through the lens of a 'submersion' into the waters, linking his baptism, the crossing of the Jordan, and his death to the drowning of the Egyptians in the Red Sea.
