# The New Exodus in the Books of Luke
**Author:** [[Jindřich Mánek]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The narrative of the Transfiguration on the mountain, contained in all the synoptics (Mark ix 2-8, Matt. xvii 1-8, Luke ix 28-36), shows Moses and Elias speaking with Jesus. Moses represents the Old Testament faith, having passed from death, while Elias was taken to heaven. They appear as precursors to the Messiah, signaling an eschatological turning point. The article explores how Jesus' own suffering and death are framed through the typology of the Exodus. Luke depicts Jesus' journey as a departure from the earthly Jerusalem to a heavenly one. Just as Moses led Israel through the Red Sea, Jesus enters a 'submersion' (baptism) and eventual death, reinterpreting the Exodus narrative. This connection is further solidified by the imagery of the baptism by John in the Jordan, which mirrors the crossing of the Red Sea, establishing a theological link between the Old Testament Exodus and the new Exodus accomplished by Jesus.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Exodus Typology
The author argues that Jesus' death and life are viewed through the lens of the Old Testament Exodus, where his baptism and suffering are likened to Israel's crossing of the Red Sea.

### Theme: Transfiguration Theology
The appearance of Moses and Elias serves as a bridge between the Old Testament and the Messiah, emphasizing the eschatological importance of Jesus' ministry.

### Theme: Baptism as Submersion
The text interprets Jesus' baptism by John in the Jordan as a form of 'submersion' that prefigures his passion, linking the desert experience to the final redemptive act.
