# The Johannine Literature
**Author:** [[John Painter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an analysis of the Johannine literature, specifically focusing on the Gospel of John, the Epistles of John, and the Apocalypse (Revelation). It examines the relationship between these texts, the questions of authorship, date of composition, provenance, and the theological themes inherent in the Johannine corpus. Special attention is given to the 'Fourth Gospel,' discussing its structure, language, worldview, and the evolving purpose of the community that produced it. It explores themes of dualism (light vs. darkness), the mission of the Johannine community, and the process of anonymous authorship culminating in the addition of chapter 21 to the Gospel.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Dualism
The cosmic struggle between light and darkness, represented as a battle between the children of God and the children of the evil one, shaping the worldview of the Johannine literature.

### Theme: Anonymous Authorship and Redaction
The argument that the Fourth Gospel is intentionally anonymous and underwent a multi-stage editing process, specifically the addition of chapter 21 to address the death of the 'beloved disciple'.

### Theme: Community Crisis and Self-Definition
The development of the Johannine community's identity during a period of conflict, particularly regarding their exclusion from the synagogue for confessing Jesus as the Christ.

### Theme: Eschatological Outlook
The tension between future apocalyptic expectations and a realized eschatology emphasizing the current presence of the Word of God.
