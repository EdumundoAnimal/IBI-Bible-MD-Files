# Encountering the Gospel of John: History, Theology, and Interpretation
**Author:** [[Andreas J. Köstenberger]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a comprehensive scholarly overview of the Gospel of John, covering authorship, historical context, theological themes, and the history of its interpretation. It examines internal evidence (such as the 'disciple whom Jesus loved') and external evidence (early church traditions) regarding authorship. It explores the gospel's purpose, specifically regarding evangelism and the aftermath of the temple's destruction in A.D. 70. Significant sections are dedicated to the relation between John and the Synoptic Gospels, major theological motifs (God, Christ, Salvation, Spirit, New Covenant Community), and the evolution of academic interpretation from early church history through the modern period.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Authorship
Analysis of both internal and external evidence pointing to the 'disciple whom Jesus loved' and the apostle John as the author.

### Theme: Historical Context (Post A.D. 70)
Arguments that the Gospel was written for an audience navigating the aftermath of the temple's destruction and tension between Judaism and early Christianity.

### Theme: Theological Motifs
Systematic exploration of Johannine theology centered on the person of Christ, the role of the Spirit, salvation, and the new covenant community.

### Theme: Literary/Canonical Relationship
A comparative study of the Gospel of John with the Synoptic Gospels, highlighting John's unique purpose and distinct theological framing.
