# A Commentary, Critical and Explanatory, on the Old and New Testaments - John 15
**Author:** [[Jamieson, Robert; Fausset, A. R.; Brown, David]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The commentary explores John 15:1-27, detailing the metaphor of the vine and the branches to explain the spiritual connection between Christ and His disciples. The author emphasizes that spiritual fruitfulness is contingent upon abiding in Christ. The text explains that God the Father acts as the husbandman, pruning believers to increase their fruitfulness. It further clarifies the transition from the servant-master relationship to a friendship based on shared knowledge and divine love. Following the commentary, a section outline by H.L. Willington categorizes the chapter into Fruit Bearing, Loving, Suffering, and Witnessing.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Organic Union with Christ
The necessity of a vital, living connection between the believer and Christ, likened to a vine and its branches, as the sole condition for spiritual productivity.

### Theme: Divine Pruning
The process of trials or discipline initiated by God the Father, aimed at purging impurities from the believer to facilitate greater spiritual growth.

### Theme: Transformation of Relationship
The shift from a relationship of servant-master to one of friendship, characterized by Christ revealing the Father's purposes to the disciples.
