# Psalm 10: Theological Macro-Theme and Exegetical Exposition
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The First-Century Chair)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants & Text-Critical Apparatus. The unity and transmission of Psalm 10 are tied to its structural relationship with Psalm 9. In the Septuagint (LXX Psalm 9:22–39), the Vulgate, and several Dead Sea Scroll fragments (e.g., 4QPs^a), Psalms 9 and 10 form a single unified acrostic composition. The Masoretic Text (MT) divides them into two separate psalms, with Psalm 10 lacking a superscription (the only psalm in Book I of the Psalter without an explicit heading aside from Psalms 1, 2, and 33).

### Grammatical & Syntactical Mechanics

Psalm 10:1: Lāmmâ YHWH ta‘ămōd bərāḥôq tat‘lîm lə‘ittôt baṣṣārâ. Lāmmâ — Interrogative particle expressing perplexity and protest rather than a request for theoretical information. ta‘ămōd — Qal imperfect 2ms from ‘āmad (to stand), communicating progressive, durative stance: "Why do you continually stand at a distance?" tat‘lîm — Hiphil imperfect 2ms from ‘ālam (to hide). Ellipsis of the direct object ‘ênêkā ("your eyes"), an established idiom (cf. Isa 1:15; Lev 20:4) denoting the intentional withholding of judicial intervention. lə‘ittôt baṣṣārâ — Preposition lə with plural construct ‘ittôt ("times of") and the definite noun ṣārâ with prosthetic bet ("the distress/siege"). The noun derives from ṣārar (to bind, restrict, press).

### Structural Mechanics & Literary Architecture

Psalm 10 is organized around a symmetry of crisis, lament, petition, and eschatological triumph: [THE ARCHITECTURE OF PSALM 10] A: THE CRISIS OF DIVINE WITHDRAWAL (v. 1) └── Lament over Yahweh's distance in "times of trouble" (lǝ'ittôt baṣṣārâ). B: THE ANATOMY OF PRACTICAL ATHEISM (vv. 2–11). 1. The Haughty Heart & Blasphemy (vv. 2–4): "No accountability". 2. The Illusion of Autonomy (vv. 5–6): "I shall never be moved". 3. The Violence of Mouth & Hand (vv. 7–10): Ambush of the helpless (*ḥēləkâ*). 4. The Theological Justification (v. 11): "God has forgotten, hidden His face". C: THE PETITION OF CRISIS & EXHORTATION (vv. 12–15). 1. The Call to Action (v. 12): *Qûmâ YHWH* ("Arise, O LORD!"). 2. The Judicial Challenge (v. 13): Why do the wicked spurn God?. 3. The Counter-Affirmation (v. 14): God has seen (*Rā’ītâ*). 4. The Imprecation (v. 15): Break the arm of the wicked (*Šǝbōr zǝrôa'*). D: THE ESCHATOLOGICAL ENTHRONEMENT & TRIUMPH (vv. 16–18). 1. The Eternal Monarchy (v. 16): Yahweh is King forever (*YHWH melek 'ôlām wā'ed*). 2. The Restoration of the Humble (v. 17): Hearing the desire of the afflicted. 3. The End of Terrifying Man (v. 18): Judgment of the orphan and the crushed (*yātôm wādāk*).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Practical Atheism
The document defines practical atheism not as metaphysical denial, but as the operational premise that God does not intervene in human affairs, leading to systemic injustice and the exploitation of the vulnerable.

### Theme: Divine Hiddenness
The psalm addresses the existential agony of God's apparent distance, converting the bewilderment of the sufferer into petitionary prayer rather than cynical abandonment of faith.

### Theme: Eschatological Vindication
The text argues that Yahweh's eternal kingship guarantees the eventual overthrow of predatory power and the vindication of the crushed, moving from lament to doxology.
