## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Counterfactual Covenant Redemption
The psalm utilizes a counterfactual protasis ('If it had not been YHWH') to force the community to contemplate their utter nothingness and absolute vulnerability apart from sovereign divine intervention. It posits an alternate universe where Yahweh was not acting on behalf of the covenant people to highlight the reality of their preservation.

### Theme: Chaoskampf and Demythologization
The text subverts Canaanite mythology (e.g., the Baal Cycle) where Yamm (Sea/Chaos) and Môt (Death) are autonomous deities. Psalm 124 demythologizes these forces, presenting them as creaturely instruments of human hostility ('ādām) that are entirely subject to Yahweh's sovereign restraint.

### Theme: The Physics of the Broken Snare
The psalm uses the metaphor of a fowler's trap (paḥ) to describe systemic, engineered entrapment. The deliverance is not an evasion through cleverness, but the structural shattering of the trap from the outside by an external force, illustrating that salvation is an act of God, not human resilience.

## 4. Key Verbatim Assertions & Quotes
> "If it had not been the Lord who was on our side—let Israel now say..."
*Source: Psalm 124:1*

> "The snare is broken, and we are escaped! What a joyous shout that is! The bird is loose; it shakes its wings, it mounts into the clear blue ether, singing as it rises toward heaven. How did it escape? Did it pick the lock? Did it untie the knots? No, the snare was broken—snapped in pieces by an unseen hand."
*Source: Charles Haddon Spurgeon, The Treasury of David (Page 8)*

> "The water has passed over our soul. What water? The water that is insolent, the water of pride. When the torrent of the world rushes by, do not let it draw your soul into its current. Seek the Rock; stand upon Christ. If the Lord had not been in us, the world would have swallowed us alive."
*Source: St. Augustine of Hippo, Enarrationes in Psalmos (Page 7)*

> "Observe the humility of the prophet: he does not say, 'We broke the net and flew away,' but 'The snare is broken'—the craftsmanship of the hunter is dissolved by the power of God, and then 'we escaped.'"
*Source: St. John Chrysostom, Homilies on the Psalms (Page 7)*
