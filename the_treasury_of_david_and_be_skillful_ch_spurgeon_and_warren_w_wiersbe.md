# The Treasury of David and Be Skillful
**Author:** [[C.H. Spurgeon and Warren W. Wiersbe]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Psalm XXXVII

TITLE.-Of David.-There is but this word to denote the authorship, whether it is a song or a meditation we are not told. It was written by David in his old age (verse 25), and is the more valuable as the result of mature experience. It is a psalm concerning the mystery of the prosperity of the wicked and the affliction of the righteous, which has perplexed so many, is here dealt with in the light of the future; and perhaps no psalm is more impressively forbidden. It is a Psalm in which the Holy Spirit is sweetly the too common repining of his people at the prosperity of the wicked, as he gives them clear views of the end of both, and shows how they are surrounded. It abounds with his own, and is twice illustrated by autobiographical statements, and abounds in remarkable contrasts. DIVISION.-The Psalm can scarcely be divided into separate sections. It resembles a chapter of the book of Proverbs, being a series of wise sayings, quite complete in themselves. It is, however, in some measure broken order, the first letters of the verses follow the Hebrew alphabet. This may have been not only a poetical invention, but a help to memory. The reader is requested to read the Psalm through without comment before he turns to the exposition.

### A Matter of Life or Death (Human Speech)

Never underestimate the power of words. For every word in Hitler's book Mein Kampf, 125 people died in World War II. Solomon was right: 'Death and life are in the power of the tongue' (Prov. 18:21). No wonder James compared the tongue to a destroying fire, a dangerous beast, and a deadly poison (James 3:5-8). Speech is a matter of life or death. When you summarize what Proverbs teaches about human speech, you end up with four important propositions: (1) speech is an awesome gift from God; (2) speech can be used to do good; (3) speech can be used to do evil; and, (4) only God can help us use speech to do good.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Power of the Tongue
Both Spurgeon and Wiersbe emphasize that speech is a matter of life and death. Spurgeon notes that the tongue can be a weapon of destruction, while Wiersbe categorizes speech into four theological propositions regarding its use for good or evil.

### Theme: The Prosperity of the Wicked
Spurgeon's exposition of Psalm 37 focuses on the mystery of why the wicked prosper while the righteous suffer, arguing that the believer must look to the future end of both parties to find comfort.
