### 1. Linguistic & Syntax Report

Textual Variants (Biblia Hebraica Stuttgartensia Apparatus). Verse 2b (mātay tāḇôʾ ʾēlay): Variant: The MT preserves the interrogative mātay, "when" + 2ms prefix conjugation tāḇôʾ, "will you come" + prepositional suffix ʾēlay, "to me". Witnesses: The Septuagint reads potē hēxeis pros me ("When will you come to me?"). The Syriac Peshitta reads 'emmat tegla' li ("When will you reveal yourself to me?"), reflecting a semantic shift toward revelatory manifestation. 1QPs^a preserves the consonantal ductus of MT with minor orthographic variation. Text-Critical Verdict: The abrupt presence of an interrogative petition within a royal vow of integrity has led some historical-critical commentators (e.g., Duhm, Briggs) to emend the text to an infinitive absolute or adverbial construct (mātay tāḇôʾ ʾēlay), "when [integrity] comes to me"). However, lectio difficilior potior favors the MT. The sudden invocation captures the Davidic king’s experiential awareness that sustained ethical blamelessness (tāmîm) is contingent upon the unmediated presence and covenantal arrival of Yahweh. Verse 5a (mölāššnî ḇassēter): Variant: MT Ketiv (Poel participle suffix 1cs functioning anomalously, or defective Poel participle construct); Qere (pointed as Poel participle masculine singular construct with hireq compaginis, or participle with suffix). Witnesses: LXX translates with a substantival participle: ton katalalounta lathra tou plēsion autou ("the one slandering secretly his neighbor"). 4QPs^b aligns with the consonantal text of the MT. Text-Critical Verdict: The Qere normalizes an archaic or non-standard denominative verb from lāšôn, "tongue"; hence "to tongue," "to slander"). The MT Ketiv represents the authentic archaic/poetic morphology. Verse 5b (ʾōtô lōʾ ʾûḵāl): Variant: MT has an absolute ellipsis: "him, I cannot [endure]." Witnesses: LXX supplies the infinitive: toutō ou synēsthion ("with this one I would not eat"), harmonizing the ellipsis with an idiomatic ban on commensality. The Targum glosses with ("I am not able to endure"). Text-Critical Verdict: The elliptical brevity of the MT is original; the omission of the verbal complement (nāśāʾ, "to bear/endure") is an established Hebrew idiom of intense revulsion (cf. Isa 1:13).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theocratic Kingship
The document argues that the Davidic king is not an autonomous ruler but a sub-vassal accountable to Yahweh. The king's authority is derived from Yahweh's holiness, and his primary duty is to reflect Yahweh's attributes of Chesed (mercy) and Mishpat (justice) in his governance.

### Theme: Covenantal Holiness
The psalm is presented as a 'Royal Vow of Integrity' or 'Coronation Oath.' It emphasizes that public governance is inseparable from private character. The king must first purge his own heart and household of deceit and pride before he can effectively administer justice in the city.

### Theme: Eschatological Righteousness
The text traces the failure of the historical Davidic kings to keep this vow, pointing to Jesus Christ as the true Davidic King who possesses absolute integrity. The ultimate fulfillment of the psalm's judicial purge is reserved for the eschaton, when Christ will cleanse the New Jerusalem of all evil.
