# Biblical Semantic Logic
**Author:** [[Arthur Gibson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text constitutes an academic critique of linguistic and semantic approaches to biblical studies. It examines common errors in biblical interpretation, such as the fallacious use of synonymy, the misapplication of referential logic, and the influence of unexamined presuppositions in linguistic analysis. Gibson addresses specific examples involving biblical translations and scholarly commentary (such as those by Blommerde, Pope, and Hill) to argue for greater rigour in logical semantic analysis, cautioning against the substitution of theological assumptions for actual semantic evidence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Fallacy of Synonymy
The author argues that biblical scholars often falsely identify different terms as synonyms without providing empirical or semantic evidence, leading to logical errors in translation and interpretation.

### Theme: Referential Root Relations
A critique of how linguistic roots are analyzed in relation to their references, noting that scholars frequently impose external logical frameworks that do not match the actual usage of the original text.

### Theme: Logical Semantics in Theology
The tension between descriptive linguistics and theological interpretation, where the author warns that scholars often force the text to conform to their own presuppositions rather than analyzing it through valid logical and semantic criteria.
