# Biblical Semantic Logic
**Author:** [[Arthur Gibson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text provides a critical examination of linguistic and semantic approaches to biblical studies, particularly focusing on the misuse of synonymy, semantic logic, and referential relations. The author critiques scholars like Lindars, Blommerde, and Hill for making fallacious claims about synonymy in biblical languages. Key arguments include the distinction between sense and reference, the dangers of assuming synonymy based on superficial parallels, and the need for descriptive, not prescriptive, linguistic analysis in theology. The text culminates in a discussion on the role of 'generality' in the theory of meaning, warning against imposing logic on biblical language in a way that ignores actual usage.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Linguistic Fallacies in Theology
Critique of the practice of identifying 'synonyms' in biblical texts without rigorous linguistic justification, often resulting in referential or modal fallacies.

### Theme: Sense and Reference
Application of Fregean concepts to distinguish between the cognitive sense of words and the actual referents in a semantic analysis of biblical texts.

### Theme: Semantic Logic
An analytical framework evaluating whether the logical relations claimed by biblical scholars (such as synonymy or identity) are empirically supported by the linguistic data.
