## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Phase 1: The Exegetical Network

Psalm 102 is organized as a concentric, chiastic lament-hymn: A: Somatic Collapse and Temporal Transience of the Individual (vv. 1–11). A1: Cry for urgent audience (vv. 1–2). A2: Visceral decay: bones like a hearth, forgotten bread (vv. 3–5). A3: Isolation among the ruins: desert owl and solitary bird (vv. 6–8). A4: Theological etiology: lifted up and cast down by divine wrath (vv. 9–11). B: The Cosmic Turn — Eternal Enthronement and Zion’s Restoration (vv. 12–22). B1: Enthroned forever (lǝ‘ôlām tēšēḇ) (v. 12). B2: The appointed time (mô‘ēd) for Zion (vv. 13–14). B3: Global doxology: Kings fearing the Glory (vv. 15–17). B4: Generational inscription for a people to be created (‘am niḇrā’) (vv. 18–22). A': Re-Emergence of Individual Mortality (vv. 23–24). A'1: Snapped strength in the midst of the journey (v. 23). A'2: Plea against premature cut-off (v. 24a). B': Cosmic Aseity and the Unshakeable Seed (vv. 25–28). B'1: Temporal foundations of the universe laid (v. 25). B'2: The cosmic garment vs. The Unchanging "He" (vv. 26–27). B'3: The eternal establishment of the covenantal seed (v. 28).

### The Grand Christological Trajectory in Hebrews 1:10–12

The premier canonical reception of Psalm 102 in the New Testament occurs in the opening chapter of the Epistle to the Hebrews. Richard Bauckham (Jesus and the God of Israel) and Beale & Carson (Commentary on the NT Use of the OT) demonstrate that the author of Hebrews incorporates Jesus directly into the unique Divine Identity of the God of Israel. The Septuagint translation of Psalm 101:26–28 (where the vocative Κύριε, "O Lord," is supplied in v. 26) allows the author of Hebrews to assign the cosmological creation, sustaining power, and ultimate folding up of the universe to the Son. The one who wept in physical frailty in the days of His flesh (Heb 5:7) is Himself the Attah Hu’ (the Unchanging Lord of Psalm 102).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Aseity
The document argues that Psalm 102:25-27, as cited in Hebrews 1:10-12, establishes the Son as the transcendent Creator who exists outside the created order. Unlike the cosmos, which is a 'garment' subject to thermodynamic decay, the Son is the 'Garment-Folder' whose existence is uncreated and immutable.

### Theme: Covenantal Immutability
The text posits that while human life is transient and subject to 'somatic collapse,' the covenantal promises of YHWH remain secure because they are anchored in His eternal, unchanging nature rather than in geopolitical or human stability.
