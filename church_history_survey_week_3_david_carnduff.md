**Author:** [[David Carnduff]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a study guide titled 'Church History Survey' for the 2008/09 semester. It focuses on the spread of the Gospel from Jerusalem to Rome as detailed in the Book of Acts. The text analyzes geographical, cultural, and ideological shifts in the early Church, examines the 'helps' and 'hindrances' to the spread of the Gospel, and outlines the characteristics, leadership structure, and missionary journeys documented in Acts. It also includes an endnote section titled 'David Wilkerson's challenge to Britain and the Church,' which discusses themes of exclusivity in the Gospel, the divinity of Christ, and predictions regarding future persecution and economic depression.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Geographical and Cultural Leap of the Gospel
The text argues that the spread of the Gospel from Jerusalem to Rome represented not just a physical movement, but a significant cultural and ideological leap from Jewish traditions to the Gentile world.

### Theme: Helps and Hindrances to Gospel Expansion
The document categorizes historical factors that aided the spread of the Gospel, such as the 'Pax Romana' and Greek culture, while identifying obstacles like religious exclusivity and rejection by Jewish leadership.

### Theme: Characteristics of the Early Church
The study highlights the reliance of early Christians on the Holy Spirit, their emphasis on hospitality, multi-national membership, and a specific pattern of discipleship focused on 'pouring life' into others.

### Theme: The Exclusivity of Jesus Christ
In the endnote, the text emphasizes the core argument that salvation is found only through Jesus Christ, specifically challenging contemporary ecumenical trends that might dilute the doctrine of Christ's divinity.
