# Lip (Biblical Concept)
**Author:** [[M. W. Meyer]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
LIP [Hebr. śāpâ, śēpātayim]; NEB also MOUTH, WORDS, SPEECH, TONGUE, TALK, (cattle from our) pens (Hos 14:2 [MT 3]; see comms.), etc.; [sāpām] (Ezek 24:17,22; Mic 3:7); NEB UPPER LIP, MOUTHS; [ḥek] (Hos 8:1); AV MOUTH; [peh] (Ps 50:16; Prov 6:2; Jer 7:28; Ezek 29:21; 33:31); AV MOUTH; NEB also various paraphrases; [mūsāqâ] (Zech 4:2); AV, NEB, PIPES; [Gk. cheilos] (Matt 15:8; Mark 7:6; Rom 3:13; 1 Cor 14:21; Heb 13:15; 1 Peter 3:10); [stóma - 'mouth'] (Luke 22:71; Rom 10:8-10; 1 Peter 2:22); AV MOUTH; also 'retort'; UPPER LIP [Hebr. śāpām] (Lev 13:45). In general, Hebraic thought regarded lips not only as physical organs but also as organs of speech. As physical organs, the lips may indeed be beautiful, "like a scarlet thread" (Song 4:3), and may be touched, as in Isaiah's vision (Isa 6:7). Like the MOUTH and TONGUE in Hebraic thought, however, the lips are primarily associated with speech; as organs of speech they frequently seem to act independently. The lips may proclaim God's praises (Ps 119:171), shout for joy (71:23), and spread knowledge (Prov 15:7); or they may utter sinful words (Ps 59:12 [MT 13]), speak insolently and dishonestly (31:18 [MT 19]), and talk of mischief (Prov 24:2). The lips may function independently of the heart, in a hypocritical manner (Isa 29:13; Matt 15:8; Mark 7:6). But ultimately the heart and mind, the central and inner life of a person, direct the utterances of the lips: "The mind [AV "heart"; Hebr. lēb] of the wise makes his speech judicious, and adds persuasiveness to his lips" (Prov 16:23; also cf. 4:23; Rom 10:8-10). The term occurs in certain Hebrew idioms. Moses' claim to be "a man of uncircumcised lips" (Ex 6:12,30) meant that he was slow of speech, "a halting speaker" (NEB). The Hebrew idioms "truthful lips" (Prov 12:19), "righteous lips" (16:13), etc., "lips" by synecdoche may designate a person uttering a speech; thus the NEB has "truth spoken" in Prov 12:19 and "honest speech" in 16:13. "Lip" (Hebr. śāpâ; Gk. cheilos) may also mean language (Isa 28:11; 1 Cor 14:21; the RSV translates ',â "language" in Gen 11:1; Isa 19:18). Three Hebrew terms require further explanation. S' , 'm refers to the upper lip, moustache, or beard; according to proper Hebrew practice, a person covered his s', 'm with his hand or his s', 'm and thus not to follow the rites for mourning when sinful Israel was to be decimated (Ezek 24:17,22). Micah spoke of false prophets who covered their s', 'm in shame and confusion (Mic 3:7). ., ḥek refers to the palate, gums, or lips, the part of the mouth (in Hos 8:1) in contact with the trumpet. Mūf'qâ, or mūfeqe¾, refers to one of the lips (or protuberances, spouts, or pipes) for oil lamps (Zech 4:2); perhaps the wicks rested by these lips, or perhaps oil was poured through a mûf'qâ. ## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Anthropology of Speech
In Hebraic thought, the lips are not merely physical features but are viewed as autonomous organs of speech that can manifest either praise or sin independently of the heart's true state.

### Theme: The Heart-Lip Connection
While lips may act hypocritically or independently, true wisdom originates in the 'heart' (lēb), which directs the lips toward judicious and persuasive speech.

### Theme: Linguistic and Cultural Idioms
Biblical usage employs synecdoche where 'lips' stand in for the speaker or the nature of their speech, such as 'uncircumcised lips' representing speech impediments.
