# Faith in Ireland: Postmodernism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Getting to Grips with Postmodernism. Objectives of this class: 1. To introduce some of the main themes and attitudes of postmodernity. 2. To discuss if and how these are found all around us in Ireland today. Postmodernism described: six reversals. 1. Disbelief in human progress (vs belief in progress). 2. I still at the centre - but who is this I? It all depends who you are talking to. 3. Certainty is neither desirable nor attainable in modernism it is both. The advantages of postmodernism: i. Opposed the autonomous arrogant self supposedly in control of the world. ii. Genuine insights of the differences humans bring to understanding reality. iii. Room for everyone to tell their story. The weaknesses and dangers of postmodernism: i. Exaggerates the difficulty of knowing anything for sure. ii. Incoherent and inconsistent. iii. Relativism is only a thinly disguised form of intolerance. iv. Pure relativism is also morally absurd. v. Unsustainable and un-livable.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Postmodernism
An analytical exploration of the shift away from modernism, characterized by a disbelief in progress, the fragmentation of the self, and the rejection of absolute certainty.

### Theme: Relativism
The critique of subjective 'truth' and the moral implications of rejecting universal standards, framed within the weaknesses of the postmodern worldview.

### Theme: Deconstruction
Refers to the methodology of language analysis (specifically referencing Jacques Derrida) which emphasizes reader interpretation over the inherent meaning of a text.
