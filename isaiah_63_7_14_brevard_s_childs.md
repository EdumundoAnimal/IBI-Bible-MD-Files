# Isaiah 63: 7-14
**Author:** [[Brevard S. Childs]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document covers scholarly commentary on Isaiah 63:7-14. It addresses the form and structure of the passage, characterizing it as a communal complaint psalm that reflects a familiar pattern within the Psalter. The commentary explores theological themes such as the memory of God's saving acts, the role of divine intermediaries like the 'angel of the presence,' and the complex tension between God as a merciful father and the experience of divine abandonment by the community. It also discusses the historical context, noting the influence of both exodus traditions and post-exilic theological developments, while analyzing specific translational and linguistic challenges, including the interpretation of 'grieving the holy spirit' and the pleading for divine intervention.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Communal Complaint Psalm
The passage is identified as a communal complaint, following a standard structure of praise, historical recollection, and urgent pleading for divine mercy.

### Theme: Divine Memory
The text emphasizes the theological importance of recalling God's past saving acts, specifically those rooted in the exodus tradition, to ground the community's current hope.

### Theme: Theological Tension of Abandonment
The commentary highlights the tension between the tradition of God as a father and the communal experience of suffering and divine withdrawal, particularly in the post-exilic period.

### Theme: Divine Intermediaries
The author examines the role of the 'angel of the presence' and the 'holy spirit' as conceptual markers for God's immanence in the midst of the people's history.
