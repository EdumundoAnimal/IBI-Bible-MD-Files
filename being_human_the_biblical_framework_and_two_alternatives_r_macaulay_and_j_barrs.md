# Being Human: The Biblical Framework and Two Alternatives
**Author:** [[R. Macaulay and J. Barrs]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text explores the necessity of establishing a proper biblical framework for understanding spirituality, specifically contrasting it with materialistic and Platonic alternatives. The authors argue that being made in the 'image of God' is the organizing principle for Christian life. Materialism is described as a force that leads to reliance on techniques and mystical experiences rather than God's truth. Platonism, or dualistic thinking, is critiqued for separating the 'material' and 'spiritual,' thereby devaluing the physical and the human experience, which contradicts the holistic biblical view of humanity, the body, and spiritual gifts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Image of God
The central organizing principle for Christian life, suggesting that to be truly spiritual is to be truly human.

### Theme: Materialism in Spirituality
The tendency to rely on specific techniques or 'religious experiences' to verify God's existence, which the authors argue undermines true faith.

### Theme: Critique of Platonic Dualism
The authors argue that separating the material from the spiritual devalues the human body and physical reality, which is contrary to biblical teaching.

### Theme: Holistic Biblical View
The assertion that all areas of life, including the body and ordinary activities, are avenues for expressing one's relationship with God.
