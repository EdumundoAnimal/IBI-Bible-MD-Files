# Being Human: The Biblical Framework and Two Alternatives
**Author:** [[R. Macaulay and J. Barrs]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text provides an analytical comparison between a biblical framework of humanity, grounded in the concept of being made in the image of God, and two prominent alternative frameworks: materialism and Platonism. The authors argue that a proper biblical understanding of spirituality is synonymous with being truly human, whereas alternative frameworks often lead to distortions, such as the devaluation of the physical body or the reliance on mechanical spiritual techniques. The document critiques modern approaches to spirituality that seek mystical or 'extraordinary' experiences as proof of faith, contrasting this with the biblical emphasis on dependence on God, moral conformity to His character, and the holistic integration of life. Specifically, the authors discuss the influence of Platonism in separating the material from the spiritual, a tendency they argue creates a false hierarchy that ultimately contradicts biblical teaching regarding the goodness of creation and the importance of the physical body in the Christian life.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Image of God
The foundational principle for the biblical framework, implying that human life is meant to reflect God's character and maintain a relationship with Him.

### Theme: Platonic Dualism
A philosophical influence that creates a harmful separation between the 'material' and 'spiritual,' often resulting in the devaluation of physical life and bodily reality in Christian practice.

### Theme: Materialism vs. Spirituality
The critique that modern culture uses 'spiritual techniques' or demands extraordinary experiences as a mechanical response to the loss of certainty in a scientific age.

### Theme: Holistic Spirituality
The argument that every area of human life is a venue for expressing spirituality, rather than limiting it to specific 'spiritual' acts or gifts.
