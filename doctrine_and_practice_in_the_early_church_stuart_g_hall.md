# Doctrine and Practice in the Early Church
**Author:** [[Stuart G. Hall]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an overview of the life, theological developments, and controversies surrounding Augustine of Hippo. It details his conversion, his engagement with Manicheism, his critique of Pelagianism, and his influential works including Confessions and The City of God. The text explores his arguments regarding original sin, the nature of the Trinity, the relationship between church and state, and his efforts to address theological disputes within the early Church.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Theological Framework
Augustine developed foundational doctrines on original sin, predestination, and the nature of the Church, often utilizing his personal experiences and intellectual background in Neoplatonism to shape Western Christian theology.

### Theme: Controversies and Heresies
The Church during this period navigated significant challenges from movements such as Manicheism, Donatism, and Pelagianism, all of which Augustine actively countered through his writings and church leadership.

### Theme: Scriptural Interpretation
The text highlights the struggle to move beyond literalistic or allegorical misinterpretations of the Bible—such as those found in Manicheism—toward a more refined, doctrinally sound engagement with the text, including the use of typology and theological synthesis.
