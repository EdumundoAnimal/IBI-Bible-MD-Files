# Doctrine and Practice in the Early Church
**Author:** [[Stuart G. Hall]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document covers the history, theology, and ecclesiastical challenges of the early Church, focusing significantly on Augustine of Hippo's life, conversion, and theological legacy. It details Augustine's engagement with Manichaeism, his intellectual journey from rhetoric to Neoplatonism, and his influence on Christian doctrine regarding the Trinity, original sin, grace, and predestination. The text also touches upon the rise of Nestorius and the Council of Ephesus, placing these events within the context of early church disputes, authority, and theological development.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Theology
Augustine developed core doctrines concerning original sin, the necessity of grace, and divine predestination, heavily influenced by his personal journey from Manichaeism through Neoplatonism to orthodox Christianity.

### Theme: Church Authority and Schism
The Church grappled with internal divisions, particularly the Donatist controversy, which tested definitions of holiness, the validity of sacraments, and the role of secular power in ecclesiastical enforcement.

### Theme: Biblical Interpretation
Debates over the nature of God, the Trinity, and humanity were central to early Church life, with different groups interpreting Scripture—such as Genesis or the Pauline epistles—to justify either literalistic or allegorical theological frameworks.
