# Doctrine and Practice in the Early Church
**Author:** [[Stuart G Hall]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a comprehensive historical and theological overview of St. Augustine of Hippo, examining his conversion, his engagement with Manichaeism, his philosophical synthesis with Neoplatonism, and his influential stances on the Trinity, original sin, and the Church. It maps his intellectual evolution from an early career in rhetoric to his mature theology. The text explores how Augustine utilized Neoplatonic thought to interpret scripture and understand spiritual realities, while also detailing his intense controversies against the Donatists and Pelagians, which helped shape foundational Western Christian doctrine. The final chapters transition into the theological contributions of the Council of Ephesus and the rise of Nestorius.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Neoplatonism
Augustine utilized Neoplatonic philosophy to bridge the gap between human intellectual inquiry and divine truth, framing his conversion as a movement from intellectual doubt to a spiritual encounter with God.

### Theme: The Doctrine of Original Sin
Augustine developed a rigorous doctrine of original sin rooted in the belief that all humanity is tainted by Adam's transgression, emphasizing that even infants require baptism for cleansing.

### Theme: Ecclesiastical Authority and Donatism
The conflict with the Donatists focused on the validity of sacraments, with Augustine arguing that the efficacy of the church's ministry is independent of the moral state of the officiating clergy.

### Theme: Trinitarian Theology
Augustine combined biblical exegesis with the intellectual framework of the Cappadocian Fathers to articulate a theology where God is an immutable, relational unity of Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
