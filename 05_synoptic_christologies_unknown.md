Christology 05 Synoptic Christologies IBI 2010-11 3/2/2011 CHRISTOLOGY Synoptic Christologies Objectives of this class: 1. To explore the different portraits of Jesus in the Synoptic Gospels, with special focus on Mark. Introduction The 3 synoptic gospels: Common events; focused on the same person; similar sources and often word for word the same - so we cannot push unique Christologies too far. Morna Hooker summarises 4 common themes in all the gospels concerning the person of Jesus: i. The gospel had its origins long ago in the purposes of God (Mk 1:1-3; Matt 1:1-17; Lk 1:68-79; Jn 1:1) ii. Jesus is the Messiah (Mk 1:1; Matt 1:18; Lk 1:69; Jn 1:17) iii. Jesus is the Son of God (Mk 1:11; Matt 2:15; Lk 1:35; Jn 1:18) iv. Jesus is anointed and empowered by the Holy Spirit (Mk 1:8; Matt 1:20; Lk 1:35; Jn 1:15). Each gospel has a distinct flavour. As each gospel sets out to explain who Jesus is they do not offer a systematic theology - they tell a narrative of which Jesus is the central figure. The Prologue of Mark 1:1-13: i. 1:1 'The Gospel of Jesus Christ' The gospel of Jesus (his teaching especially the kingdom of God) and the gospel about Jesus. It is interesting that much of Mark's narrative focuses on questions – WHO is Jesus? Jesus and the Kingdom of God: The dramatic start of Jesus' ministry in Mark 1:14-15. In him is the kingdom wrapped up. He has the authority to inaugurate the Kingdom of God. Entry into the kingdom is consistently tied to a person's response to Jesus himself. SCOT MCKNIGHT: An upcoming new book The King Jesus Gospel, Zondervan, 2011. 'Did Jesus Preach the Gospel?' Scot argues that the 4 Gospels are 'gospel' – they tell the story of Israel in the light of Jesus. Jesus' message is Self-Centered: he is the fulfillment of Israel's Scriptures. He is Israel's king forming a restored community around himself: he is the fulfillment of wisdom, of the prophets, of the temple, of a new covenant, Israel's messiah, he fulfils Torah itself. The gospel of Jesus is bound up with the kingdom of God: Jesus believed the kingdom of God was breaking into history. Jesus declares that there is a new society in the Land, a new citizenship, and that he is the king; the one in whom all this is happening through and in.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Synoptic Christology
The study of Jesus' identity as presented in the Synoptic Gospels, focusing on shared themes like his status as Messiah, Son of God, and the agent of God's purpose.

### Theme: The Kingdom of God
The theological view that Jesus is the fulfillment of Israel's history, where the kingdom is inaugurated through him and requires a personal response to his authority.

### Theme: Self-Centered Gospel
Scot McKnight's argument that Jesus' message is centered on himself as the fulfillment of Jewish Scripture, acting as Israel's king and the mediator of a new covenant.
