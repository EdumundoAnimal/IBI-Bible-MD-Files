## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an overview of Synoptic Christologies, focusing on how the Gospels portray Jesus. It identifies Morna Hooker's four themes shared across the Gospels: Jesus' divine origin, his role as the Messiah, his status as the Son of God, and his empowerment by the Holy Spirit. The text also examines the Gospel of Mark, emphasizing Jesus' role as the Son of God and Son of Man. Further analysis covers Jesus' relationship with the Kingdom of God, arguing that the Kingdom is centered on Jesus' own person. Finally, it outlines Scot McKnight's perspectives from his work 'The King Jesus Gospel,' positing that the Gospels tell the story of Israel through the fulfillment of Jesus' life, death, and resurrection, noting that Jesus' preaching is self-centered and focuses on the establishment of a new community.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Synoptic Christology
The study of how the Gospel writers portray the person and work of Jesus, acknowledging that while each has a distinct flavor, they collectively communicate Jesus' identity through narratives rather than systematic theology.

### Theme: Kingdom of God
The theological argument that the Kingdom of God is inextricably linked to the person of Jesus, who has the authority to inaugurate it and requires a personal response from individuals.

### Theme: Fulfillment of Israel
The perspective, highlighted by Scot McKnight, that the Gospel is the story of Israel reaching its climax in Jesus, who fulfills the prophets, the temple, the covenant, and the Torah.
