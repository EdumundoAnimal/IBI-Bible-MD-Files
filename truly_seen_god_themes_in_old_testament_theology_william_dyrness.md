# Truly Seen God: Themes in Old Testament Theology
**Author:** [[William Dyrness]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document is a theological examination of 'The Self-Revelation of God' within the Old Testament, contained within the book 'Truly Seen God'. The text explores how God reveals Himself through historical acts, names, and personal encounters. It discusses key instances such as the calling of Abraham, the Exodus, and the covenantal relationship established with Israel. A significant portion of the text analyzes the nature of divine revelation, emphasizing that God's self-disclosure is not merely propositional but redemptive, aiming to foster a personal relationship and call humanity to obedience. The author references various scholars, including von Rad, Pannenberg, and Childs, to contextualize these theological concepts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemptive Revelation
God's self-revelation in the Old Testament is not abstract or solely propositional; it is a redemptive act aimed at restoring a broken relationship and guiding humanity toward obedience.

### Theme: Anthropomorphism and Divine Transcendence
The text balances the 'distant' transcendence of God with 'near' concrete appearances, noting that God uses human-like attributes and direct encounters to make His nature known to people.

### Theme: Covenantal Relationship
Divine revelation is fundamentally tied to the establishment of covenantal bonds, where God makes Himself known as a personal partner who demands faithfulness and provides for the sins of His people.
