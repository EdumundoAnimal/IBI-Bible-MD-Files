# Corinthians, First Letter to
**Author:** [[Wendell Willis]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article provides an academic overview of the First Letter to the Corinthians, detailing its historical, social, and theological context. It examines the city of Corinth as a backdrop, Paul's relationship with the Corinthian church, the occasion for the letter, and the core issues Paul addressed, such as factionalism, moral controversies, liturgical practices, and resurrection theology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Occasion and Provenance
The letter was written by Paul from Ephesus to the church in Corinth to address reports of internal factionalism and specific moral and theological questions raised by the congregation.

### Theme: Social Dynamics
Corinthian society was marked by social hierarchies, cultic practices, and a culture of patronage, which directly influenced the conflicts regarding leadership and communal behavior within the church.

### Theme: Theological Conflicts
Key theological disputes involved the nature of leadership (Paul, Apollos, Cephas), proper conduct in worship, marriage and divorce, the use of spiritual gifts, and the physical reality of the resurrection.
