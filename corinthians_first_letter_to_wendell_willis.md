**Author:** [[Wendell Willis]]

The document provides an academic overview of the First Letter to the Corinthians. It covers historical context, including the city of Corinth's archaeological significance, the arrival of Christianity, and the dating of the letter (likely between 53-55 A.D.). It explores the authorship of Paul and Sosthenes, the occasion of the letter—addressing factions, moral issues, and social tensions within the church—and analyzes the major themes, including apostolic authority, church divisions, marriage, food sacrificed to idols, the resurrection, and spiritual gifts. It also discusses the rhetorical structure and the scholarly debate regarding the letter's unity.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Apostolic Authority and Factionalism
Paul addresses the emergence of rival groups (followers of Paul, Apollos, and Cephas) and defends his role as the founder and teacher against local challenges to his authority.

### Theme: Social and Ethical Issues in the Early Church
The letter serves as a response to specific concerns including incestuous relationships, civil litigation among believers, marriage norms, eating food offered to idols, and appropriate decorum during worship.

### Theme: Theology of Resurrection and Community
Paul balances the theological necessity of the resurrection with practical applications, emphasizing unity and the proper usage of spiritual gifts to resolve communal disorder.
