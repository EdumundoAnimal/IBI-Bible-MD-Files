# Corinthians, First Letter to
**Author:** [[Wendell Willis]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The letter known as 1 Corinthians is one of Paul's canonical letters, addressing a community in Corinth marked by internal dissent and complex social dynamics. Written from Ephesus, the letter serves to address a range of pastoral concerns, including factionalism, marriage, food offered to idols, and the resurrection. Scholarly consensus generally dates the letter to approximately 53-55 A.D. The text reveals a troubled relationship between Paul and the church, with recurring questions regarding his apostolic authority and the presence of various factions identified by allegiance to specific leaders such as Paul, Apollos, and Cephas. Major themes include the rhetoric of unity, the regulation of charismatic gifts, and the addressing of moral irregularities such as incest and legal disputes within the community. The letter is structurally complex, blending direct admonition with theological teaching, ultimately aiming to restore order in a city characterized by its cosmopolitanism and cultural diversity.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Apostolic Authority
Paul's ongoing struggle to assert his role as an apostle against challenges from factions within the Corinthian church that questioned his legitimacy and teaching.

### Theme: Communal Unity
The central rhetorical strategy of the letter, aiming to resolve internal divisions and party loyalties that had fragmented the church.

### Theme: Practical Theology
The application of Christian principles to specific socio-cultural issues including marriage, legal litigation, idolatry, gender roles, and the exercise of spiritual gifts.

### Theme: Eschatological Hope
The theological framework provided by the doctrine of the resurrection, which Paul uses to address both the moral conduct and the future hope of the believers.
