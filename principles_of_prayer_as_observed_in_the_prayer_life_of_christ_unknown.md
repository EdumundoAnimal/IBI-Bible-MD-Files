# Principles of prayer as observed in the prayer life of Christ
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Principles of prayer as observed in the prayer life of Christ

In surveying the prayer life of Christ I will highlight several key principles of prayer that can be applied to all believers. I will do this through exploring Jesus' practice of prayer and teaching on prayer. In Jesus' prayer for His disciples in John 17 we see that His main request for His disciples is that, "they may be one as we are one ... Father, just as you are in me and I am in you" (v 11c, 21b). In John 13-17 we see that this means that God wants us to have a spiritually symbiotic relationship of love, obedience and dependence on the Father, through being "in" Christ (14:6-11, 15, 19-21, 23; 15:5-10; 16:27; 17:11, 21-24, 26), restoring mankind's perfect relationship with God that was broken in The Fall. Jesus Himself speaks of this close relationship of absolute trust, dependence, and devotion in Matthew 11:27, and John 5:19-20,30; 17:10-11, 1. and His description of listening and looking to the Father in everything, talking to Him and truly knowing Him, depicts a lot of what our relationship with God in prayer should be like. Jesus' practice of prayer also demonstrates the reality of this relationship. Luke 5:15-16 shows Jesus' priority in spending time with His Father, as He withdraws from the crowds following Him (and all their needs) in order to pray. In Matthew 14:13 we see Jesus after John the Baptist's death trying to get away from the crowds to be alone (presumably by). When Jesus chooses to postpone this to heal and feed the people (v14-21) it is for less than a day and at the first opportunity (v 22-23) He immediately withdraws again to spend several hours in prayer. We also see that Jesus spent long periods of time in prayer, and had the wisdom in choosing solitary times and places to pray, to avoid being disturbed. He made a real effort to be alone with the Father even when opportunities were scarce, and, as we see in Mark 1:32-35 and Matthew 4:1-11, He often sacrificed basic human needs such as sleep and food in order to spend more quality time alone with Him. We also see in these accounts the private, solitary nature of much of Jesus' prayer life, 2. (compare Matthew 6:5-6), which also allowed His prayers to be intensely emotional, personal and sincere (Matthew 26:39, Hebrews 5:7). Prayer was crucial at key turning points in Jesus' ministry. Forty days of fasting (and presumably prayer) follow His initial calling (Matthew 3:13 - 4:11), whilst prayer also surrounds other important events (Matthew 14:1-23; Luke 6:12-16; 9:18-28; 22:39-46). The importance of this practice can be seen in the clear guidance, renewed vision, and direction, which the assertiveness of Jesus displays after He has heard from His Father (Luke 4:43; Luke 6:12-13 & Mark 3:14-15). We see the power of prayer in overcoming temptation in Jesus' prayer for Peter in Luke 22:31-32 (answered in Luke 24:12; Acts 1:15-22; and Acts 2:14-41) and in His exhortation to pray in Matthew 26:41, and this is also displayed when Jesus is tempted in Matthew 16:22-23; Luke 4:42-43; Luke 4:1-13; and John 6:15. The clearest example of this overcoming power of prayer however is demonstrated in Gethsemane as Jesus perseveres in surrendering His will afresh to the Father, "yet not my will but yours be done," (Luke22:42b), and we see this process in action through comparing the wording of Matthew 26:39b and 26:42, 44. This last account shows us how prayer helps in conforming our wills to God's perfect will for us. Jesus' prayers were heard because of His "reverent submission," (Hebrews 5:7), and Jesus clearly taught that loving God means to obey Him (John 14:15; 15:10). We also see this theme in The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6), which gives us a very clear perspective on what God views as His priorities in prayer.

This prayer firstly shows us that our relationship with God should be thoroughly grounded in worship, and the giving of reverence, praise and glory to God (Matthew 6: 9, 13c (some manuscripts only)). This fundamental focus on God is also seen in the Greek translation of the word for prayer (προσεύχομαι) found in Matthew, which includes the meaning, "to pray to God, that is, supplicate, worship." 3. The rest of the prayer is then primarily concerned with obedience to God. V 10 is a direct call for this obedience to become universal and absolute on earth, yet this shows us where the prayer life of Christ fundamentally differs from our own. Christ, empowered by prayer, was totally obedient to the Father (John 4:34; 17:4) and never sinned (2Corinthians 5:21) hence never needed forgiveness for sin. We conversely have all sinned (Romans 3:23) and this blocks our relationship with God. Jesus therefore shows us the way (John 14:6) to restored relationship and obedience when He says "give us our daily bread," (Matthew 6:11) in other words: give us Jesus, "the bread of life" (John 6:31-35). He provided this through His blood shed on the cross but, as He teaches elsewhere, this requires from us a humble and penitent attitude, honestly confessing our sins before God (Luke 18:9-14). Only then, through faith, can we be forgiven and transformed (Matthew 6:12 and 1 John 1:8-10) and cry out to God effectively for deliverance from future rebellion (Matthew 6:13). This great prayer of faith and intercession for a return to obedience to God through Christ's finished work also encourages us to intercession. This should be done with perseverance and all boldness (Matthew 26:40-41; Luke 11:5-13 & 18:1-8; Ephesians 6:18), and with faith in God (Mark 11:22-24) as we also pray for more labourers for the harvest (Matthew 9:36-38). We have seen how God's desire for us to be in intimate relationship with Him is demonstrated through Jesus' teaching on prayer and the priority He gave to this relationship, which gave Him the ability to be remain in the Father's will in the most testing of times. However, for us, this relationship is only possible through faith in the atoning sacrifice of Jesus which removes our sin and gives us the, "confidence to enter the holy places by the blood of Jesus, by the new and living way that he opened for us" (Hebrews 10:19-20a). Our response to this should be to, "draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith" (Hebrews 10:22a), and to seize hold, "of the eternal life to which you were called" (1Timothy 6:12b); which is the enormous privilege of knowing, "you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent" (John 17:3b).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Symbiotic Relationship with God
The text argues that Jesus' prayer life models a 'spiritually symbiotic relationship of love, obedience and dependence on the Father' intended for all believers, restoring the relationship broken by the Fall.

### Theme: Prayer as Conforming the Will
The document asserts that prayer serves as a mechanism for conforming human will to God's perfect will, citing Jesus' example in Gethsemane as the primary model for 'reverent submission'.

### Theme: Prayer and Obedience
The author links prayer directly to obedience, noting that while Christ was perfectly obedient, human prayer must involve confession and faith in Christ's sacrifice to overcome the barrier of sin.
