# The Epistle to the Romans
**Author:** [[Leon Morris]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly commentary on the Epistle to the Romans, specifically focusing on Romans 7:1-25. The author, Leon Morris, examines the theological complexities of the relationship between the Law, sin, and the human condition. He explores various interpretations of the 'I' in the text, discussing whether it refers to the apostle Paul's pre-conversion life, his present experience, or a universal description of the unregenerate or regenerate human. The text delves into the paradox of the law: while it is good and holy, it exposes sin and leads to a struggle for the individual who is 'sold as a slave to sin.' Morris discusses the nuances of the 'old way of the Spirit' versus the 'old way of the written code' and addresses scholarly debates concerning Pauline theology.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Function of the Law
Morris argues that the Law is inherently good and holy, but its function is to reveal sin and human inadequacy. It is not a means to salvation, but a mirror that proves human helplessness in the face of sinful desire.

### Theme: The Interpretation of the 'I' in Romans 7
A major scholarly debate regarding whether the autobiographical 'I' in Romans 7 represents Paul's pre-conversion Jewish experience, his current experience as a Christian, or a stylized rhetorical device representing humanity apart from Christ.

### Theme: The Conflict between Flesh and Spirit
The struggle of the believer who, while redeemed, still faces the reality of indwelling sin, illustrating the tension between the desire to do good and the inability to perform it without the enabling power of the Spirit.
