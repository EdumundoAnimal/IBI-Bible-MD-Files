# The Epistle to the Romans
**Author:** [[Leon Morris]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly commentary and Bible study resource on the Epistle to the Romans, specifically chapters 7 and the surrounding doctrinal discussions on justification, faith, and the law. It explores the theological tension between the unregenerate and regenerate states, the nature of 'good works' vs. divine grace, and the concept of 'fear of God' as a posture of awe rather than terror. The text includes specific chiasm charts illustrating the structure of Romans 3 and 4, and emphasizes that salvation comes solely through faith in Jesus Christ, excluding human boasting or reliance on one's own righteousness.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Justification by Faith
The central argument that human works, adherence to the law, or religious efforts cannot merit salvation. Justification is a gift of grace received solely through faith in Jesus Christ.

### Theme: Critique of Self-Righteousness
The text warns against the 'good works' that stem from a desire for human glory or merit. Such works are described as 'sour' or 'filthy rags' when they replace reliance on Christ.

### Theme: The Role of the Law
The law serves to reveal sin and human inadequacy rather than providing a mechanism for salvation, leading the sinner to total dependence on the Savior.
