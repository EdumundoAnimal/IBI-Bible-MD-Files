# Lesson 7. God's Son: Our Sacrifice
**Author:** [[Paul F. Perry]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Introduction

In Chapter Seven of Hebrews the preacher demonstrated the superiority of Jesus' priesthood (to the Old / Levitical) by comparing him to Melchizadek. This comparison showed the distinctive character of the priesthood exercised by God's Son. However, there is a significant aspect of Jesus' priestly ministry, which is not illumined by the comparison with Melchizadek. That aspect concerns Jesus' presentation of his life to God as an association with sacrifice. If this sacrificial aspect of Jesus' ministry is to be emphasized a different comparison must be developed. The preacher now does this in Hebrews 8:1-9:28. He compares the priestly action of Jesus with the action of the Levitical high print on the annual Day of Atonement. On that solemn occasion the high priest entered the Most High Place of the tabernacle and stood in the awesome presence of God to make atonement for the sins of the people. With this background we can gain an overview of the next two chapters. There are four sections that we should be aware of as we look at these two chapters. This will help provide a rapid overview of the text. 1. 8:1-5 Ministering - Priest 2. 8:6-13 Mediator - New Covenant 3. 9:1-10 4. 9:11-28 - Care of Message

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Superiority of Christ's Priesthood
The document argues that Christ's ministry is superior to the Levitical priesthood because he serves in the heavenly tabernacle rather than an earthly one, and his sacrifice is eternal and unrepeatable.

### Theme: New Covenant vs. Old Covenant
The text posits that the Old Covenant is obsolete and aging, having been replaced by a New Covenant based on better promises, which establishes a direct relationship with God.

### Theme: Sacrificial Atonement
The author contrasts the limited, repeated sacrifices of the Levitical high priest on the Day of Atonement with the singular, effective sacrifice of Christ, which provides decisive cleansing of the inner person.
