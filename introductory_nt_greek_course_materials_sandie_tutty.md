### Lessons 1-2: Present Indicative Active Verbs

Each Verb has: Tense: Past, Present or Future. Person: First Person - I / we, Second Person - You, Third Person - he / she / it / they. Number: Singular or Plural. Greek verbs are formed in one word that incorporates all of these components. Every Verb has a Stem (the first part that does not change) and an Ending (the second part that does change). Person & No.: 1st Person Sing., Greek: λυω, Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-ō, Translation English Simple: I loosen, English Continuous: I am loosening. 2nd Person Sing., Greek: λυεις, Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-eis, Translation English Simple: You loosen, English Continuous: You are loosening. 3rd Person Sing., Greek: λυει, Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-ei, Translation English Simple: He loosens, English Continuous: He is loosening. 1st Person Plural, Greek: λυομεν, Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-omen, Translation English Simple: We loosen, English Continuous: We are loosening. 2nd Person Plural, Greek: λυετε, Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-ete, Translation English Simple: You loosen, English Continuous: You are loosening. 3rd Person Plural, Greek: λυουσι[ν], Transliteration Pronunciation: lu-ousi(n), Translation English Simple: They loosen, English Continuous: They are loosening. Questions: If the verb is written as a question (i.e. the question mark [;] appears after it, it must be translated as a question. E.g. λυω; means "Do I loosen?" or "Am I loosening?"

### -εω Verbs

Some Greek verbs have a stem that ends with the letter ε. When the stem ends with this letter, certain contractions take place. Rules of Contraction: 1. ε + ε => ει. 2. ε + ο => ου. 3. ε + long vowel (η or ω) or diphthong => ε drops out. To Form These Verbs: Take the 1st person singular, which you will learn in its uncontracted form, e.g. φιλεω. Remove the -ω to get the verb stem, e.g. φιλε-. Select the required ending: -ω, -εις, -ει, -ομεν, -ετε, or -ουσι(ν). Apply the rules of contraction. Example: φιλε + ω => φιλω (phil-ō). φιλε + ει => φιλει (phil-ei). φιλε + εις => φιλεις (phil-eis). φιλε + ομεν => φιλουμεν (phil-oumen). φιλε + ετε => φιλειτε (phil-eite). φιλε + ουσι(ν) => φιλουσι(ν) (phil-ousin).
