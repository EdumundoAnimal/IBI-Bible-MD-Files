# The First Seven Ecumenical Councils (325-787): Their History and Theology
**Author:** [[Leo Donald Davis, S.J.]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an in-depth historical and theological analysis of the First Seven Ecumenical Councils, specifically focusing on the Council of Nicaea I in 325 AD. It examines the Trinitarian crisis, the theological challenges posed by Arius, the role of Emperor Constantine in convening the council, and the subsequent formulation of the Nicene Creed. The text also delves into church discipline, the emergence of ecclesiastical structures, and the impact of early theological controversies on the definition of Christian orthodoxy.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Trinitarian Theology
The central theological development involving the definition of the relationship between the Father and the Son, specifically addressing the consubstantiality (homoousios) of the Son with the Father.

### Theme: Ecclesiastical Authority
The evolution of church governance, discipline, and the structural organization of the church, including canon law and the role of bishops.

### Theme: Church and State Relations
The intersection of imperial power, led by Constantine, and the doctrinal decision-making processes within the Christian church.

### Theme: Biblical Hermeneutics
The tension between inherited Jewish-Christian interpretations of scripture and the emergence of Hellenistic philosophical approaches to understanding Christian truth.
