# Christ and Consumerism: New Testament Voices for an Addicted Society
**Author:** [[Thorsten Moritz]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article analyzes the relationship between consumerist ideology and New Testament teachings. It explores how early Christians navigated issues of wealth, possession, and social status within a world dominated by Greco-Roman political and economic structures. The author argues that Christ's kingdom implies a reversal of worldly values, challenging contemporary tendencies toward materialism and self-serving upward mobility. Key concepts include the symbolic nature of possessions, the danger of idolatry through consumption, and the 'limited goods' worldview of the first century. The text emphasizes that believers are called to be 'salt and light', practicing sacrificial giving and prioritizing the needs of the poor over personal accumulation, thereby reflecting God's creational values in an addicted society.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Reversal of Values
Christ's kingdom promotes an inverted social order where the poor and the outcast are prioritized over those who have achieved worldly success, directly challenging the accumulation of wealth.

### Theme: Symbolism of Possessions
Material goods are viewed as 'living symbols' that define a person's relationship to God and others; consumerism turns these into idols that compete for the human heart.

### Theme: Socio-Economic Context
The article situates New Testament economic teachings within the 'limited goods' framework of the first-century Mediterranean world, which historically viewed upward mobility as a threat to community stability.
