# Christ and Consumerism: New Testament Voices for an Addicted Society
**Author:** [[Thorsten Moritz]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document, "Christ and Consumerism: New Testament Voices for an Addicted Society" by Thorsten Moritz, provides a critical theological analysis of modern consumerism in light of New Testament texts. It examines how early Christian experiences with Roman-era socio-economic conditions and the impending "Kingdom of God" provide a framework for navigating contemporary consumerist values. The author argues that Christ's teaching demands a radical re-evaluation of wealth, material possessions, and social status, urging readers to reject modern consumerist "idolatry" in favor of sacrificial living, generosity, and an eschatological hope that reverses worldly power dynamics.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerism as Idolatry
The author conceptualizes modern consumerism as a form of idolatry that competes with divine allegiance, arguing that material accumulation is often driven by an attempt to find security in things rather than in God.

### Theme: Kingship and Reversal
Central to the text is the idea of the "Kingdom of God" as a counter-cultural reality that actively reverses worldly hierarchies, where the first become last and the marginalized are prioritized.

### Theme: Theological Reappropriation
The author emphasizes the need to carefully interpret New Testament texts within their original socio-historical context to avoid "anachronistic proof-texting" while applying core ethical principles to modern society.
