# Christ and Consumerism: New Testament Voices for an Addicted Society
**Author:** [[Thorsten Moritz]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This scholarly article examines the intersection of New Testament teachings and modern consumer culture, arguing that Christians should adopt a counter-cultural worldview. The text explores themes of biblical authority, the reversal of worldly values, the dangers of idolatry through possessions, and the socio-economic implications of the Gospel in ancient and modern contexts. It emphasizes that Christ's kingship demands a reevaluation of material values and suggests that true discipleship involves 'self-denial' and a commitment to helping the poor, challenging the consumerist pursuit of upward mobility.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Counter-Cultural Worldview
The necessity of rejecting dominant consumerist ideologies in favor of a Christian worldview centered on Christ's kingdom and self-denial.

### Theme: Reversal of Values
Biblical teaching that challenges social hierarchies and the pursuit of status, asserting that the first shall be last and the wealthy shall be humbled.

### Theme: Material Idolatry
The danger of equating possessions with identity or security, which the author categorizes as a form of idolatry that conflicts with Christian faith.

### Theme: Socio-Economic Responsibility
The biblical imperative to provide for the poor and address systemic poverty, transcending simplistic interpretations of material prosperity.
