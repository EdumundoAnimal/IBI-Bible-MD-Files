# Psalm 147
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 147 is analyzed as a cohesive tripartite structure emphasizing God's cosmic governance alongside His particular care for the exiled and broken. The text explores the restoration of Jerusalem in the post-exilic period under Nehemiah, contrasting Yahweh's majesty with the idolatry of human military power (represented by 'horses and legs'). Linguistic analysis covers Hebrew terms like nidḥê (outcasts), aṣṣəḇôṯ (trauma/wounds), ḥesed (covenant fidelity), and dābār (Word). The study synthesizes historical context, canonical intertextuality, and theological reflections from Patristic, Reformation, and modern scholars, positioning the Psalm as a bridge between creation, exile, and Christological fulfillment.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Cosmic Governance vs. Intimate Providence
The argument that the Creator who commands the galaxies and atmospheric cycles is the same God who intimately binds the wounds of the broken-hearted.

### Theme: Polemic Against Imperial Militarism
The text posits that God remains unimpressed by human 'horses' and 'legs' (military/political power), favoring instead those who live in humble dependence on His covenantal love.

### Theme: The Theology of the Word
The Word of God is presented as a dynamic, self-enacting creative and restorative force that moves from creation to the Torah and ultimately to the Incarnation of Christ.
