# A Royal "Waste" of Time
**Author:** [[Marva J. Dawn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the intersection of Christian worship and consumer culture. The author argues that worship is intended to be a counter-cultural act that resists the idolatry of consumerism. It examines how consumerism shapes modern desires, creates habits of selfishness, and creates a sense of instability. The author advocates for the church to exist as a colony of the kingdom, offering a distinct alternative to the world's focus on self-fulfillment, convenience, and materialism. By focusing on intentionality, generosity, and service, the church can foster genuine community and avoid being co-opted by the manipulative tactics of a consumer-driven society.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerism vs. Worship
The author contrasts consumerism—characterized by consumption, selfishness, and instability—with Christian worship, which is defined by generosity, communal participation, and service to God.

### Theme: The Church as a Colony of the Kingdom
The church is called to function as a distinct community, a 'colony' that rejects the prevailing culture of self-creation and materialism in favor of faithfulness to the kingdom of God.

### Theme: Resisting Idolatry
The text identifies the modern obsession with convenience, novelty, and technological consumption as a form of idolatry that Christians must actively resist through disciplined worship and community habits.
