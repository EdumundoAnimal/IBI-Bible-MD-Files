# A Royal 'Waste' of Time
**Author:** [[Marva J. Dawn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the challenge of maintaining genuine Christian worship within a consumer-driven culture. It highlights how consumerism shapes human desires, often leading to a focus on self-fulfillment, personal convenience, and materialism, which conflicts with the counter-cultural call of the Church. The author argues that true worship should be a 'waste' of time in the eyes of a productive, consumer-focused world—a radical act of devotion to God. By examining the impact of technology, market-driven logic, and the loss of traditional communal values, the text advocates for a transformative approach to worship that resists the influence of consumerist ideologies. It emphasizes that the Church must act as a 'colony' of the Kingdom, providing an alternative society where faithfulness and gratitude replace the incessant pursuit of new desires. Practical examples, such as the use of rituals to sanctify daily life and the deliberate rejection of consumerist shortcuts, are provided to illustrate how the Church can foster deeper community and true worship.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerist Influence on Worship
The argument that consumer culture prioritizes individual self-fulfillment and convenience, which undermines the communal and counter-cultural nature of Christian worship.

### Theme: The Church as a Colony
The idea that the Church should function as an alternative society or 'colony' of the Kingdom of God, resisting the surrounding secular, consumer-oriented culture through faithfulness and obedience.

### Theme: Biblical Stewardship and Gratitude
A focus on replacing the consumerist focus on 'more' and 'faster' with biblical principles of stewardship, generosity, and the sanctification of time and resources.
