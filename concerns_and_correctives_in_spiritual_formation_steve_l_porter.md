# Concerns and Correctives in Spiritual Formation
**Author:** [[Steve L. Porter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article addresses common evangelical concerns regarding the contemporary 'spiritual formation' movement. The author categorizes these concerns into several objections, including the fear that spiritual formation is inherently Catholic, New Age, or contrary to the sufficiency of Scripture. The author argues that spiritual formation, when correctly understood as the process of growing in Christlikeness, is a necessary and legitimate aspect of the Christian life. Rather than discarding these practices due to misconceptions, the author proposes a framework for evaluating spiritual disciplines—including grounding them in Scripture, general revelation, and the history of the church. The text concludes by emphasizing that while the movement has legitimate complexities and potential for misuse, it serves a critical role in fostering mature, holy living when firmly rooted in biblical doctrine.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Sufficiency and Integration
The tension between holding Scripture as the sole authority while recognizing that insights into human behavior and growth (psychology, philosophy) can be informed by general revelation.

### Theme: Experiential Spirituality vs. Theological Rigor
The debate over whether spiritual formation over-emphasizes subjective experience at the expense of sound biblical exposition and objective truth.

### Theme: The Nature of Obedience
Moving beyond external behaviorism toward an 'obedience from the heart' driven by the Holy Spirit, reflecting the 'Jesus way'.

### Theme: Evaluating Extra-Biblical Practices
The proposal of a five-fold criterion for evaluating spiritual disciplines, balancing biblical alignment with church history and rational human understanding.
