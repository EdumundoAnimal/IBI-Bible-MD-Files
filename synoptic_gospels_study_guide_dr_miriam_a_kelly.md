# Synoptic Gospels Study Guide
**Author:** [[Dr. Miriam A. Kelly]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides an overview of the Synoptic Gospels (Matthew, Mark, and Luke), exploring their authorship, dates of composition, and themes. It delves into the Synoptic Problem, analyzing the literary relationship between these texts, including the Two-Document Hypothesis and Four-Source Hypothesis. It also discusses the roles of oral tradition and the historical context of Palestine during the time of Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Synoptic Problem
The academic inquiry into the literary interrelationships, similarities, and differences between Matthew, Mark, and Luke, necessitating theories of shared sources.

### Theme: Two-Document/Four-Source Hypothesis
Proposes that Mark was the earliest Gospel and served as a primary source for Matthew and Luke, alongside a hypothetical 'Q' source for shared non-Markan material.

### Theme: Distinct Gospel Emphases
Matthew presents Jesus as the King and Messiah; Mark highlights Jesus as the Servant; Luke emphasizes Jesus as the Son of Man and the Savior of all people.

### Theme: The Role of Oral Tradition
Examines how the early Church preserved the teachings of Jesus through oral transmission before the final composition of the written Gospels.
