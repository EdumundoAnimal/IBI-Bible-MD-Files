# Studies on the Doctrine of the Trinity
**Author:** [[Wayne Grudem, Millard J. Erickson, J. Kenneth Grider]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document consists of excerpts from three systematic theology texts regarding the Doctrine of the Trinity. Wayne Grudem discusses the biblical basis and explains the Trinity as God eternally existing as three persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. Millard J. Erickson provides essential elements of the Trinity, emphasizing monotheism, the deity of each person, the distinction between persons, and divine simplicity. J. Kenneth Grider examines the Trinity as an eternal society and highlights the relationship between the members within the Godhead, noting the subordination in function but equality in essence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Doctrine of the Trinity
The theological assertion that God exists eternally as three distinct persons—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—who are one in essence, deity, and substance.

### Theme: Divine Essence and Persons
The theological distinction that while God is one in being (ousia), there are three distinct subsistences (hypostases) that are not added together but are inseparable.

### Theme: Functional Subordination
The argument that members of the Trinity may take on subordinate roles for specific divine purposes (such as the Son's incarnation) without implying an inequality in their divine essence.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "We may define the doctrine of the Trinity as follows: God eternally exists as three persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and each person is fully God, and there is one God."
*Context Source: Systematic Theology by Wayne Grudem, Page 3*

> "The threeness and the oneness of God are not in the same respect. Although the orthodox interpretation of the Trinity seems contradictory (God is one and yet three), the contradiction is not real, but only apparent."
*Context Source: Christian Theology by Millard J. Erickson, Page 9*

> "The function of one member of the Trinity may for a time be subordinate to one or both of the other members, but that does not mean he is in any way inferior in essence."
*Context Source: Christian Theology by Millard J. Erickson, Page 10*
