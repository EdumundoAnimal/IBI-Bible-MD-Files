# Christian Liberty: A New Testament Perspective
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the concept of Christian liberty from a New Testament perspective. It defines this liberty not as mere self-sufficiency or individual independence, but as a freedom conditioned by faith and expressed through love within the community of believers. The author draws on Pauline theology to argue that true freedom is found in a relationship with God, characterized by the guidance of the Spirit, and balanced by a concern for the community. The document contrasts this perspective with secular notions of liberty, such as Mill's laissez-faire individualism, and emphasizes the role of mutual responsibility in a Christian community.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christian Liberty
Defined as a freedom that is not individualistic but is fundamentally relational, rooted in faith in Christ and the guidance of the Spirit, and expressed through love for others.

### Theme: Community and Responsibility
The argument that liberty within a community is balanced by the responsibility to love one's neighbor, preventing the misuse of freedom as a license for self-pleasing.

### Theme: Pauline Theology
The reliance on the Apostle Paul's teaching that true freedom is the liberation from the 'flesh' and the power of desire, shifting the focus to service within the body of Christ.
