# Temple of Herod
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Herod undertook the restoration of the Temple in its original splendour and traditional arrangements. The buildings were demolished one after another according as the materials for the new structures were available. A host of priests became masons and carpenters and themselves took charge of tearing down and rebuilding the sanctuary, which task was accomplished in eighteen months. Nearly 10,000 workmen were employed on the other buildings. After eight years' labour (10 B.C.) the new edifice was opened for service. But this monument, which in its vast proportions and magnificence rivalled the most beautiful buildings of antiquity and far surpassed even that of Solomon, was completed only in A.D. 62 or 64 (Cf. John 2:20), at that time 18,000 workmen being still employed. For Herod doubled the artificial platform which held the Temple of Zorobabel, enlarging the sacred precincts to the south and especially to the north where the galleries reached as far as the rock of Baris and the Antonia. The Temple with its courts, galleries and porches occupied the whole of the present site of the haram esh sherif, which measures 1070 feet on the north, 1540 on the east, 920 on the south, and 1630 on the west. The Temple of Herod consisted of two courts, an inner and an outer one. The former included all the buildings of the Temple properly so called and was divided into: (1) The Court of the Priests, (2) the Court of Israel; and (3) the Court of the Women. All the space between the inner court and the outer wall of the platform was called the Court of the Gentiles. The Court of the Priests formed a rectangle of one hundred and eighty-seven cubits from east to west and one hundred and thirty-seven cubits from north to south. The sanctuary was reached by a stairway of twelve steps, which terminated in a majestic porch one hundred cubits high and the same in breadth. A door without leaves twenty cubits wide and forth high led into a vestibule eleven cubits wide. The hekal and the debir retained their ancient dimensions in length and breadth, but their height was increased to sixty cubits.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Herodian Architecture
Historical assessment of the scale, construction period, and architectural dimensions of the Second Temple renovation under Herod the Great.

### Theme: Temple Layout
The spatial organization of the Temple complex, dividing it into distinct courts (Priests, Israel, Women, Gentiles) and specific sanctuary features like the hekal and debir.

### Theme: Biblical Correlation
Connecting historical records (Josephus, Tacitus) with scriptural references such as the timing mentioned in John 2:20 regarding the building's completion.
