# Christology in the Making
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a scholarly analysis of New Testament Christology, specifically focusing on the origins of the Doctrine of the Incarnation. The text examines how early Christian thought navigated concepts of Spirit, angelology, and divine intermediaries in the context of Judaism. It explores the shift from Adam Christology to pre-existence-incarnation interpretations, the identity of Jesus as an angel or as the Spirit, and the eventual development of the Doctrine of the Incarnation. The author argues that early Christian writers did not view Jesus simply as an angel but developed a distinct understanding of his relationship with God, characterized by his post-resurrection role and spiritual indwelling.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Spirit-Christology
The author explores how early Christianity defined the relationship between Jesus and the Spirit, often characterizing Jesus as a personified manifestation or agent of the Spirit, particularly in his earthly ministry and post-resurrection role.

### Theme: Angelology in Pre-Christian Judaism
An analysis of the role of angels and divine intermediaries in Jewish thought, serving as a backdrop for understanding how early Christians distinguished Jesus from purely angelic beings while still utilizing contemporary conceptual frameworks.

### Theme: The Doctrine of Incarnation
The text investigates the evolutionary process of the Incarnation doctrine, tracking the development from early 'Adam Christology' to the later 'Logos' and 'Son' terminologies.
