# Christology in the Making
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text explores the origins of the Doctrine of the Incarnation, specifically addressing whether early Christian theology viewed Christ through the lens of 'Spirit' or 'Angel'. It provides a critical inquiry into New Testament Christology, examining the transition from a 'Last Adam' theology to a more developed pre-existence Christology. It also investigates the role of intertestamental Judaism, the concept of the 'Spirit of God' in the Old Testament, and the evolving understanding of Jesus as the 'man of the Spirit' versus a pre-existent angelic figure. The document concludes that the New Testament writers largely resisted identifying Christ as a personal heavenly being or angel, instead favoring a Christology rooted in the Resurrection and the 'Spirit' as the medium of divine encounter.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christology and Angelology
An investigation into whether early Christians categorized Jesus as a pre-existent angelic figure or as a distinct manifestation of the Spirit/God, ultimately concluding that they largely rejected identifying Jesus as a created angel.

### Theme: Last Adam vs. Pre-existence
Analysis of the transition in Pauline and early Christian thought from defining Christ in terms of his role as the 'Last Adam' to the theological development of his pre-existence as the Incarnate Word.

### Theme: The Spirit of God in Judaism and Christianity
The evolution of the concept of the 'Spirit of God' from its Old Testament roots as a 'power' or 'force' to its role in the New Testament as the defining medium of Jesus's earthly ministry and the believer's experience of the resurrected Christ.

### Theme: Post-Resurrection Christology
The argument that the central defining point for early Christian Christology was the resurrection, which transformed the understanding of Jesus from a charismatic prophet into the 'life-giving Spirit'.
