# The Historical Reliability of John's Gospel
**Author:** [[Craig L. Blomberg]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a comprehensive scholarly analysis of the Gospel of John, specifically addressing its historical reliability, date, provenance, and literary genre. It includes a detailed commentary on the 'Vine and the Branches' passage (John 15), examining the theological concepts of abiding in Christ, bearing fruit, and the distinction between true and false believers. The text also explores the relationship between John and the Synoptic Gospels, discussing how John's narrative structure and theological focus differ yet complement the other accounts. Key academic arguments concerning authorship and the dating of the Gospel are evaluated, alongside discussions of parables, exorcisms, and discourses within the text.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Historical Reliability
An analysis of the authenticity of the Fourth Gospel, balancing its unique literary structure and theological depth against the Synoptic Gospels, while defending it against claims that it is merely a product of late-century theological evolution.

### Theme: The Vine and the Branches
An exposition of John 15, emphasizing the metaphor of Christ as the true vine and believers as branches. It focuses on the necessity of 'abiding' in Christ to produce spiritual fruit and the judgment awaiting those who do not remain in the vine.

### Theme: Authorship and Provenance
Arguments regarding the origin of John's Gospel, suggesting an Asian church setting (Ephesus) rather than Jerusalem, and defending the traditional view of Johannine authorship against theories of late, multi-staged editing.

### Theme: Interlocking between John and the Synoptics
An investigation into how the Gospel of John complements the Synoptic Gospels, providing missing context to historical events and teachings while utilizing different literary forms.
