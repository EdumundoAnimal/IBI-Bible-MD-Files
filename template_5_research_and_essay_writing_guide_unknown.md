## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Research Definition and Tips

Research is a process of discovery it is investigation. Research can be defined as a method of study that, through careful investigation of all evidence bearing on a definable issue, topic or problem, arrives at a solution, and that to research is, to collect, organise, evaluate and present data. This process involves analysis and synthesis, for research is more than a compilation of information. (Vyhmeister, 1) Nancy Jean Vyhmeister. Quality Research Papers for Students of Religion and Theology. (Grand Rapids MI: Zondervan, 2001). Tips to get you started: Systematic search, adequate information, objective knowledge, specific topic. Essay format, Introduction, main body and a conclusion, with proper referencing. Correct use of language, including spelling and grammar. Clarity of expression Logical organisation Appropriate use of headings or sections Correct formatting To Begin your Essay you need to: Define issue, problem or topic Identify approach Prepare preliminary Bibliography Design a methodology Draft working outline

### Tips for essays continued

Evaluate: In an evaluation you are expected to present a careful appraisal of the issue, stressing both advantages and limitations. Evaluation implies authoritative and, to a lesser degree, personal appraisal of both contributions and limitations. Discuss: The term discuss, directs you to examine, analyse carefully, and present considerations for and against regarding the issue or topic. Analysis: It is examining the evidence piece by piece. It demands considering what each piece is, what it does, and how it fits the other pieces. Like a small boy taking an alarm clock apart to find out what makes it tick.

### Synthesis and Writing Standards

Synthesis: It begins with two or more ideas or systems. After studying how each one works, you put the parts of the old together to form a new one. The small boy is putting parts of three clocks together to make one functioning alarm piece. Analysis and synthesis are mental activities. They may appear in writing, but first take place in the researchers head. In a paper, analysis usually appears in the body of the paper, while synthesis is more evident in the development of a model or program, or in the conclusion at the end of the paper. (Vyhmeister, 47). Knowledge, Comprehension: Level 4 Application and Analysis: Level 5 Evaluation and Synthesis: Level 6 Make it easy for the person reading essay. Do not make silly errors Answer the question Stick to the point Do not use devotional language No jokes, colloquialisms, abbreviations (except for biblical books), euphemisms etc. Review work, get it proof read if possible. Do have a clear thesis, and ask: is it thesis centred, organised. Begin and end each paragraph in your own words Have you introduced your sources? Are they diverse and critical?

### Sources and Evaluation

Primary sources: Vatican 11 Documents, Bible. Secondary sources: Books that comment or interpret primary sources. Using primary sources can minimise the distance from the original. Reference Material: Concordance, Dictionaries, Encyclopaedias, etc. Reading and Evaluating Sources: (Vyhmeister, 51-56) Who is the author? Who is the publisher? Is the source in question an article or journal and is it a recognised journal in this field? Pneuma, JEPTA, The Furrow, Irish Theological Quarterly, etc. Why (not) articles, journals and not books? Why (not) the Internet? IE: Cyber Journal for Pentecostal Studies, Asian Journal of Pentecostal Studies. What is the date of publication? What is the author's purpose? Read the introduction. Is it a popular or serious or scholarly work? What style of writing is it? Is there a bibliography? How extensive is it? Are there footnotes? In the footnotes and bibliography, are the works recent and the author's specialists in their fields? What is the tone of the writing? Is it sober and objective? Is it emotional? Is the style of writing clear and easy to read? Has this source been translated from another language? In any Essay the reader is looking for: Factual accuracy Critical awareness and interaction Logical development Evidence of wide reading And Good English

### Transitional Words and Quotations

Examples of Transitional Words: Accordingly, Again, Also, Thus, Next, However, In like manner, On the whole, Last, To sum up, Likewise, Moreover, In addition, Second, Third, Finally, First, Further, And, But, Too, Then, Therefore, Meanwhile, Besides, To conclude (Vyhmeister, 96-97). Examples of how to Introduce Quotations: Accepts, Admits, Adds, Affirms, Agrees, Argues, Asks, Believes, Confirms, Declares, Defends, Denies, Describes, Discusses, Expresses, Indicates, Mentions, Objects, Opposes, Points out, Points to, Portrays, Recalls, Reports, Reveals, States, Suggests
