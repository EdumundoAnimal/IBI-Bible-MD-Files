# Hermeneutics: Epistles and Parables
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document covers two major study topics in biblical hermeneutics: the study of the Epistles and the study of Parables. Part 1 focuses on Epistles, particularly 1 Corinthians 11:17-34, emphasizing literary, historical, and theological context. It defines epistles as occasional documents and outlines a methodology for interpreting them. Part 2 contains notes on parables, specifically looking at Luke 10:25-37 (The Good Samaritan) and notes on the Feast of Tabernacles and historical/religious context.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hermeneutical Methodology for Epistles
The necessity of understanding an epistle by reading the whole letter in one setting, identifying the author/recipient context, and determining the original versus cross-cultural application.

### Theme: Communal vs. Personal Faith
Analysis of 1 Cor 11:17-34 suggests that communion is a communal act, and misinterpretation often leads to an overly individualistic focus on 'self-examination' rather than addressing communal neglect.

### Theme: Parables as Teaching Tools
The interpretation of parables (like the Good Samaritan) as tools involving satire, taunts, or proverbs intended to challenge the hearer's current application of knowledge (law) into action (mercy).
