# Thinking as a Christian about money and consumerism
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document outlines a biblical theology regarding money, possessions, and giving, specifically targeting the challenge of living counter-culturally in a consumer-driven society. It emphasizes that God is the owner of all things, making humans stewards. Work is presented as a blessing and a godly responsibility. The text warns that money and possessions should not be the goal of life, as they can breed discontent, pride, and idolatry. It emphasizes that justice is a core reflection of God's character, particularly towards the poor and marginalized. Practical steps for Christians include critical thinking about consumer culture, maintaining a budget, avoiding debt, practicing simplicity, serving others, and maintaining community accountability.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Stewardship and Ownership
The biblical understanding that God owns everything, and humans are merely stewards with a responsibility to honor Him through their work and use of wealth.

### Theme: Counter-Cultural Living
The necessity for Christians to actively resist the values of modern consumerism, which promotes selfishness, discontent, and materialism, in favor of a life centered on service and generosity.

### Theme: Justice for the Marginalized
God's character is fundamentally tied to the defense of the poor and the oppressed, and Christians are called to emulate this by prioritizing the needs of others over their own.

### Theme: Spiritual Danger of Wealth
Wealth and possessions are characterized as spiritual hazards that often breed pride, complacency, and a lack of trust in God, serving as potential idols that distract from the kingdom.
