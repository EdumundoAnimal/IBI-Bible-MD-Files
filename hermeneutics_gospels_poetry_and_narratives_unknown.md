### The Psalms and Lamentations

OT Poetry: Examples: Psalm 3, Lamentations 3. Israel's Worship: The Psalms. What are the Psalms? What different types of Psalms are there? Example: Psalm 3. What are the Psalms? The book of Psalms exposes the pulsing heart of Israel. In it we find the life-blood of the faith of God's people. In it are Israel's songs of faith, expressing joy and confidence in God and God's chosen leaders. In it are Israel's prayers out of times of despair, tragedy, and alienation. By studying the book of Psalms we put ourselves in closest touch with the ebb and flow of the people's relationship to God. The book of Psalms served as the hymnbook and prayerbook of Israel from early times, and remains the voice of God's people in contemporary Judaism and Christianity. What kinds of Psalms are there? Complaint (or lament) - Individual or communal. Thanksgiving - Expressions of gratitude for what God has done - personal or communal. Hymn - Uses language of praise to extol who God is. The Psalms and worship... Note that the Psalms allow for a broad range of emotions and responses to God, depending on circumstances and situations. God is not afraid of his people being honest and authentic in their relationship with him. These songs and poems were so meaningful that they have continued to be used by people for over 2000 years to help relate to God. Eugene Peterson on the Psalms: Athanasius said, "Most scripture speak to us; the Psalms speak for us." Peterson goes on to say: 'And oh, how they speak. They don't simply say, "Yes, God I agree. No, they argue and complain, they lament and they praise, they deny and declaim, they thank and they sing. On one page they accuse God of betraying and abandoning them and on the next they turn cartwheels and hallelujahs. ...'

### Gospels Overview

Gospels. Gospel literally means 'good news', from the greek euangelion. The gospels are theological biographies - they tell narratives and teachings from the life of Jesus, but their main purpose is a theological one. Gospels. God has given to us what we know about Jesus in the Gospels - so we have to make use of them. There are four of them: this can be seen as a problem, but it doesn't have to be. All of the gospels were told from different vantage points, probably with different audiences in mind. Gospels: Historical Context. Historical context: first-century (2nd Temple) Judaism - vital that you are aware of the Judaism of Jesus' day. IVP Background Commentary, Dictionary of Jesus & the Gospels; Lots of stuff in library on first century Judaism. Gospels: Literary context. Think Vertically: know the structure of the book and where your passage fits within that structure. Look to the book's meaning and structure BEFORE comparing with other versions of stories/sayings - honour the context the author put it in. Gospels: Literary context. Think Horizontally: be aware of what the other Gospels say about a particular saying or event - not to fill in story, but because differences often give us reason for it being told at all. Know the 'book context' for each Gospel, what makes them distinct. Gospels: Theological Context. What does the text tell us about Jesus Christ and his work in the world? ## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Special Hermeneutics
The document distinguishes between 'general hermeneutics' (principles applicable to all biblical literature) and 'special hermeneutics' (principles specific to certain genres like narratives, poetry, parables, and epistles).

### Theme: Interpreting OT Narratives
The text argues that OT narratives do not usually teach doctrine directly but illustrate it. It emphasizes that characters are not necessarily role models, that narratives are not always explicit about moral judgments, and that they have limited, specific purposes.

### Theme: Lament and Honesty in Worship
The document highlights the role of Psalms and Lamentations in allowing believers to express the full range of human emotion—including frustration, anger, and despair—to God, framing this as an act of authentic relationship.

### Theme: Reading the Gospels
The document suggests reading the Gospels 'vertically' (understanding the specific structure and theological purpose of an individual Gospel) and 'horizontally' (comparing accounts across different Gospels to understand why specific differences exist).
