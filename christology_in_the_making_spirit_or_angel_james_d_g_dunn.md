# Christology in the Making: Spirit or Angel?
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The provided text is an excerpt from James D. G. Dunn's 'Christology in the Making'. It investigates the origins of the doctrine of the Incarnation, specifically focusing on the relationship between Jesus and the 'Spirit' or 'Angel' in early Christian and pre-Christian Jewish contexts. The author examines how the Holy Spirit was understood in the Old Testament and intertestamental Judaism, and how the earliest Christians conceptualized the divinity of Jesus not as a pre-existent entity descending to earth, but as an exalted figure established through resurrection. The document systematically addresses the New Testament evidence, evaluating how various writers (Paul, the Synoptics, and the Johannine tradition) navigated the tension between Jewish monotheism and the emerging conviction of Christ's divine identity.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Origins
Dunn argues that New Testament Christology was primarily formulated in terms of resurrection and exaltation rather than a pre-existent metaphysical descent, contrasting it with later Gnostic or Patristic developments.

### Theme: Spirit-Christology
The thesis that early Christians identified the risen Jesus primarily through his relationship with the Holy Spirit, which acted as the empowering agency of his ministry and subsequent glorification.

### Theme: Polemic against Angel-Christology
The author asserts that New Testament writers explicitly avoided describing Jesus as a created angel, despite the influence of Jewish angelology, in order to maintain a distinct category for Jesus's relationship with God.

### Theme: Jewish Monotheism
The document explores the difficulty early Christians faced in maintaining strict monotheism while attributing divine status to Jesus, eventually resolving this by interpreting the 'Spirit' as the active, immanent presence of God.
