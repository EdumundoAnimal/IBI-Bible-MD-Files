# The Waldenses: Origins, Beliefs, and Persecution
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Unity was the pinnacle of medieval society. This unity was achieved through universal institutions of the Holy Roman Empire, the Roman Church and allegiance to it. Unlike monks and friars who sought to bring reform internally, the Waldenses arose as an external revolt to purify religion in the 12th century. They sought to return to the purer form of religion they saw in the NT. Hence they were forerunners of the Reformation. Popular Belief: The Waldensian movement has been associated with Peter Waldo or Valdes, having been impressed by the NT teachings it is said he gave up all his belongings and formed the 'poor men'. He preached in the streets as the Apostles had done previously. Had four Gospels and several other books of the Bible translated into the local dialect from an existing Latin form, which most people couldn't read. This movement Valdes began was known as 'The Poor' or the 'Poor Men of Lyons'. Their enemies referred to them as Vaudois (Waldensians, Waldenses, Waldensers), a term meant to be derogatory. They viewed themselves as Roman Catholics who were carrying the doctrines of Christianity further than their weaker brethren (reacting to the existing worldly lives of some contemporary churchmen). Sent a delegation to the Third Lateran Council in 1179 to obtain approval of their work from the Pope, however the council rejected Valdes as lacking proper theological training, and viewed him as spreading errors and misinformation among the people. They demanded an immediate ceasing of their aggressive evangelism. However, they continued their preaching, including allowing women to teach, quite revolutionary for their day. These activities led to the excommunication of Valdes' followers in 1184, and then labelled heretics at the Fourth Lateran Council in 1215. Herein in the Poor became even more polarized from Roman Catholic theology, and began to include radical protest in its views. Proof for existence prior to Waldo: The Nobla Leycon (The Noble Lesson) which dates from the year 1100, goes to suggest that the Waldenses did not owe their rise to Peter Waldo of Lyons, who did not appear until the latter half of that century (1160). Rorenco, Prior of St Roch, Turin (1640) was employed to investigate the origin and antiquity (oldness). He states 'They were not a new sect in the ninth and tenth centuries and that Claude of Turin must have detached them from the Church in the ninth century'. Burning Enemies: Their greatest enemies, Claude Seyssel of Turin (1517) and Reynerius the Inquisitor (1250) stigmatised them as 'the most dangerous of all heretics, because the most ancient'. What they believed: Salvation by faith, baptism by immersion, the Trinity, the fall of man, the incarnation of the Son, the perpetual authority of the Decalogue (Ten Commandments), the need of Divine grace in order to do good works, the necessity of holiness, the institution of the ministry, the resurrection of the body, the eternal bliss of heaven. They were charged with saying that there had been no Pope since the days of Sylvester, temporal offices and dignities were not meet for preachers of the Gospel. The Pope's pardons were a cheat, purgatory was a fable. Relics were simply rotten bones which belonged to one knew not whom. To go on pilgrimage served no end, save to empty one's purse, Flesh might be eaten any day id one's appetite served him. Holy water was not as effectual as if offered in a church. Also accused of scoffing at the doctrine of transubstantiation and having spoken as blasphemously of Rome as the harlot in Revelation 17. It was an evangelistic church, they had missionaries cross the Alps to Germany and Western Europe and even into England. They sold their wares as they travelled to support themselves. They fell foul of the Ecclesiastical authorities for declaring themselves independent of the Church of Rome. Pope Innocent VIII delegated Albert Cataneo, Archdeacon of Cremona to implement the directive of his Papal Bull, which said that the Waldenses were if they 'refuse to abjure, to be crushed like venomous snakes'. One such act of retribution was the chasing of a group of Waldenses into a cave, blockading the cave and burning it, either killing the group by suffocation, or to come out and die by the sword. Some 400 infants were killed. Altogether more than 3000 Vaudois were killed. Despite intense persecution, they continued to spread their Bible based message across Western Europe. They still exist as an earnest band of about thirty-five thousand believers in Northern Italy. The Waldenses sowed the seed for the Reformation, in evangelising much of Europe.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Authority
The Waldenses prioritized the translated scriptures and NT teachings over established church traditions, viewing themselves as restoring pure apostolic religion.

### Theme: Institutional Conflict
The movement was characterized by friction with the Roman Catholic Church, leading to excommunication and labeling of heresy for challenging papal authority and traditional dogmas.

### Theme: Martyrdom and Persecution
The group faced severe, systemic violence, including crusade-like military action and mass killings, yet maintained their faith across Europe.
