## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides a comprehensive academic and homiletical analysis of Psalm 7. It covers linguistic and syntax reports including textual variants and structural mechanics. It explores historical context, linking the psalm to David's conflict with Cush the Benjamite during Saul's reign. The analysis includes a canonical and intertextual report, tracing the theme of divine justice to Christological fulfillment. It also bridges the ancient text with modern philosophical concerns, such as the critique of justice as ressentiment by Nietzsche versus its grounding in ontological reality. Verse 11 in the Septuagint (LXX) softens the MT's 'God who has indignation every day,' adding a negative particle to read 'is he not angry?' likely to protect divine impassibility.
```csv
Grammatical Mechanics,Verses 3-5 employ a cascading series of conditional oaths (protasis-apodosis) using the Hebrew particle im - 'if'. If I have done this... then let the enemy pursue my soul. This is a formal legal 'oath of clearance' (cf. Job 31).
Lexical Profiles,Shiggaion: Found only here and in Habakkuk 3. HALOT suggests a highly emotional dirge, wandering song, or a rapid, rhythmic musical style. Kush: A proper name, but potentially a cryptogram or pejorative (meaning 'black' or referring to the Cushite people) used by David for a Benjamite courtier of Saul, or Saul himself. Tzedeq: Righteousness (v. 8). BDAG/HALOT notes this is not sinless perfection, but covenantal fidelity and relational innocence regarding the specific charges leveled by Cush.
Structural Mechanics,The Psalm operates as an extended chiasm of justice: A. Plea for refuge and rescue (vv. 1-2) B. Oath of innocence/clearance (vv. 3-5) C. Center: Appeal to the Heavenly Judge to awake (vv. 6-11) B'. Description of the wicked's fatal actions (vv. 12-16) A'. Vow of praise for deliverance and justice (v. 17)
```

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Justice
The concept that God serves as the ultimate, objective Judge in a universe where earthly justice often fails, countering modern secular views that see justice as mere power dynamics.

### Theme: Covenantal Fidelity
Explored through 'Tzedeq,' denoting relational innocence and faithfulness to the covenant rather than abstract sinless perfection.

### Theme: Self-Destructive Nature of Evil
The motif that sin is inherently structurally unsound, illustrated by the 'digging of a pit' metaphor where the perpetrator eventually falls into the trap they created.

### Theme: Christological Fulfillment
The trajectory of the psalm from David's temporal experience to Christ's suffering on the Cross, where He absorbs the wrath of God to offer justification.
