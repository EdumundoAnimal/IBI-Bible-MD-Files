# Dictionary of New Testament Background: Friendship
**Author:** [[C. S. Keener]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article provides a comprehensive analysis of the concept of 'friendship' within the historical, social, and literary context of the New Testament. It explores the Greco-Roman understanding of friendship, which often emphasized social standing, reciprocity, and the elite status of friends, and contrasts this with the biblical representation of friendship. The text examines how New Testament authors, particularly in the context of Jesus's relationship with his disciples and the early church, redefined friendship to include themes of selfless love, equality, and the bridging of social divides. It also discusses the role of patronage, the contrast between servitude and friendship in relation to God, and the importance of loyalty and intimacy within these divine-human and communal relationships.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greco-Roman vs. Biblical Friendship
Analysis of how Greco-Roman social expectations of friendship, based on reciprocity and social equality among elites, were transformed in the New Testament to emphasize divine love, servant leadership, and inclusivity.

### Theme: Patronage and Reciprocity
Examination of the social structures of the time, specifically how the client-patron relationship influenced the understanding of social interactions and how the early church sought to challenge these norms.

### Theme: Divine Friendship
The theological argument that being a 'friend of God' shifts from human social rank to a relationship defined by obedience, covenant, and a servant's loyalty, as seen in the examples of Abraham and Moses.

### Theme: Self-Sacrificial Love
The New Testament articulation of friendship as fundamentally sacrificial, as evidenced by Jesus's teaching on laying down one's life for one's friends.
