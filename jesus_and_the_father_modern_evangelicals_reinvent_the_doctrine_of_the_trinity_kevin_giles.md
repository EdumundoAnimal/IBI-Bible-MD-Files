# Jesus and the Father: Modern Evangelicals Reinvent the Doctrine of the Trinity
**Author:** [[Kevin Giles]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This book explores the contemporary evangelical debate regarding the doctrine of the Trinity. Specifically, it critiques the view of 'eternal subordination,' wherein the Son is considered eternally subordinate to the Father in terms of authority and function, rather than equality. The text examines historical theology, the implications of human father-son analogies, and the necessity of maintaining the traditional understanding of the divine unity and equality among the persons of the Trinity. It argues that modern attempts to redefine these relationships based on human structures often mirror ancient heresies, specifically Arianism, by undermining the co-equal and co-eternal nature of God.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Eternal Subordination
The theological position that the Son remains eternally subordinate to the Father in authority, a concept the author labels as a modern reinvention that parallels Arianism.

### Theme: Human Analogy Fallacy
The critique that using human father-son relationships to explain the divine Trinity results in projecting human social structures, such as patriarchy or marital submission, onto the nature of God.

### Theme: Nicene Orthodoxy
The assertion that the historical church councils established the co-equality and co-eternality of the Trinity, which the author argues must be preserved against contemporary evangelical reinterpretations.
