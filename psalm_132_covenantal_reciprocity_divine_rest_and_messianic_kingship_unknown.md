### Linguistic & Syntax Report

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants. Psalm 132:1 (ʿunnōṯō vs. ʿănāwāṯō): MT: וֹותֹנּ ֻע (ʿunnōṯō, Pual infinitive construct with 3ms suffix from הָנ ָע III, "all his being afflicted" or "all his hardships/troubles"). LXX: τῆς πραΰτητος αὐτοῦ (tēs prautētos autou, "his meekness/gentleness"), retroverting to an assumed Vorlage of וֹתָו ְנ ַׁע (ʿanwāṯō, from הָוָנֲע, "humility/meekness"). Apparatus & Decision: The MT reading is supported by 4QPs^e (4Q87) and the Targum (ʾulṣānēh, "his affliction"). The MT is the lectio difficilior and fits the historical context of David's intense labors and trials in retrieving the Ark (2 Sam 6; 1 Chron 13; 15–16; 22:14: "With great pains [bəʿonyī] I have provided for the house of the LORD"). The LXX likely harmonized the noun with Hellenistic virtue categories. Psalm 132:6 (šəmāʿănuhā ... məṣāʾnūhā): Suffix Analysis: The 3fs pronominal suffixes on the verbs ָָוהּנֲע ַׁמ ְש ("we heard of it") and ָָוהּאנָָצ ְמ ("we found it") lack an explicit feminine antecedent in the immediate preceding strophes. Text-Critical Resolution: The feminine noun וןֹרֲא (ʾārōn, "ark") can be construed as either masculine or feminine in biblical Hebrew (cf. 1 Sam 4:17; 2 Chron 8:11). The suffix refers proleptically to the Ark of the Covenant, confirmed by verse 8 (ʾărōn ʿuzzeḵā). The 11QPs^a scroll confirms the MT consonantal text without divergence. Psalm 132:15 (ṣēyḏāh vs. ṣayyāḏeyhā): MT: ךְ ֵר ָבֲאָךְ ֵר ָבָהּ ָידֵצ (ṣēyḏāh bārēḵ ʾăḇārēḵ, "her provision I will abundantly bless"). LXX: τὴν θήραν αὐτῆς εὐλογῶν εὐλογήσω ("her hunting / provision"). Syriac Peshitta: reads mwtb' ("her dwelling place"), suggesting an underlying confusion between ṣ-y-d and ṣ-w-d / y-š-b. MT is undisputed across all Qumran witnesses (4QPs^e, 11QPs^a).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Reciprocity
The document argues that Psalm 132 operates on a dual-covenantal dynamic: David's human resolve to build a sanctuary (v. 2) is met by God's immutable, unconditional oath to establish an eternal Davidic dynasty (v. 11).

### Theme: Divine Rest (Mənuḥāh)
Mənuḥāh is defined not as passive slumber, but as royal-theocratic enthronement following victory over chaos. It represents the permanent resting place of God's presence in Zion, which serves as the axis mundi.

### Theme: Messianic Kingship
The psalm's emblems—the Horn, the Lamp, and the Crown—are interpreted as messianic markers. The 'sprouting horn' links to the prophetic 'Branch' (Ṣemaḥ) of David, pointing toward the eschatological Davidic Deliverer, Jesus Christ.
