# The Kingdom of the Cults
**Author:** [[Walter Martin]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the theological challenges posed by various religious movements identified as cults. The central thesis argues that these groups often use Christian terminology while perverting the core biblical doctrines regarding the person and work of Jesus Christ. The author, Walter Martin, provides a defense of orthodox Christian faith, emphasizing the necessity of discerning truth from counterfeit teachings. The text references biblical warnings, particularly from the Pauline epistles and the teachings of Jesus, regarding false prophets and 'another Jesus'. It covers specific case studies, including Christian Science, Jehovah's Witnesses, the Mormons, and New Age groups, analyzing their divergent views on the nature of God, the divinity of Christ, and the nature of salvation.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Counterfeit Jesus
The text argues that cults frequently employ the terminology of historic Christianity while distorting the identity and nature of Jesus Christ to suit their own theological frameworks.

### Theme: Biblical Apologetics
A core emphasis on the mandate for Christians to 'contend for the faith' by identifying and refuting teachings that deviate from biblical orthodoxy, specifically regarding the Trinity and the deity of Christ.

### Theme: The Perversion of Scripture
The author examines how various religious movements reinterpret, take out of context, or claim 'inspired versions' of the Bible to justify unique, non-orthodox doctrines.
