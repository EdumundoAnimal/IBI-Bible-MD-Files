# The Works of Flavius Josephus
**Author:** [[Flavius Josephus]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Antiquities of the Jews, Book XX, Chapter XI

1. Now Gessius Florus, who was sent as successor to Albinus by Nero, filled Judea with such a degree of misery, that the Jews, in that city of Chazomene, and that brought with him his wife Cleopatra, by whose friendship with Florus, he was made the governor of that city, and not, to my way more different from the wickedest. This Florus was so wicked, and so violent in his actions, that he did what he could to boast Albinus to have been comparatively their best benefactor; so excessive were the mischiefs that he brought upon them. For he did not even conceal his wickedness, and was careful that it might not be discovered to all men; but Gessus Florus, as though he had been sent on purpose to show his crimes to everybody, made a point of ostentation of them to our nation, as a man that had power, or rather as one that needed sort of punishment; for he was not to be moved by pity, and never was satisfied with any degree of gain that came in his way; but that he had more regard to great than to small acquisitions, but because a partner with the robbers them-selves; for a great many had joined him in his practice, without fear, so having him for their security, and depending on him, that he would even have been a partner with them in their robberies; but the unhappy Jews, set to the nation's robbers made among them, where all under a necessity of leaving their own habitations, and of flying away, as being in danger of a greater ruin, where else in the world among foreigners (than in their own country): And what need I say any more of this, and how it came to pass that Florus who necessitated us to take up arms against the Romans, while we thought it better to be destroyed at once, than to suffer by him, as he is the cause, in the second year of the government of Florus, and the twelfth year of the reign of Nero, I shall now relate, as I am forced to do, or what miseries we were enabled to suffer, may be accurately known by what I will write in those books which I have written about the Jewish war.

### The Wars of the Destruction of Jerusalem, Preface

1. Whereas the war which the Jews made with the Romans hath been the greatest of all those that have been in our times, but in a manner, of those that ever were heard of; both of those that were in cities and those that were in nations, some were so at the same time that were not concerned in the affair themselves, have gotten together with their own histories of them, and because they have written them down after a sophistical manner; and while those that were there were ignorant of the truth, and had no care and had either out of a humour of flattery to the Romans, or of hatred to the Jews, and while these writings were filled with contradictions, and something amount-ants, but nowhere the accurate truth, while the Parthians, and those of our nation beyond Euphrates, and those of our nation beyond them, knew accurately what had been done, and how it was brought upon us, and after what manner it ended.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Providence and Historical Judgment
Josephus frames the destruction of Jerusalem and the suffering of the Jewish people as a consequence of the wickedness of Roman governors like Gessius Florus, while simultaneously acknowledging the necessity of documenting these events for posterity.

### Theme: Historiographical Integrity
The author emphasizes the importance of accurate, non-sophistical historical record-keeping, contrasting his own work with the biased or inaccurate accounts of others who wrote out of flattery or hatred.
