# Psalm 10: Theological Exposition and Exegetical Analysis
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides a comprehensive exegetical, theological, and homiletical study of Psalm 10. It explores the linguistic and syntactical structure of the text, its relation to Psalm 9 as a unified acrostic composition, and the core theme of divine sovereignty amidst systemic injustice. The analysis highlights the problem of 'practical atheism'—the lived assumption that God does not intervene in human affairs—which leads to the predatory behavior of the wicked against the vulnerable ('ani, yatom, dak). The text includes an architectural breakdown of the psalm, canonical links to the Exodus pattern and Pauline theology, and historical dialogue with figures like Augustine, Luther, and Calvin. It concludes with an application framework for modern believers to navigate divine hiddenness through lament and covenantal prayer.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Practical Atheism
The operational premise by the wicked that God does not see, does not intervene, and does not hold human actions accountable, leading to systemic injustice.

### Theme: Divine Hiddenness vs. Sovereignty
The tension between the believer's experience of God's apparent silence during injustice and the theological conviction of His eternal kingship and eventual vindication.

### Theme: Covenantal Lament
Lament is framed not as the opposite of faith, but as a robust expression of it—an appeal from a covenant partner to the divine Advocate when human institutions fail.

### Theme: Eschatological Vindication
The hope rooted in Yahweh's ultimate kingship (YHWH melek 'ôlām wā'ed), which promises the final judgment of the wicked and the restoration of the vulnerable.
