# The Spreading Flame
**Author:** [[F. F. Bruce]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document, part of the series 'The Spreading Flame,' explores the theological developments and historical figures in early Christianity, specifically focusing on Jerome, Augustine, and Leo the Great. It details the process of the Vulgate translation by Jerome, the theological evolution of Augustine regarding grace, free will, and the Pelagian controversy, and the assertion of papal authority by Leo the Great. The text also covers the sociopolitical context of the Roman Empire, the impact of the sack of Rome in 410 AD, and the ecclesiastical restructuring that led to the centralization of authority in Rome.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Vulgate Translation
Jerome's systematic revision of Latin biblical texts into a standardized version, ultimately recognized as the authorized Vulgate.

### Theme: Augustinian Theology
Exploration of the tension between human free will and divine grace, particularly regarding the Pelagian controversy and the concepts of original sin and predestination.

### Theme: Ecclesiastical Authority
The evolution of the Bishop of Rome's authority, culminating in Leo the Great's use of Petrine primacy to assert universal governance over the Western church.
