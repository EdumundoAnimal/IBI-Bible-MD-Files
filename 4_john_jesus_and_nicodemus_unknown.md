# 4 John: Jesus and Nicodemus
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Jesus and Nicodemus (3:1-15): This is the first of some highly important discourses between Jesus and people from widely different backgrounds (Nicodemus, the Samaritan woman, the Gentile official, the man at Bethesda). Nicodemus, as a Pharisee, would have stressed careful observance of the law and the traditions of the elders. A radical rebirth is required for salvation. Nicodemus came to Jesus at night, possibly out of fear or to have an uninterrupted conversation; John may be using the darkness to indicate the spiritual darkness of Nicodemus. Spiritual birth: Jesus explains that the 'kingdom of God' is a reign of God to be experienced by being 'born again'. The term regeneration is an act of God. Jesus is telling Nicodemus that a person must be regenerated by God before the end of the world to enter the kingdom. Heavenly things: Jesus refers to Jewish stories of ascension to explain heavenly truths. He compares being 'lifted up' to the bronze snake in Numbers 21:4-9, pointing to His crucifixion as a redemptive event. John's Commentary (3:16-21): John explains the mission of the Son as grounded in the love of God. 'Believe' means having complete faith in Christ. While Jesus came to save, His arrival results in judgment for those who do not believe. Extended Comment (3:31-36): These verses provide reflections on the supremacy of Christ. Jesus is from above, whereas all others are from the earth. Accepting Jesus' testimony certifies that God is truthful. The Father has placed all things in the Son's hands, and belief in the Son is the only path to eternal life, while disobedience faces God's wrath.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Born Again (Regeneration)
The necessity of a radical, divine intervention in a person's life to enter the kingdom of God, shifting the focus from legalistic observance to a spiritual transformation initiated by God.

### Theme: Supremacy of Christ
The contrast between the earthly (human) and the heavenly (the Son of God), emphasizing that Jesus possesses authority from above and serves as the exclusive mediator of divine truth and life.

### Theme: Divine Love and Judgment
The tension between God's sacrificial love for the world (kosmos) and the consequential judgment that follows the rejection of the Son of God.
