# Great Leaders of the Christian Church
**Author:** [[John D. Woodbrige]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides historical and theological overviews of major figures in the Christian Church, specifically focusing on Saint Augustine of Hippo and Leo the Great. It outlines Augustine's intellectual journey, his transition from Manichaeism to Christianity, his opposition to Pelagianism and Donatism, and his foundational contributions to Christian theology, including his concepts of original sin, divine grace, and the interplay between faith and reason. Furthermore, it details the influence of Leo the Great, particularly his role in stabilizing the church through the Council of Chalcedon and his articulation of the dual nature of Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Faith and Reason
Augustine argued that faith is not inferior to reason; rather, it is an indispensable precursor to knowledge, summarized by the phrase Credo ut intelligam (I believe in order that I may know).

### Theme: Original Sin and Divine Grace
Augustine countered the Pelagian heresy by asserting that all humanity fell into a state of sin after the Fall and cannot achieve salvation through human effort alone, necessitating divine grace.

### Theme: The Two Natures of Christ
Leo the Great solidified the church's doctrinal stance at the Council of Chalcedon, reinforcing the teaching that Christ holds both a full divine nature and a full human nature.
