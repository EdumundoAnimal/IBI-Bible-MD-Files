# Faith in Ireland: Post-Christendom in Ireland
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the concept of 'Post-Christendom' in Ireland, defined as a cultural state where Christian faith loses its central influence and coherence. It uses the Irish Civil Partnership Bill as a case study for Christian social engagement. Page 1 presents demographic poll data regarding sexuality and moral attitudes in Ireland. Page 2 outlines the institutional decline of the Church in sectors such as health, media, and social welfare, and lists seven key shifts for the church transitioning from a dominant institution to a marginalized movement.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Post-Christendom
A cultural phase where traditional Christian dominance and institutional influence diminish, requiring the Church to rethink its role in society.

### Theme: Institutional Decline
The erosion of the Church's authority and prestige in public life, including health, education, and political influence, leading to a shift from being a 'carer' to being viewed as an 'abuser'.

### Theme: Strategic Shift
The suggested transition for the Church from a centralized, controlling institution to a prophetic, witnessing minority movement on the margins of society.
