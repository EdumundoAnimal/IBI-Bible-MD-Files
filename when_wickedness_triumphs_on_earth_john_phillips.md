# When Wickedness Triumphs on Earth
**Author:** [[John Phillips]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an exegetical study of Psalm 37, exploring the theological themes of the prosperity of the wicked versus the ultimate stability of the righteous. It contrasts the 'wicked man'—characterized by pride, transient power, and eventual downfall—with the 'righteous man', whose strength resides in faith, patience, and divine alignment. The text includes practical applications regarding appropriate speech, anger management, and the danger of gossip. It concludes that despite the temporary success of the wicked in the world, the final judgment of God establishes the lasting security of those who trust in Him.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Prosperity of the Wicked
The author argues that the apparent success or 'triumph' of the wicked is merely temporal and illusionary. Their power is often built on injustice and arrogance, which inevitably leads to destruction according to divine administration.

### Theme: Stability of the Righteous
The righteous man is defined not by earthly wealth, but by a firm foundation in God. This stability is internal and eternal, contrasting with the 'shifting sand' upon which the ungodly build their lives.

### Theme: Practical Ethics in Speech and Anger
The study draws on the wisdom literature to emphasize the importance of controlled speech, the dangers of gossip, and the need to channel anger appropriately, citing biblical precedents for righteous indignation versus destructive rage.
