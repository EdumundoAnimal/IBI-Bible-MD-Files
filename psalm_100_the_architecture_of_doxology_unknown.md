# Psalm 100: The Architecture of Doxology
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 100 is a liturgical processional text focusing on Yahweh as King and Shepherd. The document provides an exegetical report covering the Kethiv/Qere variants of verse 3, grammatical construction, and structural analysis. It includes historical/cultural context regarding Second Temple worship and Ancient Near East vassal parallels. The theological core centers on God's covenant character (goodness, steadfast love, and faithfulness) and the shift from autonomous self-creation to covenantal belonging. The document culminates in a homiletical application, contrasting the burden of secular self-sovereignty with the security of identity found in the Shepherd-King, supported by Christological connections.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Ownership
The transition from autonomous human self-origination to being 'sheep of His pasture,' providing security through divine belonging.

### Theme: Theocentric Doxology
Worship as a cognitive and affective response to the objective reality of God's character, rather than an unanchored emotional reaction.

### Theme: Christological Fulfillment
The trajectory of Psalm 100 from the entrance liturgy of the Second Temple to Jesus as the 'Good Shepherd' and the ultimate gathering of nations in Revelation.
