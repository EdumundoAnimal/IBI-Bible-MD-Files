# Discovering Jesus in the New Testament: Hebrews
**Author:** [[Keith Warrington]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document, taken from chapter 16 of Discovering Jesus in the New Testament, provides a theological analysis of the Epistle to the Hebrews. It emphasizes that the original Jewish readers were tempted to revert to Jewish roots and traditions. The author of Hebrews counters this by establishing the superiority of Jesus over all preceding figures and systems, including OT prophets, angels, Moses, and the Levitical priesthood. Jesus is depicted as God's final, perfect revelation, the creator of the universe, and the Savior who provides eternal salvation. The text highlights Jesus' role as a high priest who is both divine and human, capable of empathizing with believers while maintaining his position at the right hand of God. The document also delves into the typological significance of Melchizedek and the shift from the old sacrificial system to the new covenant initiated by Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Superiority
The central argument of Hebrews that Jesus is superior to prophets, angels, Moses, and the Levitical priesthood, serving as the final and perfect revelation of God.

### Theme: High Priestly Mediation
The dual nature of Jesus as both divine and human allows him to be a high priest who can sympathize with the weaknesses of his people while offering a perfect, eternal sacrifice.

### Theme: New Covenant Fulfillment
The transition from the earthly, flawed sacrificial system of the Law to the eternal, effective redemption provided by Jesus' one-time sacrifice.

### Theme: Order of Melchizedek
A typological argument using Melchizedek to demonstrate that Jesus' priesthood is superior to, and supersedes, the Aaronic/Levitical priesthood.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "Jesus is greater than those with whom he is compared—the OT prophets (1:1-3), angels (1:4-14), Moses (3:1-6) and the high priests and the sacrificial system (3:7-10:39)."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 167*

> "The writer elevates Jesus' status and the good news he preached by describing him as 'a Son' (1:2). He is not implying that God had more than one son, or that Jesus is merely a son or even simply his son. He is, rather, saying that God used this superior medium of 'son' to reveal himself."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 167*

> "The writer completes this initial portrait of Jesus by describing him as having 'sat down at the right hand of the Majesty on high.' It is the symbolism of this picture, and not its literal presentation, that is important."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 169*

> "The author of the Hebrews quotes from Psalm 110:4 and applies it to Jesus (5:10). He thereby elevates Jesus' priesthood by association with Melchizedek. Jesus has precedence because he precedes the Aaronic priesthood, but also because he supersedes Melchizedek."
*Context Source: Chapter 16, page 175*
