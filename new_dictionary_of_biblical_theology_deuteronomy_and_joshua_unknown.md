# New Dictionary of Biblical Theology: Deuteronomy and Joshua
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text provides an analytical overview of the books of Deuteronomy and Joshua. Deuteronomy is presented as a 'sermon' that outlines a covenantal relationship between Yahweh and Israel, emphasizing the necessity of obedience to the law as a response to God's grace. It explores the tension between divine grace and the human failure to uphold covenantal requirements. Joshua describes the transition into the Promised Land, the conquest, and the fulfillment of divine promises, while addressing themes like the 'ban' and the continuity of God's leadership from Moses to Joshua.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Obedience
Deuteronomy frames the law not as a burden but as a response to God's prior act of grace, requiring Israel to choose life through faithfulness to Yahweh.

### Theme: Divine Grace and Human Failure
A central tension exists where Israel's persistent disobedience is met with the promise of future divine intervention and a new covenant.

### Theme: Land and Inheritance
The Promised Land is linked to Israel's identity and their obedience, with the conquest described as a fulfillment of God's promise made to the patriarchs.

### Theme: Holy War and the Ban
The concept of 'holy war' in Joshua is portrayed as the exercise of God's justice against Canaanite idolatry, rather than merely an exercise of military power.
