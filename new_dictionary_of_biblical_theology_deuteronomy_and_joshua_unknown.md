# New Dictionary of Biblical Theology: Deuteronomy and Joshua
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text provides an analytical overview of the theological themes present in the books of Deuteronomy and Joshua. It emphasizes the concept of covenant, the tension between divine grace and human obedience, and the recurring pattern of Israel's failure and God's faithfulness. In Deuteronomy, Moses' final discourses urge obedience as a response to grace, while acknowledging human inability to remain faithful, thus pointing towards a new covenant. Joshua continues this narrative, detailing the conquest of the land as an act of God's power while highlighting the necessity of continued faithfulness to the divine law.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Theology
The relational framework between God and Israel, characterized by divine grace, conditional blessings for obedience, and curses for disobedience.

### Theme: Human Inability and Grace
The theological tension where Israel's recurring failure to keep the law highlights the need for divine intervention, ultimately anticipating a 'new covenant' solution.

### Theme: The Theology of the Land
The Promised Land represents both a gift from God and a site where obedience must be maintained through the community's adherence to God's statutes.

### Theme: Holy War
The perspective in the book of Joshua where military conquests are interpreted not as human achievement, but as the manifestation of God's judgment and power.
