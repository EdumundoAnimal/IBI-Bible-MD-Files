This document explores the foundational theological concepts of Pentecostalism, including the 'Theology of Encounter', the role of Scripture, Oneness theology, and the understanding of spiritual gifts ('charismata') within the Pauline context. It examines the historical progression of Pentecostal belief, moving from the three-stage Holiness movement to the 'Finished Work' doctrine, and provides detailed reflections on Biblical passages related to the Holy Spirit, the Day of Pentecost, and spiritual gifts.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Theology of Encounter
Pentecostal theology prioritizes experiential encounters with God over purely intellectual or doctrinal formulations. Experience serves as the basis, while intellectual understanding follows as an attempt to interpret that experience.

### Theme: Finished Work Doctrine
A theological shift from the three-stage holiness model to a two-stage model, positing that sanctification is received at conversion as a finished work of Christ, while baptism in the Spirit remains a subsequent empowerment.

### Theme: Oneness Theology
A view that rejects the traditional Trinity, maintaining that God is indivisible and that 'Father', 'Son', and 'Holy Spirit' are personal manifestations of one God, rather than distinct persons.

### Theme: Spiritual Gifts (Charismata)
Drawing from Paul's writings, specifically 1 Corinthians, this theme emphasizes the priesthood of all believers and the functional, rather than hierarchical, nature of spiritual gifts meant to edify the body of Christ.
