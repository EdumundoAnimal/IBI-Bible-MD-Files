# Theology I Week 7: Translators vs. Transformers
**Author:** [[David Carnduff MTh]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the dichotomy between the 'translator' and 'transformer' approaches to contemporising Christian theology. The 'translator' seeks to re-express the message in an intelligible form while retaining the core content, analogous to translating between languages. In contrast, the 'transformer' is willing to make serious changes to the content to relate to the modern world. The text uses illustrations from J.B. Phillips regarding biblical translation and discusses the implications of these approaches for hermeneutics and biblical authority. Additionally, the document examines the 'Problem of Evil,' citing the Book of Job and Erickson's analysis of theological responses to human suffering and the nature of God's goodness.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Translator Approach
An approach to theology that prioritizes re-expressing the original message clearly in contemporary language while strictly retaining the original theological content.

### Theme: Transformer Approach
An approach to theology that allows for significant content modification to ensure relevance to the modern world, sometimes risking the replacement of absolute standards with relative ones.

### Theme: The Problem of Evil
The theological dilemma of reconciling a good, all-powerful God with the existence of suffering and evil in the world.

### Theme: Hermeneutics
The methodology and theory of interpreting biblical texts within a modern cultural and moral context.
