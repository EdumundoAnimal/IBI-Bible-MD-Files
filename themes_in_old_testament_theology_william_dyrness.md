# Themes in Old Testament Theology
**Author:** [[William Dyrness]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document explores the self-revelation of God within the Old Testament, emphasizing that revelation is not merely propositional but occurs through historical events, acts of salvation, and personal relationships. It examines key instances of self-revelation, including the encounters of Adam, Abraham, Moses, and the prophets, highlighting themes of God's transcendence and immanence, the redemptive nature of revelation, and the demand for a concrete response of obedience from the believer.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemptive Revelation
The argument that Old Testament revelation is primarily redemptive, intended to restore broken relationships and facilitate a covenantal connection between God and humanity.

### Theme: Personal Encounters
God reveals himself not just through propositions, but through personal interactions, specific historical events, and named appearances, requiring personal responses from the figures involved.

### Theme: Transcendence and Immanence
The tension between God's status as a transcendent deity who is beyond human comprehension and his simultaneous active presence in human affairs and history.
