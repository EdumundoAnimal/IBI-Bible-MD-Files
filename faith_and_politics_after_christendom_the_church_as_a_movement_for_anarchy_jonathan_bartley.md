# Faith and Politics After Christendom: The Church as a Movement for Anarchy
**Author:** [[Jonathan Bartley]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the complex and often contentious history of the relationship between the Christian church and state power throughout Christendom. It examines how early Christian concepts of citizenship, initially focused on the Kingdom of God, became transformed and co-opted by state institutions. The text highlights how the church's shift toward institutional mediation and government alliance led to the justification of violence, the marginalization of dissenters, and the compromise of radical Christian ideals such as non-violence and stewardship. Key figures and periods, from Augustine to the Reformation and the role of the Church of England, are discussed in the context of how Christian theology was adapted to serve political agendas, often resulting in the exclusion of groups deemed outsiders or threats. The concluding analysis suggests that the church became entangled with worldly governance, moving away from its original focus on the 'risen Jesus' and toward preserving its own institutional influence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Citizenship and the Kingdom of God
Explores the transformation of early Christian identity from a community oriented toward the Kingdom of God to one assimilated into Roman and later imperial state structures.

### Theme: The Church's Alliance with State Power
Analyzes how the institutional church shifted to provide legitimacy for political systems, including the adoption of 'just war' theories and the use of state-sanctioned coercion.

### Theme: Marginalization and Coercion
Details the historical use of church-sanctioned power to oppress dissenters, including the treatment of Jews, followers of radical movements, and those accused of heresy.

### Theme: The Theology of Stewardship and Dissent
Examines the tension between original Christian teachings of stewardship and non-violence versus the practical political outcomes of church involvement in government systems.
