# Faith and Politics After Christendom: The Church as a Movement for Anarchy
**Author:** [[Jonathan Bartley]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text examines the historical intersection of Christian theology and political power within the 'Christendom' era. It argues that the alignment of the church with the state, beginning with Constantine, fundamentally altered the original tenets of the faith, which were initially characterized by a countercultural 'discipleship' to Jesus. Key themes include the transformation of Christian citizenship, the development of 'just war' theory, the influence of Augustine on political thought, and the historical marginalization or persecution of dissenting movements. The document critiques how the church transitioned from a movement for social justice to an institution often complicit in state violence and authoritarianism.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christendom and Political Integration
The historical transition where the church shifted from a countercultural, transnational community to an institutional partner of imperial power, leading to the legitimization of state violence.

### Theme: Citizenship and Discipleship
A contrast between the early Christian understanding of citizenship tied to the 'Kingdom of God' and the later, state-centered conception of 'discipleship' which became territorially bound and subservient to state authority.

### Theme: Just War and Institutional Mediation
The evolution of Christian ethics from non-violence to the adoption of 'just war' theories by theologians like Augustine and Aquinas to provide moral justification for imperial state actions.

### Theme: Radical Dissent
The presence of groups throughout history (such as the Anabaptists or peasant reformers) who sought to reclaim the early church's radical rejection of worldly power.
