# Basic Theology: The Trinity of God
**Author:** [[Charles C. Ryrie]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text provides an analytical overview of the Christian doctrine of the Trinity. It begins by examining the Old Testament's foundational concepts of God's unity (Shema) and plural pronouns, moving through the specific roles of the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit in the New Testament. It addresses the complexity of defining the Trinity, citing scholars like B.B. Warfield, and discusses historical heresies such as Monarchianism, Arianism, and the development of the Nicene Creed. The text concludes with the practical implications of Trinitarian theology for Christian faith and fellowship.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Trinitarianism vs. Unitarianism
The text argues that while the word 'Trinity' is not explicitly in the Bible, the doctrine is derived from the synthesis of Old Testament unity and New Testament distinctions between the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

### Theme: Historical Development of Doctrine
A survey of theological history including Arianism, Monarchianism, and the Council of Constantinople, detailing how the Church arrived at the formal definition of 'three persons in one substance'.

### Theme: Practical Theology
The assertion that the triune nature of God provides the theological basis for human fellowship and the relationship between believers and God.
