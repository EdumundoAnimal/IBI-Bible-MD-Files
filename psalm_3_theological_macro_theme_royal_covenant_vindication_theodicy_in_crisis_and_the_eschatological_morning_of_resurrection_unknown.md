## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The Ancient Horizon)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report. Textual Variants & Apparatus. The Masoretic Text (MT) of Psalm 3, preserved predominantly in Codex Leningradensis (B19A), exhibits remarkable textual stability with minimal significant divergences across early witnesses. MT: (מִזְמֹור לְדָוִד בְבָרְחֹו מִפְנֵי אַבְשָלֹום בְנֹו) (v. 1a). LXX: Ψαλμὸς τῷ Δαυιδ, ὁπότε ἀπεδίδρασκεν ἀπὸ προσώπου Αβεσσαλωμ τοῦ υἱοῦ αὐτοῦ. 4QPsᵃ: Minor orthographic variants; preservation of the Davidic superscription intact. Verse 2 (MT v. 3) — הָתָועּשְׁי (yəšū‘ātāh): The feminine noun הָועּשְי (yəšū‘āh, "salvation/deliverance") appears with a paragogic he (הָ-). Morphologically, this is not a feminine directional ending or a pronominal suffix, but an archaic poetic intensive morpheme (he-directive / archaic accusative residue) underscoring total, exhaustive deliverance. The Septuagint translates this as οὐκ ἔστιν σωτηρία αὐτῷ ἐν τῷ θεῷ αὐτοῦ ("there is no salvation for him in his God"), adding the possessive αὐτοῦ (autou, "his"), likely a stylistic assimilation reflecting David's personal covenantal claim. Verse 7 (MT v. 8) — הִכֵּיתָ אֶת־כָּל־אֹויְבַי לְחִי (hikkīṯ ā ’eṯ -kol-’ōyəḇay leḥī): The MT reads leḥī (accusative of location/manner: "on the jaw/cheekbone"). The Syriac (Peshitta) and certain Targumic recensions read leḥēn as an adverbial construct ("in vain" or "gratuitously"), confusing לְחִי (leḥī, "jawbone") with חִנָּם (ḥinnām). The MT reading is firmly retained as the lectio difficilior, supported by the parallel clause שִׁנֵּי רְשָׁעִים שִׁבַּרְתָּ (šinnē rəšā‘īm šibbartā, "the teeth of the wicked you break").

### Grammatical & Syntactical Mechanics

Psalm 3 is structured around dramatic shifts in grammatical voice, verbal aspect, and pronominal direction: Strophe A: vv. 2–3 (MT) | 3rd Person Plural Mockery | Threat from Below. Strophe B: vv. 4–5 (MT) | 2nd Person Direct Address | Realignment from Above. Strophe C: vv. 6–7a (MT) | 1st Person Declarative | Experiential Rest & Awakening. Strophe D: vv. 7b–9 (MT) | Imperatival / Confessional | Universal Sovereign Victory. Verbal Aspect Shifts in vv. 2–3 (MT): The psalm opens with stative and participial proliferation: רַבּוּ (rabbū, Qal perfect 3cp — "they have multiplied") followed by רַבִּים קָמִים עָלָי (rabbīm qāmīm ‘ālāy, masculine plural participle — "many are standing up/continually rising against me") and רַבִּים אֹמְרִים (rabbīm ’ōmərīm, masculine plural participle — "many are continually saying"). The participle denotes ongoing, persistent, progressive encircling action. The Adversative Disjunction in v. 4 (MT): The transitional conjunction וְ (wə-) prefixed to the independent personal pronoun אַתָּה (‘attāh) creates a sharp adversative disjunction: וְאַתָּה יְהוָה (wə-’attāh YHWH — "But You, O YHWH"). This syntax decisively breaks the psychological and military momentum of the advancing multitudes (rabbīm). Preterite vs. Imperfect Dynamics in v. 5 (MT): The vocal sequence קֹולִי אֶל־יְהוָה אֶקְרָא (qōlī ’el-YHWH ’eqrā, Qal imperfect 1cs — "I cry aloud to YHWH") is followed by וַיַּעֲנֵנִי (wayya‘anēnī, Wayyiqtol / preterite with 1cs suffix — "and He answered me"). The narrative tense confirms an already realized experiential rescue in the heavenly realm before physical battle commences. Volitive Enclitic in v. 8 (MT): The cry קוּמָה (qūmāh) is a Qal imperative masculine singular with a cohortative/paragogic he (-āh), functioning as an urgent plea of entreaty ("Arise now!"), invoking the ancient Ark narrative formula of Numbers 10:35.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Royal Covenant Vindication
The psalm serves as a paradigm for covenant survival, demonstrating that the Davidic covenant (2 Sam 7) is indestructible and outlasts historical collapse, exile, or geopolitical subjugation.

### Theme: Theodicy in Crisis
David's prayer is an interior response to external catastrophe where empirical reality and divine promise enter irreconcilable conflict. It addresses the taunt that David is under divine abandonment (herem) and curses of the broken covenant.

### Theme: Eschatological Morning of Resurrection
The declaration in verse 5 ('I lay down and slept; I awoke, for the LORD sustained me') is interpreted as the prophetic archetype of Christ's descent into the grave and His triumph in the Resurrection.
