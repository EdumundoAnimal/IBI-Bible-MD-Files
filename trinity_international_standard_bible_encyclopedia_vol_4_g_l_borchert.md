# Trinity (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia, Vol 4)
**Author:** [[G. L. Borchert]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the development of Trinitarian theology, contrasting the approaches of Augustine, Richard of St. Victor, and Thomas Aquinas. It examines how Augustine's emphasis on the divine essence and the unity of God influenced Western trinitarianism, the later shift toward relational and interpersonal models of the Trinity in the 12th and 13th centuries, and the synthesis provided by Aquinas. Additionally, it references the historical context of the filioque clause and the theological debates surrounding the nature of divine persons.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Trinitarianism
Augustine prioritized the divine essence and unity, sometimes leading to tensions in explaining the distinction between the three persons.

### Theme: Relational Trinity
The evolution of trinitarian thought, particularly through Richard of St. Victor, which posited that love requires a plurality of persons.

### Theme: Thomistic Synthesis
Thomas Aquinas sought to reconcile Augustine's emphasis on unity with the distinctiveness of divine persons by defining them as subsistent relations.
