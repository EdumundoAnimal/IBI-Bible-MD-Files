## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides a comprehensive exegetical, homiletical, and canonical analysis of Psalm 109. It utilizes a structural approach to examine the linguistic syntax (including a macro-chiasm), textual variants between the Masoretic Text and ancient versions, and key lexical profiles of terms such as śāṭān, pəquddāh, ḥesed, and qəlālāh. The report establishes the theological significance of Psalm 109 as an individual lament that invokes divine covenant sanctions against those who have committed unprovoked betrayal and judicial perjury. It contrasts the 'Quotation Hypothesis' with the 'Covenant-Judicial Model,' ultimately affirming that the psalm is a prayer of an innocent king (typologically linked to Christ) seeking divine legal redress rather than venting personal malice. The report extends its analysis to a Christological fulfillment matrix, identifying the psalm's usage in the New Testament (Acts 1, John 15, Galatians 3) and its eschatological implications, while providing a framework for modern homiletical application.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenant Theodicy
The view that divine wrath is a just response to moral rebellion and covenant breach, rather than an expression of personal vindictiveness.

### Theme: Lex Talionis
The biblical principle of retributive justice where the punishment corresponds exactly to the crime, applied here to false accusers.

### Theme: Typological-Christological Vindication
The interpretation of David's suffering in the psalm as a foreshadowing of Christ's rejection, betrayal, and ultimate vindication by God.

### Theme: Liturgical Surrender
The practice of bringing raw human indignation and pain into the presence of God in prayer, thereby relinquishing personal vengeance to the Supreme Judge.
