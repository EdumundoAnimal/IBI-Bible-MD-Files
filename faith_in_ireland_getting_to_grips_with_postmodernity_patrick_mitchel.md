# Faith in Ireland: Getting to Grips with Postmodernity
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Objectives of this class

1. To introduce some of the main themes and attitudes of postmodernity 2. To discuss if and how these are found 'all around us' in Ireland today

### Postmodernism described: six reversals

Postmodernity reverses practically all the key characteristics of modernism Nietzsche - all knowledge is to with power. Shelly / Keats: 19th century Romanticism Existentialism: 1960s 1. Disbelief in human progress (vs belief in progress) What or who do people believe in these days? What or who has been discredited and exposed in recent years? 2. 'I' still at the centre - but who is this 'I'? It all depends who you are talking to Jacques Derrida: 1930- o The deconstruction of language - attacks 'logocentrism'. o Focus on the reader rather than the text. No 'true' meaning but only our interpretations of it. 3. Certainty is neither desirable nor attainable [in modernism it is both] Not attainable Certainty is an illusion - can never be found Not desirable Because statements of certainty are destructive, limiting and impose uniformity.

### Postmodernism themes continued

f. Individualism - each to their own 'truth' o An age of DIY spirituality, of pic 'n mix religion. o Subjective individualism lies at the heart of postmodernism g. Fragmentation o An embrace of uncertainty, chaos and meaninglessness. o No accident that the postmodern period has coincided with the birth of the internet and the information age. h. Tourists not pilgrims. o Pilgrims o Tourists

### The advantages of postmodernism

i. Opposed the autonomous arrogant self supposedly in control of the world ii. Genuine insights of the differences humans bring to understanding reality iii. Room for everyone to tell their story

### The weaknesses and dangers of postmodernism

i. Exaggerates the difficulty of knowing anything for sure ii. Incoherent and inconsistent 'There is no such thing as absolute truth' - is that true? 'There is no such thing as right and wrong' - is that right? iii. Relativism is only a thinly disguised form of intolerance iv. Pure relativism is also morally absurd. v. Unsustainable and un-livable?

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Deconstruction of Truth
The document highlights the postmodern shift away from absolute certainty, citing Derrida's 'logocentrism' and the rejection of objective meaning in favor of reader interpretation.

### Theme: Individualism and Fragmentation
Postmodernity is characterized by 'DIY spirituality' and 'pic 'n mix religion,' where the individual is the center of truth, leading to a fragmented worldview.

### Theme: Relativism
The text critiques the postmodern stance on truth, noting that the claim 'there is no such thing as absolute truth' is inherently self-contradictory and leads to moral absurdity.
