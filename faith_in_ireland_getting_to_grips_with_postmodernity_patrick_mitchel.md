# Faith in Ireland: Getting to Grips with Postmodernity
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an overview of postmodernity, listing its core characteristics, reversals of modernism, advantages, and dangers. Key themes include the loss of belief in human progress, the instability of identity ('who is this I?'), and the rejection of absolute certainty. It contrasts 'tourists' with 'pilgrims' and identifies the postmodern era as one of fragmentation, subjectivity, and individualism ('each to their own truth'). The text concludes by evaluating the philosophical weaknesses of postmodernism, highlighting its potential for incoherence, moral absurdity, and unsustainability.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Postmodern Reversals
Postmodernism is presented as a movement that challenges modernist pillars such as objective knowledge (referencing Nietzsche), the focus on progress, and the authority of the text (referencing Derrida's 'logocentrism').

### Theme: Fragmentation and Subjectivity
The document identifies a shift toward fragmentation, where absolute certainty is rejected in favor of subjective individualism ('each to their own truth') and a blend of beliefs.

### Theme: Critique of Relativism
The author argues that the postmodern position is inherently self-contradictory, specifically regarding absolute truth claims, and warns that pure relativism leads to moral absurdity and a lack of social viability.
