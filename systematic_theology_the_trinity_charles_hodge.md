# Systematic Theology: The Trinity
**Author:** [[Charles Hodge]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is an excerpt from Charles Hodge's Systematic Theology, focusing on the doctrine of the Trinity. It explores the biblical and theological foundation of the Triune God, the eternal sonship of Christ, the relationship between the Father and the Son, and the nature of the Godhead. It addresses various objections regarding the meaning of 'Son' in the Scriptures, differentiating between the eternal generation of the Son and his temporal manifestation as the Messiah. The text provides a rigorous academic and theological examination of these core Christian doctrines, drawing upon historical church consensus and scriptural evidence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Doctrine of the Trinity
Hodge argues that the Trinity is a fundamental biblical truth rather than a merely philosophical concept, emphasizing the Triune nature of God as a premise for the entire system of Christian theology.

### Theme: Eternal Sonship of Christ
The author defends the theological position that Christ is the Son of God by eternal generation, asserting that this sonship is an essential attribute of his nature, not merely a term of office or a result of the incarnation.

### Theme: Scriptural Interpretation
The text systematically addresses scriptural passages often cited by critics (e.g., Psalm 2:7, Acts 13:32-33), arguing that their primary reference is to the eternal divinity of the Son rather than just his historical mission as the Messiah.

## 4. Evidence Matrix & Verbatim Assertions
> "The doctrine of the Trinity is peculiar to the religion of the Bible. The Triad of the ancient world is only a philosophical statement of the pantheistic theory which underlies all the religions of antiquity."
*Context Source: Chapter VI, Page 1*

> "The Council declared that our Lord is the Eternal Son of God, i. e., that He is from eternity the Son of God."
*Context Source: Chapter VI, Page 4*

> "The term Son, as used in the Scriptures, expresses different relations, and therefore may be applied to the same person for different reasons; or, have one meaning, i. e., express one relation in one place, and a different one in another."
*Context Source: Chapter VI, Page 6*
