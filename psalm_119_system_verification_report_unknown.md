This document provides a comprehensive exegetical-theological analysis of Psalm 119, structured as a 'System Verification Report'. It explores the textual transmission, grammatical and structural syntax, and the eightfold lexical profile of the Hebrew terms for revelation. The analysis details the alphabetic acrostic structure, historical and cultural context (Persian period), and intertextual echoes. It emphasizes the Christological fulfillment of the Law, the patristic and reformational reception history, and the hermeneutical bridge between ancient and contemporary paradigms, ultimately framing the Psalm as a guide for the covenant pilgrim in a secular age.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenant Fidelity
The foundational argument that Psalm 119 is not about legalism, but a loving response to God's prior grace and covenantal faithfulness.

### Theme: Christological Fulfillment
The interpretation that Jesus Christ is the ultimate embodiment of the Law who perfectly fulfilled its requirements, transforming it from external inscription to internalized Spirit-led life.

### Theme: Theonomy vs. Autonomy
A dialectical contrast between the modern secular pursuit of absolute self-autonomy and the Psalmist's discovery of true liberty through submission to divine boundaries.

### Theme: Redemptive Pedagogy of Suffering
The view that affliction in the life of the believer is not meaningless, but a pedagogical tool used by God to strip away self-sufficiency and recalibrate human desires toward the Word.
