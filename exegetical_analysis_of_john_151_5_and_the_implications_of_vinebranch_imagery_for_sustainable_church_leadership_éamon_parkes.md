# Exegetical Analysis of John 15:1-5 and the Implications of Vine/Branch Imagery for Sustainable Church Leadership
**Author:** [[Éamon Parkes]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Breakdown of essay proposal

1. Definition of Problem 2. Determination of Purpose 3. Design of Methodology

### Definition of Problem:

Essay title: Exegetical Analysis of John 15:1-5 and the Implications of Vine/Branch Imagery for Sustainable Church Leadership? Problem: Church pastors and ministry leader drop-out rates are increasing dramatically, particularly in the United States. What are the causes of this and from this? How then can church leadership remain sustainable and can scripture shed light as to a solution? Learning outcome: learn or develop sustainable leadership practice that is based on scripture in an ever demanding consumer driven culture by understanding unity in Christ, fruitfulness and balance with life and ministry.

### Determination of Purpose:

What to do with the problem? 1. Why did Jesus use the vine/branch imagery as a motif of unity and fruitfulness with himself as a ‘last word’ guide of wisdom towards his disciples, (the premier examples of scriptural church leaders) 2. Carry out an exegetical (critical biblical explanation) examining of John 15:1-5. 3. From this then, to extract contextual evidence and meaning as understood by the 1st century disciples in Israel. 4. Investigate 1st century viticulture (wine making) process as used by Jesus as a parable/motif. 5. The purpose is to reconstruct a vivid picture of the events surrounding the event in the historical period of both Jesus’ final discourse and the apostle Johns reason for writing to the persecuted Christian community of ‘his’ time some years later.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Sustainable Church Leadership
The document addresses the high drop-out rates among pastors and ministry leaders in the United States, seeking to find a scriptural basis for sustainable practice in a consumer-driven culture.

### Theme: Vine and Branch Imagery
The author proposes an exegetical study of John 15:1-5 to understand the theological motif of unity and fruitfulness between Christ and his disciples.

### Theme: Historical and Contextual Exegesis
The methodology involves investigating 1st-century viticulture and the historical context of Jesus' final discourse and the Apostle John's writing to a persecuted community.
