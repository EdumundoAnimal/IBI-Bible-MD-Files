# Christ and Consumerism
**Author:** [[Alan Storkey and Gordon Wenham]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides a critical examination of consumerism from a Christian theological perspective, arguing that consumer culture functions as a rival religion that undermines traditional values, Christian ethics, and the sanctity of life. Authors Alan Storkey and Gordon Wenham critique how economic models have shifted to justify a lifestyle centered on individual utility, pleasure-seeking, and self-creation. The text specifically explores the erosion of Sunday as a sacred day of rest, the impact of consumerist ideology on marriage and procreation (addressing abortion and reproductive technology), and the shift toward viewing human existence through the lens of individual choice and autonomy rather than moral obligation to God and neighbor.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Consumerism as a Rival Religion
The authors posit that consumerism has replaced traditional faith, demanding total commitment, promising salvation through accumulation, and dictating a lifestyle that prioritizes individual desires over service to God.

### Theme: Erosion of Sacred Time and Relationships
The shift toward seven-day commerce, particularly the erosion of Sunday as a day of rest, is analyzed as a symptom of a culture that treats time and people as commodities rather than reflections of the divine image.

### Theme: Individual Autonomy vs. Moral Duty
The document critiques how 'individual choice' is elevated to the highest moral value, leading to the disintegration of traditional views on marriage, procreation, and the protection of the vulnerable, ultimately framing this autonomy as a false freedom that leads to bondage.
