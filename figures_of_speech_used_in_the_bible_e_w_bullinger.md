# Figures of Speech Used in the Bible
**Author:** [[E. W. Bullinger]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an introductory examination and specific instances of figures of speech used in the Bible. It defines the etymology of 'figure' (Greek: schema; Latin: fingere) and discusses linguistic mechanisms such as 'Interpositio' and 'Parenthesis'. Specifically, it analyzes Heb. 2:9 and 2 Pet. 1:19 as examples of parenthetical structure, arguing that understanding these linguistic forms is essential for accurate biblical interpretation.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Definition of Figure of Speech
The author defines a figure as a word or sentence that takes a form different from its ordinary, literal usage to provide additional clarity, emphasis, or force.

### Theme: Parenthesis and Interpositio
Linguistic structures in the Bible where words or sentences are inserted into a narrative; the author suggests these are often necessary to interpret scripture correctly, despite sometimes lacking explicit punctuation in original manuscripts.

### Theme: Biblical Exegesis
The necessity of studying the grammatical laws and figures of speech to grasp the intended revelation of God, rather than relying solely on surface-level reading.
