### Week 4

Greek Nouns. 5 different ways that nouns are used. 1. Nominative - Case (me) (subject of the verb). 2. Vocative - Case (oi) (noun being spoken to). 3. Accusative - Case (a) (The direct object of the verb). 4. Genitive - Case (x) (s) (Noun owns something). 5. Dative - Case (to) (Indirect object - 'to' or 'for'). Then there are Singular + Plurals. 3 Declensions. Ending change to pattern. Gender (masculine, feminine or neuter). Components of Greek Nouns: 1. Case, 2. Number, 3. Declension, 4. Gender.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greek Noun Declension
The document outlines the five primary cases for Greek nouns: Nominative (subject), Vocative (direct address), Accusative (direct object), Genitive (possession), and Dative (indirect object). It emphasizes that nouns change their endings based on these cases, number (singular/plural), and gender.

### Theme: Sentence Structure
The notes define a sentence as requiring a subject and a predicate. The subject is identified as a noun (someone or something), while the predicate contains the verb and all other information about the subject.
