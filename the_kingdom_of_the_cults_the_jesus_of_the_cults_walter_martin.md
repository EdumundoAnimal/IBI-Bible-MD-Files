# The Kingdom of the Cults: The Jesus of the Cults
**Author:** [[Walter Martin]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This chapter examines the theological variations of 'Jesus' found within various cult movements compared to orthodox Christianity. It highlights common patterns in cult missionary methodology, including the appropriation of Christian terminology, while emphasizing that these groups fundamentally alter the person and nature of Christ. The author identifies specific theological deviations in groups such as the Jehovah's Witnesses, Christian Science, and Mormonism, contrasting these with biblical teachings. The text concludes by asserting the necessity of apologetics and the defense of the faith against what the author characterizes as 'another Jesus.'

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Deviation
The author argues that cults maintain their influence by using familiar Christian terminology while fundamentally distorting the nature, divinity, and uniqueness of Jesus Christ.

### Theme: Missionary Methodology
A characterization of common cult practices including the use of their own 'inspired' translations of the Bible, targeting the youth, and utilizing counseling environments for proselytizing.

### Theme: Biblical Apologetics
The necessity of the Christian church to critically evaluate movements that depart from orthodox doctrine, viewing this as a biblical mandate to defend the original gospel.
