# The Kingdom of the Cults: The Jesus of the Cults
**Author:** [[Walter Martin]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This chapter, 'The Jesus of the Cults,' addresses the theological divergence of various cult groups from traditional Christian doctrine regarding the nature of Jesus Christ. The author argues that these groups often use Christian terminology while subverting the historical and biblical identity of Jesus. The text examines specific instances, such as Christian Science, Jehovah's Witnesses, and Mormonism, contrasting their teachings with standard biblical interpretations. The author emphasizes the importance of biblical apologetics and warns against the deceptive practices he attributes to these movements, advocating for a return to the foundational 'once for all delivered' faith. The text further discusses the historical and scriptural warnings against false prophets and teachers who promote 'another gospel'.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Distortion
The central argument that cults redefine the nature, identity, and mission of Jesus Christ to fit their unique theological frameworks, often contradicting historic, orthodox Christian teachings.

### Theme: Apologetics and Confrontation
The necessity for Christians to actively defend established doctrines and warn against religious movements that the author labels as 'cults' based on their theological deviations.

### Theme: Biblical Authority vs. Counterfeit Doctrine
The text emphasizes the supremacy of the Bible as the benchmark for truth, contrasting it with what it characterizes as the 'perverted' messages of competing religious systems.
