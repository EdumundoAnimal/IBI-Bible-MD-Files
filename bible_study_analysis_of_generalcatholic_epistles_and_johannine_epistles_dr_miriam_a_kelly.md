# Bible Study Analysis of General/Catholic Epistles and Johannine Epistles
**Author:** [[Dr. Miriam A. Kelly]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document provides an overview of the General or Catholic Epistles (James, 1 and 2 Peter, 1, 2, and 3 John, and Jude). It covers authorship, historical background, the occasion of writing, and primary theological contents for each book. Key themes include the relationship between faith and works, the nature of suffering and perseverance, the identification and rebuttal of false teachers/heresy (including Proto-Gnostic teachings), and the importance of ethical living as evidence of true faith. The analysis discusses early church scholarship, internal versus external evidence for dating, and the specific theological emphases of each epistle, such as the divinity and humanity of Christ in John and the apostolic authority in Peter and Jude.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Faith and Works
Explores the relationship between belief and action, arguing that genuine faith must be manifested through moral living and good works, countering the idea that faith can exist in isolation from a changed life.

### Theme: Apostolic Authority
Discusses the importance of established apostolic testimony and the challenge to such authority posed by early heretical groups and teachers within the church.

### Theme: Combatting False Doctrine
Analyzes the various polemics against early heresies, specifically identifying Proto-Gnostic and Docetic tendencies that threatened the understanding of the human and divine nature of Christ.

### Theme: Suffering and Perseverance
Examines the role of suffering in the life of the believer, emphasizing the need for steadfastness and holiness during times of testing or external pressure.
