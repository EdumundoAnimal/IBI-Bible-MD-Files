# The Atonement Debate
**Author:** [[David Hilborn]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This volume, originating from the London Symposium on the Theology of Atonement, explores the evangelical controversy surrounding the doctrine of penal substitution. The text examines the origins of the 'The Lost Message of Jesus' by Steve Chalke and Alan Mann, the subsequent reaction from the Evangelical Alliance, and the historical inquiry into the phrasing of the Evangelical Alliance's Basis of Faith regarding atonement and Christ's death. The discussion centers on whether the death of Christ functions as an objective, penal sacrifice to satisfy divine wrath or if such interpretations are morally problematic, while also mapping the internal debate within evangelicalism concerning traditional orthodoxy versus newer theological reformulations.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Penal Substitutionary Atonement
The theological view that Christ's death was a penal sacrifice, taking upon himself the divine wrath and punishment due to human sin.

### Theme: Evangelical Internal Controversy
The friction within the evangelical community regarding the historical definition of the atonement, sparked by challenges to traditional penal substitution models.

### Theme: Historical Institutional Revisionism
The investigation into how the Evangelical Alliance's Basis of Faith evolved and why specific terminology like 'penal' was omitted in various drafts.
