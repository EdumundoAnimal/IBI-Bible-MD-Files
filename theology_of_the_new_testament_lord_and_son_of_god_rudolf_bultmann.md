# Theology of the New Testament: Lord and Son of God
**Author:** [[Rudolf Bultmann]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text discusses the theological concepts within the Hellenistic Church, focusing specifically on the titles 'Lord' and 'Son of God' as applied to Jesus. Bultmann explores how these concepts were integrated into early Christian liturgy, mission, and worship, examining the influence of both Jewish, Hellenistic, and Gnostic backgrounds. The analysis covers the emergence of the title 'Lord' (Kyrios) in communal worship, its relation to the 'name' of Jesus in rituals like baptism and exorcism, and the development of the 'Son of God' christology in relation to divine sonship and pre-existence.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christology of the Title 'Lord' (Kyrios)
Bultmann analyzes the transition of the title 'Kyrios' from a social designation to a divine title in the Hellenistic Church, noting its central role in liturgy, prayer, and the confession of Jesus' sovereignty.

### Theme: Liturgical Use of 'The Name'
The text highlights the significance of calling upon the 'name' of Jesus in early Christian practices, including baptism and the expulsion of demons, as a means of invoking the presence and power of the Lord.

### Theme: Development of 'Son of God' Theology
The author explores the convergence of various traditions—Jewish messianism, Hellenistic divine man concepts, and Gnostic pre-existence myths—that contributed to the multifaceted understanding of Jesus as the Son of God.
