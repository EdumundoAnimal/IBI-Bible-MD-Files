# Theology of the New Testament: Lord and Son of God
**Author:** [[Rudolf Bultmann]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a segment from Rudolf Bultmann's 'Theology of the New Testament', specifically discussing the concepts of 'Lord' and 'Son of God' within the context of the Hellenistic church and early Christian theology. Bultmann explores the cultic veneration of Jesus as Lord, the significance of baptism and the Eucharist, the attribution of divine titles, and the theological paradoxes regarding Christ's human and divine natures. It examines how early Christians interpreted these concepts through both Hellenistic and Jewish theological frameworks.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Lordship of Christ
The cultic veneration of Jesus as 'Kyrios' (Lord) in the early church, which Bultmann argues is deeply linked to Hellenistic practices and the early church's own liturgical life.

### Theme: The Son of God Doctrine
The theological development of Jesus as 'Son of God', examining how this title evolved from Jewish prophetic tradition to encompass Hellenistic concepts of divinity, virgin birth, and pre-existence.

### Theme: Sacramental Life
The analysis of baptism and the Eucharist as foundational rituals for the early church, serving as methods for initiation into the community and for the 'cultic' experience of the divine presence.
