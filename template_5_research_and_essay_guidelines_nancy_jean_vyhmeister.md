# Template 5: Research and Essay Guidelines
**Author:** [[Nancy Jean Vyhmeister]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Research is defined as a methodical study to collect, evaluate, and analyze evidence to arrive at a solution. Students are guided to begin by defining their topic, designing a methodology, and creating a bibliography. Key analytical processes discussed include evaluation (appraising contributions and limitations), discussion (examining considerations for and against), analysis (taking evidence apart), and synthesis (reconstructing ideas). The document provides criteria for selecting sources, emphasizing the importance of primary sources like the Bible and Vatican II documents, alongside peer-reviewed scholarly journals. It also offers practical advice on writing mechanics, such as avoiding devotional language and using transitional words to improve flow.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Methodological Research
Research is framed as a deliberate process of investigation that moves beyond mere information compilation to analysis and synthesis.

### Theme: Source Evaluation
A critical approach to research requires evaluating the author, publisher, and academic standing of a source before using it, especially when prioritizing scholarly journals over general books.

### Theme: Academic Writing Standards
The text emphasizes the need for objective, sober language, avoiding devotional or informal tone in academic essays, even when dealing with religious topics.

### Theme: Biblical Research Sources
Specific guidance for religion studies highlights the distinction between primary (Vatican II, Bible) and secondary sources (commentary/interpretation).
