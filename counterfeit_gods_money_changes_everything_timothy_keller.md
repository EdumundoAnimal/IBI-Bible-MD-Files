# Counterfeit Gods: Money Changes Everything
**Author:** [[Timothy Keller]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the intersection of greed, materialism, and Christian theology. It uses the narrative of the Yellowstone Club bankruptcy to illustrate naked greed and the misuse of financial instruments. It then transitions to a theological perspective, defining greed as a form of idolatry where money becomes a false master. Keller argues that money provides a false sense of security and identity, blinding people to their own materialism. Using the story of Zacchaeus, the text contrasts worldly wealth with the transforming power of grace, suggesting that true freedom from the power of money comes from reorientation toward God rather than behavioral compliance.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Greed as Idolatry
Greed is framed not just as a behavioral issue, but as a spiritual condition where money replaces God as the source of security, identity, and trust.

### Theme: Blindness to Materialism
The author notes that most people do not self-identify as greedy, as money is often hidden behind other idols like status, power, or approval.

### Theme: The Reorientation of Grace
True change in one's relationship with money originates in a deep encounter with the grace of God, leading to radical generosity rather than legalistic adherence to rules.

### Theme: Deep Idols
Surface-level actions are driven by deeper 'heart' idols, such as the desire for influence, control, or physical comfort, which money is then used to achieve.
