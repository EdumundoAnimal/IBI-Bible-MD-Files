# Spiritual Formation and Evangelical Objections
**Author:** [[Steve L. Porter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores and addresses eight common objections raised by evangelicals against the spiritual formation movement. It systematically evaluates concerns such as spiritual formation being too Catholic, being overly experiential, relying on New Age practices, or being contrary to the sufficiency of Scripture. The author argues that spiritual formation, properly understood, is rooted in biblical sanctification and that extra-biblical insights (like psychology or historical traditions) can be synthesized with biblical authority if evaluated against specific criteria. The text emphasizes that biblical exposition remains paramount while affirming the value of practical disciplines like journaling and silence, provided they are practiced within a biblically informed framework.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Biblical Sufficiency
The central concern that spiritual formation might neglect or contradict the Bible, which the author counters by arguing that biblical authority is the primary lens through which all practices must be validated.

### Theme: Experiential Spirituality
The tension between subjective religious experiences and objective biblical truth, concluding that experiences are valid as long as they facilitate deeper knowledge of and obedience to God as revealed in Scripture.

### Theme: Criteria for Practice
The proposal of a five-point criterion system for evaluating spiritual disciplines—ensuring they are supported by biblical teaching, theological sense, and historical wisdom.
