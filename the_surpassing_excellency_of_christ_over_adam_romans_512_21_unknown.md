# The Surpassing Excellency of Christ Over Adam: Romans 5:12-21
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article examines the Pauline comparison in Romans 5:12-21 between Adam and Christ, asserting that Paul uses this typology to demonstrate the superior work of Christ in redemption. It analyzes the federal headship of both figures, noting that while Adam acts as a representative bringing condemnation, Christ acts as a representative bringing righteousness and life. The author highlights that the passage is not merely about similarities, but about highlighting the 'dis-analogy' between the two: where sin increased, grace superabounded. By examining powers, mandates, acts, and verdicts, the author concludes that the text serves to deepen the understanding of both human sinfulness and the all-encompassing nature of grace in redemptive history.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Federal Headship
The theological concept that both Adam and Christ acted as representatives for humanity, where their singular acts had consequences for all whom they represented.

### Theme: Superabounding Grace
The argument that the redemptive work of Christ is not just equal to the fall of Adam, but vastly exceeds it in its reach and effectiveness, turning the 'dis-analogy' into an argument for Christ's supremacy.

### Theme: Redemptive History
The framing of Romans 5 within the broader narrative of scripture, placing Adam and Christ as bookends to the drama of salvation.

### Theme: Forensic Justification
The legal, forensic nature of the shift from condemnation to justification, as presented through Paul's language in the Epistle to the Romans.
