# An Introduction to Pentecostalism
**Author:** [[Allan Anderson]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document consists of an academic analysis of Oneness Pentecostalism and its theological conflict with traditional Trinitarian doctrine. It examines the historical origins of Oneness theology in North America, including the emergence of the United Pentecostal Church (UPC) and the Pentecostal Assemblies of the World (PAW). The text argues that Oneness theology, which holds that Jesus is the full manifestation of God, creates significant theological and exegetical challenges, particularly regarding the interpersonal relationship between Father and Son, which the author argues is rendered 'illusory' by Oneness doctrine. The analysis explores themes of the Godhead, baptismal theology, the critique of 'separate persons' in the Trinity, and the historical debates regarding 'Second Work' versus 'Finished Work' holiness perspectives within the Pentecostal movement.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Oneness Theology
The argument that God is a single person who manifests as Father, Son, and Spirit, rejecting the traditional concept of three distinct persons in the Godhead.

### Theme: Trinitarian Critique
The author critically analyzes the internal contradictions within Oneness theology, specifically questioning how it handles the relational passages in the New Testament.

### Theme: Historical Context
The text documents the 1916 schism in the Assemblies of God and the subsequent formation of the UPC and PAW as distinct Oneness denominations.

### Theme: Theological Illusoriness
A core argument that Oneness theology reduces the relationship between the Father and the Son to a temporary, non-real illusion, undermining the biblical account of their mutual love.
