# Pneumatology: The Gifts of the Spirit
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Objectives

1. To try and answer some key questions regarding the nature, relevance and use of the gifts of the Spirit in Paul. There are different kinds of gifts, but the same Spirit. There are different kinds of service, but the same Lord. There are different kinds of service, but the same God works all of them in all men. 1 Corinthians 12:4-6

### 1. A Spectrum of Views on the Gifts of the Spirit

What are we actually talking about? 9 gifts of 1 Cor 12 (Esp healing, prophecy, tongues emphasis on miraculous) where anything is a spiritual gift (Moltmann - those fighting for justice, Little more than 'natural abilities', some gifts continue 'teaching' for example (cessationist)). Paul gives several lists containing different types of gifts. 1 Cor 12:8-10: word of wisdom, word of knowledge, faith, gifts of healings, working of miracles, prophecy, discernment of spirits, kinds of tongues, interpretation of tongues. 1 Cor. 12:28: apostles, prophets, teachers, workers of miracles, helps, administration, kinds of tongues. Rom. 12:6-8: prophecy, service, teaching, exhortation, leadership, mercy. Eph. 4:11: apostles, prophets, evangelists, pastors, teachers. 1. Supernatural gifts. 2. For the edification of the church.

### 2. A Theology of Spiritual Gifts: James Dunn

James Dunn: the case for charismata being the visible, evident supernatural workings of God through the Spirit in the church, for the common good. In other words, in all of these the Spirit is immediately perceived in clear, dramatic and supernatural expression - working for the common good.

### 3. Terminology

In 1 Cor 12 for example Paul uses several words of the same phenomena. i. energemata (workings) of God. ii. diakoniai (acts of service) given by the Lod. iii. phanerosis (manifestation) of the Spirit. iv. pneumatika (things of the Spirit the church). v. charismata (gracious gift) granted by the Spirit. So charismata does not necessarily in itself mean 'spiritual gifts' in 1 Cor 12-14 and Romans 12.

### 4. What this means for how we interpret 1 Cor 12-14?

Context: - The Corinthians are full of themselves! - An emphasis on those who are 'spiritual' 'pneumatikoi'. What are the key points of chapter 12? See how this leads up to another ad hoc list list - gifts given by the Spirit as he wills - Apostles, Prophets, teachers, workers of miracles, helps, administration, kinds of tongues. 'The rhetoric of Paul's whole discourse to this point appears deliberately to broaden out the notion of the locus of the Spirit's work' [Turner]. NOT just (as the inner logic of Dunn's argument appears to require) that only immediate and powerful and visible workings of God are spiritual gifts. even 'ordinary' weak 'services' are charismata as well. Thus Turner is questioning the whole separation of charismata as equalling 'supernatural spiritual gifts'. The same essential point is made in Romans 12:6-8. Fee agrees with Turner: the Romans would not have understood charismata as meaning 'spiritual gifts' - but rather varied sorts of gifts that are generously given by God. They are not specifically linked to the Spirit (606).

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Definition of Charismata
The document contrasts the view of James Dunn, who defines charismata as visible, supernatural workings of the Spirit, with the view of Turner and Fee, who argue that the term refers to varied gifts generously given by God, not exclusively linked to supernatural manifestations.

### Theme: Broadening the Locus of the Spirit
The text argues that Paul's discourse in 1 Corinthians 12 is intended to broaden the understanding of the Spirit's work to include 'ordinary' or 'weak' services, rather than restricting it to dramatic, miraculous displays.
