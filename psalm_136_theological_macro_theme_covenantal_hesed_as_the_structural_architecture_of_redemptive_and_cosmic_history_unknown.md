## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The Ancient Near Eastern & Canonical Chair)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants The Masoretic Text (MT) of Psalm 136 is exceptionally stable across codices (Leningradensis B19A, Aleppo Codex), primarily due to its highly formalized, repetitive liturgical refrain: כִּי לְעֹולָם חַסְדֹו (kî lǝʿôlām ḥasdô). ● Dead Sea Scrolls (11QPs^a / 11Q5 Col. XIV–XV): Demonstrates orthographic continuity with minor plene spelling variants (e.g., insertion of vav as a mater lectionis in nominal and verbal forms: כיא לעולם חסדו). The refrain remains verbatim across all 26 stichs, confirming the antiquity of the antiphonal structural architecture. ● Septuagint (LXX Psalm 135): Renders the refrain uniformly as ὅτι εἰς τὸν αἰῶνα τὸ ἔλεος αὐτοῦ (hoti eis ton aiōna to eleos autou). The translation of ḥesed with eleos (mercy/pity) rather than dikaiosynē or charis narrowed the semantic breadth from covenant loyalty/solidarity to affective mercy, establishing a translational trajectory that influenced the Latin Vulgate (quoniam in aeternum misericordia eius). ● Syriac Peshitta & Targum: The Aramaic Targum expands several historical references to harmonize with rabbinic midrashic traditions regarding the crossing of the Red Sea (v. 13 rendered with explicit reference to twelve divided pathways for the twelve tribes), whereas the MT preserves the concise poetic dual construct גְּזָרִים (gǝzārîm, "parts/divisions").

### Grammatical Mechanics

The macro-syntax of Psalm 136 is governed by an asymmetric causal-responsive framework: ● The Imperatival Head (vv. 1–3, 26): The psalm opens and closes with the Hiphil imperative plural הוֹדוּ (hôdû, from the root יָדָה, yādâ). In the Hiphil, yādâ denotes not mere psychological gratitude, but public, covenantal confession and formal acknowledgment of divine sovereignty and acts. ● The Participial Spine (vv. 4–25): The vast majority of the colon-A lines do not utilize finite verbs; instead, they employ the preposition לְ (lǝ-, "to/for") prefixed to an active participle acting as a substantival apposition to the object of praise: ○ לְעֹשֵׂה (lǝʿōśēh, v. 4): "To the [One] making..." ○ לְרֹקַע (lǝrōqaʿ, v. 6): "To the [One] pounding out/stretching..." ○ לְמַכֵּה (lǝmakkēh, vv. 10, 17): "To the [One] striking down..." ○ לְגֹזֵר (lǝgōzēr, v. 13): "To the [One] dividing..." ○ לְמוֹלִיךְ (lǝmôlîk, v. 16): "To the [One] leading/causing to walk..." ○ לְנֹתֵן (lǝnōtēn, v. 25): "To the [One] giving..." ● The Causative Refrain (kî causal clause): Every colon-B consists of the causal conjunction כִּי (kî, "for/because"), the prepositional temporal construct לְעֹולָם (lǝʿôlām, "to eternity / age-enduring"), and the singular noun חַסְדֹו (ḥasdô, "his steadfast covenant love"). Grammatically, the kî functions as a structural ground clause: the cosmos does not merely exist by divine power, nor does Israel survive by historical coincidence; every physical and historical reality is causally sustained by and rooted in the divine ḥesed.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Covenantal Hesed
Hesed is defined as binding obligation, relational loyalty, and unmerited constancy within a covenant (bǝrît), rather than mere kindness or mercy. It is the metaphysical foundation of the universe.

### Theme: Antiphonal Liturgy
The psalm functions as a responsorial litany where the leader chants historical or cosmic actions and the congregation supplies the immutable theological key (kî lǝʿôlām ḥasdô), forcing the worshiper to interpret all reality through covenant loyalty.

### Theme: Divine Violence as Covenantal Justice
The destruction of oppressors (Pharaoh, Sihon, Og) is not seen as raw violence but as the necessary dismantling of imperial totalitarianism to liberate the enslaved, serving as an objective prerequisite for the liberation of the victim.
