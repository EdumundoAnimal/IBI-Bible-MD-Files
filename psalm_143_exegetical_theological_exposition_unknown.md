# Psalm 143: Exegetical-Theological Exposition
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### PHASE 1: THE EXEGETICAL NETWORK (The First-Century Chair)

1. Linguistic & Syntax Report Textual Variants (BHS & Dead Sea Scrolls Apparatus) ● Superscription (mizmôr lədāwid): The Masoretic Text (MT) preserves the simple Davidic attribution. The Septuagint (LXX, Psalm 142) appends the historical notation: Hote auton ho huios katadiōkei ("When his son [Absalom] pursued him"). While not part of the proto-MT consonantal stratum, this early Alexandrian tradition attests to a longstanding Jewish hermeneutical association with the crisis of 2 Samuel 15–18. ● Psalm 143:9 (’ēlekā kissîtî): The MT reading kissîtî (literally "unto you I have covered/hidden") presents a syntactical crux. ○ BHS Apparatus / Critical Emendations: Many editors propose emending to ḥāsîtî ("I have taken refuge," from ḥāsāh), supported by several medieval Hebrew manuscripts, the Peshitta, and the Targum, which harmonizes with standard Psalmic idiom (cf. Ps 7:1; 11:1; 31:1). ○ Dead Sea Scrolls Evidence (11QPsᵃ / 4QPs): Fragmentary evidence from Qumran reflects consonantal stability around the root k-s-h, suggesting a pregnant, elliptical construction in ancient Northwest Semitic: "To you I have fled for covering/refuge" or "Unto you I have hidden myself" (reflexive-passive sense). The MT reading should be retained as the lectio difficilior. ● Psalm 143:10 (bə’ereṣ mîšôr vs. bə’ōraḥ mîšôr): MT reads bə’ereṣ mîšôr ("in a land/ground of levelness/uprightness"). LXX renders en tē gē tē eutheia. Certain manuscripts and parallel liturgical traditions suggest ’ōraḥ ("path"), yet ’ereṣ is textually secure across codices (Leningradensis, Aleppo) and underscores an eschatological domain of ethical and physical security rather than merely an individual track.

### Grammatical Mechanics

● Psalm 143:1–2: ○ Syntax of the Imperatives: The opening utilizes four imperative/cohortative requests: šəma‘ (Qal imperative, "Hear!"), hǎ’ăzînāh (Hiphil imperative with paragogic heh, indicating urgency: "Give ear!"), ‘ănēnî (Qal imperative with 1cs suffix: "Answer me!"). ○ The Prepositional Modifiers: be’ěmunātəkā ("in your faithfulness/firmness") and bəs˓idqātəkā ("in your righteousness"). The preposition bə functions instrumentally and locatively: the psalmist appeals not to his own integrity, but to the sphere of God's covenantal fidelity. ○ The Subjunctive Prohibition (wə’al-tābô’ bəmišpāṭ): The conjunction wə with the negative particle ’al plus the Qal imperfect 2ms of bô’ creates a vetitive (prohibitive) clause: "And do not enter into judicial litigation with your servant." mišpāṭ here designates a formal forensic assembly or trial. ○ Universal Negative Causal Clause: kî lō’-yiṣdaq ləpānêkā kōl-ḥāy. The causal particle kî introduces the legal ground: lō’ (absolute negation) + yiṣdaq (Qal imperfect 3ms of ṣādēq, "to be just/righteous, declared innocent") + ləpānêkā (preposition lə with plural construct pānîm and 2ms suffix: "in your sight/presence") + kōl-ḥāy (universal totality construct: "all living"). ● Psalm 143:3–4: ○ Causal Sequence of Persecution: kî rādap ’ôyēb napšî (Qal perfect: "For the enemy has pursued my life"); dikkā’ lā’āreṣ ḥayyātî (Piel perfect: "He has crushed to the ground my vitality"). The verbal shift from perfects describing historic devastation to the consecutive imperfect wattit‘aṭṭēp (Qal imperfect with waw consecutive 3fs from ‘āṭap, "and my spirit enveloped itself / grew faint within me") charts the internal psychological collapse precipitated by external existential threat. ● Psalm 143:10: ○ Double Object Construction: lammədēnî la‘ăśôt rəṣônekā (Piel imperative 2ms with 1cs suffix: "Teach me" + preposition lə with Qal infinitive construct ‘āśôt: "to do" + rəṣônekā, construct noun with 2ms suffix: "your sovereign will/delight"). ○ Pneumatological Jussive: rûḥăkā ṭôbāh tanḥēnî (Nominal phrase with substantive adjective: "Your good Spirit" + Hiphil imperfect/jussive 3fs with 1cs suffix from nāḥāh: "may it lead me").

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Justification by Grace
The document argues that Psalm 143:2 serves as the absolute negation of human merit, establishing that no living person can be declared righteous before God on the basis of personal performance. This is presented as the scriptural bedrock for Pauline soteriology.

### Theme: Pneumatological Guidance
The text highlights the 'Good Spirit' (rûaḥ ṭôbāh) in verse 10 as the agent of sanctification, moving the petitioner from the exhaustion of self-justification to moral alignment with God's will.

## 4. Key Verbatim Assertions & Quotes
