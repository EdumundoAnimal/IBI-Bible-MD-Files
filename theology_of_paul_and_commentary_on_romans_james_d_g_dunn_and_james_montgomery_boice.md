# Theology of Paul and Commentary on Romans
**Author:** [[James D. G. Dunn and James Montgomery Boice]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document contains theological analysis and commentary regarding Paul's writings in the Epistle to the Romans, specifically focusing on Romans 7:7-25. It covers themes of sin, the law, and human nature. The texts explore the role of the law in provoking sin, the function of conscience, and the internal struggle described by Paul in his personal reflection. It also breaks down individual Ten Commandments as a lens for modern moral application.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The Paradox of the Law
The law is viewed as holy and good, yet it acts as a catalyst for sin by highlighting forbidden desires, essentially provoking the human propensity toward transgression.

### Theme: The 'I' in Romans 7
An analysis of the personal pronouns used by Paul, debating whether the description of internal struggle is biographical, a pedagogical tool, or an existential reflection on the human condition.

### Theme: Sin as a Power
Sin is characterized not merely as an act, but as a manipulative, indwelling power that uses the law to deceive and eventually produce spiritual death.
