# The Atonement Controversy in Welsh Theological Literature & Debate 1707-1841
**Author:** [[Owen Thomas]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This work explores the historical development of theological thought in Welsh Calvinistic Methodism, specifically focusing on the controversy surrounding the extent and nature of the Atonement. The text analyzes how 19th-century Welsh theologians grappled with shifts toward more liberal, Arminian, and 'modified' Calvinist viewpoints, contrasting them with the traditional doctrines established by early leaders like John Elias. It highlights the influence of American evangelism, liberal education, and the personal convictions of key figures such as Lewis Edwards. The introduction details the decline of strict Calvinist adherence in favor of views that emphasized the universal offer of the Gospel and human responsibility, while still attempting to maintain a framework of divine sovereignty.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Calvinism and the Atonement
The central struggle to define the extent of Christ's sacrifice (limited vs. universal) within the Welsh Calvinistic Methodist tradition during the 19th century.

### Theme: Influence of Liberal Education
The impact of secular education and higher criticism on traditional Reformed theology, leading to a shift away from strict adherence to the 1823 Confession of Faith.

### Theme: American Revivalism
The introduction of American evangelistic methods and Arminian-leaning revival theology into Welsh churches, which challenged established Calvinist orthodoxies.
