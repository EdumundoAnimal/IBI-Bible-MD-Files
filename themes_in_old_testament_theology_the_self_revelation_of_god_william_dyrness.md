# Themes in Old Testament Theology: The Self-Revelation of God
**Author:** [[William Dyrness]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document explores the self-revelation of God within the Old Testament, emphasizing that revelation is not merely propositional but occurs through historical events, divine speech, and personal encounters. It highlights key instances of God's self-revelation to figures like Abraham, Moses, and the Israelites, noting that these revelations are fundamentally redemptive and establish a covenantal relationship. The text discusses the significance of God's names (Jahweh, El Shaddai), the nature of anthropomorphisms, and the ongoing tension between God's transcendence and His immanent personal presence. It concludes that God's revelation always calls for a concrete life of obedience and relationship, serving as a medium for redemption.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Redemptive Nature of Revelation
Revelation in the Old Testament is not merely for information but is tied to God's redemptive purpose, aiming to restore the relationship broken by sin.

### Theme: Divine Transcendence vs. Immanence
The tension between God's holiness/transcendence and His willingness to make himself known through human language, anthropomorphisms, and personal encounters.

### Theme: Covenantal Relationship
The argument that God's self-revelation is foundational to the covenant relationship, requiring a response of faith and obedience from His people.
