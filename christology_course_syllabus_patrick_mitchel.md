# Christology Course Syllabus
**Author:** [[Patrick Mitchel]]

## 1. Verbatim Source Text
### Aims

At the end of this course you should be able to: understand the theological content of important biblical passages concerning the person of Jesus; reflect on how the various pictures of Jesus in the New Testament fit together within contexts the overall story of the bible; think through implications of the biblical claims about Jesus for personal faith and others practical ministry; better equipped to handle and interpret the Bible, especially in terms of its diversity and unity, for putting together an overall Christology; understand, and interact critically with, different interpretations of the biblical material in church history, both ancient and modern.

### Course Purpose Statement

Our primary purpose together is to know Jesus better and worship him more deeply. From this basis, we will discern some implications for our lives in the world and in the Church.

### Course Format and the Student's Role

This course should promote thought - it is not just about the transfer of lots of facts and information from teacher to student. For this to happen I want to make space for active learning - to discuss questions that may arise but also to focus on practical implications of the truths we are studying together. So lectures will be supplemented by regular seminars, as well as discussion in class. This means that for the course to be successful, the student, needs to come prepared to engage thoughtfully with the lectures, with other students and especially with the reading for the seminars. As we do so, we will learn together more of Jesus and of how what we are learning applies to life outside the classroom.

### Course Description

Our primary purpose together is to know Jesus better and worship him more deeply. To do this we will examine biblical and historical material relating to the person of Christ. Our main focus will be to explore in some depth relevant biblical texts. One aim is for us to be able to put together a united 'big picture' of Jesus in the New Testament through appreciating the many diverse portraits contained in the NT. We will engage with diverse theological perspectives throughout the course, both ancient and modern. Since just 'knowing' what the bible teaches is not enough, throughout this course we will build bridges to our lives outside IBI as we think through how what we are studying impacts our lives and service within the church. This is the emphasis of the New Testament and so it will be ours as well.

### Assignments for AT201-T: Biblical Theology: Christology

Students will be able to choose to submit work for 2 seminars. In each piece of submitted work they will be required to include written notes of 1,500 words on a Christological passage or topic studied in a seminar during the course. Interacting with relevant reading, provide detailed exegetical notes and discussion (with footnotes and bibliography) on key Christological words and concepts within the passage leading to some conclusions of what the author is saying about the person and role of Jesus. (c 1000 words) A concluding discussion of some implications of the passage. This may focus on personal faith a specific way you may connect it to church ministry, or other areas. Be creative! But make sure to relate your discussion in this section to points made in the exegesis. (c. 500 words)

### Assignments for AT312-T: Biblical Theology: Christology

Students will submit work for one seminar topic held during the course (topics will be divided up amongst the students) They will be required to present a class seminar on their topic of study. They will hand in written notes of 3000 words. In these notes students will discuss, the selected Christological passage (or topic) - Exegesis (examining the meaning of key words and concepts in relevant texts and how they fit within the overall theology of the book) and discuss different Christological views among contemporary authors - arguing for their own interpretation of the passage / topic. (2,400 words. 70%) The paper should also conclude with some theological and practical reflection on the personal significance of the passage for personal and church life today. (600 words, 20%) The actual seminar presentation (content, how presented, handling of discussion etc) will be assessed by peer and self-evaluation (evaluation forms will be provided) Final evaluation is by the teacher. (10%)

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Diversity and Unity
The course emphasizes the need to construct a 'big picture' of Jesus by synthesizing the diverse portraits found throughout the New Testament, moving beyond mere knowledge of facts to a deeper theological understanding.

### Theme: Practical Application of Theology
A central theme is the bridge between academic study and life in the church. The assignments explicitly require students to connect exegetical findings to personal faith and practical ministry, reflecting the belief that theology must impact the life of the believer.

### Theme: Critical Engagement with History
The syllabus mandates interaction with both ancient and modern interpretations of Christology, requiring students to evaluate different scholarly perspectives and historical developments in how Jesus has been understood.
