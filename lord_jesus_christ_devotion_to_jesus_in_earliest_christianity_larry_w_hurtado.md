# Lord Jesus Christ: Devotion to Jesus in Earliest Christianity
**Author:** [[Larry W. Hurtado]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document is a historical and theological examination of early Christian devotion to Jesus as reflected in texts such as the Epistle to the Hebrews, Colossians, and other early Christian writings. It explores how early Christians navigated Jewish traditions of monotheism while formulating a high Christology that elevated Jesus to a divine status, including his role as high priest, redeemer, and the mediator of salvation. The author analyzes the emergence of proto-orthodoxy, the use of the Old Testament to support Christological claims, and how these beliefs were practiced in early Christian liturgy and devotion.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: High Christology
The theological development within early Christianity that ascribed divine attributes and status to Jesus, positioning him as superior to angels and the mediator of God's redemptive work.

### Theme: Proto-orthodoxy and Diversity
The argument that early Christianity contained a diverse range of beliefs and practices, with a emerging 'proto-orthodox' trend seeking to harmonize these traditions, particularly through a standardized canon.

### Theme: Jewish-Christian Synthesis
The creative appropriation and reinterpretation of Jewish Scriptures (Old Testament) and traditions (such as Melchizedek and priesthood) to articulate the significance of Jesus without abandoning monotheistic foundations.
