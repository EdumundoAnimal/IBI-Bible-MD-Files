# Lord Jesus Christ: Devotion to Jesus in Earliest Christianity
**Author:** [[Larry W. Hurtado]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text provides an academic analysis of the Epistle to the Hebrews and its role in shaping early Christian devotion to Jesus. It emphasizes how the text asserts Jesus' superiority over angels, the Aaronic priesthood, and the old covenant, presenting him as a transcendent high priest. The author argues that Hebrews utilizes various scriptural traditions, including the Melchizedek figure, to substantiate the belief in Jesus' divine standing. Furthermore, the analysis highlights the theological themes of redemption, the redemptive work of Jesus, and the practical exhortations for Christian faith and endurance. Later sections address Pauline texts, specifically Colossians and the nature of christological hymns in early Christian worship.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Christological Superiority
The argument that the Epistle to the Hebrews defines Jesus as superior to angels and the old covenant priesthood, establishing his unique divine status.

### Theme: Melchizedekian Typology
The strategic use of the figure of Melchizedek to ground Jesus' high priestly role in scripture, independent of the Aaronic line.

### Theme: Early Christian Devotion
The historical transition toward worshipping Jesus, characterized by rituals, prayers, and beliefs that centralize his role in salvation.
