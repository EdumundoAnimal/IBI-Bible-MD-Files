# Comparative Analysis: Matthew vs. Mark Narratives
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Important Differences 1. Canaanite (Matthew) v. Syrophoenician (Mark) Canaanites were the old (non-existent) enemies of Israel. This would not be implicit in the term Syrophoenician (Syrian Phoenicia). Matthew may be trying to use as derogatory a term as possible so the impact of the exorcism is greater. Jesus heals even the old hated enemies of Israel. 2. Son of David This term is used by the woman in Matthew. It is a Jewish term which would be understood by Jews as a messianic designation. It is also a huge leap of faith for a Gentile to make. It is interesting that Matthew, writing to the Jews to demonstrate the coming of Messiah in fulfilment of prophecy, uses this term. Jesus also ministers to what the Jews would see as the worst of people. 3. The woman kneels This implies worship. Matthew may be trying to emphasise the fact that a Gentile has accepted Jesus for Who He is. She also calls Jesus 'Lord'. This may not be that significant. 4. The woman is condemned more in Matthew than in Mark Again, this may relate to Matthew making a point about the Jews rejecting and a Gentile accepting. Despite being rebutted, she perseveres. Shows her total acceptance and faith? The disciples' remarks could actually be positive here - they could be asking Jesus to send her away by healing her. 5. Jesus commends her faith Matthew could be contrasting her with the Pharisees. How does this story in these two versions illustrate the principle of reading the gospels horizontally and vertically?

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Horizontal Reading of the Gospels
Comparing accounts of the same story across different books of the Bible to identify thematic differences and authorial intent, such as Matthew's focus on Jewish fulfillment versus Mark's Gentile context.

### Theme: Authorial Intent in Matthew
The argument that Matthew uses specific descriptors, such as 'Canaanite' and 'Son of David', to reinforce Jewish messianic expectations and to highlight the irony of a Gentile demonstrating greater faith than the Pharisees.
