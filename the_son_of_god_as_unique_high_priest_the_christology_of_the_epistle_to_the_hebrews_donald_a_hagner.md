# The Son of God as Unique High Priest: The Christology of the Epistle to the Hebrews
**Author:** [[Donald A. Hagner]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the Christology of the Epistle to the Hebrews. It emphasizes the duality of Christ as both fully divine and fully human, acting as the unique High Priest. The work argues that Jesus's humanity allows him to be a sympathetic mediator, while his divinity ensures the efficacy of his sacrifice for the sins of the community. Key concepts include the Melchizedekian priesthood, the superiority of the New Covenant over the Old, and the practical application of Christ's role in the lives of believers.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: The High Priestly Role of Christ
Christ is defined as a unique High Priest who bridges the gap between humanity and God, necessitating both his divine nature and his human experience of suffering.

### Theme: Humanity and Divinity Duality
The author argues that for Christ to serve as a mediator on behalf of humanity, he must possess both human nature to relate to the community and divine nature to provide eternal atonement.

### Theme: Supersessionism of the New Covenant
The text discusses how the New Covenant, inaugurated through Christ's sacrifice, replaces the ineffective Old Covenant, rendering it obsolete through the finality of Christ's work.
