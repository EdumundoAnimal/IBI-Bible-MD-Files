## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document provides an exegetical, historical, and theological analysis of Psalm 127. It focuses on the structural architecture of the psalm, contrasting autonomous human striving with covenantal reliance on divine providence. The report includes linguistic analysis of the Masoretic text, grammatical mechanics, and explores the theological motifs of the 'builder,' 'heritage,' and 'rest.' It also maps the psalm across the canonical landscape, drawing connections between the Davidic Covenant and Christological fulfillment, while contrasting ancient pagan concepts of self-sufficiency with modern secular technocratic worldviews.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Divine Providence vs. Autonomous Striving
The central argument that human labor, whether in construction, city-watching, or child-rearing, is futile ('shaw') unless underpinned by YHWH's sovereignty.

### Theme: Covenantal Teleology
The transition from physical structures in the Old Testament to spiritual realities in the New Testament, where Christ is the true Builder of His Church.

### Theme: Sabbath Rest as Theological Trust
Rest is not viewed as lost productivity but as a physical confession of reliance on God's active, continuous cosmic governance.

### Theme: Generativity and Heritage
Children are repositioned from economic liabilities or life-project assets into 'nakhalah' (grace-gift) and divine 'arrows' for kingdom work.
