# The Theology and Provenance of John 15:1-17
**Author:** [[Fernando F. Segovia]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article critically examines the literary and theological context of John 15:1-17. Segovia argues that these chapters do not form part of the original farewell discourse found in John 13:31-14:31 but represent a later addition intended to address internal community crises. The author analyzes the 'redactional approach,' suggesting that an editor or disciple-redactor added these verses to combat false doctrine and theological deviations (specifically docetic-like tendencies) by re-emphasizing the necessity of 'abiding' in Jesus and adhering to the commandment of mutual love. The study concludes that John 15:1-17 mirrors the situational concerns found in 1 John, aiming to differentiate 'true' believers from those who have strayed from the community's original understanding of Christ.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Literary Redaction
The argument that John 15:1-17 was inserted into the Gospel narrative as a later addition by an editor to resolve community-specific problems, rather than being part of the original discourse.

### Theme: Sitz im Leben
The author relates the composition of John 15:1-17 to the specific socio-theological context of the Johannine community, drawing parallels with the situation addressed in 1 John.

### Theme: Abiding in the Vine
The theological imagery used to enforce community cohesion and doctrinal adherence, warning that failure to 'abide' (remain in the true vine) results in spiritual exclusion.

### Theme: Internal Christian Controversy
The text posits that the discourse addresses internal discord regarding Christology and ethical behavior rather than an external conflict with the synagogue.
