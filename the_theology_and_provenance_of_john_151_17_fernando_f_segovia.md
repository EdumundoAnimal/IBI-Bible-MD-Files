# The Theology and Provenance of John 15:1-17
**Author:** [[Fernando F. Segovia]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This article addresses the literary and theological status of John 15:1-17 within the Johannine Gospel. The author examines the problem of the farewell discourse's apparent lack of unity, noting that many scholars suggest these chapters were added later. Segovia reviews various critical approaches—historicizing, transpositional, and redactional—ultimately arguing that the unit of 15:1-17 serves as an important addition to address internal community crises. The author analyzes the text's structure, focusing on the metaphor of the vine and branches, and connects the themes of 'abiding' and 'love' to a specific sectarian or intra-community context, arguing that the passage reinforces the need for believers to remain in the hierarchy of love and in unity with the original Johannine tradition.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Literary Unity of John
The debate regarding whether John 15:1-17 constitutes an original part of the farewell discourse or a later redactional addition.

### Theme: Redactional Approach
The theory that John 15:1-17 was added by an author or disciple-redactor to address specific theological concerns after the initial gospel composition.

### Theme: Sitz im Leben
The argument that the inclusion of this passage is a response to internal 'inner-Christian' or 'intra-church' controversies, specifically regarding faithfulness and adherence to the love command.

### Theme: Theology of Abiding and Love
The interpretive framework defining 'abiding' in Jesus as the practical enactment of mutual love, which serves as a test of authentic belief within the community.
