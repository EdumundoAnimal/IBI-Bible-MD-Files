# Systematic Theology: The Holy Trinity
**Author:** [[Louis Berkhof]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This excerpt from Systematic Theology by Louis Berkhof explores the doctrine of the Holy Trinity. It traces the historical development of the doctrine, starting from pre-Reformation contexts and the challenges posed by early heresies such as Monarchianism and the views of the Arians. Berkhof details the Trinitarian nature of God, examining the distinct roles and eternal relations between the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit. He provides an extensive exegetical basis for the deity of each person of the Trinity, citing biblical evidence, and addresses the theological concepts of essential vs. personal properties. The text further discusses the economic Trinity versus the ontological Trinity, clarifying that the external works of God are inseparable yet reveal the distinct subsistence of the three persons. The study culminates in an overview of the Holy Spirit's role in redemption and the Church, emphasizing the unity and distinctness of the three persons in the Godhead.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Ontological vs. Economic Trinity
Distinguishes between God's inner eternal nature (ontological) and His external works in the history of redemption (economic), noting that while roles differ in the latter, the divine essence remains undivided.

### Theme: Divine Subsistence
Explains how the one essence of God subsists in three distinct persons, maintaining that each person possesses the whole divine essence without being a separate entity or dividing the Godhead.

### Theme: Historical Heresies
Analyzes the impact of historical movements like Sabellianism, Arianism, and the views of early Church fathers on the formulation of the Trinitarian dogma.
