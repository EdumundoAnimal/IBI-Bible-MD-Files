# Systematic Theology: God in Three Persons
**Author:** [[Wayne Grudem]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Chapter 14: God in Three Persons: The Trinity. This chapter explores the biblical basis for the doctrine of the Trinity, defining it as God eternally existing as three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit. It addresses the progressive revelation of this doctrine from the Old Testament to the New Testament, examines the Filioque controversy, and discusses the importance of this doctrine for the Christian faith, particularly regarding atonement and the nature of God's relational being.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Doctrine of the Trinity
God eternally exists as three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and each person is fully God, and there is one God.

### Theme: Progressive Revelation
The argument that while the doctrine is not explicitly named in the Old Testament, there are plural indicators in Scripture that culminate in the clear New Testament revelation of the Trinity.

### Theme: Filioque Controversy
A historical theological dispute regarding whether the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father alone or from the Father and the Son, affecting the unity of the church.

### Theme: Atonement and Deity
The necessity of the full deity of Christ for the efficacy of the atonement, arguing that if Christ were a creature, he could not provide salvation for humanity.
