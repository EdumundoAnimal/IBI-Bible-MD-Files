# The Whole Gospel: Oneness Pentecostal Perspectives on Christian Initiation
**Author:** [[David K. Bernard]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This paper explores the theological framework of Oneness Pentecostal perspectives regarding Christian initiation, centering on the interpretation of Acts 2:38. The author argues that true biblical salvation comprises repentance, water baptism in the name of Jesus Christ, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit evidenced by speaking in tongues. The paper provides a historical analysis of figures such as Charles Parham, William Seymour, and William Durham, examining their contributions to the understanding of the baptism of the Spirit as the standard for Christian salvation. It also discusses the historical transition within Pentecostal movements toward recognizing Acts 2:38 as the normative experience for new believers.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Acts 2:38 as Normative Salvation
The central argument that Acts 2:38 provides the comprehensive, mandatory framework for Christian conversion, consisting of three essential steps: repentance, water baptism in the name of Jesus, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit.

### Theme: Baptism of the Holy Spirit
The understanding that the Holy Spirit is an essential element of full salvation, typically accompanied by the sign of speaking in tongues, serving as a seal for the believer and a protection against the Tribulation.

### Theme: Historical Development of Pentecostal Doctrine
A trace of how various early Pentecostal leaders and organizations, including the Azusa Street Mission and early UPCI, struggled with or coalesced around the doctrine of full salvation versus separate experiences of justification and sanctification.
