# THE WHOLE GOSPEL: Oneness Pentecostal Perspectives on Christian Initiation
**Author:** [[David K. Bernard]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The document, titled 'The Whole Gospel: Oneness Pentecostal Perspectives on Christian Initiation', was presented at the 30th Annual Meeting of the Society for Pentecostal Studies in 2001. It examines the foundational doctrine of Acts 2:38, which encompasses repentance, water baptism in the name of Jesus Christ, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit as essential steps for Christian salvation. Bernard explores the historical development of these beliefs within Oneness Pentecostalism, including the views of figures like Charles F. Parham, William J. Seymour, and William Durham. The paper addresses debates surrounding the 'trinitarian formula' versus the 'Jesus Name' baptism and explores how these theological perspectives relate to broader Christian understandings of salvation, initiation, and the baptism of the Holy Spirit.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Acts 2:38 Paradigm
The foundational scripture for Oneness Pentecostal salvation, argued as the normative pattern for Christian initiation involving repentance, baptism in the name of Jesus, and receiving the Holy Spirit.

### Theme: Baptismal Formula
The theological dispute regarding the necessity of baptizing in the name of 'Jesus Christ' rather than the Trinitarian formula of 'Father, Son, and Holy Ghost,' based on the authority of early church practice.

### Theme: History of Oneness Pentecostalism
A trace of the origins and internal development of the movement, focusing on the contributions and varying perspectives of key early leaders such as Charles Parham, William Seymour, and William Durham.
