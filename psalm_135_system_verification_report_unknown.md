## 1. Cleaned Document Text
Psalm 135 is a post-exilic liturgical hymn serving as an anthological masterwork that weaves together classical biblical texts to ground a Second Temple community in theological certainty. It emphasizes Yahweh's sovereign aseity, covenantal election, and polemical supremacy over lifeless, man-made idols. The text provides a linguistic and syntax report of textual variants, lexical profiles of key Hebrew terms, and a concentric (chiastic) structural analysis. Furthermore, it incorporates primary academic commentary, historical-cultural context regarding ANE polemics, and a canonical intertextual tapestry. The homiletical section applies these findings to modern contexts, specifically warning against modern secular idols—digital and algorithmic systems—that fragment human identity, contrasting them with the life-giving nature of the Creator.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Polemical Monotheism
The text asserts Yahweh's absolute ontological supremacy over all manufactured deities, characterizing idols as works of human hands that are spiritually dead, blind, and mute.

### Theme: The Law of Spiritual Assimilation
Based on Psalm 135:18, this principle states that individuals inevitably reflect the nature of their ultimate object of worship; worshipping lifeless systems leads to human fragmentation and spiritual atrophy.

### Theme: Sovereign Aseity
The theological belief that God is self-existent and possesses absolute freedom to act in creation and history, unconstrained by external forces or tribal geography.
