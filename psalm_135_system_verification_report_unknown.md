### Lexical Profiles

səgullāh, v. 4: Root signifies personal private property, sovereign treasure, royal possession exempt from common distribution. In ANE legal documents (Ugaritic sglt, Akkadian sikiltu), it designates a king's personal asset over against state property. Used covenantally in Exodus 19:5, Deuteronomy 7:6, 14:2, 26:18, and Malachi 3:17. nā‘îm, v. 3: Delightful, intrinsically lovely, aesthetically and morally harmonious. The predicate refers either to the act of praise or the Name itself (ם ש ה הו ְי); syntactically, it echoes Psalm 133:1, reinforcing covenantal beatitude. ’aṣabbê, v. 15: Plural construct of ’eṣeb, from the root meaning "to shape, carve, labor," but carrying semantic undertones of grief, pain, and fabrication. Translates not merely as "statues," but as "manufactured fabrications that produce futility and sorrow" (cf. Isaiah 44:9–20). rūaḥ, v. 17: Breath, wind, spirit, dynamic life-force. In context, it is the animating energy derived exclusively from the Creator (Gen 2:7; Ps 104:29–30). Its absolute absence in the idols highlights their ontological deadness.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Polemical Monotheism
The document argues that Psalm 135 serves as a polemic against pagan idolatry, specifically the Mesopotamian Mīs Pî ritual, by contrasting the living, breathing God of Israel with the lifeless, manufactured idols of the nations.

### Theme: Sovereign Aseity
The text emphasizes that God possesses unconditioned aseity and eternal goodness, acting with absolute freedom across creation and history, independent of human manipulation or external constraints.

### Theme: Law of Spiritual Assimilation
Based on Psalm 135:18, the document posits an anthropological law: 'You become what you worship.' Trusting in lifeless, manufactured systems leads to spiritual atrophy, while worshiping the living God leads to flourishing.
