# The New International Dictionary of the Christian Church
**Author:** [[J. D. Douglas]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This volume serves as a comprehensive dictionary for the Christian Church, covering historical, theological, and doctrinal concepts. Specifically, it explores major entries such as 'Trent' and 'Trinity'. The entry for 'Trent' provides an overview of the Council of Trent, detailing the debates regarding Scripture and tradition, the rejection of the Pelagian heresy, and the subsequent Catholic doctrines formalized in the 16th century. The entry for 'Trinity' examines the biblical and historical development of the doctrine of the Trinity, defining the belief in the Triune God, the equality of the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit, and the historical debates against Unitarian and modalistic interpretations. It also includes biographical and theological context for thinkers like Ernst Troeltsch.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Council of Trent
An ecclesiastical assembly that aimed to solidify Roman Catholic dogma, define the canon of Scripture, and address the theological challenges posed by the Reformation.

### Theme: Doctrine of the Trinity
The foundational Christian belief in one God who exists as three distinct persons—Father, Son, and Holy Spirit—maintained against various historical heretical interpretations.

### Theme: Historical Theology
The analytical study of how Christian doctrine has been articulated, defended, and documented through councils, scholarly debate, and historical periods.
