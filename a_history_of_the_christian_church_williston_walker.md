# A History of the Christian Church
**Author:** [[Williston Walker]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The text documents the early ecclesiastical challenges faced by the Roman Empire and the Christian Church under Constantine, focusing specifically on the Donatist schism in North Africa and the Arian controversy. The narrative details how Constantine sought unity in the empire but was confronted with internal theological divisions. In North Africa, the Donatists challenged the validity of sacraments performed by clergy who had surrendered scripture under persecution. Meanwhile, the Arian controversy arose in Alexandria, pitting the views of Alexander against Arius regarding the nature of Christ, specifically whether the Son was co-eternal with the Father. The text further chronicles the political maneuvering involving bishops, councils at Tyre and Jerusalem, and the shifting exile and restoration of Athanasius, reflecting the complex interplay between imperial authority and emerging dogmatic orthodoxies in the 4th century.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Ecclesiastical Unity and Imperial Policy
The Roman Empire under Constantine viewed the Christian Church as a potential unifying force, leading to active imperial intervention in theological disputes and schisms to maintain social and religious order.

### Theme: The Arian Controversy
A fundamental theological crisis concerning the nature of Christ; Arius argued that the Son was a created being and not co-eternal with the Father, while his opponents defended the divinity and eternal nature of the Son.

### Theme: Sacramental Validity and The Donatist Schism
A crisis regarding whether the efficacy of sacraments depended on the moral purity of the officiating clergy, particularly those who had lapsed during the Diocletian persecutions.
