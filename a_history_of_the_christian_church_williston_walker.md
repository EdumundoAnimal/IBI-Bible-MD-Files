# A History of the Christian Church
**Author:** [[Williston Walker]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This text covers significant historical theological conflicts, specifically the Donatist controversy in North Africa and the Arian controversy centered in Alexandria. It discusses how these internal disputes threatened the unity of the church under Constantine and his successors. The text details the intellectual origins of Arianism, citing the influence of Origen and Lucian of Antioch on Arius, and describes the subsequent Nicene debates, the exile and reinstatement of figures like Athanasius, and the political involvement of the Roman Emperors in doctrinal decision-making. The final section examines the reign of Constantine's sons and the continued, often violent, friction regarding Nicene orthodoxy.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Imperial Intervention in Theology
The Roman state, under Constantine and his heirs, increasingly intervened in internal ecclesiastical disputes to preserve political unity, often resulting in forced exiles and mandates regarding doctrine.

### Theme: Development of Trinitarian Doctrine
The intellectual conflict between Arianism, which argued for the derived nature of the Son, and the emerging Nicene orthodoxy, which affirmed the full divinity and co-eternity of the Son, served as the crucible for core Christian Christological definitions.

### Theme: Ecclesiastical Schism
The Donatist controversy illustrates how the question of church purity and the validity of sacraments performed by clergy who had compromised during persecution led to long-term divisions within North African Christianity.
