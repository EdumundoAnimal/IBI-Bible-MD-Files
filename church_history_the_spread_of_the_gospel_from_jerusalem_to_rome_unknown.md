# Church History: The Spread of the Gospel from Jerusalem to Rome
**Author:** [[Unknown]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
This document consists of study notes analyzing the historical development and spread of Christianity from its Jewish roots in Jerusalem into the Roman-Hellenistic world. Key themes include the contrast between Jewish and Greek thought processes (Hebrew linear/concrete vs. Greek cyclical/abstract), the influence of Hellenism on early Christian theology, and the structural support provided by the Roman Empire (Pax Romana) for the expansion of the early church. The notes detail the background of Paul, the role of Acts as a record of the gospel's expansion, and the challenges faced in bridging Jewish and Gentile cultures.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Hebraic vs. Hellenistic Thought
A comparative analysis contrasting the concrete, verb-oriented, and holistic nature of Jewish faith against the abstract, noun-oriented, and dualistic nature of Greek philosophical thought.

### Theme: Expansion of the Gospel
Historical exploration of how the early church moved beyond its original 'cocoon' in Judaism to reach the wider Roman Empire, facilitated by roads, stability, and the missionary efforts of figures like Paul.

### Theme: Contextual Theology
The necessity of navigating the synthesis between original Hebrew roots and the prevailing Hellenistic culture, exemplified by the work of authors like Luke and the challenges faced by early Christians in Antioch.
