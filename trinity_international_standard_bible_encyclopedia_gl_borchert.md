# Trinity (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia)
**Author:** [[G.L. Borchert]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article explores the historical and theological development of the doctrine of the Trinity, focusing on major figures and councils. It traces the influence of Augustine, who emphasized the unity of the divine essence, often at the expense of distinctness in the divine relations. The text contrasts this with the work of Richard of St. Victor, who proposed a model based on the analogy of love, and Thomas Aquinas, who developed the concept of subsistences through acts of understanding and willing. It also notes the historical tension surrounding the filioque clause and the schism between Eastern and Western churches. The provided text serves as a scholarly overview of how different theologians struggled to balance divine unity with the distinctness of the three Persons.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Trinitarianism
The theological framework centered on the divine essence, which prioritizes unity to the point where the distinction of persons becomes secondary or conceptually challenging.

### Theme: Analogy of Love
Richard of St. Victor's approach that views the Trinity as a communal expression of love, requiring a plurality of persons for charity to be perfected.

### Theme: Relational Subsistence
The Thomistic synthesis that views the divine Persons as subsistences defined by internal acts of divine understanding and will.
