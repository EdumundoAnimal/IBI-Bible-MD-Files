# Trinity (International Standard Bible Encyclopedia)
**Author:** [[G.L. Borchert]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The article discusses the historical development of Trinitarian theology, moving from St. Augustine's Neo-Platonic influence to the scholastic contributions of Richard of St. Victor and Thomas Aquinas. It examines the distinction between the persons of the Trinity and the divine essence, noting how medieval councils like Toledo and Lateran IV addressed the filioque clause and the nature of relations in God. The text concludes by mentioning the impact of the Reformation on Trinitarian discourse.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Augustinian Trinitarianism
Augustine’s approach centered on Neo-Platonic simplicity, struggling to balance the absolute oneness of the divine essence with the plurality of the three persons, often leading to potential ambiguity regarding their distinctness.

### Theme: Scholastic Development
Later theologians like Richard of St. Victor expanded the model by suggesting that love itself necessitates a plurality of persons, moving away from simple substance-based definitions toward relational ones.

### Theme: The Filioque Clause
A theological and ecclesiastical point of contention concerning the procession of the Holy Spirit, contributing to the Great Schism between Eastern and Western churches.
