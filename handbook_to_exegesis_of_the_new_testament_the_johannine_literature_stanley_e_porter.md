# Handbook to Exegesis of the New Testament: The Johannine Literature
**Author:** [[Stanley E. Porter]]

## 1. Cleaned Document Text
The Johannine literature, comprising the Gospel of John and the Apocalypse of Revelation, presents a complex intersection of genre, theology, and historical context. Scholarly consensus highlights the unique dualistic worldview within these texts, wherein the world is depicted as being under the power of evil, awaiting the triumph of the Lord. Distinct from other New Testament writings, the Johannine literature utilizes specific imagery, such as the Lamb in the vision of the throne, to illustrate the sacrificial nature of salvation and the necessity of 'bearing witness'. The text further examines the provenance of these works, suggesting they may have been shaped by the post-70 CE trauma of Jewish-Christian separation, where confession of Christ led to expulsion from the synagogue. Linguistically, the Fourth Gospel is noted for its limited, repetitive vocabulary, utilizing central concepts of belief, knowledge, and love to convey the underlying message of divine revelation.

## 3. Thematic Analysis Matrices
### Theme: Johannine Dualism
The theological framework that sets the world under the power of evil, while emphasizing the inevitable triumph of the coming Lord.

### Theme: Provenance and Historical Crisis
The argument that the Johannine literature reflects the trauma of expulsion from the synagogue for those who confessed Christ as the Messiah.

### Theme: Theological Purpose
The writings served to persuade readers that Jesus is the Messiah and that through faith, they may have eternal life.

### Theme: Literary Characteristic
The use of a limited, repetitive, yet profound Greek vocabulary focusing on themes of belief, witness, love, and knowledge.
